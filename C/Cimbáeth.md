``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Cimbáeth was an Irish hero who appears in legend as an early king of Ireland, remembered under the variant spellings Cimbaoth and Kimbay. Chronicle history opens near his day, and he is dated to about AD 300. With his kinsmen Áed Ruad and Díthorba, named brothers in some tellings and cousins in others, he agreed that each man would govern a seven year turn, so that the three terms formed a twenty-one-year cycle. A council of poets, nobles, and druids watched over the pact, and seven magicians, poets, and lords of Ulster guaranteed that the agreement was kept. The rotation held until Áed died. His daughter Macha Mong Rua then claimed his turn. Cimbáeth and Díthorba refused her and met her in war. Macha carried the day. She married Cimbáeth and cut down Díthorba. After the wedding, Macha held power over her husband and obliged him to raise the royal fort of Emain Macha.

:::
```
