``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Calan Mai, also Dydd Calan Mai and Galan Mai, is the Welsh term for May Day. The day marks the beginning of summer in Wales. It was never kept to the scale of the Goidelic feasts of Beltaine. In early Welsh tales Gwynn ap Nudd and Gwythyr fab Greidawl contended. Each year the two rivals vied for the fair Creiddylad on this day. Bonfires burned across the land until the nineteenth century. Ghosts wandered the countryside and the dyn hysbys grew strong. The festival was held opportune for courtship and the regeneration of nature.

:::
```
