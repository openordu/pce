``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Carmina Gadelica is a collection of Gaelic prayers, charms, and invocations. Alexander Carmichael gathered these texts. The verses call on saints such as Bride, Columba, Peter, Paul, and Michael. They also invoke Christ and the Trinity. This mix points to a pre-Reformation source for some pieces. Carmichael drew most of his material from Roman Catholic groups. The words also stayed current among Protestants, though less often. The main aim of the texts is to give safety. Many pieces deal with healing. They try to prevent or cure the evil eye. The works also hold much knowledge about the healing use of plants. This care covers household members and farm animals. Some charms guard the home and its tools. The collection mirrors daily work, seasons, festivals, and the human life cycle. Carmichael captured a spoken tradition. Teachers and clergy saw these charms as mere superstition. Fear of shame made many informants nervous. His calm and polite manner won their trust. He wrote down the name of each informant. This method followed his teacher John Francis Campbell. Critics dislike his habit of editing passages for print. That choice was normal for his period.

:::
```
