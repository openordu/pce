``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Colum Cille, Irish Columb Cille, means dove of the church. MacKillop counts thirty-two saints named Colum or Columba. The best-known became the patron of Christianity among Scottish Gaels. He was born of the Ui Neill kindred at Gartan in Donegal around 521. He was baptized Crimthann, but one story says an angel gave him his church-dove name. He studied at Moville and Clonard, then lived as a deacon at Glasnevin. After a plague around 544 he founded Doire, later Derry, where the place-name Doire Choluim Chille honours him. Irish tradition credits him with thirty-eight monasteries, and his houses became known for copying manuscripts. A quarrel over a copied psalter or the rights of sanctuary was said to have fomented a war. He left Ireland and in 563 founded Iona on the island the Gaels called I-Choluim Chille. From there he ministered to the settlers of Dal Riada and preached to the Picts. His converts included Bridei, a king of the Great Glen. Legend adds a Loch Ness monster that sank at his command and a many-eyed beast of Lough Swilly that he slew. He came back to Ireland in 575 to speak for the Irish of Dal Riada, and in 585 to defend the bardic order from banishment. He died on Iona in 597. His bones went to Ireland in 878, were stolen by Danes in 1127, and were then lost. Adomnan wrote his life a century later, and the eulogy Amra Choluimb Chille is often named the most ancient surviving composition in Irish letters. His feast day of 9 June is kept in Scotland and Ireland, and in Australia and New Zealand too.

:::
```
