::: magazinestyle
Comala is a character in Macpherson's Ossian, the literary epic of 1760. She is the daughter of the villainous Stamo. Her story binds her to Fingal, the poem's hero-king, whose love she gains and mourns. Macpherson presented his verse as his translation of an ancient Gaelic bard. Readers of the day took Comala for a true figure of Celtic legend. She is instead a creation of the eighteenth century, one of the Ossianic inventions that reshaped Europe's picture of the Celtic past.

:::
