``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Cailleac Buillia is a spelling the Irish poet W. B. Yeats used. He wrote the name this way for the famous Celtic hag. The form points the reader toward the fuller headword Cailleach Bhéirre. That figure is the sovereignty goddess of the Beare peninsula. She approaches her suitor as a repulsive elder who begs affection. Love transforms her into a radiant maiden. She endures a series of seven youthful cycles. Every partner ages toward death while she remains fresh. People tie her mostly to the Munster coast of Ireland. Some situate her instead at Slieve Daeane, a hill near the town of Sligo. Yeats retold her tale in his own poetic voice. Scholars still debate exactly who she stands for.

:::
```
