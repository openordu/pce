::: magazinestyle
Cadwaladr is a name that several figures have borne across Welsh history. The word draws on cad, which means battle in Welsh. The title also holds the sense of gwaladr, one who arranges or orders. The most celebrated bearer was the prince and saint of Gwynedd. People called him Cadwaladr Fendigaid, which translates as the blessed. Cadwaladr Fendigaid died at Rome in the year 664 or 687. His father was Cadwallon, the noted seventh-century leader. The fame of Cadwaladr carried through the legend of northern Wales.

:::
