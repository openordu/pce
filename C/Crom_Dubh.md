``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Crom Dubh is a legendary chieftain of pre-Christian Ireland. His name means black crescent in Irish. Oral tradition made him out of the old god Crom Crúaich. They gave the god human shape and a human story. Saint Patrick overcame him as the faith spread through the island. An older tale keeps the contest in a different key. Patrick had received three bulls. To Crom Dubh's mind he had been shy in thanks, saying only thanks to god as each beast arrived. Patrick set his words on three paper slips and weighed them against the bulls. The totals matched, and the chieftain converted at once. His defeat lives on in the calendar. Domhnach Chrom Dubh, his Sunday, is another name for Lughnasa, the Garland Sunday. It falls on the final Sunday of July or the first of August. That is the day of the Croagh Patrick climb. Many readers see in Patrick's royal adversary the elder god Crom Cruach wearing a human face.

:::
```
