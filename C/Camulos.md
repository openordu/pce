``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Camulos is a British god. His name opens the place-name Camulodunon, today Colchester in Essex, and the compound holds the sense of the fort of Camulos. Koch records the god's name extensively across the Continent, in inscriptions from Iberia, including Galicia, as far east as Galatia. Camulodunon was a great Iron Age stronghold, raised before the Romans came. It started as the main stronghold of the Trinovantes and later became their civitas under Rome. Around the year ad 5 the Catuvellauni leader Cunobelinos took the site and raised a new capital at Sheepen Farm. Straight dykes between the rivers Colne and Roman enclosed a territory of thirty-one square kilometres, and no other oppidum in Britain matched its size. Camulodunon became the chief mint of Cunobelinos, whose coins carry the name in short forms such as CAM, CAMV, and CAMVL. When Claudius invaded in ad 43, the settlement became the seat of the new province of Britannia. A colonia for veteran soldiers was founded after ad 49, and a temple honoured the divinized Emperor Claudius. The temple and the seizure of land for colonists fed the revolt of Boudicca in ad 60 or 61, when the colony was destroyed and its people killed. Camulodunon recovered slowly, and London took over as the capital of the province. The invading Romans linked Camulos to their war god Mars. Some scholars pair Camulos with the Irish ancestor Cumhall, the sire of Fionn Mac Cumhaill.
```
