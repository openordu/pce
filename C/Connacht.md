``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Connacht is the most north-western province of Ireland. Its far west is the Atlantic and its east border the Shannon. The modern counties are Galway, Mayo, Sligo, Leitrim, and Roscommon. Of the four provinces it is the most westerly and the smallest, about 6,610 square miles. The other three are Leinster, Munster, and Ulster. Their modern bounds date from the 17th century. Before the conquest it stood, as Cóiced Connacht, it stood as one of five provinces, when Mide or Meath counted separately or Munster counted as two. The name comes from the Connachta, dynasties tracing descent from Conn of the Hundred Battles, who held land mainly west of the Shannon with their royal seat at Crúachu in Roscommon. Tradition usually derives the province's name from Conn Cétchathach. Ptolemy's geography of the second century records the region roughly as the Nagnatae. The term also names three chief dynasties. These are the Uí Briúin, Uí Ailello, and Uí Fhiachrach. Another early name for the country of the Connachta and their neighbours is Leth Cuinn, the half of Conn, marking the northern half of Ireland. In the Táin Bó Cuailnge the ruler of Connacht is Queen Medb, whose royal residence is Cruachain in Co. Roscommon. For much of that narrative the words Medb and Connacht work almost as one. Elsewhere in the Ulster Cycle the great hero of the province is Cet mac Mágach. Myth grants the land to Sreng once the Fir Bolg fall in the first fight at Mag Tuired. Other notable rulers include Cathal Mór of the Wine-Red Hand, a personification of the province, along with Crimthann Cass, Eógan, Guaire, and Lairgnéan. Over the fairies of Connacht rules Finnbheara, who resides at Cnoc Mheadha near Tuam, Co. Galway. Macpherson's Ossian of 1760 gives Alenecma as its name for Connacht. In Irish the phrase Iar Chonnacht, remote western Connacht, names the barony of Moycullen, a district now counted within Connemara. The Annals of Connacht appeared in Martin Freeman's edition in Revue Celtique, volumes 50 and 51, in 1933 and 1934.
```
