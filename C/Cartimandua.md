``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Cartimandua is a queen of the Brigantes, a powerful tribe of northern Britain. She ruled during the Roman invasion in the first century. Tacitus records her long reign in his histories. The Brigantes held most of the northern frontier zone. Their lands included York and the country around Hadrian's Wall. Roman armies kept garrisons in forts along that line. Cartimandua kept a pro-Roman policy to hold her power. This policy sparked a civil war between her two husbands. Her first husband Venutius turned against her. She chose her second husband Vellocatus to rule beside her. Rome backed the queen and helped her keep the throne. Tribal identity survived strongly under Roman rule. Seven dedications to the goddess Brigantia survive today. A reigning woman was not unique among the Britons. Boudica led her own tribe in the same age.

:::
```
