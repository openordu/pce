``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Cyfarwyd is the Welsh word for a storyteller. The term links etymologically to knowledge, guidance, and perception. It descends from a Proto-Celtic root that means to know and to see. An early Welsh plural form gives the sense of guides. Those guides once traced the old boundary lines of a landholding. Later use of the word means tale or narrative. The cyfarwyd was the guide, the expert, and the well-informed person. He then became the storyteller of medieval Wales. Welsh bardic triads tie the poets to this body of lore. Sources say a cyfarwyd could relate tales in the king's hall after a feast. His stock of tales and his methods live on in the Mabinogi. Giraldus Cambrensis names Bleddri among the famous storytellers of Wales. The storyteller is not counted among the officers of the royal court in the law texts.

:::
```
