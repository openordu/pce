``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Cynddylan fab Cyndrwyn was a Welsh prince of Powys in the 7th century. He fought as an ally of Penda, king of Mercia. In 642 his side slew Oswald of Northumbria at the battle of Maserfelth. Cynddylan fell on 15 November 655, most likely at the battle of Winwæd. Two poems record him. Marwnad Cynddylan is a 71-line elegy sent to a king of Gwynedd. Canu Heledd is a cycle of 113 carvings spoken in the grief of his sister Heledd. The cycle shows a land ruined after his fall. His kinsmen, the Cyndrwynyn, do not appear among the kings of Powys after the 9th century. Later rulers there traced descent from the line of Cadell. MacKillop heads the entry with the alias forms Cyndallan and Kyndylan. He calls him prince of Pengwern in Powys, a man of the late sixth and early seventh centuries. The unnamed ninth book of poems once given to Llywarch Hen carries his story. Heledd, taken to be his sister, speaks the surviving poems. She mourns his great hall, a place once filled with life and now left ruined and empty. The central poem, Stafell Cynddylan, keeps that grief in its very title. Ifor Williams printed the text with notes in Canu Llywarch Hen at Cardiff in 1935.
```
