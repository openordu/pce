``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Crimthann is one of the commonest personal names of early Ireland. The Irish word behind it means fox. MacKillop counts kings of Munster, a crowd of Fianna figures, and a saint among the bearers. The best known is probably the pre-Patrician high king Crimthann Mór mac Fidaig. The saint Colum Cille carried this name before he took his Christian name. A Leinster king of the same name was raised to power by Conn Cétchathach. That king fought both Conn and Cumhall, and their war provoked the battle of Cnucha. The name also appears as Crimthan, Criomthann, Cremthann, and Criofan. Another bearer is the ward of Diarmait whose story the Becfola episode tells. The high king of that name died through the sorcery of a supernatural woman. His nephew, the son of Lugaid Riab nDerg, died at Cúchulainn's hand in another tale.

:::
```
