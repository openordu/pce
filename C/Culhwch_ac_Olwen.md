``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Culhwch is a Welsh prince at the centre of a famous tale. The story carries his name together with that of the maiden Olwen. A jealous stepmother places a destiny upon him with a solemn oath. Her words doom him to a dangerous quest. He must find Olwen, and the giant her father makes the task harder by setting impossible requirements. King Arthur joins his kinsman on the road to fulfilment and hunts a great boar named Twrch Trwyth. That hunt already lived in the ninth century as an oral tale. It also generated place-name lore. The name Carn Cabal, read as horse's hoof, is explained as a cairn named for Arthur's dog Cafall. The same hunt binds Culhwch to the topographical wonders of Historia Brittonum. Bedwyr and Cai appear as Arthur's chief companions. An old verse and the Life of Cadoc share that ranking.

Culhwch is a rich store of early Welsh tradition. Its language matches Late Old Welsh usage. That usage sets the work apart from the four branches of the medieval Mabinogi tales. It keeps a rare verb for said that appears almost nowhere else. Scholars link the text to the Triads. Bromwich and Evans date the written redaction to the closing decades of the eleventh century. They set it near the year one thousand one hundred. Pigs act as both totem and cause of doom. The oath tyghaf tyghet, or I swear a destiny, drives the whole reckoning. The account of the boar grew from older oral tradition.

The dictionary rounds out the frame of Culhwch ac Olwen. Cilydd wedded Goleuddydd, who bore the boy in a pig-run and died soon after. The new wife laid the oath on her stepson. Culhwch's arrival at court echoes the arrival of Lug Lamfhota at Nuadu's hall in the battle tale of Mag Tuired. Glewlwyd Gafaelfawr the porter at first shut the gate against him. Cynddelig the guide, Gwrhyr who read every tongue, and Menw the shape-changer joined Cai and Bedwyr on the road. Custennin the herdsman was won over with a gold ring, and his wife proved to be the sister of Goleuddydd. The giant hurls three poisoned stone spears at the party, and each throw comes back on the thrower. He yields only to forty tasks. The great boar Twrch Trwyth carries razor and shears and comb between his ears. The hunt drives him from Ireland to Wales and on to Brittany and Cornwall. The razor and the shears come free at the Severn estuary, and the comb falls in Cornwall. Ysbaddaden has his match in the Irish Balor, whose eyelids others had to prop open.
```
