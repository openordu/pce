``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Culhwch is a Welsh prince at the centre of a famous tale. The story carries his name together with that of the maiden Olwen. A jealous stepmother places a destiny upon him with a solemn oath. Her words doom him to a dangerous quest. He must find Olwen, and the giant her father makes the task harder by setting impossible requirements. King Arthur joins his kinsman on the road to fulfilment and hunts a great boar named Twrch Trwyth. That hunt already lived in the ninth century as an oral tale. It also generated place-name lore. The name Carn Cabal, read as horse's hoof, is explained as a cairn named for Arthur's dog Cafall. The same hunt binds Culhwch to the topographical wonders of Historia Brittonum. Bedwyr and Cai appear as Arthur's chief companions, a ranking shared with an old verse and with the Life of Cadoc.

Culhwch is a rich store of early Welsh tradition. Its language matches Late Old Welsh usage, which sets the work apart from the four branches of the medieval Mabinogi tales. It keeps a rare verb for said that appears almost nowhere else. Scholars link the text to the Triads. Bromwich and Evans date the written redaction to the closing decades of the eleventh century, about the year one thousand one hundred. Pigs act as both totem and cause of doom, and the oath tyghaf tyghet, or I swear a destiny, drives the whole reckoning. The account of the boar grew from older oral tradition.

:::
```
