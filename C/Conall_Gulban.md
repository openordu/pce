``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Conall Gulban was a son of Niall Noigiallach, the man of nine hostages, and founded the kingdom of Tír Chonaill in County Donegal. The byname means beak or sting. In T. F. O'Rahilly's reconstruction Conall and his brothers Eógan and Énna were identical with the three Collas who razed the Ulster capital Emain Macha. Conall led the Clann Conaill in his own right. The rise of the two north-western kingdoms, Tír Chonaill under Conall and Tír Eógain in Tyrone under Eógan, marked the political map of early Ireland. Oral tradition adds a heroic death. A giant carried off Eithne Uchtsholas, daughter of Leinster's king, and Conall pursued the abductor to Ben Bulben in County Sligo, a mountain whose name, corrupted from Beinn Ghulbain, preserves his own. There the pursuit killed him. His descendant was the saint Colum Cille. Tradition also places his haunting presence at the falls of Assaroe.

:::
```
