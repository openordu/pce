Cornwall/Kernow is the southwesternmost part of Britain, long a home of the Brythonic Celtic language Cornish. Around 1100 the tongue ran from the river Tamar to Land's End, and close to twenty thousand of the county's roughly twenty-one thousand people spoke it. The centuries after saw Cornish give ground, retreating westwards until only in the far west kept it alive into the early modern period. By about 1800 the language was almost gone, but scholars such as Henry Jenner then worked to revive Cornish in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.

:::
