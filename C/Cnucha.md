``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Cnucha carries a double story of figure and hill. As a person she is an obscure heroine or goddess of Ireland. Cnucha nursed the boy who grew into Conn of the Hundred Battles. A fever took her life. Her father Connad laid her to rest in ground near Dublin. The burial spot took the name Castleknock in her honor. The same name belongs to the ground itself. Older Irish cnuchac means a hillock. The early place-name Cnucha matches the modern Castle Knock. Castle Knock lies four miles west of the centre of Dublin. This hill is the site of the great battle of the Fenian Cycle. Cumhall, the father of Fionn mac Cumhaill, was killed in that fight. The early tale Fotha Catha Chnucha, a title meaning the cause of that battle, tells how the fight began.

:::
```
