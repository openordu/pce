::: magazinestyle
Classical commentators matter to Celtic studies because they left the earliest outside accounts of Celtic peoples. Greek and Roman writers described Celtic societies, religions, and customs from direct contact or close in time. Their testimony often stands alone, so scholars weigh it with care when reconstructing early Celtic belief. MacKillop lists the most helpful ancient authors for Celtic culture, religion, and myth. The group includes Julius Caesar, Posidonius, Strabo, Tacitus, Polybius, Diodorus Siculus, and Ptolemy. It also includes Pliny the Elder, Ammianus Marcellinus, Solinus, and Eusebius. Caesar recorded the Gauls from his own campaigns. Posidonius travelled among Celtic peoples and shaped later Greek accounts. Strabo and Ptolemy preserved geography, while Tacitus and Ammianus wrote later but valuable history.

:::
