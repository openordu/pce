``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Cesair is Ireland's first settler in the Book of Invasions. In the Mythological Cycle she rules as queen over Ireland's first invasion. Writers spell her name Cesair, Cessair, Ceasair, Cesara, or Kesair. The account was set down only after Ireland turned to Christianity. The scribes were monks who wanted to present the island as a sacred land. They wove Irish myth together with biblical events. Lebor Gabala Erenn names Bith her father, Birren her mother, and Noah her grandfather. Denied the Ark after disgrace at home, she fled the Flood by sailing from the Holy Land to Inis Fail. Noah stored beasts in his vessel, while Cesair loaded hers with people, fifty women and three men. She arrived in Ireland four days ahead of the Flood. Another account lands her at Dun na mBarc forty days before the Flood. The waters never covered that ground. Her path wound about. She left Meroe and sailed the length of the Nile. She then came toward the Caspian and Cimmerian seas and over the drowned Alps. Nine days carried her to Spain and nine more brought her to Ireland. She came ashore at Bantry Bay in County Cork. There she portioned the soil among her people. With only three men she made up three bands, each containing a dozen women and a single man. Her father Bith and Ladra, her brother and helmsman, died from overmuch sexual strain. Fintan mac Bóchra, the one man left and a patron of poets, ran from the pursuing women. Feeling inadequate, he escaped by taking salmon form. Abandoned, Cesair died of a broken heart. Her women bore names of founding mothers, among them German, Espa, Alba, Traige, and Gothiam. Their presence stands for a compact of all the world's peoples. Many obscure place-names trace to her retinue. Others echo the goddesses of old Ireland, Banba and Búanann among them. Some writers treat Cesair as a land goddess in her own right. She might be a child of the soil goddess Banba. She may also be Berba, the spirit of the River Barrow, and Heriu, a name close to Ériu. So Cesair could embody each of those divinities. Each of the three men claimed a chief bride. Fintan wed Cesair, Bith wed Bairrind, and Ladra wed Banba, putting Cesair beside Banba, Fódla, and Ériu. Her name serves as a poetic synonym for Ireland.
```
