::: magazinestyle
"Cattle Raid of" serves as an index pointer to the táin class of Old Irish narrative titles. Táin stands as the conventional opening word in the titles of a group of Old Irish stories about cattle raids. The finest achievement of classical Irish literature is Táin Bó Cuailnge, called the Cattle Raid of Cooley in English, and it forms the key text of the Ulster Cycle. Its central action sets Queen Medb of Connacht against the Ulster hero Cúchulainn, while the White Bull Finnbennach faces the Brown Bull Donn Cuailnge. The earliest texts survive in the Book of the Dun Cow, from about 1100, and the Book of Leinster, from about 1160.

:::
