``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Coel appears in the poems of Taliesin as a 6th-century king of Aeron in Ayrshire, Scotland. Welsh tradition calls him a British ruler with two agnomens, Hen meaning old and Odebog meaning adulterous or hawkwood. Popular assertion makes him the original of the nursery rhyme king, Old King Cole. Popular etymology makes him the eponymous founder of Colchester. Medieval legend adds that he fathered Elen, the purported Welsh-born St Helen and mother of Constantine, the third-century Christian emperor. One older reading, recorded by Monaghan, treats Coel as an ancient Celtic god. Writers later shrank that god into a plain human king. That reading now stands as one tradition among several. The rhyme and the town name keep his memory alive either way.

:::
```
