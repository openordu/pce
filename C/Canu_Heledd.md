::: magazinestyle
Canu Heledd stands among the lengthiest and most closely unified of the early Welsh englynion. This cycle dates from the ninth or tenth century. Its narrator Heledd is the last survivor of the royal house of Powys. She laments the passing of her kingdom and above all King Cynddylan, who reigned early in the seventh century. Several commentators suspect an earlier version that the anonymous author shaped to still-hard conditions two centuries later. Heledd holds herself to blame for the ruin of Cynddylan's court at Pengwern in Powys. Her figure renews the old Celtic idea that a goddess personifies the land. The moving centre of the cycle, Stafell Cynddylan, remains especially resonant.

:::
