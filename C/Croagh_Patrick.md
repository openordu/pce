``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Croagh Patrick is the holy mountain of Ireland. The ridge of quartzite stands about 2,510 feet high above Clew Bay. It rises six miles WSW of Westport in Co. Mayo. Its sharp outline cuts a triangle from the shoreline. Neolithic ruins rest on the summit from ages before the Celtic arrivals. The Irish form Cruach Phádraig means Patrick's stack. Older books call the peak Cruachan Aigle for Aigle. That man of legend died on the slopes with rocks inside his pockets. Patrick kept his forty-day fast on the top. There he is said to have driven the serpents out of Ireland. The demon Corra fled from him into a mere at the mountain's south foot. That water is Lough na Corra. Several spots on the slopes hold a reputation for sanctity. The old festival climb survived in Christian dress. On the final Sunday of July, a day joined to Lughnasa, tens of thousands climb the peak. Some go barefoot, and some climb on their knees. Local speech calls the day Crom Dubh's Sunday, Domhnach Chrom Dubh. Popular usage names the mountain The Reek.

:::
```
