``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Cathbad is a druid of the Ulster Cycle, the early Irish tales of the kingdom of the Ulaid. His social function is generally given as druí, the druid. Scholars use him as a test case for how far the early Christian Irish literature opens a window on pre-Christian society and religion. The essay of Jackson, The Oldest Irish Tradition, made him a standard example. The Táin Bó Cuailnge carries the tale of Cú Chulainn's boyhood deeds. In it Cathbad inspires the hero to take arms at an unusually early age by declaring the day auspicious. In Longas mac nUislenn he foretells the birth of Derdriu and the tragedy she will bring. He also appears in Mesca Ulad and in Fled Bricrenn. He is the father of Findchoem, a noblewoman of amusing envy who is the wife of the contending hero Conall Cernach. The older stories give him a darker past. He longed for the bookish Assa, murdered her guardians to reach her, caught her as she bathed, forced himself upon her, and made her his taken concubine. A version of Conchobar's begetting story, Compert Conchobuir, shows him as a fénnid, a tribeless warrior, as well as a druid. He compels Nes, the future king's mother, into the match. The tale allows that another lover, or else a worm swallowed in a drink, could have impregnated Nes. Cathbad, as druid and soothsayer, decides the day of the birth. He arranges the birth to fall on the same day as the birth of Jesus, an event he also foresees. In this way he fixes the destiny of the infant king. After this he instructs Conchobar's champion Cú Chulainn and sires Findchoem.
```
