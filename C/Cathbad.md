``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Cathbad was a druid in the stories of the Ulster Cycle. He served the king Concobar mac Nessa at his seat of power. He longed for Assa, a bookish maiden, and had her guardians murdered so that he might reach her. The slaughter wrecked her peaceful life and turned the girl into a fighter who went among men under the name Nessa, that is, ungentle. Cathbad would not be put off. He caught Nessa at a moment, while she bathed and was stripped of her weapons, forced himself upon her, and then held her close as his taken concubine. Yet Nessa was keener than he. Skilled in the art of augury, she saw that a worm drifting in a wineglass would give her a hero for offspring, and she drank it down, in time conceiving the coming king Concobar mac Nessa. Cathbad afterwards instructed Concobar's champion Cuchulainn and sired the heroine Findchoem, who employed that same manner of begetting. He also turns up in the tale of Deirdre, whose birth he foretold, warning that she would carry sorrow to Ulster. Some versions of the birth-tale make him the actual father of Conchobar rather than a mere court seer. His daughter Findchóem became mother of the great champion Conall Cernach. The poet Macpherson cast a druid named Caithbath in his Ossian poems of 1760. That character appears drawn from Cathbad.

:::
```
