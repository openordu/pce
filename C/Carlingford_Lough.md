``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Carlingford Lough is a sea inlet of striking size. It runs ten miles long and two miles wide. The water separates County Down from County Louth. Down lies in Northern Ireland, Louth in the Republic. Dundalk stands nine miles to the southwest. The Newry Canal links the lough to Lough Neagh. The town of Carlingford sits on the southern shore of Louth. Much of the great Cattle Raid of Cooley unfolds on this water. Shallow river crossings mattered to the early Celts before ferries and bridges came. Clashes at fords appear in old tales. A fight between Cuchulainn and Ferdiad in the Tain Bo Cuailnge stands as the chief of them. Ritona stood among the Continental Celtic goddesses of river crossings and was honoured at Trier in modern Germany. Irish and Scottish Gaelic folklore also knows the washer at the ford. In Celtic place naming the word lough anglicizes the Irish loch and marks a lake or an arm of the sea. Lough Corrib illustrates the pattern. It spans 70 square miles across Connacht in counties Galway and Mayo. It ranks as the second largest body of water on the island. The early Irish form Loch nOirbsen hints at Oirbsiu or Oirbsiu Mór, a flood-linked title of the sea god Manannán mac Lir. A later oral account claims that Manannán drowned at that spot. Lug Lámfhota was thought to keep a dwelling beneath its surface. The surrounding country beside Connemara still carries a wealth of stories.

:::
```
