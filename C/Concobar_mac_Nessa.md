``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Concobar mac Nessa is a ruler in the Ulster cycle. In one tale a woman refused the poet Aithirne. Aithirne then composed a satire against her. The verse raised blisters so severe that it killed her. In reply the warriors of Ulster set fire to Aithirne's house. The greedy poet burned with his home. The sagas do not show Concobar as an ideal king. He rules a warring province rather than all of Ireland. The hero Cú Chulainn overshadows him in his own court. Poets compared the pair to Agamemnon and Achilles. His mother Nes, and not a far-reaching father, placed him on the throne. The women of the tales also wrong him. He took the young Derdiru against her will. Her folk, led by Fergus, then went over to queen Medb. His legend ties his life to Christ. His birth matches the birth of Jesus, and his death the crucifixion.

:::
```
