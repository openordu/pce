``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Concobar mac Nessa rules Ulster from Emain Macha through most of the action in the Ulster Cycle. Some translations call its stories the Red Branch tales after his palace. The sagas do not show him as an ideal king. Cú Chulainn dwarfs him in fame. Poets likened the pair to Agamemnon and Achilles. His mother Nes placed him on the throne. One tale shows his harsh rule. The poet Aithirne demanded a woman, and her refusal cost her life under his satire. Ulster warriors burned the poet inside his own house in revenge. The birth tales fuse his story to the great cattle raid. Ness, daughter of Eochaid Sálbuide, carried him. The druid Cathbad told her the hour favored begetting a king on a queen, and he fathered the child himself. Another version names Fachtna Fáthach as his father. Fergus mac Róich wanted Ness for a wife. She agreed on one term. Fergus must let Concobar hold the kingship for a single year. Ness then worked matters so Fergus never won the throne back. Concobar grew into a popular king known for prudence and sound judgment. Custom gave him the first night with every bride in the realm. Three palaces served him. Cráebruad, the Red Branch, is the famous one. Tête Brec kept his gleaming weapons. Cróebderg, the Ruddy Branch, stored heads taken in battle and other spoils. His lusts mark the darker tales. He pursued the unwilling Deirdre in the tale called Longes mac nUislenn. His killing of the Sons of Uisnech drove his son Cormac Connloinges out of Ulster to Medb of Connacht. Macha's curse, the debility of the Ulstermen, laid him low during the great raid. Some variants blame him for it. In those tellings he goaded Macha into the race that brought the pangs. Cet mac Macha wounded him with Mesgegra's calcified brain ball. The physician Fingein sewed the wound shut. It turned fatal years later when the ball worked loose. A Christian ending adds that he died on Good Friday, just after learning a just man had died. His family spreads wide. His sisters were Deichtine and Findchóem, mother of Conall Cernach. He married four daughters of Eochaid Feidlech. They were Medb, Eithne, Clothra, and later Mugain. His sons included Cormac Connloinges, Cúscraid Menn, Furbaide Ferbend, Follomain, and Glaisne. His daughters included Bláithíne and Fedelm Noichrothach. Several commentators call him a Celtic model for King Arthur. His sword was named Gorm Glas. His chief poet was Ferchertne.
```
