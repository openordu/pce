``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Clonmacnoise is a monastery on the eastern bank of the Shannon in Co. Offaly, thirteen miles south of Athlone. St Ciarán founded it on 25 January 545. It grew into the leading early monastic site of Ireland, one that in church standing only Armagh could rival. In early Christian art and learning it had no equal. Its scribes produced Lebor na hUidre, the early Irish codex Book of the Dun Cow. Raiders, native and foreign, attacked the site countless times. Monks held on until an English force stormed it in 1552. The monastery then lay abandoned. Surviving remains include five high crosses, parts of nine churches, and two round towers. More than five hundred early gravestones survive, many bearing inscriptions. Seven high kings (ard ríthe) found burial here. They include Ruaidri Ua Conchobair, the final ruler of that line, who died in 1198.

:::
```
