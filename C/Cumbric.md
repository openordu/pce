``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Cumbric is a term used by scholars for the Brythonic speech of the early Middle Ages. It belongs to the area between the river Mersey and the Forth-Clyde isthmus. The evidence for it comes from several kinds of sources. These include proper names in Welsh, English, Irish, and Latin records. Two legal words survive from an early legal text, the Laws of the Britons and the Scots. These are galnes and kelchyn. Some place-names borrowed into English also survive. The poetry of the early Welsh Cynfeirdd may preserve further traces. Cumbric is not seen as a distinct language. Old Welsh, Old Breton, Old Cornish, and Cumbric were very close. They were likely hard to tell apart. The term itself is an English form of the Welsh root Cymraeg. Irish glossaries used the word Combrec for any Brythonic speech. Cumbric may have survived late in some places, perhaps in shepherd counting.

:::
```
