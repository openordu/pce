``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Callanish is a prehistoric stone circle in Scotland. It stands on the island of Lewis and Harris, within the Outer Hebrides. The ring of standing stones in the region is well preserved. The great circle was raised by the Megalithic people. They set the pillars some four thousand years before the Celts. The Celts later changed the ring for their own rites. Some call it one of the best preserved dolmens or druidical circles in all of Europe. It stands near Stornoway, sixteen miles to the west, close to the eastern shore. Folk belief holds that the stones shift and move when no one watches. The Lewis Gaelic name for them is Tarsachan Chalanais. That name means the giants of Callanish, drawn from a Norse word for a giant. Every eighteen years the full moon rises behind a woman-shaped hill. The moon crosses over her body and then sets behind her head. Local legend says the stones were giants once. They turned to rock for refusing to accept Christianity.

:::
```
