``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Céadach haunts Irish and Scottish Gaelic folklore from the early nineteenth century. He also appears as Céadach Mór, as Cétach, and as Ceudach in Scottish Gaelic. This quarrelsome interloper in Fenian tales sometimes wears skins and stays eager to join the Fianna. Abartach ranks among his many other names. His best-known tale sends him toward certain death, yet he returns a hero. His wife opposes the war, so Fionn mac Cumhaill must promise to raise sails if her husband is lost. Céadach wins the war yet falls to a blade. Dying, he warns Fionn to keep the sails down. Her breath would unleash ruin across a hundred miles. She carries the body to an island where three brothers fight giants that revive nightly. Her plea wins their magic, and Céadach lives again. In payment he kills every giant, then hides and finds a hag reviving the slain with churn drops. He beheads her.

:::
```
