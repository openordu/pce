``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Celtchair was an Irish warrior. The Celts treated hospitality as a grave duty. Celtchair broke that rule when blood touched the Fidchell board. King Concobar Mac Nessa sat there playing against the hero Cúchulainn. To clear his good name, Celtchair accepted three hard trials. He completed two of them. Then he slew a dread dog that tormented the land. The beast's poisonous blood spattered him and brought his death. His reputation remained unspoiled. Writers spell his name Celtchar, Celtar, or Keltchar, often with the patronymic mac Uithechar or mac Uthechar, and sometimes call him Celtchair Mór, the Great. He ranks among the leading fighters of the Ulster Cycle. Several tales picture him huge and grey, and he may boast of his killings. In the tale of Mac Da Thó's Pig the warrior Cet shamed him, claiming an earlier meeting had left him unmanned. He is the usual owner of the lance Lúin, which craved blood so fiercely that an idle spell meant a rest in a cauldron of black fluid or poison, else its shaft would catch fire. He killed Blai Briugu over an affair with his wife. With his daughter Niam he rid the land of pests. Conganchnes mac Dedad fell there, as did the black hound Dóelchu. A drop of the dead hound's blood ran along his spear and passed through his body.

:::
```
