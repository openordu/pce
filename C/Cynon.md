::: magazinestyle
Cynon is a Welsh name of doubtful older meaning. Some readers take it as great hound or divine hound. Many figures of early Welsh history carry it, saints and kings and heroes alike. One Cynon ruled as king of Gwynedd in the year 817. The best-known tale-bearer is Cynon son of Clydno Eiddyn. In the romance Owain he first tells the hero of a strong knight. That champion throws back every challenger. That report sends Owain on his own quest. A River Cynon also flows in Glamorganshire, running eighteen miles into the Taff.

:::
