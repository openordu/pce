::: magazinestyle
Celtic spiritual belief was never set down in writing. People passed it on through speech, poetry, art, and rite. Scholars must therefore piece the belief together from evidence found far apart in time and place. Most texts come from Ireland, which holds few carved images. Most images come from the Continent, where few Celtic texts survive. The Celts kept a great many gods. Few divinities appear in writing more than once. The Celts also held an Otherworld, a flawless but spare realm that people could enter only at rare moments. They reached it at such festivals as Beltane and Samhain and in border places such as the bogs and the islands. Its residents passed into our world more freely than we could cross into theirs. The Celts did not view the world as two warring sides. They spoke of balance, winter against summer and man against woman, night against day. The goddess of sovereignty stood for abundance and plenty. She kept a dark side that brought death and ruin. Their faith rested on place, with hundreds of divinities known from springs, rivers, hills, and points of land. People saw themselves as keepers of the holy earth.
