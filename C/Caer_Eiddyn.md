``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Caer Eiddyn is the Welsh form that the dictionary enters as a variant of Din Eidyn. Din Eidyn was the great hill stronghold of the Gododdin, a warrior people of the northern British lands. The place could stand beside Dumbarton Rock as a defence of equal fame. Most scholars judge the later Edinburgh Castle to hide this old fort on its rocky peak. The walls of that later fortress now cover the ground where the citadel rose. Caer Eiddyn thus names the same summit that looked toward the Firth of Forth. The title calls up an age when a British kingdom held the ground of Lothian before Scots came.

:::
```
