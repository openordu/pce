``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The name Cong comes from an Irish word for an isthmus. A monastery stood there in County Mayo, at the north-east end of Lough Corrib. Saint Féichín founded it in the 6th century. Austin canons rebuilt it in the 12th century. The site sits on the neck of land that joins Lough Corrib and nearby Lough Mask. Writers sometimes connect it with the nearby plain of Mag Tuired. Ruaidri Ua Conchobair held the high kingship last of all. He entered the monastery in 1183 and died there in 1198. The famous Cross of Cong now rests in the National Museum of Ireland. It survived while the monastery buildings fell. The ruins were partly restored in the 19th century. They stand in the south-east part of the village.

:::
```
