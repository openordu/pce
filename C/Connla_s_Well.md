``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Connla’s Well, also called the Well of the Maiden, is an Irish mythic spot. Whom the site honours stays unclear, be the name that of a fairy or a mortal woman. Early Irish belief treats it as a fount that feeds inspiration and knowledge, a cousin of the Norse well of Mimir. Its place is a riddle too, and it shifts from text to text. It may lie under the sea, in Tír na nóg, the Land of Youth, or in Tír Tairngire, the Land of Promise. One Dindshenchas, a body of place-name lore, puts a well of this name near Tipperary. The site spans the sea floor, the Otherworld, and Irish soil alike. Nine hazel boughs lapped the water with nuts that tumbled into it. Those nuts held wisdom, knowledge, and inspiration. Fat salmon fed on the nuts, and the fish came away carrying mottled marks. Each salmon carried spots that counted the nuts it had taken. To eat the nuts, to swallow the salmon, or to sip the water was said to confer wisdom. All were barred from the well except Nechtan and his three cupbearers. Sinann, granddaughter of the sea god Lir, went there seeking wisdom. She broke some rule of the place. The waters rose against her and took her life. The Shannon bore her body to its own banks, and the river took her name. The river goddess who won the well spent her life as the price. Some traditions make this well and the Well of Segais a single site. Some texts call it the Well of Cóelrind. The motif of wisdom drunk from a special well carries the tag D1811.1.2.

:::
```
