::: magazinestyle
Carrigcleena names two large Irish rocks held sacred to Clidna. The Irish carraig Chliodhna reads plainly as Clidna's rock. This countryside belief endured into the late nineteenth century. The better-known stone, seven miles SSW of Mallow in County Cork, once seemed a door to the Otherworld. People said Clidna dwelt in a greensward set within a circle of stones. Her kindness would spare the district from blighted corn and diseased cattle. Mischievous spirits also kept away. A second stone, tied to Clidna as well, rests near the strand at Inch. This pebble lies two miles southeast of Ross Carbery in County Cork.

:::
