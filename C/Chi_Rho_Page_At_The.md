``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The chi-rho page opens Saint Matthew's Gospel inside the Lindisfarne Gospels.. This book dates from the 600s or early 700s. The manuscript now sits in London at the British Library. Its shelf mark is Cotton Nero D. IV. Old English glosses run beside the Latin text. The gloss godspell translates euangelium. Bishop Eadfrith wrote the book. He ruled from 698 to 721. This work is related to the books of Durrow and Kells. Those books are Irish products of the Columban group. The chi-rho page holds strong Celtic ties. Scholars often cite it for Northumbria's cultural mix. Lindisfarne was founded in 635. The monastery flourished in later centuries. Vikings sacked the island in 793. The name appears in Latin as ecclesiae Lindisfarnensis. Two Welsh names for the island survive. Celtic words for island of healing may lie behind them. The famous page remains a treasure of Old English study.

:::
```
