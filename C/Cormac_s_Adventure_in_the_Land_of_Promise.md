``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Cormac's Adventure in the Land of Promise is the English title of the Old Irish tale Echtrae Cormaic. The text survives in the Book of Ballymote, the Book of Fermoy, and the Yellow Book of Lecan. Cormac mac Airt, high king of Tara and grandson of Conn Cétchathach, meets a stranger at dawn on the rampart. The man carries a silver branch with three golden apples. Its music puts every listener to sleep. Cormac trades the branch for three wishes. The stranger takes his daughter Ailbe, then his son Cairbre Lifechair, and last his wife Eithne. Cormac follows them into a mist and reaches a hall in the otherworld. There a pig will not cook until each guest tells a true story. When Cormac recounts his losses, the meal is ready. His host holds a golden cup that shatters under a lie and seals itself again under the truth. The host proves Eithne, Ailbe, and Cairbre faithful, and the cup becomes whole. He reveals himself as Manannán mac Lir, the sea god. Cormac keeps the branch and the cup for the rest of his reign. After his death both leave Ireland and are seen no more.

:::
```
