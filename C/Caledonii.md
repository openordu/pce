::: magazinestyle
Caledonii is the name for a P-Celtic people of ancient Scotland. The tribe formed one part of the larger Pictish population. Their country stretched from the valley of the River Tay to the Great Glen. The Romans took this name for the whole land, calling it Caledonia. The confederation reached northward to the county of Sutherland. Being P-Celtic, the Caledonii stood nearer to the Britons than to their distant kin. The Britons are the ancestors of the modern Welsh. The other group, the Q-Celtic Scots, came into the country from Ireland.

:::
