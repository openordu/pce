``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Conn of the Hundred Battles ranks among the greatest legendary kings of Ireland. Tradition counts him among the early high kings who shape the island's legendary history. The old lists place his name beside Cormac mac Airt, Mongon, and Guaire as the great rulers remembered in the tales. His full Irish title is Conn Cétchathach. The epithet means of the Hundred Battles, or the Hundred-Fighter. The annals date this shadowy king of Irish pre-history to the second century. Some readings treat him as an ancestor-deity of Irish kings. Others put his name on rulers of the Otherworld. The Lia Fáil, the Stone of Prophecy, speaks first to him. It shows him the kings of his line yet to reign and the coming of St Patrick. He stands as the eponymous ancestor of the Goidels of the midlands. The province of Connacht carries his name. His grandfather is Tuathal Techtmar. His father is Fedlimid Rechtaid or Óenlám Gaba. His mother may be Medb Lethderg. He takes Tara from Cathaír Mór while still young. A later addition says the Fianna of Fionn mac Cumhaill helped him. Once crowned, he walks the ramparts of Tara each day, in case the sidh people or the Fomorians take Ireland unawares. Wars with Dál nAraide, east of Lough Neagh, earn him his title. His great rival is Eógan Mór, also called Mug Nuadat, of Munster. The two split Ireland along Eiscir Riada, the low ridge of mounds that runs from Dublin to Galway Bay. Conn's northern share is Leth Cuinn. He invades Munster and beats Eógan. On his return Eógan stirs revolt and falls at the Battle of Mag Léna, near Tullamore. Accounts of Conn's own death differ. Thirty Ulstermen under Tiobraide Tireach, dressed as women, may have killed him. In Baile in Scáil, the Phantom's Frenzy, he meets the Sovereignty of Ireland and learns his line's future. In Echtrae Airt meic Cuinn a blight strikes the land because Conn has mated with the disreputable Bé Chuma. His earlier wife, Eithne Tháebfhota, a daughter of Cathaír Mór, brought three harvests a year. When Conn's men kill Eochaid Bélbuide, he compensates Fergus mac Léti with land taken from the noble-born mother of one of the killers. She becomes Fergus's slave. The Fenian Cycle often names him. Fionn mac Cumhaill is born in his reign or that of Cathaír Mór. Some versions say Conn killed Cumhall, Fionn's father, over the abduction of Muirenn. His children include Art mac Cuinn and Connla, the youth a fairy woman seduces in Echtrae Conli. A daughter, Sárait, bore the three Cairbres. Cormac mac Airt counts as his grandson. His druid is Máel or Corán. The Lord of the Isles of Clan Donald claimed descent from Conn and from Colla Uais. It celebrates a king who won a hundred battles. In the tradition the name works as a victory title carried by the high king.
```
