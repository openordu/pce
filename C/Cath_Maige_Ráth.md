``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Cath Maige Ráth is the Irish title for the battle narrative of Mag Rath. The dictionary gives this headword a single line and points readers to Buile Shuibne. That tale holds the fuller account, and it makes the battle the turning point of its hero's life. Buile Shuibne is a twelfth-century Irish work in the Cycle of Kings. English readers know it as The Frenzy of Suibne or The Madness of Sweeney. It ends a trilogy of tales about Suibne Geilt, a minor king in seventh-century Ulster. Suibne went mad at the battle fought at Moira in 637. His mad wanderings then fill the rest of the tale. The fuller story of the fight therefore sits inside that text rather than under this title. MacKillop defines rath as a circular earthen rampart, normally timber-reinforced or palisaded, that ringed old Irish homes. More loosely the word names the fortified dwelling itself. That dwelling was the home of an early chief or king, ringed by such a rampart. Rath is the leading element in a great many Irish place-names. In some names it is rendered as royal seat. The word is nearly interchangeable with the older term dún. Irish oral tradition often holds that a rath houses fairies.

:::
```
