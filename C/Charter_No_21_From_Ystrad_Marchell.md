::: magazinestyle
Record number 21 from the abbey at Ystrad Marchell is an early document on an indulgence. That charter matters for the end of the medieval era. Ystrad Marchell was a Cistercian abbey near Welshpool. It lay on flat land three kilometres north of the town. Little remains above ground today. Workmen dug out the church in 1890. The digging showed a grand plan. The church was 62 metres long. Its completion stays uncertain. Stone carvings from the 1200s came to light. These carvings show great skill. A carved capital now sits as the font at Buttington. The community weakened through the late medieval years. By 1528 the buildings lay as ruins. Abbot John ap Rhys sought to stop the decline. He gained the right to issue an Indulgence in the 1520s. Two printed copies of that Indulgence survive. They are the oldest printed Welsh documents. The design came from printed material. The abbey faced money trouble by the 1300s. Monks at Buildwas then watched over it. The house held a Welsh nunnery.
