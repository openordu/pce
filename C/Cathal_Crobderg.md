``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Cathal Crobderg ruled Connacht as its king until his death in 1224. Tradition makes him brother or son of Ruaidri Ua Conchobair, the final high king of Ireland. His byname means the wine-red hand in Irish. Legend traces his start to a union outside marriage. The king's lawful wife tried and failed to end the pregnancy. Relatives carried the infant off to a far district. A wine-red mark on one hand always gave him away. He hid the stain under a glove. Years later a passing stranger told him the throne of Connacht stood empty. He quit the rye fields at once. Showing the birthmark won him the crown. James Clarence Mangan retold these tales in a poem on thirteenth-century Connaught, written about 1845.

:::
```
