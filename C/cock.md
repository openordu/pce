::: magazinestyle
The cock or rooster stands among the symbolic animals of Celtic life. Portrayals pair the bird with the Gaulish Mercury, a mark of sacred rank among the Continental Celts. Temples of northern Britain also held figures of large birds beside human female figures, and the shapes fit the cock. Hens served the table, yet the cock drew its own body of belief. People said its harsh call turned away harm that broke from an unlooked quarter. That guarding force proved greatest in March. A black cock carried luck, while people took a white cock as unlucky. Each Celtic land kept a native name for the bird, such as Irish coileach, Welsh ceiliog, and Breton kilhog.

:::
