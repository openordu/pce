``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Crannóg names an ancient fortified lake dwelling built by hand upon a lake. The word comes from the Irish crann, meaning tree or timber. Celtic peoples made them from brush and ringed each one with fences of wattle. Builders set the platforms on pilings or on a natural island. The water gave the dwellers both defence and drinking supplies. Examples stand in Ireland, Scotland, and Wales. Round houses on these platforms may reach back to times before the Celts. Such sites served people through the earliest Christian centuries, and a few held on into the 1600s. Lough Gara in Co. Roscommon holds more than 300 examples. Rebuilt crannógs stand today at heritage parks. Cragganowen, near Ennis in Co. Clare, Ireland, is among the best known. A second sense of the word names the saint Carantoc.

:::
```
