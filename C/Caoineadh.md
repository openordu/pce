``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Caoineadh is the Irish lament, a keen raised in a strong and broken voice. Its oldest form turns on a strict order of the line, and the grief of the one struck down rises and falls on a known beat. The most celebrated keen mourns the close of the older Gaelic world, and tradition credits it to a poet of the war years. A second celebrated lament is drawn from the hand of a woman whose spirit still stays near, and by the whole of the eighteenth century the word came to mean any song of sorrow. The keen ends it as a personal cry for the old home and the world it lost.

:::
```
