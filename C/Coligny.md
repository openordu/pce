``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Coligny is a site in the east of France, in Burgundy. In 1897, near the settlement of the same name, came to light a large bronze calendar. Its fragments now lie in the Museum of Gallo-Roman Civilization in Lyon. The find is the best picture we have of the Celtic year. The calendar was cut on a great bronze plate. It covers a span of five years and runs by the moon, not the sun. The months count twenty-nine or thirty days, and in the five-year plan two extra months are put in to bring the moon back in step with the sun. Each day holds a small hole for a peg, so that the reader could mark the passing days. Much of the writing is in Gaulish, set out in Roman letters. The month names are the heart of it. They include the mayor whose spring is linked to the Irish Samhain and to the word for summer, and the name that holds the seed of the word for winter. Both the year and its months fall into halves of fifteen days, and the marks for luck and little luck, for the good and the bad, follow the days. The calendar is our only Celtic calendar, and the one calm look into how the old Celts counted time. Its year speaks of a people who divided their time by the moon and by the great turning points of the sun, let at the cold of midwinter, at the start of the warmer months, and at the fall of the leaves.

:::
```
