::: magazinestyle
Christianity reached the Celtic lands through Britain and grew in Ireland. Early missionaries worked there in the fifth century. Patrick the bishop came from Britain to lead the faith. Monasteries rose as centres of teaching and of law. The Irish Church kept close ties to Latin Christendom. Its canon law shaped later Western Church books. The Cáin Adomnáin of 697 limited the harms of warfare. The idea of one pure Celtic Church does not hold. Irish Christians felt they were one group among the West. The synods of Ráith Bressail and Kells aligned Irish practice with the wider Latin world.

:::
