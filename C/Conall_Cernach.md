``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Conall Cernach, also called Cearnach, is a leading hero of the Ulster Cycle whose power and resolve come close to those of Cúchulainn. He was Cúchulainn's foster-brother and almost a twin, his constant companion, and later the avenger of his death. With Cúchulainn and Laoghaire Ulster counts him among its three first champions. His father is the poet Amairgin and his mother is Findchóem. An assault by his uncle nearly killed him as a child, and the name Cernach, of the victories or triumphant, marks his survival. His usual wife is Lendabair, daughter of Eógan mac Durthacht, though Niamh appears elsewhere as his consort. Fedelm the Nine-Times Beautiful left her husband Cairbre Nia Fer for him, and their love child was Fiachna. His son Rathend drowned during a flight from Ercol. The tales give him startling feats. He once swallowed a boar whole, one so large that sixty oxen were needed to pull its carcass. On Medb's prompting he killed a man while sharing that man's wife's bed. He killed Anluan and Mesgegra, Mesgegra's brain became the ball Cet later hurled at Concobar mac Nessa. He appears in the tale of Mac Da Thó's Pig, endures the torments Macha imposes during the Cattle Raid of Cooley, helps Fráech in the Táin Bó Fraich, and joins the feast in Briccriu's hall. His revenge on Lugaidh for Cúchulainn's death was brutal beyond measure. Oral tradition says Connachtmen including Cet killed him in turn at Ballyconnell in County Cavan, a town whose name allegedly derives from him. Some commentators read him as a horned god turned into a man by the storytellers.
```
