``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Continental Celtic covers the ancient Celtic tongues of the mainland. The group holds Gaulish, Celtiberian, and Lepontic. These languages all died out in antiquity. Their records come from coins and stone inscriptions. The scripts they used were uneven. Vowels kept the late Indo-European five-way system. Six diphthongs survive in the oldest records. The sound p was lost in many positions. Celtiberian retained more of the old case endings. These tongues hold the earliest written Celtic evidence. Their grammar informs the study of the whole family.

:::
```
