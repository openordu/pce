``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Champion’s portion names the choice share of meat at a Celtic feast. MacKillop traces the term to Old Irish curadmir and gives Modern Irish curadhmír. The strongest warrior received the curad-mir, the best cut of the beef or pork, often read as the thigh. When two warriors claimed it, they fought at once. The duel could continue to the death. MacKillop calls the winner the hero of the hour and records hero's portion as another name for the custom. Records place the custom around the first century BCE. Two great Irish tales turn on this rule. In the feast of Bricriu, the champion's portion drives the hero to prove himself the boldest. In the story of Mac's pig, Conall Cernach earns the right to carve the great boar. The Greek writer Posidonius set the same scene in Gaul. He reported two warriors dueling for the prized cut. Athenaeus carried his account onward. MacKillop cites Posidonius as proof of the custom's great age. Homer gave Ajax a like honour after victory. Welsh tradition keeps the theme softly. It grants the chief man the best seat and the finest cup.

:::
```
