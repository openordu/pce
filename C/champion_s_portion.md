``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Champion's portion names the choice share of meat at a Celtic feast. The strongest warrior received the curad-mir, the best cut, often read as the thigh. When two warriors claimed it, they fought at once. The duel could continue to the death. Records place the custom around the first century BCE. Two great Irish tales turn on this rule. In the feast of Bricriu, the hero fights to prove himself the boldest. In the story of Mac's pig, Conall Cernach earns the right to carve the great boar. The Greek writer Posidonius set the same scene in Gaul. He reported two warriors dueling for the prized cut. Athenaeus carried his account onward. Homer gave Ajax a like honour after victory. Welsh tradition keeps the theme softly. It grants the chief man the best seat and the finest cup.

:::
```
