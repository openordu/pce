``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
A cúirt was an 18th-century Irish meeting of amateur poets. The old bardic schools collapsed during the 1600s. They lost their patrons and pupils, so cúirteanna took over poetic learning. Most such courts gathered in Munster. A few similar courts met in Ulster. The best-known cúirt stood near Cork at Blarney. Diarmaid Mac Sheáin Bhuidhe founded it around 1700. These courts copied much from the disliked English court. They named sheriffs and issued mock warrants. Poets clashed in jesting contests. One bard sang a lament for his dead horse. As the Irish language weakened, the cúirteanna faded. They fell out of use by 1800.

:::
```
