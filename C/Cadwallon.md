::: magazinestyle
Cadwallon served as a name for several early Welsh figures. The root cad points toward battle in the Welsh tongue. The rest of the title may carry the sense of a scatterer or a ruler. The most celebrated Cadwallon was a political leader of the seventh century. Around the year 629 he invaded Northumberland. Cadwallon drove the Irish out from the north of Britain. He became the father of Cadwaladr Fendigaid, the later saint of Gwynedd. The deeds of Cadwallon shaped the ties between early Welsh and English power.

:::
