``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Cumhall is the father of Fionn and the leader of Clan Baiscne in the Fenian Cycle. The initial C- falls away in the patronymic mac Umail/mac Cumhaill. He wanted Muirenn of the White Neck, and she did not return his love, so he carried her off. Her father Tadg and his protector Conn Cétchathach took vengeance for that deed. The feud set the descendants of Cumhall against Clan Morna, the warriors of Tadg under Goll. Variant texts name Fuinche or Torba as the mother of Fionn. In the best-known account of his death, the Fotha Catha Chnucha, Goll mac Morna slays him. The tale sets the killing at the Battle of Cnucha, the fight at Castletown. In other tellings Conn Cétchathach alone strikes him down, or the keeper of the crane bag does the deed. Scottish folk-tales lay the deed on the villain Arca Dubh. Muirenn bore Fionn after the death, hiding in the house of Bodhmall, sometimes told as her sister. His brother Crimnal survived the battle and later joined the Fianna. The old claim tying him to the god Camulos or to King Cole finds no support among modern scholars. Macpherson recast him as Comhal in Ossian (1760). Yeats borrowed him for the poem 'The Blessed' (1897).

:::
```
