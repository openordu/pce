``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Caradawg was a hero of Wales. In the Mabinogion he is named a son of the king Brân the Blessed. Brân set out for Ireland to make war on the ruler who kept Brânwen, Caradawg's aunt, in an abusive marriage. Caradawg, then a youth, waited behind to govern Wales. The usurper Caswallawn cut down the royal retainers but spared Caradawg. Caradawg could not keep his father's throne, and he died of heartbreak over it. A different Caradawg held the names Freichfras, meaning of the strong arm, and Briefbras, meaning short-armed. The literature lists this second Caradawg among the advisers of king Arthur. Koch also notes a warrior of that name whose byword tells of his strong arm. His standing put him among the chief men of the king's court. A saint's Life speaks of him as a powerful ruler of the lands of the people of the sea-girt shore. The old British tales join him to the council of king Arthur, and the later French poems keep his name in the roll of the knights of the king.

:::
```
