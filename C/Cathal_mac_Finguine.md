``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Cathal mac Finguine, also written Fionghuine, ruled Munster as a strong king until his death in 742. His royal seat stood at Glendamain. The satire Aislinge Meic Con Glinne takes its name from the seer Mac Conglinne. In that vision tale, a demon of gluttony is said to possess the king's body. Cathal serves as its chief target. A poet working long after Cathal's death told how Mór Muman came to him. She appears in Irish tradition as the goddess who grants sovereignty. That later verse writer, however, mixed up two men. Cathal's own grandfather bore the same name, and the visit belongs to the older man.

:::
```
