``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Connemara is a district of west Co. Galway, lying west of Lough Corrib. The Irish name is Conmaicne Mara, Conmac's people of the sea. Another Irish form is Iarchonnacht, western Connacht. At first Connemara matched the barony of Ballynahinch almost exactly. It now informally takes in eastern parts of the barony of Moycullen. Ross, Joyce's Country, joined it on the north side. The district holds the largest surviving Gaeltacht, the Irish-speaking community. Many commentators therefore treat it as a hallmark area of traditional Irish culture. The tourist industry has exploited the area. Even so, Connemara stays a living store of folk culture, and many collectors have drawn on it. James Berry (1842-1914) reshaped many Connemara narratives in the early 1900s. His approach recalls William Carleton. See G. M. Horgan's 1964 Dublin edition of those reshaped Connemara tales. Tim Robinson's Connemara (Galway, 1990) is another key source.

:::
```
