``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
In the Celtic tradition the craftsman covers the artisan, the carpenter, the potter, and the wright, but the term does not always include the smith. The Irish forms are ceardai and saor, and the Welsh form is crefflwr. In Ireland the smith has his own word, gabha in Irish and gof in Welsh. The craftsman of the Tuathá Dé Danann is Luchta, a carpenter. Luchta often works with Goibniu, a weaponmaker and wright, and with Credne, a metalsmith. In folk-tales Goibniu bears the name Gobbán Saor, meaning Gobbán the wright or craftsman. Partholón in the Lebor Gabála, the Book of Invasions, is the master artisan who stands at the head of every craft. The descriptive title applied to Lug Lámfhota is Samildánach, meaning the possessor of many talents or crafts. In the Mabinogi, the third branch called Manawydan, Llassar Llaes Gyfnewid owns the cauldron of regeneration and earns his fame for fine craftsmanship.

:::
```
