::: magazinestyle
The name Canomagus means hound lord in Latin. It appears on a single Romano-British inscription. The stone stood at Nettleton Shrub in Wiltshire. It belongs to a shrine raised to Apollo. Such titles link a lord to the hunting dog. Dogs held a strong place in Celtic belief. A hound could guard the way to the otherworld. The title may echo the god Nodens. Nodens also ruled over dogs and healing waters. The inscription offers little beyond this one name. Yet it shows how the Romans named British powers. Canomagus joins Apollo to the loyal hound. The word survives as a slim record of Romano-British worship.

:::
