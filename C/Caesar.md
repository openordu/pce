::: magazinestyle
Gaius Julius Caesar was a Roman general, statesman, and historian. He lived from 100 to 44 BC. His seven-volume Gallic War, the De Bello Gallico, serves as a much-quoted window on early Celtic religion. Caesar leaned on reports from his subordinates and perhaps drew heavily from the writer Posidonius. He conquered the Gauls and then the Britons. He took a patronizing view of the Celts, calling them swift to battle yet quick to lose heart. He saw them as superstitious and ready to cede to druids for arbitration. He held that the soul did not die with the body. He gave Roman names to many native gods, ranked by the people's esteem.

:::
