``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Conán mac Morna is the more famous of the two Fenian figures named Conán. He often carries the agnomen Máel or Maol, usually rendered the Bald. In Old Irish máel implied servility, because servants wore shorn hair, and applied to things the words imply bluntness. Conán is the comic figure of the band, though he is no mere clown. Lady Gregory called him Conan of the Bitter Tongue, and his bluster recalls Thersites of the Trojan tale, while his trouble-making recalls Loki. Fat and at times foolish, he invites comparison with Falstaff, yet no tale shows him fleeing a fight. The other Fenians often detest this mean and greedy man, but once he backs Fionn the two are seen together almost as a team. He is bald, and a fleece from a black sheep hangs down his back. Some tales turn that fleece into a wig. Many tales name him a brother of Goll mac Morna. In Feis Tighe Chonáin, A Feast at Conán's House, Fionn feasts at his house and tells of the Fenians' adventures.

:::
```
