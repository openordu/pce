``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Conaing is several figures of early Irish genealogy and of the island's civil and church history carried it. The word looks related to a Germanic royal title, Old English cyning, king. Some commentators have confused the name with that of Conand, the leader of the Fomorians, but the two are distinct. The name therefore sits at a crossroads of native genealogy and borrowed royal vocabulary. A separate Breton tradition names the nain, a dreadful demon-like being of Breton folklore. Dark and threatening, the nain looks down at road-goers much as a stone gargoyle might. His hands carry the claws of a cat. His feet are the hooves of a satyr. Beneath his dark elf-locks his tiny red eyes burn like coals. He roams around old dolmens, above all those of Morbihan, said to be the work of a vanished race. He also crosses paths with travellers on empty moors and quiet roads. His preferred day is Wednesday, and his chief night-feast falls on the opening Wednesday of May. Folk belief held that nains sprang from a cabalistic alphabet cut into several megalithic stones. In the same way as the gorics, nains stand as keepers of buried treasure.

:::
```
