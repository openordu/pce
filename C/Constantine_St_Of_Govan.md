::: magazinestyle
Constantine of Govan was a saint of early medieval Alba. People honoured his cult in Kintyre, Ayrshire, and Galloway. The name became common with northern royal houses across the 7th to 10th centuries. A series of legends grew around one or more saintly Constantines. His feast day fell on 11 March. The sainted emperor Constantine kept his on 10 March. Local royal-saint cults of the same name also rose in Brittany, Cornwall, and Ireland. The Aberdeen Breviary merged all these legends into one life. It made him the son of a Cornish king. It married him to the daughter of Brittany's king. It cast him as a monastic miller in Ireland and a disciple of Columba and Kentigern. He preached in Galloway and died as a martyr in Kaintre.

:::
