``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Cóir Anmann is the Irish title of a learned treatise. English readers may call it the Fitness of Names, or else the Elucidation of Names. The work explains the sense and the associations of many personal names of early Ireland. It does for names what the Dindshenchas does for place-names. The same title also serves as an epithet of the goddess Ana. One passage identifies the wheel ancestor Ánroth with the mythic king Rothechtaid Rotha, the Great Traveller of the Wheel. Whitley Stokes printed the text with a translation in his Irische Texte of 1897.

:::
```
