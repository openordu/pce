``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Cathach is an old Irish book of psalms. It is the earliest surviving relic tied to Saint Columba. The volume once held the larger share of the psalm book. The old book counted about a hundred and ten leaves. Age and repair have harmed many pages. Its painted letters show the curved style of La Tène. The style appears in the Lindisfarne Gospels and in Durrow. The like ornament marks the Book of Kells. The scribe wrote the pages before the year six hundred fifty. That dates the book near the life of the saint. The tale Betha says the saint wrote it himself. In that tale he copied a book in secret. His host became angry and claimed the copy. The high king passed a ruling over the case. He held that each book keeps its own transcript. Still the saint did not give the work back. The quarrel helped drive the saint over the sea. He went from Ireland to the land of Alba. The O'Donnell house held the relic for ages. The caretakers came from Tory Island. In the late eleventh century the book entered a shrine. The shrine stood at Kells and took several hands. Only six early Irish shrines remain today. The faithful carried the relic in their wars. It served their owners as a charm for victory. The shrine was closed for good in the fourteenth age. In the year 1691 the relic travelled to France. A silver casing was added in the year 1723. The Royal Irish Academy took it in the year 1843.

:::
```
