``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Celtic languages are a subgroup of the Indo-European family. A shared set of sound changes defines the group. Proto-Celtic stands between Indo-European and the later Celtic tongues. Scholars split the family into Continental and Insular branches. Continental Celtic includes Gaulish, Lepontic, and Celtiberian. These ancient languages of the mainland have all died. Latin, Greek, and Germanic replaced them. Gaulish survives in near six hundred inscriptions. Insular Celtic holds the living languages today. It splits into Goidelic and Brythonic groups. Goidelic covers Irish and Scottish Gaelic. Brythonic covers Welsh, Breton, and Cornish. A known division is the P-Celtic and Q-Celtic split. The old sound kw became p in the P-Celtic group. Celtiberian kept the q for most of its history. The modern name came into use in the late 1500s.

:::
```
