::: magazinestyle
Cynan is a hero of Welsh legend. He is the brother-in-law of a half-historical king named Maxen. Cynan helps Maxen rise to rule as emperor of the western Roman lands. For that service he gains control of Brittany. There he silences the local women by removing their tongues. He acts to guard his own warriors' Welsh speech from any rival. Like Maxen, Cynan seems invented for a political end. He is tied to statecraft, not to worship or ritual. MacKillop stresses how many early Welsh figures carry the name, saints and kings among them. Two bearers dominate the record. One is Cynan Garwyn ap Brockfael, a king of Powys whom Taliesin may have praised. The other is Cynan Meiriadog, also spelled Meriadoc or Meriadach, a legendary holy man of the fifth century. He led Welsh settlers across to Armorica and so stands at the head of the Brittany founding story. In Breton legend he answers to Conan Meriadoc or Konan Meriadek.
