``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Cúchulainn stands as the Ulster Cycle's greatest fighting man. As a boy he carried the name Sétantae. The tale of his birth marks him apart from other men. His mother Deichtine was the sister of Conchobar, king of the Ulaid. The stories give him a divine father, Lug, and an earthly father, Sualtaim. He won his warrior name after he killed the hound of the smith Culann. That name means the hound of Culann. His boyhood deeds tell how this came about. He also grew up as the foster-brother of the hero Conall Cernach. Conall was the son of Amairgin, a bard as well as a fighter. The bitter satirist Aithirne once came to probe Amairgin. He sought an opening that would let a sharp taunt strike home. Amairgin chose words that gave the satirist no foothold. Later Cúchulainn won Emer, the daughter of the lord Forgall. He trained in the art of the sword under the war teacher Scáthach overseas. In the feast of Bricriu he beat the other Ulaid peers in hard trials. The dark Munster hero Cú Roí pressed him hardest there. In the great cattle raid he held the pass alone for his people. Death came through the breaking of his own taboos, not by a better sword. Wounded by a venomous spear, he tied himself to a pillar and slew the waterdog that drank his blood.

:::
```
