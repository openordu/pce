``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Clanranald, the books of, is a Scottish Gaelic family history of the Clan Donald. The account opens in the dream time of Irish legend. It records how the Sons of Míl Espáine arrived in Ériu. This opening places the clan within a shared Gaelic legendary history. The author drew on the doctrines of professional poets. A later section covers the rise of the House of Somerled. It also treats the Lordship of the Isles. The author used a lost chronicle source. One such source had an Iona orientation. After the forfeiture of the Lordship in 1493, the text focuses on the Clanranald branch. The Southern Clan Donald appears when their fortunes matter. The narrative becomes detailed when it reaches the 1640s. It sets out the Highland wars led by Montrose and Alasdair Mac Colla Ciotaich. The author used eye-witness accounts for battles. He also drew on the caithréim style of martial exploits. The final section returns to chronicle form. This part keeps a Hebridean focus and runs to 1689. The work has an experimental quality. It appeared when old Gaelic conventions faced new ideas. The author MacMhuirich sought to counter anti-Highland bias. He wove praise and funeral verse for MacDonald chiefs into the work.

:::
```
