``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Cerne Abbas Giant is a human figure cut into turf and chalk on a slope in Dorset, England. The site lies in the parish of Cerne Abbas, north of Dorchester. Workers cut away the turf to reveal white chalk below. The outline shows an ithyphallic man who grips a fine club in his right hand. Cross lines mark the ribs, and one line marks the waist. The figure bears no name, and no one knows who carved it. The White Horse of Uffington is another turf-cut figure, dated to the Iron Age.

The known history is largely modern. The first printed account of the Giant appeared in 1763, and another came out in 1764. No medieval source names the figure. Some scholars therefore think the carving is not much older than the 1700s. Other scholars read the pose and the club as Roman-era work. They note that other Iron Age belted, unclothed warriors in Europe were recorded. Stuart Piggott saw the club as like that of Hercules. He and others read the figure as a Roman Hercules, or as a god the local Durotriges held to stand for him.

The Giant carries a huge club held aloft. The Irish god Dagda carried a similar club, and a continental Celtic god named Sucellus carried a mallet-like weapon. Many therefore argue the outline has Celtic links. The Roman and 18th-century readings remain two competing views. The figure still has no agreed name. The defenders of the Hercules reading note its club and its nudity. Those who favor a deep ancient age point to the Uffington pony as proof that such figures can be very old.

:::
```
