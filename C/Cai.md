``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Cai is a name carried by two old figures of the Celtic world. In Irish legend it names the first brehon, or judge, of Ireland. This Cai belonged to the Milesians, the people who came to claim the island. He was schooled in Egypt, where he learned the law of Moses, and he helped bring about the marriage of Mil to Scota, a daughter of the pharaoh. At the time of the great flood he went with Moses into the desert. He later left Moses, came to Ireland, and judged the people who settled there. This Irish Cai blends in the tales with the Arthurian Cai, also known as Kay, of the court of King Arthur. The Welsh Cai fab Cynyr stands among the oldest heroes of the Arthurian stories. He appears in the earliest tales of Arthur as one of his chief men. In Pa Gur, who is the gatekeeper? he is as forward as Arthur and far more active. He fights witches, monsters, and hags, and he slays the cruel Cath Palug of Anglesey. In Culhwch and Olwen he is named first among the companions of the court. His powers are set very high. His breath lasts nine days and nine nights under water; he goes nine nights without sleep; no doctor can close the wound he opens; and when he wills he can stand as high as a great forest tree. When strong rain falls, a clear span of water keeps dry about him. Such supernatural marks lead many to read Cai as a free figure of an older belief, drawn perhaps from a plain soldier of the sub-Roman age. The name comes from the Latin Caius, common among the Britons, or from a Celtic word for things that close and fasten, such as a brooch or a locked court. His father's name, Cynyr, is Celtic, and the word comes out in an old British stone in Britain.

:::
```
