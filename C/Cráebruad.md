``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Cráebruad is the great hall of the Ulster king Conchobar mac Nessa at Emain Macha.
The name is Irish for red branch.
Spelling variants run from Cráeb Ruad and Craobh Ruadh through Craob Rua to Crave Rua and Crevroe.
Writers translate the name into English as Red Branch.
The hall took its name from the great roofbeam inside it, a branch that workers may have painted.
Weapons were banned inside the hall to cut down fighting among the guests.
The warriors stored their arms in a second royal house, the Tête Brec.
Nineteenth-century texts used Red Branch for the hall and for its warrior band alike.
Writers also styled the Ulster stories the Red Branch Cycle.
The townland of Creeveroe in Northern Ireland may echo the hall's name.

:::
```
