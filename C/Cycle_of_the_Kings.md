``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Cycle of the Kings ranks among the four main classes of early Irish narrative lore. Scholars also know it as the Historical Cycle. The term gathers sagas, poems, and anecdotes about Irish kings and dynasties. The tales center on the kings of Tara and other rulers from all five provinces. They stand with the Ulster, Fenian, and Mythological cycles as core traditions. The stories test a ruler's courage, wisdom, and judgment under trial. Idealized figures such as Cormac mac Airt and Conaire Mór fill the cast. The cycle preserves traces of an archaic Celtic sacral kingship. In that old model the roles of ruler, judge, and priest stayed undivided. Society ranked classes by honour, with a warrior aristocracy held high. Without a full cash market, people gave surplus as gifts. Each gift carried a personal duty between giver and receiver. A king needed noble birth and skill as a war leader. He shared his wealth to hold the loyalty of followers. Scholars such as Georges Dumézil and Donnchadh Ó Corráin shaped its study. The corpus includes the tale Scél Éogan ocus Cormaic. The manuscripts Laud 610 and Rawlinson B 502 preserve these texts. MacKillop sets its focus on the provincial and lesser kings. He dates the material from the third to the seventh centuries, a mix of legend and history. The tales weigh kingship as much as the kings themselves. Commentators rate the material less magical than the Mythological Cycle. They find it less romantic than the Fenian and less heroic than the Ulster tales. Myles Dillon fixed the name in his book The Cycles of the Kings, printed at Oxford in 1946. Dillon also allowed that more than one such cycle existed. Alan Bruford split off the Dalcassian Cycle in 1969. His set gathers the tales of the tenth-century Brian Bórama and his kin. MacKillop fills the cast with Conn Cétchathach, Becfola, Niall Noigiallach, Labraid Loingsech, and Fergus mac Léti. He adds Muirchertach mac Erca, Mongán, Conaire, and the lovers Ailinn and Baile. He names Cath Maige Mucrama and the Dream of Macsen Wledig among the important narratives. The Destruction of Dind Rig also stands in the set.
```
