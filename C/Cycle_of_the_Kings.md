``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Cycle of the Kings ranks among the four main classes of early Irish narrative lore. Scholars also know it as the Historical Cycle. The term gathers sagas, poems, and anecdotes about Irish kings and dynasties. The tales center on the kings of Tara and other rulers from all five provinces. They stand with the Ulster, Fenian, and Mythological cycles as core traditions. The stories test a ruler's courage, wisdom, and judgment under trial. Idealized figures such as Cormac mac Airt and Conaire Mór fill the cast. The cycle preserves traces of an archaic Celtic sacral kingship. In that old model the roles of ruler, judge, and priest stayed undivided. Society ranked classes by honour, with a warrior aristocracy held high. Without a full cash market, people gave surplus as gifts. Each gift carried a personal duty between giver and receiver. A king needed noble birth and skill as a war leader. He shared his wealth to hold the loyalty of followers. Scholars such as Georges Dumézil and Donnchadh Ó Corráin shaped its study. The corpus includes the tale Scél Éogan ocus Cormaic. The manuscripts Laud 610 and Rawlinson B 502 preserve these texts.

:::
```
