``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Cocidius was a god of the north and west of early Britain, honoured by soldiers along Hadrian's Wall. The Romans compared him with Mars. Some twenty stones bear his name. Five join him to Mars. One altar joins him to Silvanus, god of the woods. Silvanus appears as a hunter with a dog and a stag. On some carvings Cocidius wears a shawl or hood. Elsewhere in the north he appears horned and is linked with hunting. His shrine at Bewcastle in Cumberland marks him as a soldier's god. An inscription gives him the title Riocalatis with Teutates. Another links him to Vernostonus. Two silver plaques show him with spear and shield. He may be one with Segomo, the war god of the Continental Celts. He may also be associated with Teutates, the ruler of the people worshipped in Britain. More tentatively he may be linked with Belatucadros, the fair shining one, and with Mogons, the powerful one. His name may not be Celtic. Its ending could match a Welsh word for reddish, tied to 'red god' Da Derga.

:::
```
