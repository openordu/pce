Derbforgaill, also spelled Dervorgilla or Devorgilla, was a name borne by several Irish heroines. The mythic Derbforgaill was a princess of Lochlainn who loved Cuchulainn from afar. She heard stories of the hero's prowess without ever seeing him. She and a servant flew to Ireland in the shape of swans. Cuchulainn, not knowing their identity, cast a stone and struck one swan from the air. The bird fell to earth as the woman Derbforgaill. He sucked the blood from her wound and saved her life. Because he had tasted her blood, he was forbidden to marry her. He gave her instead to his foster-brother Lugaid Riab nDerg. The marriage was happy. Her rivals later challenged her to prove her beauty. She defeated them in a contest of casting urine through a pillar of snow. The jealous women mutilated and killed her in revenge. Cuchulainn avenged her by killing 150 women of the household, and Lugaid died of grief. Derbforgaill (also Dervorgilla or Devorgilla) was a name borne by several Irish heroines. One Derbforgaill of Leinster was a real woman of the 12th century. She married Ua Ruairc, the O'Rourke chieftain of Breffni near Sligo. While her husband was on pilgrimage, she ran off with Diarmait mac Murchada. He is also called Dermot MacMurrough, and she may have gone willingly or been taken. The war between husband and lover drew Norman warriors into Ireland. Their coming destroyed the old Celtic way of life. England later used that breach as its claim to Ireland. History derides her as an adulteress. She acted on the ancient Irish belief that a woman held the right to choose her own consort. A second Derbforgaill belonged to myth. She was the shape-shifting daughter of king Ruad. She conceived a passion for Cúchulainn. She and her maidens turned into swans to fly to him. Cúchulainn threw stones and brought her to earth. He fell to his knees and sucked the stone from her flesh. That act made them blood kin. It thwarted her wish to share his bed. She married his friend Lugaidh Riab nDerg. She died when other women, enraged in a contest, fell upon her. Cúchulainn avenged her by killing 150 women. The dictionary also prints the spelling swarm Dearbhfhorgaill, Derbhorgill, Derbforgaille, Devorgill, and Devorvilla. It routes the Modern spelling Dearbhorgaill to this record. In repentance the historical Derbforgaill founded the nuns' church at Clonmacnoise.
