``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Cywyddwyr are the Welsh poets who wrote in the cywsydd metre during the late medieval centuries. Their era ran from roughly 1300 into the 1500s. The name joins the word cywsydd to an old term for men. In this the Cywyddwyr followed the earlier Gogynfeirdd court poets, beginning about 1300. Their rise came after the English conquest of Gwynedd in 1282, which removed the native royal courts. A new class of gentry, the uchelwyr, became their patrons. Poets such as Dafydd ap Gwilym made the cywsydd the leading form for praise and song. Its lines carried the strict sound-patterns of cynghanedd. They praised the gentle families, and moved freely between their houses and the growing towns. The fashion ended with the decline of the poetic order in the 16th century.

:::
```
