``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Cuilenn is a fairy king of Irish lore. His name rests on the Ring of Gullion, a broad belt of hills thrown up by old volcanic force. The central summit, Slieve Gullion, passes for his home. Some lore instead sets the Cailleach under the burial mound that crowns the height. A pool near the top is said to grey the swimmer of its waters. The goddess Aine had vowed never to take a grey-haired lover. Her sister Milucra therefore conjured a pool to snare Fionn. He rose from it aged and silver. Fionn's men, the Fianna, forced Milucra to offer a cure from her golden horn, yet she kept his hair silver. Another telling sends Cuilenn to the rescue with a golden cup that bore the hero wisdom. The dictionary glosses his name as the wood of the holly tree. It calls him lord of a sidh mound at Sliab Cuilinn in Armagh. It tells how Fionn mac Cumhaill sank into feeble old age after a bath in the nearby lake. Cuilenn restored him with a drink poured from a golden cup. The cup vanished the moment the drink was done. Some stories say that drink is where Fionn's supernatural knowledge began. The same account names his daughters, Aine and Milucra.

:::
```
