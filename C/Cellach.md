``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Cellach appears in Irish legend as a hero. His father was Cormac Mac Airt, the ruler of Tara. Cellach wronged a woman, and her father sought revenge. Cormac met that father in combat and lost an eye. Irish law barred a scarred king from ruling. Cormac therefore gave up his seat at Tara. Myth gives Cellach only this role: he brought about the end of his father's rule. The name Cellach or Ceallach may mean strife or contention, or bright-headed. Many figures of early Irish history and legend carried it. One carried it as a kinsman of Cormac mac Airt, a nephew or a son. He wronged a Déisi princess and died at the hand of Angus in revenge. A second Cellach became the forebear of the Kelly dynasty, the Irish Uí Chellaig and Ó Ceallaigh lines.

:::
```
