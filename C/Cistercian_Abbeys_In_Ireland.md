::: magazinestyle
The Cistercian order was a stricter renewal branch of Benedictine monasticism urged on by Saint Bernard of Clairvaux. Around ten of these monasteries already stood in Ireland when Bernard died in 1153. Their growth stayed spectacular in Ireland, and roughly thirty houses had been founded locally before 1272. Mellifont Abbey in County Louth became the mother house for many later foundations. These houses reshaped Irish spirituality, architecture, and farming methods. They remained a strong force in both English and Gaelic Ireland until the Reformation.

:::
