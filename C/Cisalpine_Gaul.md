::: magazinestyle
Cisalpine Gaul was the Celtic name for northern Italy. The Po river crossed the middle of the land. The Boii and the Senones were leading Celtic peoples there. The Senones sacked Rome about 387 BC. Roman power grew and the Gauls lost ground. Hannibal drew many Celts into his own armies. Rome then fought the Cisalpine tribes for control. Roman colonies rose at Bononia and Parma. Paved roads such as the Via Flaminia served the new towns. The Celts south of the Po gained Roman citizenship in 89 BC. Those north of the Po joined Rome by 42 BC.

:::
