``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Cathubodua is a battle goddess of the Gauls. Her cult appears in the record of the Haute-Savoie region of what is now eastern France. One inscription alone carries that evidence. The tribal surname Boduogenos, which means people of Bodua, preserves her name. The name points to a raven of battle. Writers have connected her to the war goddess Badb. Scholars place her before the Irish Badb in time. MacKillop proposes that she and Cauth Bodva are one figure. Women dressed in black, acting as druids, imitated the Badb in the thick of fighting. They yelled from the sidelines to stir the men to bolder deeds. That was the scene when the druidic school on Anglesey met its ruin. Whether Badb and her fellow goddesses of war began as Celtic figures is a matter of argument. Some scholars trace them to an earlier, pre-Celtic layer. Most scholars think her bird shape reaches back to an unnamed Neolithic goddess. Artworks of that age commonly drew her in bird form.

:::
```
