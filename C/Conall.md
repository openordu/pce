``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Conall is an old and widely borne Irish name. It comes from a wolfish sense and reaches English use as Connell. Legend and half-recorded history both carry it, and the herald Conall Cernach is its most famous bearer. The name is often mixed up with the unrelated Connla, so readers should mind the difference. Conall son of Eochaid heads the oldest tale of the name. His five sons attacked the fairy mound of Grian at Cnoc Greine, and she turned the whole wicked family into badgers. Hunters killed and ate them. Conall fought her in return. She dusted him with fairy dust, and he wandered to nearby Carn Conaill in Limerick and stumbled to his death there. A second Conall lives in oral tradition as a teller of tales. His sons killed one of his king's sons by accident, so the king sent him to Lochlann, the Norse world, for his life. He bought safety by telling three tales in succession, one of a one-eyed giant, and the third revealed he had once saved the king of Lochlann himself. English tellings call him Yellowclaw. Conall Cernach of the Ulster stories was the Victorious One, son of the bard Amairgin. He alone walked out of Da Derga's burning hostel. Conall Gulban carried the name into history. He founded the northern kingdom of Donegal, and his name reached the slopes of Ben Bulben, where a giant in the story of the freeing of Ewin brought him down.

:::
```
