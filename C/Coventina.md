``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Coventina is a goddess of wells and streams whom Romano-British worshippers honored during the Roman occupation of Britain. A shrine with a well stood at Carrawburgh in south Northumberland, beside Hadrian's Wall. Carved reliefs from the site show her as a water-nymph. One relief depicts her reclining and tipping water from an urn. Another shows her among water plants, pouring her vessel over them. Inscriptions style her Augusta and Sancta, high and holy. Devotees carried her cult to Spain and Gaul. Excavations in 1876 recovered about 14,000 ancient coins from her well. The hoard shows how many worshippers cast offerings into the water.

:::
```
