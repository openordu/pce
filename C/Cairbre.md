``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Cairbre was a name often given to Irish men in early times.
Kings, heroes, and poets all carried it.
Later scribes wrote it as Cairbri, Cairpre, or Caipre.
One old reading links the word to a charioteer.
The name also covered several saints.
Cairbre Lifehair stands out as the best-known bearer.
A prince named Cairbre was also a son of Niall Noigiallach.
That son built his own royal line.
The barony of Carbury in County Kildare keeps his name.
The old tales also recall the poet Cairbre mac Eadaoine.
He was the son of the god Ogma and the poet Etan.
Cairbre came to the great court at Tara.
He received a poor welcome there.
King Bres mac Elatha handed him a stale loaf.
Cairbre answered with a sharp satire.
The verse raised boils on the face of Bres.
A ruler so marked must give up the crown.
Bres then surrendered his kingship.
The song of Cairbre served the Tuatha De Danann.
Its power reached the second battle of Mag Tuired.
When Cairbre died, his mother Etan perished in grief.

:::
```
