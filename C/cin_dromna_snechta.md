``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Cín Dromna Snechta is an early Irish codex of great renown. The volume is now lost. The name means the Book of Druim Snechta. Ancient glossaries define cin as a stave of five vellum sheets. So this codex was smaller than its Irish peers. Modern Monaghan held the probable monastery site. Druim Snechta gives the modern townland Drumsnat. Many later manuscripts cite this book. Lebor na hUidre and Lebor Laignech draw from it. The Great Book of Lecan and the Book of Ballymote cite it too. Egerton 88 relies on its texts as well. Scholars recovered its contents from scribal notes. The codex carried chiefly supernatural tales. It also held some earliest records of Fiannaiocht. Genealogies and legendary history filled its pages. Copies of its texts survive more archaic. They belong to the Older Irish horizon. The exact writing date stays disputed. Some scholars favor the eighth century. Others suggest the first half of the ninth. A late ninth or tenth century date has support too. Geoffrey Keating lacked this manuscript. His Foras Feasa failed to cite it. The book vanished before the seventeenth century. Notable tales linked to it include the Voyage of Bran. It also held Conla adventure and other early sagas.

:::
```
