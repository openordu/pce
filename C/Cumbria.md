``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Cumbria is the northernmost English county in the far north-west. It borders Scotland along the Solway Firth. The modern county holds the historic districts of Cumberland, Westmorland, part of Lancashire, and part of the North Riding of Yorkshire. The English name first appears in the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle, which records Cumbraland in 945 and Cumerland in 1000. The name means the land of the Cymry, or Britons. Its first part is a Brythonic loanword that matches the Welsh term Cymru. This region was one of the last places in Britain to keep Brythonic speech outside Cornwall and Wales. The kingdom of Rheged probably included part of Cumbria. Its ties bound it to Strathclyde, whose rulers dwelt at Dumbarton. Northumbria gained control in the seventh century. Latin sources of the tenth and eleventh centuries used Cumbria for a kingdom that covered parts of southern Scotland and northern Cumbria. Only three men are named as kings of the Cumbrians.
```
