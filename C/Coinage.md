``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Celtic coinage arose in the late 4th to early 3rd century BC, during the Middle La Tène period. Celtic peoples traded with the Greeks and supplied mercenary fighters, and Greek models guided their earliest coin dies. Greek art supplied the models. Yet the pieces rarely met Greek weight or metal standards and mostly circulated only where they were struck. Three broad zones mark the prototypes: an eastern silver belt along the Danube, several southern silver groups, and a northern gold belt. The stater of Philip of Macedonia became a dominant model for many issues. Over time the Celts built a Greek and Celtic blend, then distinct Belgic and Armorican styles. The Roman conquest of Gaul did not end Celtic coinage at once, and legends continued in both Gaulish and Latin. In Britain gold lasted until the Claudian conquest. The Celtic Coin Index at Oxford has records and images of more than 30,000 coins. Coin legends are useful evidence for early Celtic languages, though no full sentence is known to appear on a coin.

:::
```
