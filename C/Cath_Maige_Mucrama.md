``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Irish speakers title this tale Cath Maige Mucrama. English readers know the work as The Battle of Mag Mucrama. It belongs to the Cycle of the Kings. One scholar reads the work as a political scripture. True history blends with symbolic fiction, and the story aims to persuade rather than divert its audience. Lugaid mac Con comes home from exile to fight for the high seat. He overcomes Eógan (3), king of Munster and his own brother, together with the king of Tara. Victory makes him ruler of both the northern and the southern half of Ireland. On the eve of battle, Art mac Cuinn begets a son. That child is Cormac mac Airt, later numbered among the most famous early kings of Ireland. Eógan (3) also fathers a son that night, Fiachu Muillethan, ancestor-founder of the Eóganacht line of kings. Both rivals fall in the coming struggle. Mag Mucrama, the plain of the fight, lies west of Athenry in Co. Galway. M. O'Daly issued the most recent text edition for the Irish Texts Society, volume 50, in 1975.

:::
```
