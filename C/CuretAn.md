``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Curetán, also called Curitan, served as bishop of Rosemarkie on the Black Isle in Ross-shire. He held this see from about 690 to 710. He witnessed the Synod of Birr in 697. That council issued the Law of the Innocents. The law shielded women, children, and clerics from war. His see lay among the northern Picts. His Life, however, calls him an Albanus of Hebrew origin. It states he baptized Nechton, king of the Picts. His name carries a Celtic diminutive, while Boniface is his Latin name. His feast day falls on 16 March. A fair in Fortrose still marks the day.

:::
```
