Solinus, Caius Julius, was a Roman geographer of about AD 200. His compendium, the Collectanea Rerum Memorabilium, runs to fifty-seven chapters. It gives short and unreliable sketches of the world the ancients knew. The book treats the social habits and religious rites of many nations. It describes the British goddess Sulis, also called Sul, and reports that Ireland had no snakes.

:::
