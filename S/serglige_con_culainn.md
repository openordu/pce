::: magazinestyle
Serglige Con Culainn is a medieval Irish tale whose title means the wasting sickness of Cu Chulainn. It is a work of the Ulster Cycle written in a mix of prose and verse. The story reads as a supernatural adventure that opens a view into the Irish Otherworld. The action begins at the festival of Samain. Strange birds appear, and Cu Chulainn tries to bring one down for his wife. He misses his mark, and such a poor throw has not happened since he first took up his weapons. He then dreams of two women who come from another world. They beat him, and he loses the power of speech for a whole year. A stranger tells him that the Otherwoman awaits him, and he meets the two again at the place of that dream. In the end the two lovers part when a god shakes his cloak between them. The text survives in the old book Lebor na hUidre.

:::
