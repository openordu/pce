``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The sluagh marks the band of unforgiven dead who haunt Scottish folk tradition. The Scottish Gaelic form sluagh-sithe derives from the older words for folk, host, and army. Another name, sluagh na marbh, calls them the host of the dead. These spirits are not the fairies, though they stand as the most formidable of the fairy host of Scotland. They ride out only after dark. They may come from any quarter save the east, and often take the shape of a crescent of grey birds. The host can lift a person bodily and carry him aloft from one island across the sea to another. They sometimes drag a man from a dangerous cleft in the rocks. They usually bring no good to mortals, and folk say they also harm cattle. The Gaelic cry 'O shluagh!' calls on the fairies for help. The band numbers souls who died in old Scotland without being forgiven. Their wrongs trapped them in this world. They could not move on to the Otherworld. They lived in the dark and never walked in the day. The host waged endless war upon itself. Each morning the field held the bloody stripes they had left behind. The unforgiven dead stayed bound to the night and to one another in a war that never truly ends.

:::
```
