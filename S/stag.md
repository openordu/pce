``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The stag is a mythic animal of the Continental Celts. The grown male of the deer held an honoured place in early cult. It also played a vivid role in later folk tale. Old English gave the word stagga, the source of the modern English name. The Goidelic tongues lack a single word for the animal. The horned gods of the ancient Celts, most famously Cernunnos, bear stag antlers. King of the wooded north, the stag won praise for its speed and sexual force in the rut. The antlers signal the year, falling in autumn and growing again in spring. The toughness of the horn called to mind male sex, and carvers cut antlers into phallic charms. Images of the stag run through Celtic art. They begin with seventh-century BC rock carvings and pass through bronze figures and coins of the pre-Roman and Romano-Celtic ages. In the Camonica valley of the Italian Alps, the stag joins the imagery of the sun. The Celtic Dis Pater that Caesar describes in the first century BC may himself be a deer-god. Among British cult, the god Cocidius stands alongside the male deer. Through Welsh and Irish legend that same creature lures humans into the Otherworld. Fionn mac Cumhaill hunts an enchanted stag that is the transformed Irish god Donn. Once Christian rites took hold, the stag served as a path to paradise and was carved on burial stones. Such stones stand at Mount Stewart Gardens in County Down. Stags also meet women in the old lore. Flidais, the Irish goddess of wild creatures, holds the stag under her hand. The war-goddess Mórrígan is able to assume the shape of a stag when she wills it. The stag stands as one of the three shifts of Tuan mac Cairill. In the opening branch of the Mabinogi, Pwyll pursues a stag that is the prize of Arawn, lord of Annwfn. In the fourth branch, Gilfaethwy becomes a stag and Gwydion a hind to father Hyddwn, the little stag. In Culhwch ac Olwen, the speaking Stag of Rhedynfre aids Culhwch in his chase of the boar Twrch Trwyth. Stags sprout antlers in the spring and shed them in the fall. The stag thus marks the turn of the seasons and stands for the wild and the hunt. It is the great beast of the woods.
```
