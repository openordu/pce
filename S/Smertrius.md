``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Smertrius is best read as a Latin title rather than the true name of a Gaulish god. Writers link him to both Hercules and Mars, and Smertrius also serves those gods as an occasional epithet. A carving found in 1711 under the cathedral of Notre-Dame in Paris shows the figure. There he is a muscular god facing a rearing snake. He brandishes a club or firebrand, set beside Esus, Cernunnos, and Tarvos trigaranus. Near Trier in Germany a vast enclosure surrounds a sacred spring. It gives him the cult partner Ancamna. Coins found at that site prove the shrine older than the Roman conquest. Philology ties the word Smertrius to the name of the goddess Rosmerta. A tribe called the Smertae lived in the far north of Scotland, in the old county of Sutherland.

:::
```
