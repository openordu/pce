``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Isle of Skye lies off the west coast of Scotland. It is the largest island of the Inner Hebrides, about 670 square miles in extent. Its Gaelic name, An t-Eilean Sgiathanach, means the winged isle, and visitors also call it the Misty Isle. Ptolemy knew it in the second century as Sketis, though he set it badly out of place on his map. The island holds a rich oral tradition and appears often in Irish and Scottish Gaelic narrative. Its early fame rests on Scáthach, the amazonian teacher of Cúchulainn and other heroes, whose school stood there. An Irish name for one island fort was Dún Scáthaig, the fort of Scáthach. That name implies the isle itself bore hers. The ruin called Dunscaith, once held by the barons of Sleat, carries her attribution still. Writers on the island's lore include McCulloch's collections and Otta Swire's 1952 volume on the island and its legends.

:::
```
