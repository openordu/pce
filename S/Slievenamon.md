``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Slievenamon is a mountain of 2,368 feet in County Tipperary. It stands ten miles east of Cashel and six miles south-east of Fethard. Its Irish name, Sliab na mBan, means the mountain of the women. Older forms add Ban Finn, of the fair-haired women. Some writers call it Ireland's Parnassus. The mountain stands on the plain of Femen. That word means femininity, and the magical sídh of Femen lies near the peak. Oral tradition holds that women once raced up its slopes. The prize was the privilege of lying with Fionn mac Cumhaill, and the mountain keeps their name. Other Fenian tales set more deeds on its height. Fionn won his thumb wisdom here by catching it in a cairn's door-jamb on the way to the Otherworld. The usual story gives him that wisdom from the salmon instead. Here too he killed Cúldub, who was entering the mound with a pig. Here he chose Gráinne for his wife, and here the Fianna hunted boar. An older report gave Bodb Derg supernatural pigs on the mountain. They came alive again once eaten. For the height's name, W. B. Yeats and other writers preferred Knockfefin.

:::
```
