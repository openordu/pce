``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Sláine, printed with the forms Sláinge and Slangia, names two distinct figures of the pseudo-history Lebor Gabála. That work is the Book of Invasions. The first Sláine is a son of Partholón and reputedly the first physician in Ireland. In the Partholonian division of that land he takes Leinster. The second Sláine leads the Galióin, a part of the Fir Bolg, in the same pseudo-history. He divides Ireland with his brothers and takes Leinster for himself. The Fir Bolg invasion traditions hold that he came ashore at Inber Sláine. That place is now where the Slaney River meets the sea at Wexford Harbour. Tradition usually buries him at Dind Rig in County Carlow, a fortress once called Duma Sláine. That name means the mound of Sláine. The Dindshenchas instead records his burial at Slane in County Meath.

:::
```
