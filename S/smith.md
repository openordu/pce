``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The smith stands as a figure of Irish and continental Celtic myth. Working metal ranked among the chief sources of Celtic wealth from the start. The smith melted ore of iron and gold and shaped it into tools and ornament. The land held the craft in high regard. In the tales, the smith bore the power of magic and of the alchemist. Irish tradition set the god Goibniu over this craft as smith to the Tuatha Dé Danann. Early Welsh narrative named its smith Gofannon, a figure also spelled Govannon. The lore tied the smith to the Glas Ghaibhleann, the magic cow of plenty. Through that bond, the maker of tools stood near the wealth of the land. Folk honor gathered around the smoke, the forge, and the hand that turned ore into blade and ring. The headword blacksmith cross-refers the reader to Goibniu, to Gofannon, and to smith.

:::
```
