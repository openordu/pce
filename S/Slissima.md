``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Slissima is a character of James Macpherson Ossian, the poems published between 1760 and 1763. The poem makes her the sister of Cuchullin and the mother of Althan and Ardar. The same name falls within the Gaelic onomastic pool that MacKillop traces through the entry for liss. The Irish word liss names the bounded ground of an ancient dwelling, an area held within a rampart. Old Irish les and liss gave modern Irish lios. The Welsh cognate is llys, meaning a court. The bounded ground first held a storage place set within a circular mound, a trench, or both. By extension a liss names the outer court or garth around a chieftain stronghold. The same word later serves as one of several terms that oral tradition uses for a fairy fort. MacKillop places Slissima inside the wider Gaelic naming world. That world covers the liss, the rampart, and the fairy fort.

:::
```
