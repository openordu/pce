``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Sucellus is a Continental Celtic god. His name carries the sense of the good striker, one who strikes to good effect. Gaul held his worship at several places, and Britain knew it too. Sculptors show a very masculine figure. His beard and hair curl, and he stands beside his cult partner, the goddess Nantosuelta. In his left hand he holds a long-shafted hammer or mallet. Critical opinion has never settled what the hammer means. Some call the tool a weapon. Others call it a cooper's mallet or a fencing piece. A further reading takes it as an emblem of rule, a wand or sceptre in another dress. Writers tie Sucellus to the Roman Dis Pater and to the Irish Dagda. The hammer reading as a life-giving force stands beside that older comparison. Reinach published the paired cult of Sucellus and Nantosuelta in 1896, and Linckenheld followed in 1929. Green's study of Celtic religious art set the images in order in 1989.

:::
```
