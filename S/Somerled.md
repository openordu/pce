``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Somerled, whose Norse-rooted name means summer sailor, died in 1164. He was a Scottish Gaelic leader and the first holder of the title Lord of the Isles. Legends gathered thickly around his persona. He descended from Irish kings settled in the Hebrides from the seventh century on. In culture he may have carried more Norse than Gaelic ways, yet his buildings at Iona carry much Celtic design. His dealings with the kings of Scotland cut both ways. He backed David I during an invasion of England in 1138. He then supported a failed rising against Malcolm IV in 1153. His main work was taking the Hebrides and Argyll back from Norse control. Those lands answered to the Manx king under Norse overlordship. He beat the Norse with Irish allies in 1156, then again in 1158. After that the Manx king fled to Norway. His title Ri Innse Gall, king of the Hebrides, passed to his successors. In 1354 the Latin form Dominus Insularum appeared, and English took Lord of the Isles from it. Died 1164 in battle near Renfrew, leading yet another rising against Malcolm IV. His son Dugal founded the MacDougall line. His son Raghnall began the MacDonalds of the Isles. Together his progeny formed Clan Donald. W. D. H. Sellar traced his origins and ancestry in the Scottish Historical Review of 1966. Nigel Tranter later made him the hero of a 1983 historical novel.

:::
```
