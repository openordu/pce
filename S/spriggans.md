``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Spriggans are Cornish folk figures. They are the ghosts of a race of giants, reduced to fairies. The spriggan of Cornwall stands as a dour and ugly warrior among the local fairy host. The spriggan rivals the piskie of the same county in folk renown. Spriggans are now very small in size, yet they can swell up to monstrous shapes through shape-shifting. The figures haunt old cairns, cromlechs, and barrows across Cornwall. They stand guard over buried treasure in those places. They also bring storms that wreck buildings and ruin the crops. They chase an intruder from a fairy gathering rather than hurt him. They are old thieves. They steal butter and other valuables from the fields. They guard other fairies as bodyguards. Like the piskies they may also carry off children. Some stories set them in old mounds and ruins, watching a buried treasure. Other Cornish tales give a giant the same charge of the land.

:::
```
