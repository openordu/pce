``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Sanas Cormaic is the Irish title of the work known in English as Cormac's Glossary. Tradition ascribes it to Cormac mac Cuilennain, king-bishop of Cashel, who died in 908. Its entries explain many old and rare words, among them names drawn from Irish literature. Modern commentators reinterpret many of Cormac's judgements. He places Manannan mac Lir on Man rather than in otherworldly Emain Ablach. In many instances he is an uncritical euhemerist, citing gods and goddesses as kings or heroes. None the less the Glossary remains a constantly cited source for the oldest traditions. Whitley Stokes printed one English version in his Three Irish Glossaries of 1862. John O'Donovan translated it again, and Stokes edited that version for a Calcutta printing of 1868. Kuno Meyer issued a 1912 version built on the Yellow Book of Lecan text. Paul Russell traced the work's growth in a 1988 Cambridge study.

:::
```
