``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Shan Van Vocht is the anglicized spelling of the Irish name for the poor old woman. She personifies Ireland and was current in that role in the eighteenth century. Her figure may descend from the Cailleach Bhéire, the hag of Beara, whose own story makes her a sovereignty bride. A song titled Shan Van Vocht was born of the United Irishmen's rising in 1798. That song has been called the Marseillaise of Ireland. Its verses speak of French ships and walking men coming to free the land. The same old woman stands behind the later personifications of the nation. The spéir-bhean of the bards, Dark Rosaleen, and Cathleen ni Houlihan all descend from her. In her modern political dress the figure kept her ancient work. She stands for the land itself and waits for a man able to win her.

:::
```
