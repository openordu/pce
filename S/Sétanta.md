``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Setanta was the boyhood name of the great Ulster hero Cu Chulainn. When he was a boy he carried this name. When he grew old enough to fight he took a new name that stood for a debt of honor. The name Cu Chulainn came from a grown choice. It meant the hound of Culann, and it bound him to duties he would never lay down. Monaghan reads the change as a mark of honor, not vanity. Koch sets the boy in the Ulster Cycle and tells how the name was earned. The young Setanta slew the monstrous hound of the smith Culann. In fairness he offered himself as the guard the hound had been. The folk of Ulster named him the hound of Culann from that day. So the boy Setanta became the man Cu Chulainn, the greatest warrior of the province. MacKillop records the same bond in a single line. In the Irish spelling the name is Sétanta, and Setanta is the boyhood name of Cuchulainn. The two names never part. The one name carries the child who killed the hound. The other carries the champion who paid for it with his own service.

:::
```
