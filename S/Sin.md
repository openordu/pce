``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Sin is an Irish heroine. She is a fairy woman. Scholars see her as a remnant of an early goddess. She made wine from water. She made swine from leaves. She fed the warriors she had called up with her spells. She appears in a tale of the king Muircertach mac Erc. Sin seduced the king and drove him mad. She sought to punish him for killing her family when she was a child. Her magic turns his power against him. MacKillop glosses her name with the Irish word for storm. The tale makes that reading plain. The king meets her alone on his hunting mound. A green mantle clothes her, and she says her search for him has been long. Her beauty fills him with love, and he would give all Ireland for one night with her. She grants it under three conditions. He must never speak her name. The mother of his children must never see her. The clerics of the house must leave whenever she enters. He asks her name so he can avoid it. She answers with a string of threatening titles. Sigh, sough, storm, rough wind, winter night, cry, wail, groan. The storm word among them is Sin herself. To please her the king turns out his queen and their children. He fills his hall with craftsmen and their wives. Saint Caimach lays his curse on the house and the reign. The damsel answers for herself. God, she says, she does believe in, and Eve's line brought her forth. Yet she can work wonders. For seven nights the wine and the pigs flow from her power. On the eve of Wednesday after Samain, foul weather recalls the winter to the king. He speaks of the storm raging outside. She hears her forbidden name in it and tells him he is doomed. Then drowning and fire fill his sleep with visions. Sin sets his house ablaze and rings it with phantom attackers under Tuathal Maelgarb. The king climbs into a wine cask to escape. He drowns in it while the fire burns his head.

:::
```
