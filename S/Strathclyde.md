::: magazinestyle
Strathclyde was a Brittonic kingdom of the Clyde valley. Its Gaelic name element srath means valley. The kingdom counted as P-Celtic or British, inside Scotland. It stretched from the Clyde watershed south toward the Anglo-Saxon kingdom of Mercia. It flourished from the fifth to the eighth century. Its capital sat on the fortified rock of Dumbarton. In 844 Cináed mac Ailpín formed the new kingdom of Scotland. Strathclyde was folded into it, beside the Picts and the Scotti of Dál Riada. Its men then grew steadily more important in the country's life. The name returned in 1974 when Scotland redrew its districts. The new region gathered areas from the former shires of Ayr plus Lanark, Renfrew, Dunbarton, Stirling, and the Argyll country.

:::
