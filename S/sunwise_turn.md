::: magazinestyle
sunwise turn names the ritual motion that imitates the daily path of the sun. MacKillop records the belief that the gesture could bring good fortune or ward off evil across Celtic lands. The worshipper walks clockwise around an object, building, or monument, keeping the focal object on the right at every step. The motion matches the modern clockwise sense. The custom is attested in the oldest Celtic literature and remains in use from the Christian era down to modern times. When Medb begins her campaign against Ulster in the Tain Bo Cuailnge, she and her army make the turn before they march. The wider practice is also called circumambulation.

:::
