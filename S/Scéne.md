``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Scéne, also called Skena and Scénae, was an obscure Irish heroine. She enters the Book of Invasions as the wife of the great bard Amairgin. She died before her people the Milesians could take the island. Her memory now rests mostly in a place name. Inber Scéne may be an imaginary place name in south Kerry. Some writers place the site at the Kenmare River mouth. That river runs through the modern county of Kerry. Others link the name to the broad water of the Shannon. This link rests on the evidence of a Spanish cleric. Orosius wrote his account of the world in ad 417. His testimony leads later commentators to the comparison. An older written form of the name is Inverskena. Both spellings point to the same honored woman. Amairgin was the first poet of Ireland. At the instant of landing in Ireland he recited a poem. In that verse he compares himself to the wind, the wave, and the bull. He also names the salmon, the sword, the plant, and the spear. This act gave lyric poetry to Ireland, so the texts claim. Commentators suggest Welsh storytellers took him as a model for Taliesin. Her own part in the story stays small and sad. She dies while her folk still press toward Ireland. She stands beside Amairgin, the poet of his people. Good detail in her tale beyond this is scarce. The drowned valley keeps her name alive across the years.

:::
```
