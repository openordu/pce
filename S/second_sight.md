``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Second sight is a folkloric motif. Some people can see things hidden from the physical eye. The belief runs across the ancient Celtic lands. Folklorists read it as a remnant of the Celtic belief in an Otherworld that is sometimes visible and sometimes hidden. Druids and bards were thought to be seers. They practiced divination to see what was hidden in the past, present, and future. In the Scottish Highlands, a person with the gift was called a seer, and the vision itself had its own Gaelic name. People with the sight could see the ghosts of the dead walking among the living. They could also see the Fetch, a double that appeared before a death. The Fetch walked the path the funeral would take. Fairies, hidden from most eyes, were visible to them too. The gift was rarely seen as a blessing. It was rarely a gift to desire. Seers often wished to be free of it. The power was usually inherited, but a trauma or a spiritual awakening could bring it on too. MacKillop records the Brahan Seer Kenneth MacKenzie as a central example of second-sight tradition in Gaelic Scotland.

:::
```
