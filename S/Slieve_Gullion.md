``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Slieve Gullion is a high peak of about 1,893 feet in County Armagh. It stands five miles south-west of Newry and eight miles west of Dundalk. The mountain is one of the best-known heights in Ireland. It guards the Gap of the North, the route from Ulster to the south, and tradition celebrates it widely. On the south slope sits a cruciform passage tomb. Local people call it Calliagh Birra's House, a link to the Cailleach Bhéire. The mountain takes its eponym from Cuilenn, a lord of the sídh in Fenian narrative. One story survives in several versions, and each shows the adventures here leaving Fionn mac Cumhaill weaker or smaller. In a common telling a lake bath on the mountain leaves him a feeble old man. Fionn's warriors then lay siege to the sídh. Cuilenn comes out with a golden cup. Its contents restore the hero and hand him and a companion knowledge beyond the natural. The folklorist Michael J. Murphy kept the mountain before the public in two books, At Slieve Gullion's Foot of 1940 and Mountain Year.

:::
```
