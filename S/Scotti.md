``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Scotti is one of the Latin names used for the Irish people, chiefly those of the north-east. The Q-Celtic invaders who crossed from Ulster into Argyll from the sixth century applied the name to themselves. They founded the petty kingdom of Dál Riada there. Three centuries on, these invaders, also called Gaels, led the merged kingdom with the Picts and the P-Celts of Strathclyde. Their name then stretched over the whole realm of Scotland. The kingdom is still known as Alba in Scottish Gaelic. The Lebor Gabala claims the Scotti descended from Queen Scota of Egypt. Elsewhere the same document confuses them with the Scythi of Scythia.

:::
```
