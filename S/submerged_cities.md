``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
submerged cities names the Celtic tradition that whole cities have been inundated. Flood legends in Celtic lands carry this motif. The best-known Celtic examples are Ys in Finistère from Breton tradition and Cantre'r Gwaelod on Cardigan Bay in Welsh tradition. In Ireland, drowned cities are thought to lie under Lough Neagh, Lough Ree, and the Shannon River. The Shannon City resurfaces every seven years, and those who glimpse it meet a fatal end. Lancarrow, in the Cornish record, lies buried beneath the sands. The wider tradition also covers the Land Under Wave.

:::
```
