``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Samain is the Irish form of the name printed for the great November feast, beside Samhain, Samhuinn, and Sauin. The name parses from Old Irish sam, summer, and fin, end, so it marks the close of the warm season. It heads the four chief feasts of the Celtic year and falls on 1 November. The Coligny Calendar of the first century BC attests its age under the month name Samonios. That same source teaches that the dark half precedes the light, which backs the belief that Samain opened the year. Caesar recorded that the Gauls honoured Dis Pater, a god of death and winter cold, in this season. Classical writers add that Teutates received victims drowned in vats, and Taranis received burnt sacrifice. The Christian calendar answered with All Saints' Day and Halloween. Samain was the principal feast of early Ireland, drawing assemblies of all five provinces to Tara every third year. The lighting of winter fires at Tlachtga formed a key rite. Ceremonies recalled the Dagda's unions with the Morrigan, Boand, and Indech's unnamed daughter. Samain time was thought favourable for a woman to conceive. At Mag Slecht in County Cavan, worshippers offered human sacrifice to Crom Crnaich. Some popular writers wrongly make that idol the giver of the feast's name. The texts place great deeds at Samain. Fomorians exacted tribute then, Aillen mac Midgna burned Tara, and Aillen Trechenn wrought havoc. Cuchulainn met otherworldly damsels then, and Caer and Angus Og flew off in swan form. The feast stood between the year's halves, when borders to the Otherworld dissolve and spirits cross freely. People read portents in games and in barmbracks, cakes hiding a ring or a nut for the unmarried. Bonfires burned across parts of Ireland and of Gaelic Scotland, and boys went door to door on Samain eve. In Cork a procession followed a leader dressed as the White Mare. On Lewis in the Hebrides, Protestant islanders still gathered ale and other stores for a mock ceremony. They called on Shoney of the sea, asking him to make their grounds rich in the year ahead. Turnips were hollowed out and fitted with candles.
```
