``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Segomo is a war-god of ancient Gaul. Writers give him a cult in Britain and perhaps Ireland too. The name means victor or mighty one. It appears in the forms Segamonas and Neta Segamonas. MacKillop reads Segomo as more a title than a true name. Both Gaulish Mars and Hercules carry it as an epithet. On that reading no one can say whether this was a distinct native god. He may be one face of those better-known figures. Evidence of the cult survives in stone. A statue of a mule dedicated to Segomo survives at Neuvy-en-Sullias on the Loire. The mule matters, since writers link that animal with Mars. A bronze horse found at the Bolards shrine, near Nuits-Saint-Georges in eastern France, may belong to Segomo alone. The early Irish name Nia Segamoin, the servant of Segomo, echoes his one-time presence in Ireland. Some writers make him the same god as Cocidius, honoured in Roman Britain.

:::
```
