``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Sheela na Gig is a stone carving of a naked woman. With her hands she pulls open her own vagina. MacKillop records the Irish source of the name as Síle na gCíoch, Sheila of the breasts. The carvings come from medieval Ireland and other lands, and many examples survive in place. Most stand in Ireland. England, Wales, and Scotland hold smaller numbers, and a few are claimed for France. Their pose would often be judged obscene, yet builders set many of them into Christian churches. Their true origin, date, and meaning have never been pinned down, and the guesses have not been few. Some read them as borrowings from French Romanesque images of the sin of lust, raised as a warning. Others take them for fertility figures that served as a barrenness cure. Recent feminist writers suggest they hold the memory of the primal earth mother. Her rule over life and death came before Christianity.

:::
```
