``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Imbolc is a Celtic holiday. It is also called Imbolg, Óilmelc, Oímelg, Olmec, or Candlemas. The Celtic year had four parts. The points between the equinoxes and solstices were major festivals. Imbolc came on February 1. On that day winter ended and spring began. Its name means in the belly. Cattle and sheep were pregnant with spring's young. It also linked to a word that means to wash. The name Óilmelc refers to the lactation of ewes. Weather magic and divination were common on the day. In Ireland the day was given to Saint Brigit. She is the Christianized form of a Celtic goddess. The Isles of Man and Britain kept their own folkways for the day.

MacKill sets the feast in the wheel of the year. It is the first of the four gates of the old calendar, and its sisters are the days of the dark, the fire, and the harvest. The visible turn of the light, the slow gain of the day, is taken as its true root, and with it the promise of the spring. The name of the milk gives the same sign, the ewe coming to the fullness of the milk. From the earliest of time the day hangs to the fire-saint, and after the new faith was set on the saint of Kildare and the mother of the flock. Yet the older wearing of the day has not lived on as with its fellows; the words of the farther north and of the island keep the name of the saint, while the common name in the isle carries the blessed mother's own day of the candles, and the far west of the sea finds it in its own feast.

:::
```
