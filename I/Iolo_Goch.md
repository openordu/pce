``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Iolo Goch worked as a Welsh poet across the years 1345 to 1397. His surname Coch means red and refers to his hair colour. His family were freemen who farmed in the Clwyd valley, and they lost most of it at the Edwardian conquest. He probably trained as a chorister at St Asaph cathedral and drew patronage from churchmen of that diocese all his life. He also served powerful patrons across Wales, including the Penmynydd family in Anglesey, Sir Hywel ap Gruffudd, and Sir Rhys ap Gruffudd. His fame rests mainly on his link to Owain Glyndŵr and on describing Glyndŵr's court at Sycharth. He likely died before Glyndŵr's rising of 1400. His greatest gift to Welsh poetry was his use of the cywydd for traditional praise poetry, building on the love poetry of the older poet Dafydd ap Gwilym.

:::
```
