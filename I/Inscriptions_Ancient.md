``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Ancient inscriptions in the Celtic world are the earliest written texts that Celtic peoples left behind. A common myth calls early Celtic culture almost wholly non-literate. The record actually starts about the middle of the sixth century, not long after writing at Rome. Caesar says the Gaulish druids kept their sacred knowledge unwritten and could spend years in memory. Yet in the same passage he tells that Greek characters appeared in daily public and private life. Another author, Diodorus Siculus, writes that people of former Gaul threw letters into funeral fires, so the dead could read them. The two Latin-Cisalpine bilingual texts from Todi mix Roman script for Latin with native Celtic letters. Most Hispano-Celtic, or Celtiberian, inscriptions use a Celtic form of the Iberian script. The famous bronze tablets from Botorrita form the chief corpus.

:::
```
