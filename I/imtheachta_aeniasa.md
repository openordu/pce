``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Imtheachta Aeniasa is the Irish prose retelling of Vergil's Aeneid. The title means the wanderings of Aeneas. The work reaches back to the early twelfth century. Vergil lived from 70 to 19 bc. Three later manuscripts from the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries carry the full text. These copies join other pieces about classical times. That arrangement forms an early cycle. It exposes what the copy editors valued. They sought close prose accounts of a far older period. The Latin source was reshaped for Irish story norms. The re-writers built two frames for the tale. Two frames were added: an opening prologue and a closing epilogue. The opening leans on fifth-century Latin prose. That prose was taken as a translation of a Trojan account. The account was linked to Dares Phrygius. It spells out why Aeneas was driven into banishment. The story keeps his later tale told at Dido's court. Dido rules Carthage in that part. That second telling follows Vergil. It often trumps the prologue. The natural order is not fully rebuilt. The epilogue sets his travels inside Roman history. It claims every world ruler comes down from Aeneas. These frames mark the historical outlook of the Irish work.

:::
```
