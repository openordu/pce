``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Irish Drama in the modern sense took shape in two streams, one in English and one in Irish. The first language revival near the close of the 19th century drove activists to join Irish and theatre. Douglas Hyde and other pioneers faced hard tasks, including learning stagecraft and creating the working vocabulary of the stage. The old word for a play, cluiche, gave way to dráma, and the coined term amharclann won favour for theatre. The Gaelic League pushed drama at its Oireachtas competitions. In 1923 the co-operative An Comhar Drámuíochta began putting on plays at the Abbey Theatre in Dublin. Funded by modest government money, it worked for twenty years and joined the Abbey in 1942. In 1928 Galway gained Taibhdhearc na Gaillimhe, its Irish-language theatre. Ernest Blythe made the Abbey a bilingual national theatre from 1941 to 1967.

:::
```
