``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Ipf and the Goldberg are prehistoric fortified sites in central Europe. The summit plateau of Ipf rises 210 metres above the river Sechta. Its steep slopes make the position strong. Only the eastern slope needed great ramparts. Those ramparts still hold stone walls up to five metres thick. The oval summit measures 185 metres across. People fortified Ipf in the Late Bronze Age. Settlement here dates back to the Late Neolithic. An aristocratic residence stood on the summit in the later Hallstatt period. Iron forges worked the ore at nearby Osterholz. Rich finds included Late Hallstatt pottery and sherds of Greek amphorae.

:::
```
