::: magazinestyle
Irish Literature: early prose is prose fiction written in the Irish language from about 700 down to around 1650. Few manuscripts from the Old Irish period survive, because the wet climate and acid soil destroyed them, so later copies carry the same tales. One lost book, called Cin Dromma Snechtai or the Book of Druim Snechta, is known only from notes in later texts. Early writers sorted tales into echtrai, meaning adventures, and immrama, meaning voyage stories. The Mythological Cycle covers the Tuath De, the tribe of the gods, whose members modern scholars read as older pre-Christian deities. The Ulster Cycle tells of the royal hall of Emain Macha and its hero Cu Chulainn. The Kings' cycles gather the sagas of the ancient rulers, while the Fenian cycle, Fiannaiocht, follows the warrior band of Finn Mac Cumaill. These medieval cycles took shape in monasteries, where monks copied a range of leading texts. After the Anglo-Norman invasion of 1169, romances imitating continental tales of courtly love and magic became common. By the 1100s the older heroic style declined, and patronage moved from the church to the secular bardic order.

:::
