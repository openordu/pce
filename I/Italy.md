``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Italy in antiquity was home to several Celtic-speaking peoples, and the encyclopedia article devoted to them centres on the wide plain watered by the Po and on the country around it. The clearest evidence clusters north of the Apennines, bounded by the Alps, the lands of the Etruscans and the Adriatic. Among the tribes named are the Lepontii, whose language early inscriptions at Castelletto Ticino confirm as Celtic, the Insubres, builders of Mediolanon, the future Milan, and the Cenomani around Bergamo and Brescia. Further south the Boii settled near Bologna, their name recalling cattle owners, while the Lingones and Senones held land toward the Adriatic. Around 400 bc these groups pressed south, plundered Etruria and in 387 bc defeated the Roman army at the Allia and briefly occupied Rome under Brennos. The Romans finally confined the Celts to the Po valley, founding Arminium, Cremona and Placentia and defeating them near Mutina in 193 bc. The northern Celts then became heavily Romanized and faded from the record, yet left lasting marks on Roman metalworking and wheeled vehicles and cast a linguistic shadow over Latin and the Romance languages.

:::
```
