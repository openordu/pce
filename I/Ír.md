``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Ír stands in MacKillop's Dictionary of Celtic Mythology as the third-born of Míl Espáine and the figure whose name, under the Lebor Gabála [Book of Invasions], becomes the seed of the island's own. The pseudo-history records that Ír took the foremost oar in the Milesian advance against the magical tempest raised by the Tuatha Dé Danann. A shattered oar-stave and a backward fall into the sea ended him before a foot touched shore. Three feminine personifications, Ériu, Banba, and Fodla, give the country its later poetic shape, but the genealogical reckoning still keeps Ír's name on the island. T. F. O'Rahilly's 1946 Early Irish History and Mythology argued that Ír was constructed after the fact to fit political claims. The figure is often conflated in later catalogues with Íth, the earlier Milesian prince who dies at a different moment in the invasion cycle.

:::
```
