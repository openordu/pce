``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Írusán, in MacKillop's Dictionary of Celtic Mythology, is the King of the Cats of Irish memory, a felid the size of an ox with a throne in the cat-realm. The founding story opens when Senchán, Ireland's chief poet, lifts his satire against the cat kind. Írusán emerges from a cave at Knowth, hoists Senchán across his back, and bounds off with the poet. The route passes Clonmacnoise, where Saint Ciarán strikes the cat dead with a red-hot poker, sparing the poet. MacKillop treats Írusán as a single-character article that holds the poet-cat conflict together as one of the bardic tradition's small cautionary tableaux, a figure of retribution against a poet who had overstepped.

:::
```
