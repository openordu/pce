``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Iberian Peninsula names a broad territory of south-west Europe shared by Spain and Portugal. Koch reviews the Celtic peoples who lived there through archaeology. The evidence rests mainly on the castros, the walled hillfort settlements common across the region. These townships spanned the whole tract, from the Atlantic shore outward to the Ebro, and stayed in use for over a thousand years. They vary greatly in size, from under two hectares in Galicia to about twenty in the central Meseta. The largest example, the oppidum of Ulaca near Ávila, covers roughly sixty hectares. Almagro-Gorbea divides the occupation into a proto-Celtic phase and three later Celtic phases. Early castros appear in the twelfth century BC. The eastern fortresses later took up writing, coinage, the rotary quern, and imported fine-woolled sheep.

:::
```
