``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Saint Illtud was a Brythonic holy man remembered as a teacher and abbot in south Wales. His life survives in Breton and in Welsh hagiography. The oldest Brythonic Latin saint's Life, the first Life of Samson, records him as Eltutus. That text belongs to the seventh or eighth century. It shows Illtud as a learned man, ordained priest by Saint Germanus and teacher to Samson. The Life of Saint Brioc adds that Illtud studied alongside Brioc under the same master. The ninth-century Life of Paul Aurelian counts David, Gildas, Paul, and Samson among his students. Those details place his career in the fifth century. More than twenty churches in Wales and several more in Brittany take his name or sit under his patronage. Tradition ties him to a hermitage on Ynys Bŷr (Caldy Island) and to the monastery of Llanilltud Fawr.

:::
```
