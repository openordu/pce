::: magazinestyle
íath nAnann, in MacKillop's Dictionary of Celtic Mythology, is an Irish phrase meaning 'land of Anann', a poetic way of naming Ireland. The genitive Anann is the form of the mother-goddess Ana in early Irish. The phrase sets íath, the common Irish word for land, country, territory, estate, against the goddess's name. MacKillop treats íath nAnann as a single poetic compound, parallel to the more familiar Ériu, Banba, and Fodla as feminine personifications of the country. The 2008 entry is brief, cross-referencing Ana and the broader tradition of land-as-goddess naming.

:::
