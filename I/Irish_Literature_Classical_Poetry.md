Irish Literature: Classical poetry was the most honoured kind of Irish writing between about 1200 and 1650. It was composed by trained poets who worked in a fixed language called Classical Modern Irish, and it followed the strictest system of metre in the whole Irish verse. This code of metre, known as dan direach, grew through the Middle Irish period and was fixed near the start of the 1200s. The poets, often called the filidh or the bardic order, held their craft as a hereditary profession. They spent many years at schools of poetry to master the language, the metres, and the works of the great poets before them. The main duty of such a bard was to praise his patron, usually the head of a leading family. Praise of valour, noble birth, and open generosity ran through these poems. Each work was built to fit one person, naming his titles, parents, home, and deeds. A master poet could win a fine reward, and the best prize was the post of court poet, which carried land held free of tax. Of the many poems in strict verse that survive, around fifteen hundred have been published.

:::
