``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Iudic-Hael is a Breton prince and later saint preserved in a Latin hagiographic text, the Life of the Breton church. His father was the Breton king Iud-hael, a name that means 'Generous lord' and that lives on in Welsh as Ithel. A foundation tale recounts that when wariness from a day of hunting had made the king fall asleep, Iud-hael dreamed of a steep mountain in central Brittany, topped by a marvel of metal that rose toward the sky, one side strung with weapons and the other with church vessels. In the dream a woman named Adler addressed the king's son enigmatically, and the famed seer Taliesin son of Don, staying at a monastery overseen by Gildas at Ruys, read the vision and foretold that the son would be a great secular leader and then a master of the Breton church. The Life also enfolds a fine Latin praise poem whose themes come close to the earlier Welsh Gododdin. As for the name Iudic-Hael itself, it appears to join the honorific Hael, drawn from a Celtic word for generous, with another token linked to the Latin for judge, a term applied to post-Roman chieftains.

:::
```
