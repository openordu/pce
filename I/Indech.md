``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Indech, also spelled Inneach, is an Irish god. In the tale of the second great battle of Mag Tuired he was a Fomorian champion. That battle is also called Cath Maige Tuired. He met misfortune in that contest. He was the son of Domnu, a goddess of fertility. Some describe her as a version of Danu herself. In one version the goddess Mórrígan drains his blood and vitality before the battle. She does this to show where her loyalty lies. In a variant reading Ogma kills him later in the fight. Ogma fought for the Tuatha Dé Danann, the people of the goddess Danu. Indech also had an unnamed daughter. She is one of three females, with the Mórrígan and Boand, with whom the Dagda has ritual intercourse at Samain. The ancestry of Irish mythic figures is tangled. Some scholars hold that the tribes of divinities stand for waves of immigrants. Marriage across those groups linked their gods. Indech stands as the doomed champion of the older race. His fall clears the way for the Tuatha Dé Danann to rule the island. The account of his end differs from one version to the next. Yet each telling keeps him as a Fomorian warrior of note. His battle with the gods marks the old order giving way to the new. The Fomorian host that he championed stood for the hostile powers of the deep. Their defeat at Mag Tuired fixed the rule of the Tuatha Dé Danann over Ireland.

:::
```
