``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Insular Celtic names the branch of Celtic languages that grew in Ireland and Britain. The family splits into two main groups. Goidelic appears in the ancient record as Primitive Irish in ogam inscriptions, and it gives rise to Irish, Scottish Gaelic, and Manx. Brythonic surfaces in the closing centuries before Roman rule and in the Roman period as Old British, and it becomes Welsh, Breton, Cornish, and Cumbric. Most evidence also places Pictish among the Brythonic languages. Some scholars argue that Insular Celtic formed one unified language after Proto-Celtic. Others back a Gallo-Brittonic group joining British and Gaulish, or a continuum of dialects in contact. Only Goidelic and Brythonic survived into medieval and modern times. These Insular languages developed extensive written literatures and shared such changes as the loss of syllables and the weakening of consonants.

:::
```
