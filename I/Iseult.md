``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Iseult is a Cornish or Breton heroine. Her great romance pairs her with the knight Tristan. Her husband King Mark stands in opposition. James MacKillop assigns the name to two women in the Arthurian cycle. One is Iseult of Ireland. The other is Iseult of Brittany, also called Iseult of the White Hands. The Irish Iseult loves Tristan through a potion, even though she once vowed blood revenge on him. He killed her father. Tristan, now her escort, carries her toward her promised husband, King Mark. After many secret dealings she dies next to her wounded lover. Mark once kept a single strand of golden hair. Tristan went to Ireland to find its owner. Her mother brewed a wedding-night drink from secret herbs. Iseult served it to Tristan on the home voyage. The draught bound the pair in endless love. She still became the bride of Mark. Her maid Brangien took her place in bed on the honeymoon. Many tribulations followed. Mark set a test of purity. Iseult had to cross a stream that would drown an unfaithful wife. Tristan came disguised as a beggar and helped her cross. She could swear that no man had touched her except her husband and the beggar. The pair finally parted in grief. Tristan married the second Iseult, who bore the name of his beloved. That marriage went unconsummated. Jealousy led her to lie. She denied that the Irish Iseult was on the vessel Tristan awaited. He died of a broken heart before his love could reach him. Writers compare the two lovers to the Irish figures Deirdre and Gráinne. The Welsh form of the name is Essyllt. Variants are Isolda, Yseult, and Ysolt. The Celtic motif of sovereignty colors the tale. The goddess who chooses the king decides who may rule.

:::
```
