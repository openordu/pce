``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Indo-European names a large family of related languages that all descend from one common ancestor. Linguists infer that ancestor by the historical comparative method. Languages that share deep parallels in words, sounds, and grammar must have grown from a single speech. Latin and the Romance languages show the pattern in a recent form. Because Latin spread with the Roman Empire and later broke into French, Italian, Portuguese, Spanish, and Romanian, scholars can see the family history from scratch. For Indo-European the ancestor belongs to a far earlier horizon before any written record. Celtic forms a single member among the twelve subdivisions in this family, so every Celtic tongue counts as Indo-European. Irish and Welsh stand as siblings, while Irish relates more loosely to Indic speech as a distant cousin. The Anatolian names appear first in the record, followed by Greek and by the Indic and Iranian groups.

:::
```
