::: magazinestyle
Ossory is the anglicized name, the dictionary explains from the Irish Osraige, of a medieval realm whose extent nearly matches today's diocese of Ossory across County Kilkenny and neighbouring ground. The entry is compact but anchors a dense web of cross-references in the volume: the annals and saints' lives repeatedly stage figures of this kingdom, from the invading druid of the Osraige in the settlement tales to the patron of the diocese honoured at Kilkenny. The name itself, by the folk etymology the dictionary's Irish headword preserves, invokes the deer-people, and the kingdom's location between Leinster and Munster gave its fighters a recurrent role in provincial wars recorded in the narrative literature. MacKillop's file keeps Ossory distinct from Ormond, its eastern neighbour, and from the Osraige people-name, which carries its own signpost entry.

:::
