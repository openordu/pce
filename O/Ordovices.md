::: magazinestyle
Ordovices, whose name the dictionary glosses as hammer fighters, were a people of pre-Roman and Roman Wales, cited by the ancient geographer Ptolemy in the second century of the common era. MacKillop 2008 sets their territory as stretching between the Welsh border at Herefordshire and Anglesey, the island of the druids. Their name, on the offered gloss, may connect them with the prehistoric axe-making centre at Graig Lwyd in Caernarvonshire, an industry whose product travelled across the region and may have earned the tribe its hammering reputation. The dictionary's most vivid notice comes from Tacitus, who describes the ad-59 Roman campaign against the Ordovices, when the legions faced women in black robes, hair loose like furies, torches in hand. The entry cites Barry Cunliffe's Iron Age Communities in Britain and stands with the dictionary's other tribal entries as part of its archaeological and historical coverage of Celtic Britain.

:::
