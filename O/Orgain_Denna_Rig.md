``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Orgain Denna Rig, the Irish title usually rendered The Destruction or Plunder of Dind Rig, is a ninth-century narrative of the Cycle of Kings that survives in three manuscripts: Rawlinson B502, also called the Book of Glendalough, the Book of Leinster, and the Yellow Book of Lecan, with the story repeated by Geoffrey Keating. MacKillop 2008 retells the revenge plot that brings Labraid Loingsech, ancestor-deity of the Leinstermen, to power. His uncle Cobthach, king of Brega, so wastes away with envy of his brother Lóegaire Lore that he earns the name the Meagre One of Brega; he murders Lóegaire by knife and poisons his son Ailill Ane, seizing Leinster. The speechless heir Móen Ollam regains speech when a hurley strike makes him cry out, earning the name Labraid. Exiled in the west, he wins the hand of Muiriath through Craiphtine's sleep-music, aided by King Scoriath of the Fir Morca, and returns to storm Dind Rig, near Leighlinbridge in County Carlow, with the harper's music lulling the defenders. The tale's grim climax has Labraid lock his uncle and thirty other kings inside an iron house heated by 150 bellows, his own mother choosing death with a call to save his honour. Afterward Labraid repopulates Leinster with 2,200 foreigners bearing broad spears, the origin of his epithet Loingsech. The dictionary points to the iron-house episode's counterpart in the Mabinogi's Branwen branch and the Book of Leinster's date of 307 bc for Cobthach's death.
```
