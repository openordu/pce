``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The ollam was the highest rank among the seven levels of the Fili. The Fili was the Irish bardic order. The title also appears as ollamh and ollave. Someone at this rank had to recite all 250 chief tales. The bard also had to recite 100 of the lesser ones. The same word ollam appears in later legendary kingship. Ollam Fodla, also called Ollamh Fodhla, was a fabled king. He ruled at Tara. He is said to have set up the fair held every three years at Samain. Old Irish names this fair an oenach. He stood tenth in the line of ard ri. Ard ri means the high-kingship. The line was counted from Éremón. His renown rested on poetry and wisdom. He is also credited with drawing up an early code of laws. He shares the honor of dividing the country into five parts. Several other figures share that same credit. The bardic ollam and the legendary king Ollam Fodla thus share a single title across very different roles.

:::
```
