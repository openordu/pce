``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Oileáin Árann (Aran Islands), an island group in the county of Galway known to English speakers as the Aran Islands, are three islands that stretch across the entrance to Galway Bay. They are Inis Mór (Inishmore, 'Great Island'), Inis Meáin (Inishmaan, 'Middle Island'), and Inis Oirr (Inisheer, 'Eastern Island'). The landscape is set with imposing stone forts and churches in stone. Some of the forts date originally from the Late Bronze Age, about 1200 to 600 bc, though they were rebuilt at times into the medieval period. The monumental cliff-top fort of Dún Aonghasa on Inis Mór matches this archaeological pattern and may have served ceremonial purposes. The Dindshenchas Érenn traces the origin of Dún Aonghasa to a legendary ruler named Aonghas. The islands are also rich in ecclesiastical remains, with a tradition of early monastic settlement, and they preserve a distinctive limestone landscape and a living Irish-speaking community. The islands have long drawn scholars, antiquarians, and writers interested in Irish language, folklore, and material culture.
```
