``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Ogma is an orator-warrior among the Tuatha Dé Danann. MacKillop 2008 ranks him among that people's three principal champions. Lug Lámfhota stands with him, and the Dagda does too. He serves as patron of poetry and eloquence. Tradition fables that he devised the ogham alphabet, whose name is a philological cognate of his own. His eloquence earns him the sobriquet Cermait, honey-mouthed. That title also belongs to a son of the Dagda, and the overlap breeds confusion between the two figures. He also bears the sobriquet Grianainech, sun-countenance. His father is usually named as Elatha. Eithne is the usual choice for his mother. The Dagda counts as his brother. By Étan, a daughter of Dian Cécht, he fathers two sons: Tuireann and the satirist Cairbre mac Ethne. Ogma holds a leading role in Cath Maige Tuired and in the events that build toward that fight. Lug Lámfhota arrives at the Tuatha Dé court for the first time. Ogma meets him there in a trial of strength. Each must throw a flagstone over the wall of the royal hall, and Ogma loses. Bres held the kingship, and his reign went badly for the champion. Ogma endures humiliation under him. He is forced to haul firewood like a common labourer. In the battle he crosses weapons with Indech the Fomorian, though the texts disagree over which of them fell. The name Ogma resembles the name Ogmios. Ogmios is the Gaulish god of eloquence, and commentators have long hunted the connection. Two leading early twentieth-century scholars, Thurneysen and van Hamel, rejected any such link. Van Hamel pressed the case in his 1934 paper for the British Academy. Monaghan's encyclopedia, the base source here, adds received lore from a later age of the story. In the age of the Milesian conquest, an Ogma falls fighting a Milesian invader. The same account weds his brothers Mac Cuill and Mac Greine to the three land goddesses of Ireland. That pairing makes a trio of parallel gods. Monaghan also distinguishes a different Mac Cecht, the champion of the doomed king Conaire. At the hostel of Da Derga, that hero slays the man who beheaded the king. He then gives the severed head its last drink. MacKillop's profile and Monaghan's lore differ in scope but agree on the core. Ogma stands for speech made power: a warrior whose true weapon is eloquence.
```
