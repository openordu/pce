``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Oidheadh Chlainne Tuireann, also titled Aided Chlainne Tuireann, is the Irish title for the Mythological Cycle prose tale that English readers know as the tale of Tuireann's children and their fate, a second member among the Three Sorrows that storytelling preserves. The dictionary dates the core story possibly to the eleventh century, with the earliest surviving text from the sixteenth. Tuireann, a son of Ogma and Étan (1), becomes father, by the divine Ana, of the three sons Brian (1), Iuchair, and Iucharba, or in variant texts Brigit. The plot turns on Cian, father of Lug Lámfhota, whom the brothers murder by stoning after he hides from them as a pig. Lug sets an éiric, a blood price, of eight retrieves: the apples of the Hesperides, the healing pigskin of King Tuis of Greece, the poisoned spear later called Gáe Assail, two chariot steeds of Sicily, seven self-renewing pigs of Assal, the whelp Failinis, a cooking-spit from Inis Fionnuire, and three shouts on the hill of Miodhchaoin in Lochlainn. The brothers perform the first seven tasks through inexhaustible daring, but at Miodhchaoin's hill their opponents' spears mortally wound all three even as Miodhchaoin and his sons perish. Brian fulfils the final demand with feeble shouts, and Lug, still angry, refuses Tuireann the healing pigskin for his dying son. Tuireann buries the three in one grave and soon dies himself, while Lug keeps Gáe Assail as his favourite sword and Failinis as his lapdog. The dictionary records the tale's poor critical standing, dismissed by Myles Dillon as of interest chiefly to a folklorist.

:::
```
