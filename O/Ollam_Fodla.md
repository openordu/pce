::: magazinestyle
Ollam Fodla, in the fuller spelling Ollamh Fodhla, is a legendary king of Tara whom MacKillop 2008 credits with founding Tara's great fair, a gathering convened on a three-year rhythm at Samain. The dictionary counts him the tenth ard ri, or high king, in line from Éremón, the Milesian ancestor, and esteems him for poetry and wisdom. Two further institutional firsts gather around his name: tradition makes him the framer of the first laws, and he stands among the several figures credited with dividing the country into five parts. The entry sits close to the dictionary's article on the ollam, the master-poet who trained twelve years and mastered 350 tales, and the shared root is no accident: the king's very name invokes the highest rank of learned craft. His legend thus fuses kingship, law, and poetry into a single founding gesture at Tara, the assembly-place where those institutions later lived.

:::
