::: magazinestyle
óenach, in MacKillop's Dictionary of Celtic Mythology, is the Old Irish word commonly translated as 'popular assembly' or 'fair'. MacKillop records the form as a short entry that points the reader toward aonach, the modern Irish spelling, where the full treatment of the assemblies sits. The 2008 dictionary lists widely reported early-Ireland instances at Tara, Tailtiu, Tlachtga, and Uisnech, with athletic contests and other competitive gatherings. The modern Irish word aonach covers the surviving fair tradition, with four annual openings in the calendar year and Millstreet in County Cork as one named example. MacKillop treats óenach as the philological headword for the assembly tradition that survives in the modern aonach page.

:::
