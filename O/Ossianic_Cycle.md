::: magazinestyle
Ossianic Cycle is the heading under which MacKillop 2008 routes readers to the Fenian Cycle, the great body of narrative about Fionn mac Cumhaill and his warrior band. The dictionary's note under the cross-reference carries its own historical weight: the immense popularity of James Macpherson's Poems of Ossian, from the late eighteenth century through much of the nineteenth, meant that the word Ossianic, in that age, stood for early Celtic culture entire, not merely the Fenian material. The term thus marks a stage in reception history, when European readers met the Fianna through Macpherson's contested fabrications and applied the narrator's name to everything ancient and Celtic. The dictionary's practice keeps the cycle's proper heading at the Fenian Cycle entry, where the tales, the band, and the scholarly framing live, and treats Ossianic as a period label with a documented career of overreach.

:::
