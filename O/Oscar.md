``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Oscar stands among the leading warriors of Fenian narrative. MacKillop brands him the Galahad of the Cycle. He is Oisin's son and Fionn mac Cumhaill's grandson. MacKillop lists the forms Osgar, Oscur, and Osca, and reads the name as deer-lover, built on os, fawn. Monaghan's entry names the poet Oisín as his father. It names the mortal Eibhir as mother. It ties the deer sense to his grandmother Sadb, once a deer. Monaghan has him enter the Fianna in his father's steps. He leads its fiercest fighters, the terrible broom that sweeps battlefields bare. He takes command after Fionn dies. MacKillop finds no trace of Oscar in Fenian literature before the 11th century. Some commentators therefore suggest the name began as an alternative form of Oisin. As a boy he is so clumsy that the Fianna leave him home. Storytellers then make him the Cycle's ideal hero, bravest, steadiest, and most often victorious, first to answer any fearsome challenger. Oral tradition makes him wrestle Goll mac Moma to prove he is the strongest Fianna of them all. He sails overseas with Labraid Lámderg, called the red hand. The tale Toraighecht Dhiarmada agus Ghráinne shows Oscar siding with the lovers Diarmait and Gráinne against his own grandfather. MacKillop names Étaín (3) as his usual consort, a paler lover than Diarmait. Monaghan instead gives him the wife Aidín, who grieves herself to death when Oisín carries him into the Otherworld. The Cycle peaks at Cath Gabhra. Oscar deals the hated high king Cairbre Lifechair a mortal wound, and the dying king spears him through the heart. Oscar dies with a joke on his lips, and Fionn weeps as never elsewhere in the Cycle. A huge cairn at Benn Étair, today's Howth, covers him. Death does not end him. In the Christianized tale, the Fianna escape hell. Oscar holds the rearguard with an unbreakable thong while his comrades break free. In the Acallam na Senórach, Oisin tells St Patrick that only God could beat Oscar. MacKillop also records a second Oscar, the son born to Oisin and Niam during their life in Tír na nÓg. A third Oscar appears in the Poems of Ossian that James Macpherson published between 1760 and 1763. That verse cycle is built on the Fenian hero. He is Ossian's son and Fingal's grandson. Temora kills him off in Book 1 after Cairbar's druid Olla goads him at a dinner. Malvina mourns him as his widow. Macpherson's vogue spread the name across Europe. Marshal Bernadotte of France reigned as Charles XIV. His wife Désirée Cleary came from a Dublin merchant's family. The two named their son Oscar. He reigned over Sweden and Norway as Oscar I (1844-59).
```
