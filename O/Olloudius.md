``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Olloudius names, in the Roman period, a native Celtic god whose cult MacKillop 2008 attests on both sides of the channel, in Gaul and Britain alike, standing close to the Gaulish Mars, for whom his name sometimes serves as an epithet. The surviving artefacts steer the god's character away from war: his links to Mars concern healing, fertility, and peaceful protection. One English site, in the Cotswolds, yields his image: a small head on an elongated body, capped and cloaked instead of armoured, one hand holding an offering saucer, the other a double cornucopia, an iconography of prosperity. At Customs Scrubs in Gloucestershire the same deity holds sword, shield, and spear, yet still carries a cornucopia beside the weapons. The dictionary adds a Continental coda: with the same spelling again, the Narbonenses paid cult to Olloudius at a site close to today's Antibes in France. The name itself the dictionary derives from a compound suggesting great tree, and the entry is cited among the epigraphic evidence for Celtic deities in the volume's survey of Romano-Celtic religion.

:::
```
