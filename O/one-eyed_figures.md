::: magazinestyle
one-eyed figures are a recurrent motif in Celtic folklore, where the menacing giant with a single orb set in the middle of the brow is widespread. The pattern matches the international folk tale type F531.1.1.1 of Polyphemus in Homer's Odyssey. Most Celtic instances appear in Gaelic traditions across Ireland and Scotland. T. F. O'Rahilly argued in 1946 that the lone orb evokes the sun, as shown by the epithet Deirgderc (red eye) given to king Eochaid. The lack of two orbs did not bar Eochaid from kingship, since a king could not display any bodily shortcoming, such as Nuada's loss of an arm. The single-orb Goll mac Moma might also be called Áed the fire-god; in many Fenian tales, Goll's solar origin is hidden behind a leather patch.

:::
