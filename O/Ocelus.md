``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Ocelus was a Welsh and British god. The obscurity of this deity is complete, for only a few inscriptions name him. The Romans linked him with Mars. MacKillop gives the name in Latinized form and counts three known inscriptions, all from the Roman occupation. He reads Ocelus as presumably indigenous. The god is dearly linked to Mars. Twice the god's name works as a title for Mars. One inscription pairs that use with the epithet Lenus. Caerwent in Gwent keeps two of the recorded calls. Invokers named Ocelus there beside their Mars. A much-reduced statue survives there. Only two pairs of feet remain on it. One pair is human. The other pair belongs to a goose. The goose feet point to a cult image that once showed the god standing on or beside the bird. The Roman army carried his cult, for the inscribed stones are military in setting. Caerwent was the civitas capital of the Silures, so the worship there had a local British base. The pairing with Mars follows the regular Roman habit of equating native gods with their own.

:::
```
