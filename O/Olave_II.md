``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Olave II, whose name the dictionary renders as the Norse Olaf, reigns in Manx legend as a ruler who planted the island's royal line, also called Goddardson as the son of Goddard Crovan. MacKillop 2008 credits him with justice and strength and above all with the great sword named Macabuin, the enchanted blade that gives the story its turn. When a mischance of Olave's strips Macabuin of its enchantment, the sword's maker, Loan Maclibhuin, seeks vengeance against his king. The tale resolves swiftly: Olave cuts the smith down, took Emergaid the Fair, the smith's daughter, to wife, and the couple's children found the dynasty of Man. The entry stands as the dictionary's bridge between Manx dynastic legend and the wider Celtic narrative world, since the same Loan Maclibhuin appears in Fenian tradition as the smith of Diarmait's sword. In MacKillop's telling, Olave's legend explains a dynasty rather than a cult, rooting the island's monarchy in a smith's broken enchantment and a king's decisive violence.

:::
```
