::: magazinestyle
Osraige, with the variant spelling Osraighe, is the Irish name that MacKillop 2008 files as a pointer to the medieval kingdom of Ossory. The people-name lies behind the anglicized kingdom name: the Osraige were the folk of the Ossory territory, roughly coextensive with the modern diocese in County Kilkenny and adjacent areas, and the dictionary's article on Ossory carries the fuller political and ecclesiastical account. The entry belongs to the dictionary's running practice of giving Irish-language forms their own headings, so that a reader who meets Osraige in an annal entry, a genealogy, or a saint's life can route back to the governing article instead of stumbling on an unfamiliar inflected shape. The form preserves the people's own name for themselves, the deer-people by the common etymology, which the anglicized Ossory half-conceals.

:::
