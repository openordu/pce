``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Oonagh, with the variant Oona, is the anglicization that MacKillop 2008 gives for the name of Úna, wife of Finnbheara, king of the Irish fairies. The cross-reference matters because the fairy queen lives in the dictionary under her Irish form, where tradition crowns her beauty above every other woman, gold hair falling clear to the ground, with seventeen sons to her name. Other traditions grant her a dwelling apart at Cnoc Sídh Úna, Knockshegouna, in County Tipperary, a fairy hill distinct from her husband's court. The dictionary assigns the figure the folk motif F252.2, marking her as the standard beautiful fairy bride of oral tradition. Readers who meet Oonagh in anglicized folktales, song, or place-lore should follow the spelling back to Úna, where the fuller account of the fairy marriage stands.

:::
```
