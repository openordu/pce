``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Oengus Mac ind Oc is a god of the Tuath De, born of the Dagda and of Boand. He is a figure of youth and beauty, and his name means 'Oengus the Young Son'. Oengus is a common name in the Goidelic languages, and its Scottish form is Aonghas, anglicised Angus. Koch connects the name to the Old Celtic *Oino-gustus, 'chosen one'. Some scholars link the god behind Oengus to the Gallo-Brittonic Maponos and the Welsh Mabon. Oengus is bound to the great tomb of Newgrange, the Brug na Boinne, which is often called his home. He appears in the Mythological Cycle, in Fiannaiocht, and in the Ulster Cycle. Two themes run through his stories. The first is a game with time, where one day stretches into many days or even forever. The second is a close tie between the doings of the gods and the fates of mortal rulers and heroes. In his birth tale, related in Tochmarc Etaine, the Dagda sends away Eithne's husband and casts a spell so nine months pass as one day. In Aislinge Oengusa, 'The Dream of Oengus', the god dreams each night for a whole year of a lovely young woman and falls ill with love. His parents find the girl, named Caer, in Connacht. In another tale Oengus tricks his father into granting him the Brug na Boinne for 'a day and a night', which he keeps for all time. In the later love story of Diarmaid and Grainne, the young god is the supernatural foster father of Diarmaid and shields the lovers from Finn mac Cumaill. Koch sets him in the divine tribe, son of the Dagda and of Boand, the spirit of the Boyne. His birth tale in the Wooing of Etain makes the Dagda send the husband away and cast a spell so nine months pass as one day. The Dream of Oengus, a fore-tale of the Cattle-Raid, tells how the girl Caer came to him in dreams for a year. He won the mound of Brug na Boinne from his father on the trick of a day and a night, held for all time. In the Pursuit of Diarmaid and Grainne he fosters the hero Diarmaid. Two threads recur: the bending of time, and the knot that ties gods to the kings and heroes of mortals.
```
