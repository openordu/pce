``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Ormond is the anglicized name, recorded by MacKillop 2008 in its Old Irish shape Aurmumu and its modern Irish shape Urmhumha, naming the old East Munster territory. The dictionary dates the division to pre-conquest times, when the province of Munster was split into two halves, of which Ormond was the eastern, a division that later gave the earldom of Ormond its name. The territory is coextensive with most of County Kilkenny and north Tipperary, the lands between the midland plateau and the southern coast. The entry belongs to the dictionary's set of provincial-geography notices, where anglicized territorial names preserve the shapes of early medieval kingdoms long after the kingdoms themselves dissolved. Readers of Ormond place-lore, the Butler earls, or the twinned territory of Desmond are directed to the Munster article for the provincial frame.

:::
```
