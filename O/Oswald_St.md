``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Oswald, St, was a king of Northumbria whose reign began in 634/5 and ended with his death on 5 August 642. His father, the pagan king Æthelferth of Bernicia, fell fighting Rædwald of East Anglia, backer of the rival Eadwine, at the battle of the river Idle in 617. After that, Oswald and his brother Oswydd passed eighteen years in exile among the Gaels. He spent most of that time on or near Iona, whether on the island itself, under Abbot Uirgnou the Briton, or in monasteries and secular courts shaped by Ionan influence. The princes came home as devout Christians, fluent in Irish and devoted to the cause of the Iona community. Oswald overthrew Cadwallon of Gwynedd, who had held the north for about a year, at a field near Hadrian's Wall. The Welsh Latin witnesses name the place Cantscaul or Catscar, while Beda calls it Hefenfelth, the heavenly field. Adomnán of Iona set the credit for victory to Colum Cille, who, he says, had appeared warning Oswald in a dream before the battle. After conquering, Oswald set off a second conversion of Northumbria, sending to Iona for a bishop. The man who came was Aedán, later Saint Aidan, who in 635 placed his church on Lindisfarne, over the water from the royal fort at Bamburgh, as the spiritual centre of the north. Aedán knew no English, and Oswald would often interpret for him. In all, the connection between Oswald and the Gaelic and British Christian world of his age gave Northumbria a marked Celtic colour.
```
