::: magazinestyle
Oirbsiu stands as a little-understood epithet belonging to the sea-divinity Manannán mac Lir, recorded by MacKillop 2008 with its genitive form Oirbsean and the fuller shape Oirbsiu Mór, possibly connoting inundation. The dictionary attaches the name to place-name evidence: the old Irish form Loch nOirbsen survives in Lough Corrib, the great lake of seventy square miles in County Galway and Mayo. The entry suggests how an epithet of a god of the sea could settle onto an inland sheet of water, preserving the divine title in geography long after cult knowledge faded. Commentators cited in the wider article treat the word as one more strand in Manannán's dense web of names, alongside his better-known titles. A reader who meets Orbsen, the anglicized genitive, in genealogical or topographical writing is meant to follow it here.

:::
