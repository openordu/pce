Owain Lawgoch, whose Welsh byname the dictionary translates as red hand, figures in Welsh folklore as an ancient ruler of Britain kept alive by Sleeping Warrior stories, as MacKillop 2008 records. The type is familiar from the wider Celtic world: the hero who did not die but sleeps with his warriors in a cave or hill, waiting for the hour when the country needs him. The dictionary's illustrative story sends a simple drover named Dafydd, who strays into Owain's chamber and carries off a gold lump, a theft from the sleeping king's treasure that folklorists read as the human intruder's ticket out or his doom depending on the telling. The dictionary assigns the tale folk motif E502, placing the Welsh hero in the international catalogue of sleeping-warrior legends. Owain Lawgoch's entry thereby preserves the political edge of the type, a national champion whose awakening would restore Welsh rule, distilled into a one-paragraph folkloric notice.

:::
