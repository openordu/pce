::: magazinestyle
Orbsen is the genitive form of Oirbsiu, the obscure epithet of the sea-divinity Manannán mac Lir, as MacKillop 2008 records it. The genitive matters in practice because it is the shape that survives in Irish place-lore: the old form Loch nOirbsen, the lake of Oirbsen, stands behind the modern name of Lough Corrib, and genealogical and topographical tracts cite the god in exactly this inflected shape. The dictionary files the grammatical form as its own heading so that a reader who meets Orbsen in a source can find the epithet's entry rather than stall on an unfamiliar case-ending. The entry is a small piece of the dictionary's larger method, in which inflected and anglicized fragments of divine names get their own signposts back to the governing article.

:::
