``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Ordinalia is a trio of plays written in Middle Cornish. Scholars date the set to the end of the 1300s. Three pieces form the whole cycle. The first is Ordinale de Origine Mundi, known today as Origo Mundi. It opens with the world's creation, then moves from Adam and Eve to the brothers Cain and Abel, and then to the ark of Noah. Moses later discovers three rods that join to form the rood. A comic turn shows the rood raised as a beam that fits nowhere in the temple that Solomon finishes. The prophet Maximilla ends the first play when her gown catches fire. The middle work is called Passio Christi, and it retells the Passion. It runs from Christ's temptation in the desert to the Crucifixion. The torturers find cruel fun in their task, so the scene is both tragic and comic at once. The third work is Resurrexio Domini, the Resurrection of the Lord. Devils carry off the corpse of the thief whom the earth throws out, and the whole cycle closes at the Ascension. The story records the fall and the recovery of all human beings. A large part of the plot is drawn from apocrypha books. The weightiest debt lies with the Holy Rood legend. The same concerns recur in Gwreans an Bys and in Pascon Agan Sarfth. A blend of biblical and Cornish place names points to writers working at Glasney College. Actors staged the set across three days inside open-air theatres named plen-an-gwary, cut into the ground. Two of these stages still stand, one at Piran Round and another near Penwith. The one old copy survives as Bodleian Library MS 791. A run of the whole cycle took place in 1969, and a fresh English verse translation is now in hand.

:::
```
