``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Tochmarc is the Irish word for a wooing or courtship. MacKillop explains its work as a title word. The word opens the names of a whole class of early Irish narratives. Each such tale is a courtship story, and the class stands beside the pursuit tales, the Tóraigheacht. Several full titles matter here. The Tochmarc Becfhola tells how Becfhola, a queen of Tara, loved the fairy Flann ua Fedach. Her choices unseated a king's peace. The Tochmarc Emire carries the wooing of Emer. The Tochmarc Étaíne is the Irish title of the story English readers call The Wooing of Étain. It belongs to the Mythological Cycle. Its date may reach back to the eighth or ninth century. Only broken and disjointed fragments survive. The Yellow Book of Lecan keeps them. R. I. Best recovered the long-missing pages in the 1930s. Even so the whole text stays disconnected. The tale opens with Étain found by Angus Óg. Her entanglement with Midir and her magical transformations follow. Behind them stands the Dagda's trick. He won Boand away from her husband Elcmar, lord of the Brug na Bóinne at Newgrange. That trickery produced Angus Óg himself, the god of poetry. The wooing tales thus join romance to the fate of kings and gods alike.

:::
```
