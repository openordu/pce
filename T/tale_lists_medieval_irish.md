::: magazinestyle
The medieval Irish tale lists are catalogues of the stories of early Ireland. These catalogues record the titles known to medieval readers. A story relates how the poet Urard sought the king of Tara. Urard had suffered a great injustice from a northern branch of the royal line. The king was called Domnall mac Muirchertaig, who died in 990. Urard asked the king for judgement about his misfortune. The king then asked the poet to give news. The old word for news carries both tidings and stories. Urard spoke of many tales he knew. Among them he named Orgain Cathrach Mail Milscothaig. That title means the destruction of Mael Milscothach's fort. The king chose to hear that particular story. The tale hid the poet's own misfortunes. When the king saw this, he promised full reparation. Today scholars agree that neither list gives a true record. Both catalogues hold additions and changes. Some titles appear in only one of the two lists. Still, the lists reveal the tales that readers knew and favoured. Most titles belong to the stories of the Ulster Cycle. Only a few titles relate to Finn mac Cumaill and the Fianna. The lists give a window into the literary world of medieval Ireland.
