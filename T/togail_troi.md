``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Togail Troí is the medieval Irish tale of the fall of Troy. The title means the destruction of Troy. It ranks as the best loved and most far-reaching classical story in Ireland. The text has a long and layered history. An early Irish list of tales already names it. It is the only classical piece kept in the great codex called the Book of Leinster. The first rendering came in the tenth century. The oldest surviving copy dates to the eleventh. Togail Troí is a free retelling. The bare tale of Dares is stretched out and told in the native manner of the Ulster sagas. Mac Eoin set out three prose forms and one rare verse form. A later scholar offered a different grouping. Every copy except one Dublin manuscript belongs to a shared line. The copies differ by how far the text was expanded. The longest form takes in long passages from the poems of Publius Papinius Statius. It adds a sequel about the rebuilding of the city. Printing every form is the key to seeing how Ireland received the classical tradition.

:::
```
