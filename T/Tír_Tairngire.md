``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Tír Tairngire is the Land of Promise, one of the otherworldly paradises of early Irish tradition. The name comes from the Irish words for prophecy and promise. Manannán mac Lir is closely linked with the place, although he does not rule there. Reaching it is the goal of many eachtrada, the adventure tales. Brendan seeks it in his Navigatio. Cormac mac Airt visits its Fountain of Knowledge in the adventure of Cormac. Manannán carries his three-day-old son Mongán there to gain otherworldly knowledge, and the boy stays for years. The youth Ciabhán is taken there, and some texts have him elope with the beautiful Clidna. After Clidna's adultery there with Gaidiar, a son of Manannán, Bé Chuma is driven out. The Tuatha Dé Danann brought the rowan tree out of this land. In the tale of Art's otherworld adventure, the rulers are Dáire and Rigru Rosclethan, a pair called sinless. They couple only to beget their otherworldly son Ségda Sáerlabraid. Dillon and Chadwick note an Icelandic anticipation in Odainsakr, the field of the undying, and in Land lifanda manna.

:::
```
