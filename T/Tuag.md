``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Tuag is an Irish heroine. At the age of 15 she drew the eye of Manannan Mac Lir, the god of the sea. She had grown up at Tara under the care of the high king Conaire. She refused every suitor. MacKillop adds a second telling of her end. Tuag was kept under the guard of women. So Manannán turned for help to the druid Fer Fidail. The attempt failed, and both Tuag and Fer Fidail were killed. Monaghan's account keeps the gentler version. Fer I, a dwarf and bard of Manannan, came to her. He sang a sleep song to her and carried her asleep away. On the way he set her down to rest. The waters of the Bann rose and took her. MacKillop sets the drowning at Inber Glas, the Bann's mouth. The place was sometimes called Tuagh Inbir in her memory. The wave Tonn Túaige, Tuag's Wave, also keeps her name. Her name remains on the Bann and its mouth, Tuag Inber. So she is held a river-goddess like Boand and Sinann.

:::
```
