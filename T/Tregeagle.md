``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Jan Tregeagle is the central figure of the best-known Cornish ghost stories. MacKillop records both name forms, Jan and John. He traces the real man to a plain steward who served the Earl of Radnor. Legend lifts the steward into something far darker. The ghost-story figure is a Faust-like lawyer who bargains his soul away to the devil. He murders his own wife and steals the estates of an orphan to do it. Some accounts show him as a hostile giant of terrible size. Not every tale makes him cruel. In one story the dead man is raised to give testimony at a trial. That portrait gives even a damned ghost a kind of civic use. He can also get the worst of things. One tale condemns him to empty the deep water of Dozmere Pool using a leaking limpet shell. The pool is a great and lonely water in the Cornish uplands, and the shell never stops leaking. His task has no end, and the Devil drags him away any time he stops to rest. A clergyman once set him a like labor, to drain a pond with a leaking vessel. His stubborn greed keeps him bound to the earth even after death. The Cornish still recall him, and loud roaring over the moors is said to echo his cry. An old genealogy draws no line between the harsh lawyer and the ragged giant. His tale stands with the staple ghost legends of Cornwall, mixing murder, a devil's bargain, and unending penance.

:::
```
