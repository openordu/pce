``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Teutates is a deity of the continental Celts, a god who held the tribe together. His name carries a root that means tribe or people, like the old Irish tuath. Because of this root he works as a title, letting a Roman god take in a local protector of the people. The Roman poet Lucan set Teutates with Taranis and Esus as the trinity of Gaulish gods. He wrote that Gauls near Massalia offered their prisoners of war to this trinity. Dedications join Teutates with the god of strength Mars, with the northern hunter Coocidus, with Apollo, and with the messenger god Mercury. Such stones turn up across the empire. They appear from Seckau in Austria to Rome and Castor by the north road, and as far as the British woad. The find at Sausset in Gaul pairs Teutates with Mercury in one clue. The scholiast on Lucan took Teutates to be a Mars who was fed blood. Other notes read him as a Mercury who received a drowning in a great kettle. The same root floods the German word for the people, the whole folk, braced and sound. Thus the deity keeps the vow a tribe swears to its own people's god. His wide line of names runs over all the land of the Celts.

:::
```
