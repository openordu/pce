``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Tadg is an Irish name found in myth and history. The best known bearer was Tadg mac Nuadat. He was Fionn Mac Cumhaill's grandfather. Readers link the name Tadg to the god Nuada. The name also marks a son of Nuada. Another Tadg fostered King Cormac Mac Airt. A third Tadg was the son of Ciabhan who met the fairy queen Clidna. MacKillop sets the name among the commonest men's names in Irish. The root sense is poet. The phrase tadhg na sraide, Tadg on the street, is the Irish for the man on the street. The transliteration Thady was once common and now sounds archaic. Other forms were forced into English shapes as Timothy, Thaddeus, and Theophilus, with no true link to those names. In Northern Ireland the spellings Teague and Taigue came to carry scorn for Catholics. Among the many bearers of the name, early literature returns most often to Tadg mac Nuadat, the grandfather of Fionn. Two more Tadgs of the tales carry their own weight. Tadg mac Céin was a son of Cian and a grandson of Ailill Aulomm. His adventures brought him the sight of Clidna, held the noblest and loveliest of women. She was attended by three brightly coloured birds. In the Otherworld he reached a strange island under two sons of Bodb. A woman there said the line of Ireland's kings from Conn Cétchathach dwelt upon it. He met Connla and his fairy woman, and he saw the apple tree that had lured Connla. Back in the mortal world he learned a year had passed. He joined Cormac mac Airt in war, but the alliance proved a poor bargain. Tadg had set his heart on Tara. Cormac heard of it and bribed the charioteer to break off the circuit short of the full circle. The prize thus slipped away, and Tadg repaid the betrayal by killing the man. Pedigrees of Munster and Leinster cite this Tadg as an ancestor-deity. Tadg mac Nuadat stands at the head of the Fenian line. He was a druid, the son of Nuadu Necht, and an aspect of Nuadu Airgedam. His daughter Muirenn Munchaem bore Fionn to Cumhall. Tadg opposed the marriage because the union threatened his ancestral seat. He persuaded Conn to turn Goll mac Moma loose on Cumhall, and Goll slew him. In variant texts Fionn overcame Tadg later and took the Hill of Allen from him. Some commentators go further. They read Tadg as an alias for Nuadu himself, the old god wearing a mortal name.
```
