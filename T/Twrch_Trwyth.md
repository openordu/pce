``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Twrch Trwyth is the great magical boar of the hunt imposed on Culhwch. The name carries the Welsh word for a male boar. Culhwch must hunt the beast as one of his impossible tasks. A comb and a razor lie between its ears. The groom Ysbaddaden Bencawr needs those implements to trim his hair. So hard a quarry demands the help of Mabon and of Arthur himself. Addressing Arthur, the beast tells how it was once a king. Magic changed it into its present shape as punishment for some unspecified evil. A likelier reading sees in the animal another shape of the divine boar honored in the oldest Celtic mythology. Allusion to the beast appears in other early texts beside the hunt tale. Rhys collected the folklore traces in 1901. The annotations of Bromwich and Evans guide readers through Culhwch ac Olwen, and Carey prints a miscellany study of 1992. The beast appears in the lists of early Welsh lore beside other marvels.

:::
```
