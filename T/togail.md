``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Togail is an Irish word that means destruction. MacKillop gives it the same work as tochmarc. It opens the titles of a whole family of early Irish narratives, and the plural form is Togla. The greatest of these tales is the Togail Bruidne Da Derga. English readers know it as the destruction story of Da Derga's Hostel. Its written form reaches back at least to the eleventh century. The story itself was likely composed in the eighth or ninth. Manuscript books place it oddly. It is named a tale of the Ulster Cycle, yet its scenes and people belong to Leinster. Two great manuscripts carry the text. One copy rests in the Lebor na hUidre, the old Dun Cow book. A second rests within the Yellow Book of Lecan. The opening continues straight out of the Tochmarc Étaíne, with a glowing sketch of that story's princess. The true center is King Conaire Mór. Fate hunts him without mercy through the tale. Before his just rule at Tara begins, a host of gessa, or taboos, lands on him. He must always favor birds in his kingdom. He may not ride sunwise around Tara or withershins around Brega. He may not hunt the crooked beasts of Cerna. On any ninth night he must not be away from Tara. No house where firelight shows after sunset may shelter him, and no three red men may walk before him. Raiders may not land in his reign. No lone visitor may come after sunset. He may not judge between two of his own subjects. The tale then shows him break every one of these bonds without meaning to. His three foster-brothers, sons of Donn Désa, take to raiding, and he banishes them. The marauders of Cualu go the same way. At sea the exiles join reavers led by the one-eyed Briton Ingcél Cáech. With Medb's exiled sons, every one named Maine, they first ravage Britain. Ireland comes next under the same host. A local king dies in Britain alongside Ingcél's own kin. The host lands at Howth while Conaire rides toward Da Derga's hostel near Dublin. A wild man of the wood entices him on the road. In the hostel a hideous seer woman, Cailbe, comes to him. The tale ends as the taboos said it must, with the king's death in the burning house. The story is thus the model destruction tale, where a king's broken vows and a fired hostel end an age.
```
