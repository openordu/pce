``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Tóraigheacht an Ghiolla Dheacair is the Irish title of a comic Fenian tale of the sixteenth century. English readers know it as the pursuit tale of the Hard Gilly, the Difficult Servant. The tale lives in both manuscript and oral tradition. Another name calls it the adventure of the Gilla Decair. The Gilla Decair comes before the Fianna as a deformed churl leading a raw-boned mare. Fifteen warriors, Conán mac Moma among them, agree to mount the beast. The mare quickens under the weight and carries the riders off through wild country, including the Land under Wave. In one folk variant the warriors' weight breaks the animal's back. In the usual versions Fionn and the Fianna take ship to hunt for the missing men. Their search lands them at last in fairyland. The Hard Servant proves to be the magician Ábartach, who wants Fianna aid against a rival king. The tale ends as Fionn weds Taise of the White Arms, and her hand frees the captured men. Goll alone holds out, and he demands fourteen noble women and the magician's wife as damages. All the women vanish the moment the party reaches home. Standish Hayes O'Grady printed the tale in the second volume of Silva Gadelica in 1892. Jeremiah Curtin recorded a Hiberno-English oral version. Krappe traced the Dioscuri parallels, and Heffernan compared the combat at the fountain with Yvain.

:::
```
