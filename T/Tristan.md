``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Tristan and Isolt form one of the best-known love stories of the Celtic world. Their tale has been retold in many forms across the centuries. In the usual version Tristan is a knight of Cornwall and Isolt is an Irish princess. The story weaves together love, betrayal, and a bond that cannot hold. A 6th-century stone near Fowey in Cornwall may tie the legend to history. The carving reads DRVSTANVS HIC IACIT CVNOMORI FILIVS, which some read as Drustanus, that is Tristan, lies here, son of Cunomorus. Cunomorus has been linked to King Mark. Frankish records name a King Cunomorus who ruled about the year 560. Modern writers have retold the tale in fresh forms. Thomas Hardy set one version at Tintagel in Cornwall. A. S. D. Smith wrote a long Cornish poem, Trystan hag Ysolt. Joseph Bedier gave the best-known modern version in 1945. Some accounts of the romance appear under the name Drystan ac Esyllt.

:::
```
