Tacitus, Cornelius, lived from AD 55 to about 117. He was a Roman historian and biographer. Judges often rank him the most reliable of the classical commentators. Five of his volumes survive. The short Germania, written about AD 98, is a store of ethnographic detail. It describes peoples living north of the Alps. There is no proof that Tacitus himself ever saw those regions. His remarks on the Celts sit incidentally inside the text. The Loeb Classical Library carries the Germania as number 35, and his Agricola and Histories in later editions.

:::
