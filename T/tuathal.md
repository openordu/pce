``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Tuathal is an Irish term for one of the Celtic ritual directions. To move tuathal is to move counterclockwise, against the sun's path. The Celts saw this as moving against the natural order. It is the opposite of deosil, which follows the sun. Today some call this motion widdershins and hold it to bring bad luck. See DIRECTIONS. MacKillop, under the same headword, records an older use of the name. The high king Tuathal sought revenge against Leinster for an old wrong. He first made war upon that province, and in the war the Leinster king Eochaid was killed. Tuathal then laid upon Leinster a heavy annual tribute called the Borama. That tribute exacted five thousand cows, five thousand sheep, five thousand hogs, and five thousand cloaks each year. MacKillop's article runs beyond the excerpt's cut-off point. The two tuathals are separate Celtic ideas. One names a ritual direction moved against the sun. The other names an early Irish king who set a famous tribute upon a conquered province.

:::
```
