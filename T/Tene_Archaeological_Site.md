::: magazinestyle
La Tène is an archaeological site at the eastern end of Lake Neuchatel in western Switzerland. The site gave its name to the Iron Age's later phase, the La Tène culture. Along with Hallstatt, it is one of two sites that most shaped the development of archaeology and Celtic studies. La Tène lies on the northern shore of the lake, in the marshes of Epagnier, near the outlet of the river Thielle. The remains include two bridges across the river, houses, and wooden palisades. The site is best known for its watery deposits, above all weapons and other high-status metal objects. A local antiquarian named Hansli Koop found the site in 1857 after he saw wooden stakes under the water and drew up the first weapons. Early work ran from 1880 to 1885, and yearly digs followed from 1906 to 1916. Dendrochronology dates one bridge, Pont Vossard, to about 250 bc. Some twenty-five hundred objects have come from the site, including many swords, spearheads, shields, and brooches. Scholars have read the site in many ways: as a lake village, an arsenal, a fortified town, a cult site, or a settlement with port facilities and bridges.

:::
