``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Téa, or Tea, is an Irish goddess or heroine. The great royal hill of Tara took its name from her. MacKillop fills the thin record out. She was the queen of Éremón, the Milesian leader. The Fir Bolg once knew the hill by the name Druim Cain. The renaming made the ground Milesian. The pseudo-history Lebor Gabála, the Book of Invasions, keeps her one request. Téa begged her husband to mark her grave. She wanted a rampart round it shaped like the rampart she had seen before she left Spain. Whatever the root sense of the name Téa, later readers took it as one element in Temair. The same book of place-names gives Temair Luachra as the seat of the south Munster kings. Tara was the seat of the high king of Ireland. The tale of Téa is one of the name-giving stories of the old tradition. The record does not tell more of her deeds or her family.

:::
```
