``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Tegid Voel is a Welsh figure of the Penllyn country. His name joins the Welsh teg, beautiful, with the ending voel, bald. He is best known as the husband of the sorceress Ceridwen. The pair keep house in the folkloric Hanes Taliesin, the Tale of Taliesin. His lake, Llyn Tegid, carries his own name. English speech calls the same broad water Lake Bala. MacKillop adds a sober note from the records. The early Welsh genealogies and pedigrees cite his name too. Monaghan's account makes him a giant and a dark god of the lake depths. Koch reads him as an obscure god set beside the famous Ceridwen. In the tale his wife brews the cauldron that makes the poet Taliesin. He stands quietly behind that famous birth. The giant of Penllyn thus joins lake lore, bardic wonder, and the old family lines of Wales.

:::
```
