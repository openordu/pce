``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Tir fo Thuinn is the land under the wave in Irish myth. The name carries the Irish tonn, wave, and MacKillop lists its forms. Scots Gaelic gives Tir fo Thuinn, while other spellings run Tir fa Thonn, Tir-fa-Tonn, and Tir-na-Thonn. It is one of the names of the Otherworld, the place of the fairies. The imagined realm lies under the seas. It ranks among the refuges where the Tuatha Dé Danann might have fled once the Milesians beat them. The Hard Gilly leads the Fianna there in the Tóraigheacht an Ghiolla Dheacair, the Pursuit of the Hard Gilly. On some Gaelic Scottish ground, Tiree among the isles, the same land is called An tEilean Uaine, the Green Island. The folk held that the sea and the lake hide the road to this realm. Cities stand on the bed of the water.

:::
```
