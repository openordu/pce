``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The tarroo-ushtey stands as the water bull of the Isle of Man. This creature is far less fierce than most water beasts. Other monsters of the deep ate people and cattle. The tarroo-ushtey only ruined the farmers' crops. Manx tradition sets it apart from the worst of the water animals. A wand of rowan would turn it away. The dictionary also gives the spelling theroo ushta. It notes the creature is a malevolent bull of Manx lore. Its form derives clearly from the tarbh uisge. The tarbh uisge is a figure of Scottish Gaelic tradition. Among its habits, the beast frequents swamps and shallow water. At times it walks the open fields beside the farmer's cattle. Even with that reach, the tarroo-ushtey is less malign than the cabyll-ushtey. The cabyll-ushtey is the water horse of the same island.

:::
```
