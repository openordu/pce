``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Three is a significant and powerful number for the Celts. It mattered on the continent and on the islands. Only the number five appears more often in myth and ritual. The number itself was sacred. Anything that came in three parts stood for this religious value. In Wales the short poems called the Triads carry a great deal of myth. In Ireland the stories show kings and heroes suffering the threefold death. Many Celtic divinities appear three at a time. The Deae Matres are the mother goddesses of Gaul. The triple-horned bull appears in Britain. Triplicity often seems a way to strengthen a figure. Goddesses are most often tripled, as in the Morrigan, Macha, and Brigit. Gods are sometimes raised into a trinity too. A specific triad marks the children of the Tuatha Dé Danann. Three children are born from one union in that triad. Two are drowned by their grandfather's order. Only Lug escapes. The number three is also read as a full cycle. That cycle runs past, present, and future. It also runs mother, father, and child.

:::
```
