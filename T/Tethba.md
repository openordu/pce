``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Tethba is the name of a region of pre-Norman Ireland. Its ground is coextensive with the modern counties of Longford and Westmeath. MacKillop sets the heart of the country more tightly. Tethba adjoins the Shannon and centres on the modern town of Granard. The land runs south of Lough Sheelin. It runs north of the Inny River too. Monaghan's account keeps the personified face of the same name. In that reading Tethba is an Irish goddess or heroine. She gave her secret name to the lands she loved. Those lands now form part of County Westmeath, in the Irish midlands. Stories of this kind belong to the land-sovereignty goddess. She was the consort of the king, and her secret name held the land. Both readings can stand together. The person and the district share one name, as Irish places often do. The name also appears as Tethbae, Tethbe, Teathbha, and Teffia. Margaret Dobbs published the study of the territory and its people in 1938.

:::
```
