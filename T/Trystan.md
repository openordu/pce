``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Trystan is the Welsh form of the romance hero Tristan. MacKillop treats the name as a variant spelling of Tristan, and he is also called Drystan and Drystan fab Tallwch. In the Welsh tale he is the hero and Esyllt is the heroine. Esyllt is the wife of King March ap Meirchion. The two eloped to a forest. A rout of soldiers of March laid siege to them. When it seemed Trystan would win, King Arthur came to make a truce. He told the two men to share the woman, one for summer and one for winter. March chose the winter, when the nights are long. Trystan claimed the yew stays green all year. So he said Esyllt would stay with him through winter too. And so he kept her. The Welsh Triads tie Drystan to March, and the name looks Pictish in origin. It has been joined to Drust, a shadowy king of the Picts dead by 780. The figure of Trystan may thus grow from Pictish lore carried into Welsh story.

:::
```
