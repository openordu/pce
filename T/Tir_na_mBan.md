``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Tir na mBan is the land of women in Irish myth. The name carries ban, the genitive plural for women. MacKillop lists the forms Tir na mBan, Tir inna mBan, and Tir na mban. It stands far off the coast as a floating isle. The island holds only beautiful and inviting women, and many Irish heroes visit it. Máel Dúin reaches the isle in his own voyage tale, the Imram Curaig Máele Dúin. Each male visitor gains a consort there. The women serve the best of food and the most enchanting music. Bran is summoned by the queen of the land in the old Voyage of Bran text. He stays for many years, yet counts them as one. The fairy queens rule this land and take lovers from the world of men. Men who set foot there do not grow old. MacKillop classes the place as an Irish form of a widespread folk motif, numbered F112.

:::
```
