``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Tintagel is the site of a ruined castle on the north shore of Cornwall. The name comes from the Cornish din, fortress, joined with tagell, a neck or constriction. The rock stands on a 300-foot cliff, almost cut off from the mainland. Camelford lies five miles to the south-east. MacKillop sets the record against the legend. Tintagel was truly the royal seat of the Dumnonians before the seventh century. Its legendary aura came mostly from medieval invention. Writers once described the site as monastic. It appears in no saint's life, and it has no cemetery. In the tales of Arthur its fame rivals that of Glastonbury. Uthr Bendragon, the Uther Pendragon of later romance, conceived Arthur here upon Igerna. Her husband was the Duke of Cornwall. Later stories set King Mark's home in this castle. The place also entered music. Sir Arnold Bax wrote his symphonic poem on the site in 1917. It carries the name Tintagel. By the late twentieth century the site had become a very popular tourist goal. The old claim about tin traders belongs to the picture of the harbour below. Charles Thomas printed the castle studies from 1986 to 1993.

:::
```
