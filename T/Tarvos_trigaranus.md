``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Tarvos trigaranus is the Latin name of a presumably Celtic deity. The name means bull with three cranes or egrets, and perhaps three-horned. Two stone sculptures preserve the figure, one in Paris and one in Trier. As the name promises, the figure is clearly a bull. Three long-legged marsh birds stand with him. They may be cranes or egrets, birds with deep standing in Celtic tradition. Workers uncovered the Paris stone in 1711 beneath the cathedral of Notre-Dame. There Tarvos trigaranus appears beside Esus, Cernunnos, and probably Smertrius. The Paris panel carries a dedication to Jupiter. The Trier stone carries much the same imagery, is dedicated to Mercury, and omits the name Tarvos.

:::
```
