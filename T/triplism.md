``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
triplism names the Celtic preference for the number three as a resonant symbol. The form runs from the earliest inscriptions through the medieval tales of Wales and Ireland. Pythagoras, in classical tradition, cited three as the perfect number for its signal of beginning, middle, and end. Celtic tradition mirrors that logic: a tripod or three-legged stool holds steady on any surface. Three covers life in the shape of male, female, and progeny. Three covers time as past, present, and future. Three covers the visible order as the heavens above, the soil below, and the realm beneath. Georges Dumézil (1898 to 1986) read the same pattern into the tripartite division of early European society: farmers, warriors, and clergy. Joseph Vendryes (1952) noted that triune figures often share one dominant personality and two ciphers. The three sons of Uisnech make the pattern vivid: Noise is a developed character. His brothers Ardan and Ainnle stand out only through the inflection of their speech. The three Machas show the same shape in the otherworld. The reverse of Vendryes's thesis holds true for the Mórrígna: Badb, Macha, and the Mórrígan share much yet remain easy to distinguish.

:::
```
