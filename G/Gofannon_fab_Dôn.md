``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Gofannon fab Dôn is a smith god of early Welsh tradition and a son of Dôn. His name joins Welsh gof, 'smith', to a divine suffix, and it is related to but not the same as the Irish name Goibniu. Modern writers often treat the two as cognate craft gods. In Culhwch ac Olwen, the giant Ysbaddaden sets Culhwch the task of sharpening the iron tools of Amaethon, and Gofannon must do the work. In Math fab Mathonwy he kills Dylan, the strange sea-born child of his sister Arianrhod. A poem in Llyfr Taliesin names Caer Gofannon, the god's stronghold. Another poem speaks of the seven spears of Gofannon, so he was remembered as a maker of famous weapons. A mould from Corbridge on Hadrian's Wall shows a smith god with tongs and a hammer above an anvil, and this image fits the Romano-British setting of such craft deities. His story survives in fragments, but it places a divine craftsman inside the family of Dôn and the older mythic layer of Welsh literature.

:::
```
