``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Glewlwyd Gafaelwar is a Welsh hero and guardian figure who became the gatekeeper of Arthur's court. The oldest text that names him is a dialogue poem that asks, 'Who is the gatekeeper?' (Pa Gur yv y Porthaur?). There he stubbornly refuses entry to Arthur, Cai, and a crowd of the best men in the world. In Culhwch ac Olwen his role reverses. He bars the gate of Arthur's court at Celliwig against Culhwch, then goes inside and praises the young man to Arthur in a long and boastful speech. The poem hints that he held the gate only on three high festivals, with deputies at other times. His epithet suggests that guarding the gate was his fixed office. Some tellings place Arthur's court at Camelot rather than Celliwig. He does not appear in Chrétien de Troyes's Yvain or Erec et Enide, and his place in the Welsh romances marks a native figure pushing back into plots borrowed from French. His stand at the door has an Irish parallel: the gatekeeper Camall mac Rágail first denies Lug entry at Tara in Cath Maige Tuired.

:::
```
