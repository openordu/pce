``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Glastonbury is a British mythological site. Some regard the small southwestern English city as the location of Avalon, the Otherworld island. Ruins of Christian buildings lie below the pyramidal Tor. Glastonbury Abbey once stood there, and legend buries King Arthur and Queen Guinevere beneath it. The Chalice Well, also named Blood Spring, holds iron-red water. Joseph of Arimathea is said to have raised Britain's first Christian church. Legend also names Glastonbury a doorway to Annwn, the realm ruled by Gwynn ap Nudd, king of the fairies. Iron Age settlement on the Tor marks its ancient past. Archaeology fills in the longer story. Neolithic people built timber and brushwood trackways across the Somerset Levels, and the Sweet Track was dated by tree rings to the winter of 3807–3806 BCE. The Meare Heath trackway shows that work on the same scale continued in the second millennium BCE. Arthur Bulleid's late nineteenth-century digs uncovered an Iron Age lake village at the marsh edge. Roman finds are modest, though pottery and tiles hint at a villa near the later abbey. Beckery was a small medieval daughter-house of the abbey. The bank called Ponter's Ball bank up to ten metres wide and three and a half metres high, runs for about a kilometre toward the town, and its purpose is still debated.

:::
```
