``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
glashtin names the Manx counterpart of the Irish each uisce, the Scottish kelpie, and the Welsh ceffyl dwfr. MacKillop treats the glashtin as the mischievous water-horse of the Isle of Man. He notes the spelling variants glashan, glaistyn, and glastyn as the same creature. The glashtin could take human form but could not hide his horse ears. MacKillop links the figure to Child ballad no 65 and the wider family of water-horse legends. The creature appears in Manx oral tradition as one of the most fully attested of the Celtic water fae. MacKillop places glashtin as the canonical Manx headword.

:::
```
