::: magazinestyle
The Gaelic Society of Glasgow was an institution set up in the Scottish city of Glasgow to encourage the Gaelic language and the wider culture of Scotland. Its chief literary work was a journal bearing the name the Transactions of the Society of Glasgow, which held papers on Scottish Gaelic society and culture, from history and linguistics to folklore and writing craft, and it appeared in both English and Scottish Gaelic. The Society, known in Gaelic as Comunn Gailig Ghlascho, issued five volumes of the Transactions between the years 1887 and 1958. The contributions, which had generally been read to members before printing, tended to an academic tone. Volume three emerged in print in 1908 under the name The Old Highlands, and volume four followed in 1934 as The Active One.

:::
