::: magazinestyle
The Gaeltacht autobiographies form a distinctive body of twentieth-century Irish writing in the Irish language. These books record growing up and daily life in the small, western and island communities of the Irish-speaking west of Ireland. The authors were ordinary people who set down the customs, work, and family routines of the villages and coastal areas they knew in an earlier age. Short accounts of Irish-speaking life had appeared in language journals since the 1890s, and longer personal narratives then followed. The most celebrated works come from the Blasket Islands and describe the dramatic lives of that island's community in plain, strong prose. The steady success of these books proved that a real market existed for this type of regional autobiography.

:::
