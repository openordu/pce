``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Gwenhwyfar is the wife of Arthur in Welsh tradition. French and English forms of her name include Guenevere, Guinevere, and Guenièvre, and Latin texts of Geoffrey of Monmouth give Guenhuuera and Guanhumara. She stands in the earliest stratum of Arthurian story, beside Cai, Bedwyr, Medrawd, Celliwig's court, and Caledfwlch the sword. In Culhwch ac Olwen, Arthur counts her among his prized possessions and will not give her up, and the tale names her chief of the queens of this island. Triad 56 calls Arthur's three great queens by her name alone. The theme of her abduction comes first in Caradog of Llancarfan's twelfth-century Life of Gildas, where Melwas carries her off, and the dialogue poem of Melwas and Gwenhwyfar rests on the same story. Historia Regum Britanniae has her forced to marry the usurper Modred while Arthur is away. One triad blames a quarrel between her and her sister Gwenhwyfach for the battle of Camlan, and another lists her among the faithless wives. The later romances give the triangle its familiar shape with Lancelot. Her name matches Old Irish Findabair, from find, 'white', and siabair, 'phantom'.
```
