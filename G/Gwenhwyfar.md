``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Gwenhwyfar is the Welsh counterpart and possible origin of Guinevere in the Arthurian story. Her name may read as white and fair. She is the wife of King Arthur in the native tale. Geoffrey of Monmouth wrote that she came of noble Roman stock and was raised in a court of Cornwall. Giraldus Cambrensis said that he saw the bodies of Arthur and Gwenhwyfar drawn from the ground in the year 1192, writing her as a second wife. Just as Guinevere does, she falls into shameful love, but with Medrod rather than Lancelot. After her fall she closes her life as a nun. Some scholars place her name beside the Irish Findabair. Her figure lives at the edge where the Welsh and the copied tales meet, and the reader should seek the fuller life of the greatest queen in the legend of the king.

:::
```
