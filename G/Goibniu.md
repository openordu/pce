``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Goibniu is the smith of the Tuatha Dé Danann, one of the tribe's three master craftsmen. The old book of the taking of Ireland lists his trade beside the carpenter Luchta and the bronze-worker Créidne. Before the great fight on the plain, the three work in a single rhythm and turn out swords, spears, and javelins so quickly that the whole army stays armed. Ruadán, the son of the Fomorian king and of the goddess-lady of the deep, tries to take the smith's skill, and Goibniu lays him low with the boy's own spear. Goibniu himself takes a wound and walks to the holy spring on Slane to heal. The tales keep a wide cow of abundance to his name, and the iron hills of the north mark the cradle of his craft. He pours a cup that spares his people the slow fall of age. Later stories turn the old smith into the clever builder who shapes the household of the Gaels, while his Welsh counterpart guards a kindred gift of the same forge.

:::
```
