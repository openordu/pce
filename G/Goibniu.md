``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Goibniu is the Irish smith god, one of the Tuatha Dé Danann's craft masters. He forged a working silver arm for King Nuada after the battle took his own, though some versions give that deed to Dian Cécht. A king with a blemish had to step down, so Bres took the throne. Lebar Gabála Érenn lists Goibniu beside Luichne the carpenter, Créidne the metalworker, and Dian Cécht the healer. Lost law tracts carried his name, among them Bretha Goibnenn. The three craftsmen work side by side in Cath Maige Tuired to turn out swords, spears, and javelins for the war against the Fomoiri. Ruadán, son of Bres and Bríg, comes to kill him and wounds him, but Goibniu drives a spear through the young man. Ruadán's mother then invented keening, the wild sound of grief. The Early Modern tale Altromh Tige Dá Medar says the feast of Goibniu kept the Tuath Dé free of age and death. His cauldron's guests drank and grew younger. He healed his wounds at a sacred well on Slane hill, owned the wonder cow Glas Gaibhleann, and was tied to County Cavan. Modern folklore remembers him as Gobbán Saor, the skilled mason. His Welsh counterpart is Gofannon fab Dôn, and both names descend from an old word for smith.

:::
```
