::: magazinestyle
glaistig names the usually malevolent solitary female fairy of Scottish Gaelic tradition. The headword also covers the spellings glaestig and glastig, plus the label maighdean uaine, the green maiden. MacKillop notes that the glaistig haunts lonely pools, and that she is sometimes half-woman, half-goat. She can appear as a beautiful woman near children or travellers in the dark. MacKillop links the figure to the wider family of Celtic water fairies. The Cornish equivalent is the Gorrion, the Welsh is the Gwragedd Annwn. MacKillop treats the glaistig as one of the most fully attested of the Scottish water fae.

:::
