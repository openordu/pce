``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Gaul was an ancient Celtic land. It covers lands now part of France. It also included areas of modern Belgium, Germany, and Switzerland. In Roman usage the name means the region bounded by the Rhine, the Alps, the Mediterranean, the Pyrenees, the Atlantic, and the English Channel. The narrower term Gallia Transalpina, Gaul beyond the Alps, marks it off from Cisalpine Gaul in northern Italy. Lepontic inscriptions show that Celtic speech was already present in northern Italy by the sixth century BCE or earlier. Roman writers such as Livy tell of settlers coming from beyond the Alps in the fifth century. The Po valley became a base for raids south, including the sack of Rome around 390 BCE, until Rome won at Telamon in 225 BCE. Julius Caesar brought the northern lands under Roman power. He wrote about them in his Gallic Wars. Much of what we know about continental Celts comes from Caesar. His truthfulness is hard to gauge. He wanted political power over the Celts. The Gauls were not one tribe. They were many tribal peoples. They shared a speech and broadly similar beliefs and customs. The Latin Gallia meant 'land of the Galli', and the same root appears in Greek Galatai, the name of the settlers of Galatia.

:::
```
