``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
gwawd was at first a cognate of the Irish word fáith, meaning seer or prophet. MacKillop notes that the Welsh word drifted to mean lampoon or ridicule in later usage. Some early sources also used gwawd for panegyric and praise poetry. MacKillop treats gwawd as the broader Welsh term covering the bards praise and the bards blame. The fuller record of the Welsh seer and prophet class sits under the headword gweledydd. MacKillop links gwawd to the wider family of Celtic bardic offices.

:::
```
