``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Gereint is a Welsh legendary hero.
He is also called Geraint, or Geraint fab Erbin.
He is a kinsman of King Arthur.
The name probably rests on a real man of the past.
Which man is not certain.
A mix of figures is possible.
Two historical Geraints stand out. 
The first is Gerontius. 
He was a general born in Britain.
The Emperor Constantine III gave him an army.
The Roman garrisons named him emperor.
He held Britain, Gaul, and Spain from 407 to 411.
The second is the king Gerontius of Dumnonia.
Aldhelm wrote to this king in 705.
Later writers call him Gerent.
The Arthurian deeds of the hero fit the years between these two reigns.
The Gododdin names a hero, Geraint of the south.
The Life of Saint Teilo names a king Gerennius of Cornwall.
The Life of Saint Cybi makes Gereint the great-grandfather of the saint.

Early Welsh verses, cast in three-line englyn metre, speak of Gereint.
They place him at a battle called Llongborth.
Scholars date these verses about the years 800 to 1000.
In the poem Arthur holds the title emperor.
Some read that Arthur fought in person at the battle.
The site of the battle is not sure.
Langport in Somerset is a possible place.
Copies survive in the old Welsh books.

He appears in the tale of Gereint and Enid.
He wins Enid by service to her royal father.
After the wedding he rests and forgets his lands.
Enid tells him the people speak of his fault.
He treats Enid harshly for repeating the words.
He rides out to prove the talk false.
He takes Enid with him and orders her to stay silent.
At Owein's castle he overcomes the knight who guards the perilous game.
The quarrel ends.
Gereint and Enid are reconciled.

The tale is one of the Three Romances of the Mabinogion.
Scholars place it near the Old French poem Erec et Enide.
That poem is the work of Chretien de Troyes.
```
