``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Gaillimh, called Galway in English, is the county town of County Galway in western Ireland. The settlement grew where the river Galway flows into Galway Bay, fed by the river Corrib that runs down from Loch Coirib toward the sea. A record from the year 1149 enters its Irish name, and the form may carry the sense of the place of foreigners. A ford and a fishing village stood on the site at first, and the town later became a busy trading port. Anglo-Norman forces seized the position in the 1230s and raised a castle with a defensive wall. The annals treat the place as a town from 1247, and the earliest walls date to the long reign of Edward I. Merchant families then governed the place and grew rich through trade with Spain and France. When Cromwellian soldiers took the town in 1652 the economic power of those families broke, and commerce fell until new markets rose near the close of the 1700s. Galway keeps an easy link to the nearby Gaeltacht, and boats between the town and the Aran Islands run often. The National University of Ireland maintains a college here, and the cathedral was raised in 1965.

:::
```
