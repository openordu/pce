``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Gaul covered the land that Romans named Gallia. From the mid-first millennium BC to the fifth century AD, Celtic speakers shaped this region. Their reach also extended into parts of Central Europe and Spain. Greek writers, and then Roman writers, applied the names Keltoi and Celtae, meaning the Celts, to these peoples. The same continental groups also carried the names Galli and Galatae, meaning the Gauls and the Galatians. Gallia means the land of the Galli. The root of the name Kelta remains unclear. The names Galli and Galatae most likely connect to the Old Irish word gal, which means boldness or ferocity, and to the Welsh word cal. Latin and Germanic speech grew stronger inside Gaul as the Roman period advanced.

:::
```
