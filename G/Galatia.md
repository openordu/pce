``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Galatia was an ancient Celtic land in central Anatolia, now part of Turkey. Around 278 BCE Gauls crossed into Phrygia at the call of the Bithynian ruler Nicomedes I. They came in family groups, so the move was a true migration. Three principal tribes shared the land: the Tolistobogii, the Tectosages, and the Trocmi. They kept their Galatian tongue and Celtic customs for centuries. Their assembly place, Drunameton, was a sacred oak grove whose name recalls the druids and the word nemeton. Each tribe answered to four chiefs, called tetrarchs, of whom one judged and may have been a druid. The strongest ruler, Deiotaros of the Tolistobogii, took the royal title from the Roman Senate. Rome made Galatia a province in 63 BCE. St. Paul wrote to the Galatians, the only Celtic people named in the Bible.

:::
```
