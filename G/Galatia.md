``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Galatia was an ancient Celtic land. It stood in central Anatolia. That region is now part of Turkey. Celts settled there during the 3rd century BCE. Classical sources say the Gauls crossed into Phrygia in 278 BCE at the call of Nicomedes I, the Thracian ruler of Bithynia. They came in families, so the move looks like a migration and not a slow mixing of mercenaries. The incomers may have gathered survivors of the Celtic invasion of Greece under Brennos. Three tribes held the land: the Tolistobogii in the west, the Tectosages around Ancyra, and the Trocmi in the east. They kept their Galatian speech and many Celtic customs for centuries under Roman rule. They met at a sacred oak grove called Drunemeton. Four tetrarchs governed, three of them military, and the fourth served as a judge, perhaps a druid. St. Paul mentioned the region in his epistles. He wrote to the residents of the land, but the community he addressed may have been largely non-Celtic and taken its name from Galatian neighbours.

:::
```
