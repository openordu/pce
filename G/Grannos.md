``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Grannos, also spelled Grannus, is a Gaulish healing god whom Roman worshippers joined to Apollo. The pairing belongs to interpretatio romana, the habit of matching Celtic gods with Greek and Roman ones. He governed healing waters across Europe and Scotland, and his cult was shared with the goddess Sirona, whose name means something like 'star goddess'. Eighteen inscriptions name him. Only one, from Bonn, calls him Grannus alone. Elsewhere the name serves as a byname of Apollo. Offerings appear in the Rhineland, at Musselburgh near Edinburgh, at Brigetio in Hungary, at Astorga in Spain, in Västmanland in Sweden, and at Ephesus in Turkey. Aachen was known as Aquis Granni, 'the waters of Grannus', which points to a sacred spring. His name seems to spring from the same root as Grian, the Irish sun figure, and the heroine Gráinne has been proposed as a further cognate. In the Auvergne, worshippers set grain sheaves on fire and sang to him as friend, father, and mother, a sign of a dual or unclear gender.

:::
```
