``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
In ancient Ireland a bard or druid used a ritual posture to cast a poet's execration. The practice is known as the glám dícenn. The Irish word pairs glám, meaning satire or lampoon, with a verb form meaning to destroy outright. The caster stood on one leg in a crane-like pose. He closed one eye and stretched out one arm. The pose was not theatre. The ritual carried weight as a weapon of war. It could raise blisters on the target's face. It could cost the target his life. Society at every level shunned a man once the curse was laid. The form compares with the hija wielded by early Arab poets. A Hag used the glám dícenn against King Conaire in Da Derga's Hostel. She cursed him for breaking his sacred vows, called geis.

:::
```
