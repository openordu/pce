``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Glauberg is an Iron-Age hill-fort crowning a long, narrow plateau in the eastern Wetterau of Hesse, Germany, long recognised as a high-status seat of the closing Hallstatt and the early La Tène period. Several stone ramparts ring the summit, and a massive northern annexe bank drew extra springs within the defended bounds, the chief entrance being the Stockheimer Pforte tangential gate. Excavations from 1994 at the southern foot exposed a substantial sanctuary, marked out by deep ditches, a 'processional avenue' some four hundred metres long, and two burial mounds. The Tumulus 1 tombs yielded princely La Tène grave-goods, among them a beaked bronze flagon originally filled with a honey-sweetened mead and reminiscent of the Dürrnberg find, a gold torc, an iron sword, spears and a wooden bow. Around the graves stood fragments of several life-size sandstone figures; the almost complete statue nicknamed 'Glaubi', over 1.8 m tall, portrays an armoured nobleman in a leaf crown who mirrors the accoutrements of the burial, implying veneration of ancestral, princely dead.

:::
```
