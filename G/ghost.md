``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
A ghost is a folkloric figure in Celtic belief. The Otherworld and the land of the dead blend together in the Celtic mind. Ghosts of dead humans are hard to tell from the diminished divinities called fairies. Both groups used the same magical parts of the landscape. They frequented bogs, lakes, and islands. They shared roughly the same habits. Ghosts, like fairies, could take humans away to the Otherworld. Not every dead person became a ghost. Only those whose lives suffered an interruption returned. A young mother who died in childbirth might come back for her child's safety. An unsettled soul could haunt house and family. Some elders returned out of long habit even after a full life. By MacKillop's reading, the older Celtic record carries no broad ghost lore of the dead returning to plague the living. Most Celtic hauntings are later European borrowings, drawn in during the post-medieval period. Celtic words for ghost are often loanwords from other languages. Irish anam comes from Latin anima. Irish sprid comes from English spirit. Scottish Gaelic tannasg, Manx scanjoon, and Welsh ysbryd are native terms. Cornish bucca gwyn means white hobgoblin. Breton teuz names the ghost in that language.

:::
```
