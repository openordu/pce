``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The glastig is a Scottish and Welsh fairy. Her name means the gray-handed one. She was a gray thin woman, sometimes a giant. Her long yellow hair reached the ground behind her. She wore the fairy green, so some called her the green glastig. She rarely appeared in company, though a pair was sometimes seen. Glastig hung around farms with cows. She also loved friendly people of low intelligence and cared for them. Her voice was preternaturally loud and carried for miles. As a spirit of milk cattle, she demanded the first milk each morning. A stingy dairyman lost his milk, which she dried up as punishment. Regular libations of milk set on the doorstep kept her near. Like the brownie, she cleaned well when guests were coming. People never spoke of her or praised her labor, for then she moved to another farm. MacKillop records the spelling glastig as a variant of glaistig, the Scottish Gaelic water fairy of lonely pools, and groups the spellings glaestig and glastig with the canonical headword.

:::
```
