::: magazinestyle
Giraldus Cambrensis, who signed his Latin works as Gerald de Barri, was a prominent writer of the late twelfth century. He came from a mixed Norman and Welsh family. He planned an early church career and hoped one day to lead the diocese of Saint David's, called Tyddewi in Welsh. Rome granted him only the rank of archdeacon of Brecon. He knew King Henry the Second and King John. He studied in Paris twice during the 1170s under scholars trained by followers of Peter Abelard. His relatives fought in Ireland from 1169 onward, so he travelled there with Prince John and gathered material for two books, the famous of them the Topography of Ireland. He toured Wales in 1188 with Archbishop Baldwin to preach a crusade. That journey produced the Itinerary and a Description of Wales. He was chosen bishop of Saint David's in 1198 and fought for years to win recognition, even visiting the pope, but he failed. He later resigned his church office and spent his last years writing.

:::
