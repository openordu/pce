``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Gogynfeirdd are the Poets of the Princes, a name also given as Beirdd y Tywysogion in Welsh. These court poets of medieval Wales worked from about 1100 until the late thirteenth century. Koch places their verse mainly in the Hendregadredd Manuscript and the Black Book of Carmarthen, with the Red Book of Hergest also important. Much of the surviving poetry praises or laments the princes of Gwynedd. Meilyr Brydydd stands as the first of the line, and he sang in honour of Gruffudd ap Cynan. Gwalchmai ap Meilyr and Cynddelw came after him. The poets wrote in awdl and englyn measures, forms that later entered the strict metres of Welsh verse. Some held court posts such as the pencerdd (chief of song) or the bardd teulu (household bard). Their craft passed down within families as a hereditary calling.

:::
```
