``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
galloglass names the foreign soldiers who served in Ireland from medieval to early modern times, characteristically heavily armed. The Irish form gallóglach joins gall, meaning foreigner, with óg- or óg-lach, youth or warrior. MacKillop notes that the name implies a Norse origin, but the soldiers more likely emerged from Norse-influenced areas of the Hebrides and Gaelic Scotland. Their everyday lives are well attested in numerous Irish records. Their exploits have been the focus of romantic fiction; Howard Breslin's novel The Gallowglass, published in 1958, is the most cited modern treatment.

:::
```
