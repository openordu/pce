``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Glossaries were a prized and durable part of early Irish learning. A collection often began with the interlinear and margin notes that readers had written beside a single manuscript, and copyists then gathered those notes together, joining each term to its commentary. Over time small sets merged, and ordering by letter set them free of any one work, so a glossary could hold facts that the original text had lost elsewhere. The general lists took in Cormac's, O'Mulconry's, and O'Davoren's collections, while the Lecan register gave short one-word answers.

:::
```
