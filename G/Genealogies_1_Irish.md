``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The entry 'Genealogies [1] Irish' records the medieval Irish genealogical corpus, which details how the ruling houses and clans of Ireland claimed their ancestry. This corpus is the most extensive of its kind for any European country. Its chief recensions survive in two twelfth-century manuscripts, the Book of Glendalough and the Book of Leinster, which name some twelve thousand persons, mostly male and often historical figures who lived between the sixth and twelfth centuries. The texts trace most dominant families back to Míl Espáine, while various subject peoples descend from pre-Gaelic groups such as the Fir Bolg. Two text forms occur: single-line pedigrees down the paternal line and branch genealogies of whole families. Writers used these pedigrees to press claims over land and territory, so forging lineages became common, yet much independently verifiable material stays accurate.

:::
```
