::: magazinestyle
Evans, Gwynfor, was the first UK Member of Parliament elected for Plaid Cymru, the Party of Wales. He was born in Barry, south Wales, in 1912 and went to school at Barry Grammar before studying at the University of Wales at Aberystwyth. Evans became active in nationalist and pacifist movements in the late 1930s and was a conscientious objector during the Second World War. He became president of Plaid Cymru in 1945 and held the post for 36 years. Evans worked to make the party a mainstream force and turned from the policies of his predecessor Saunders Lewis, building economic programmes on Scandinavian models. He won the Carmarthen by-election of 1966, held after the death of Megan Lloyd George, and so became the first Plaid Cymru MP. In 1970 he lost the Carmarthen seat, regained it in 1974, and lost it again in 1979. In 1980 he threatened a hunger strike unless the government reversed its decision against a Welsh-language television channel; the government gave way, and Evans counted the outcome among his greatest achievements. He resigned the presidency after the 1983 defeat and remained honorary president of the party until his death in 2005.
