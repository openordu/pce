``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Gwartheg y llyn are the fairy cattle of Wales. The Welsh name names cattle of the lake. The Plant Annwn, the tribe of the Otherworld, keep these cattle below the surface of the lakes. The Plant Annwn also keep teams of white dogs there, named Cwn Annwn. In Scotland and in Ireland such fairy cattle had red bodies with white ears. The Welsh form, by contrast, stands wholly white, ghost-like from crown to hoof. These Welsh cattle can breed with the stock of mortal herders. They serve as a mark of the Otherworld herds. MacKillop marks them as the Welsh answer to the crodh mara of Gaelic Scotland. The Welsh beasts are usually thought milk-white themselves. When one of these fairy cattle joins a mortal drove, the farm overflows. The household gains top milk, fine cream, and rich butter. The blessing holds for as long as the fairy beast stays with the mortal herd. The fairy cow thus serves as a marker of how the mortal world meets the Otherworld through shared beasts. The breed of these cattle stands as proof of the Otherworld's reach into the mortal field. Their milk
brings the taste of the lakeside realm into the farmer's pail. The
folk who kept them passed down the lore from parent to child. The
lore kept the name gwartheg y llyn alive across many generations.

:::
```
