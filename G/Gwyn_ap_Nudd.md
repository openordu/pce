::: magazinestyle
Gwyn ap Nudd is the mythological king of the Welsh Otherworld, an Annwn whose power lessens as the age of the church advances. His name means white and holy. Despite that he carries a blackened face when he leads the pack of fairy dogs called the hounds of Annwn. In the tale of the hunt Culhwch has to seek his help in the chase of the great boar. In the same story Arthur forces Gwyn into an endless combat with his enemy for the love of the maiden Creiddylad, so that they must battle each year at the turn of May. When the sixteenth century turns he becomes the sovereign of the Tylwyth Teg, the fair folk. Scholars read his name beside that of Fionn mac Cumhaill, and some follow the thought that both hide the hero and god Lug. A gift of the Welsh author H. Elfed Lewis opens a verse in his honour. The reader will meet him over and again in the oldest Welsh hunt.

:::
