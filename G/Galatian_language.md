::: magazinestyle
The Galatian_language was the Celtic tongue carried into Asia Minor after Celtic combat bands struck Greece in 279 and 278 bc. The tongue then took firm root in Galatia, the north-central Anatolian region that now makes up part of Asiatic Turkey, and it was in place there by 260 bc. Later classical writers, when their accounts are accepted, hold that the speech endured at least until the sixth century ad. The record left behind is tiny. No continuous Galatian inscription survives. Its attested store of roughly one hundred and twenty words is gathered only from Greek authors and from proper names written within Greek inscriptions. Galatian proves itself closely akin to Gaulish, a finding that supports Jerome's early notice comparing the tongue with that of the Treveri.

:::
