``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Greek and Roman accounts form the earliest written evidence on the ancient Celts. Herodotus located Keltoi beyond the Pillars of Heracles in the fifth century BC. Later writers spread Celtic lands across Gaul and into Iberia. Caesar worked from his own campaigns and wrote fully about the tribes, druids, and warfare of the Gauls. The lost ethnography of Posidonius survives through Diodorus, Strabo, and Athenaeus. These authors noted feasting, head-hunting, and the courage of warriors. They recorded Gaulish words and Galatian names that lasted for generations. Tacitus and others set down the customs of the British and Irish. The tradition runs from the sixth century BC into late antiquity.

:::
```
