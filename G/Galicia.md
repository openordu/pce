``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Galicia is a region on the Atlantic side of north-west Spain. Its Celtic past reaches back to ancient Gallaecia, a land larger than the modern province. In pre-Roman times the Castro Culture built hill-forts, called castros, across the territory. Many Galician place-names carry the old Celtic ending -briga, meaning a hill or a hill-fort. The Romans organised Gallaecia into three districts, the Conventus Lucensis, Conventus Bracarensis, and Conventus Asturum. Peoples such as the Celtici, the Arrotrebae, and the Brigantii bore Celtic names. Modern Galicia keeps a Celtic-linked tongue and culture, including a strong tradition of bagpipe music tying it to Atlantic Europe.

:::
```
