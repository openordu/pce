``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
John of Cornwall was born about 1125 and died between 1199 and 1202. Although he came from Cornwall, he lived most of his years away from it. In 1155 a bishop of Exeter asked him to render the Prophecies of Merlin into Latin verse. He produced 167 hexameter lines with prose remarks and glosses placed between them. People long dismissed the version as passages borrowed from the seventh book of Geoffrey's History. Many readers now judge that it holds matter unknown to Geoffrey. Some blocks seem to spring from Cornish, Breton, and Welsh originals. He studied in Paris under Thierry of Chartres and took theology with Peter Lombard. In the late 1170s he was suggested for the see of Saint David's. He lost that office because his Welsh was weak. He later taught theology and settled a dispute touching the abbey of Oseney.

:::
```
