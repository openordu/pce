``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Jupiter was the Roman name given to several Celtic gods. Among them were Brixianus, Ladicus, Latobius, and Poeniunus. Under the interpretatio romana Roman scholars matched Celtic gods with their own familiar ones. Some Celtic god names survived as a surname attached to Jupiter. Other names vanished into the great figure of the sky lord. Under a like process called interpretatio celtica, natives of the Roman colonies shaped the imperial god to their own worship. MacKillop notes the continental Celts gave Jupiter local epithets. Classical writers placed him lower in local esteem than Gaulish Mercury, Apollo, and Mars. Taranis and Bussumarus each share traits with Jupiter, yet neither is the same as a Gaulish Jupiter. Some say he was not a true sky god at all. Many images hold a wheel, a solar sign, so some call him a sun god. He reads better as a god of abundance and fertility. His sculptures show a mature bearded man, usually bare. When he grips a thunderbolt he is Taranis, the thunder god. About one hundred and fifty tall columns honoured Jupiter. They ran from the east of Gaul into the Moselle valley and the Rhine lands. The top figure often tramples a snake-like monster. That pose may carry him into the sky or link him to trees. The horseman fighting a serpent is a pose the Roman Jupiter never took. Rhys, an old scholar, sought to tie Jupiter to Nodons and other heroes. Recent writers give that claim little weight.

:::
```
