::: magazinestyle
Bibracte is a notable Gaulish dig found in eastern France. At the Roman conquest of 52 BC, Bibracte ranked as a fortified city. The stronghold rose about 2500 feet on Mt Beuvray, close to the town of Autun. Julius Caesar described Bibracte in his war histories, and Vercingétorix defended it. Workers first excavated the city in the nineteenth century. Five campaigns begun in the 1980s uncovered thousands of domestic artefacts. Those finds argued that early Gaul held a richer civilization than older records had implied.

:::
