``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Baile Binnberlach is a tragic prince of early Irish tradition. His name reads as Baile of the clear voice. He was heir to the Ulster throne. He was the lover of Ailinn of Leinster. The story of their love, Scel Baili Binnberlaig, dates from the eleventh century. Baile was travelling south from Emain Macha when he heard a false tale. It said that Ailinn was dead. He died of grief at the news. Ailinn, travelling north, heard of Baile's death and also perished. A yew tree grew from his grave, and an apple tree from hers. Then poets and seers felled the trees. The men of Ulster wrote on the yew, and the men of Leinster on the apple. The poet W. B. Yeats drew on the tale often. His poem Baile and Ailinn keeps the story alive.

:::
```
