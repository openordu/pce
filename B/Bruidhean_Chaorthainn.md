``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Bruidhean Chaorthainn is an Irish tale title. It is a prose narrative of the Fenian Cycle. In English it is the Fairy Palace of the Quicken Tree. The story appears in many oral versions across Ireland. It is also known in the Scottish Highlands as Bruighean Caorthuinn. Geoffrey Keating cited it as early as 1633. He called it typical of the unhistorical Fenian tales. Midac is the son of the villainous Colgán of Lochlainn. The Fianna raised him as one of their own. He built his hostel of quicken trees beside the Shannon. His dinner invitation lured Fionn and his men into a trap. Inside the men could not cry out or rise from their chairs. Midac wished to cut off Fionn's head. The Fianna, led by Diarmait, freed their leader instead. They brought him the head of the King of the World.

:::
```
