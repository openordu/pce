``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Barzaz-Breiz is a collection of Breton song gathered during the nineteenth century. The collector was Theodoro Hersart de La Villemarque. He grew up near Kemper, along the western shore of Brittany. He spent time in Paris as a young Breton student. There he studied the Celtic past of his own country. The old manuscript books held no trace of the lost Breton stories. He decided to go instead to the people who still knew the songs. He recorded the verses of farm labourers and the poor travellers of his home region. He shaped this living material into a set of pieces. The pieces of the set span many centuries. A prophecy song said to belong to an early century opens the work. He added more songs to the set in later editions, one in 1845 and another a generation later. He revised the work all his life, always changing words and their forms. The last edition placed the Breton verse lower on the page in smaller type. He set most songs as history or as sung ballads. He gave the Breton words to each tune, and he added the songs of the day. This work carried Breton culture to a wide public. It changed how people read the Celtic past. The recorder became a central name in the study of Breton song and story.

:::
```
