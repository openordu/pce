::: magazinestyle
Bog myrtle is an Irish name for the shrub Myrica gale. The shrub has bitter leaves with a strong fragrance. Elsewhere English speakers know the plant as sweet gale, as sweet willow, or as buck bean. Rural communities in Ireland once carried bog myrtle as palm on Palm Sunday. Custom barred the bog myrtle from use as a switch for driving cattle. The ban reflected the belief that the shrub had scourged Jesus. An infusion of the branch tips yields a yellow dye for cloth. The same infusion served tanners in preparing hides. Modern Irish roideog and roilleog, Scottish Gaelic roid, and Manx roddagagh name the shrub across the Celtic languages.

:::
