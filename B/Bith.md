``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Bith is a figure of the pseudo-history Lebor Gabala, the Book of Invasions. The text names him as a son of the biblical Noah, one refused passage on the Ark. Following the counsel of his daughter Cesair, he made a shrine figure. The figure ordered him to craft a vessel and shelter in it. Bith, Cesair and their company sailed for seven years before touching Ireland. They arrived as a band with fifty women and merely three men. Bith wed one of Cesair's companions and gathered sixteen further women. Then he went to live in the north, where he perished before the waters swept in. Adna was another of his sons. The mountain Sliab Betha, called Slieve Beagh today, carries his name in Northern Ireland.

:::
```
