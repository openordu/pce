``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Belenus is a Continental Celtic god. The name also reads as Bel, Belenos, Belinos, and Belu. His worshippers spread from Italy across to the islands of Britain. A main shrine stood at Aquileia on the Adriatic coast. His power rested on the springs that restored health. Those waters bore the name Aquae Borvonis. The place is now called Bourbon-les-Bains in France. People also honoured him in the region of Aquitaine. His worship reached into the land we now call Austria. Ancient writers compared him with Apollo. An inscription found at Inveresk in Scotland echoed that view. That shrine carried the words Apollini Granno. A nineteenth-century idea linked the name Bel to the Semitic Baal. No scholar accepts that link today. A ruler of pre-Roman Britain named himself Cunobelinus. That title means the Hound of Belenus. The Welsh god Beli Mawr may borrow his name. The fountain of Bérenton may take his name as well. A spring called Barenton stands in the grove of Brocéliande. That forest sits in the region of Brittany. The feast of Beltaine may take its name from him. That link stays only a guess. Geoffrey of Monmouth in his book of 1136 made him a plain mortal. A fancy folk tale tied him to Billingsgate fair beside the Thames. That tale is pure fanciful wordplay. The healing herb Bellinuntia also keeps his name. Some scholars regard him as a sun god and a healer. He stands near the related god Borvo.

:::
```
