``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Bononia was an early town in northern Italy. It grew into the city now called Bologna. In later pre-Roman times Celtic speakers ruled the area. During the power of the Boii tribe the site was named Felsina. Bononia came to be the Roman name for the conquered colony. The name may be Celtic, related to the old name of Vienna. Written sources for this period are few. A couple of lines in Livy survive. Velleius Paterculus and Servius also mention the town. Archaeology tells little of the fourth and third centuries bc. Most evidence comes from tombs dug up in the eighteen hundreds. These lay in the city's western quarter. Workers named the plots after the landowners. It is really one large cemetery. Zannoni wrote of one hundred ninety Gallic graves. A fresh review found seventy-seven from the Boii period. The richest burials held bronze helmets and iron swords. Inhumation was common; cremation came late. The cemetery holds both Etruscan and Celtic dead. The Boii thrived across the countryside rather than in one city. Cato said they counted over one hundred separate tribes.

:::
```
