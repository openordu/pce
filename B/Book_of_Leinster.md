``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Book of Leinster carries the Irish name Lebor Laignech. It is also called Leabhar Laighneach, and at times the Book of Noughaval or Oakvale, from the Irish Lebor na Nuachongála. Scribes finished the codex after the year 1150. For Irish myth and story it ranks just behind the Book of the Dun Cow. Some of its matter, such as the Dindshenchas lore that tells of places, dates from the eleventh century. It holds the Lebor Gabála, a text of the Táin Bó Cúailne, the tale of Deirdre, and the Bó Brama tribute. The surviving pages are split between two Dublin institutions, Trinity College and the Franciscan house. A facsimile appeared in 1870. Later editors issued the whole in six volumes.

:::
```
