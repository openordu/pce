::: magazinestyle
Brycheiniog was a small and pleasing Welsh kingdom seated in the valley of the river Usk, and it stayed free of every bordering state for six whole centuries from the fifth age into the tenth. The same stretch of land had earlier been the smaller kingdom of Garthmadrun under its celebrated ruler Tewdrig. Story traced the line of Brycheiniog back to a princess named Marcell, whose marriage to an Irish lord brought her a son named Brychan, the very man who gave the region its name and who shows up again and again in early Welsh writing. The family that Brychan built endured until roughly 940, and then the southern kingdom of Deheubarth took charge. From the sixteenth century up to 1974 the district lived on as the county of Brecon, and these days that territory rests within the larger county of Powys.

:::
