``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Brittany is a former duchy and province of north-western France on the Armorican peninsula. It covers the departments of Finistère, Côte d'Armor, Morbihan, Ille-et-Vilaine, and Loire-Atlantique. The region spans 18,630 square miles, more than twice the size of Wales. Celtic-speaking peoples have held it since before Roman times. Its name derives from the Brythonic refugees who left the isle of Britain in the fifth century. In Breton it is called Breizh, and in Welsh Llydaw. The coast is Arvor in Breton literature, and the interior is Argoad. In early Christian times Brittany held three petty kingdoms: Domnonia, Cornouaille, and Bro Waroch. Legend says the emigrants were led by St Cynan Meiriadog. The Breton language is P-Celtic and linked to Welsh and the extinct Cornish. Breton and Welsh share words but are not mutually comprehensible. The dialect of the south-west is Gwened, named for Vannes, the capital of Morbihan. Traditional Breton symbols are the ermine, the triskele, and the biniou.
```
