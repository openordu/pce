``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Balor is an Irish god. He is also called Balar, Bolar, and Balor of the Evil Eye. He reigned as king of the Fomorians, the monstrous race who held Ireland before the first invaders. His daughter was the fair Eithne. A prophecy warned that a son born to Eithne would kill his grandfather. Balor shut Eithne in a tower to keep every man away from her. He hoped that this would spare his own life. Koch takes his fullest early portrait of him from the second battle at Mag Tuired. That tale was set down in the ninth or tenth century. There Balor leads the Fomoiri against his grandson Lug and the Tuath Dé. Balor's greed undid his plan. He stole the magical cow Glas Ghaibhleann from a smith. Lugh struck him down in that battle with a slingshot or a spear made by the smith god Goibniu. Lugh cut off Balor's head. Even in death, Balor's remaining eye could split boulders. The eye was his ruin from the start. Koch tells how Balor won the eye as a boy, spying on his father's druids at their magic. Its heavy lid took four men to lift, and its gaze struck soldiers of the Tuath Dé helpless. When Lug's stone flew through it, the fatal power turned back on the Fomoiri. Monaghan calls the eye a third eye set at the centre of Balor's forehead. It spat flames and destruction. His consort Cethlion carried poisons of her own, and they killed the god Dagda. In later folk-tales Balor loses his role as a war leader and becomes a monstrous villain. Scholars see him in two lights: a sun god, or a clash between the outgoing year and the one arriving. The tale ties him to the Hebrides, and modern storytellers place his fortress on Tory Island. He is linked to Mizen Head in County Cork and to Land's End in Wales.

:::
```
