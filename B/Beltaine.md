``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Beltaine numbers among the four great feasts of the Celtic peoples. Its name was carried by speakers of Irish, Scottish Gaelic, and Manx. Those tongues use the word for the rites that open the bright half of the year. The feast comes on the first day of May under the Gregorian reckoning. Scotland sets it on the fifteenth of that month instead. Beltaine stands at the close of the dark, heavy half of the year. It counts among the four great feasts of the Celtic calendar. The others are Imbolc in February, Lughnasa in August, and Samain in November. The people of early Ireland knew the day as Cetshamain also. Their Welsh neighbours marked it as Cyntefin and Calan Mai, while Cornwall and Brittany kept their own forms as well. Folk honoured Beltaine as a fine time to start a great task. The story in Lebor relates that the Partholonians and the Milesians both landed in Ireland on this festival day. A rich fund of custom grew around the event, chiefly around its fires and its round bannocks. Great bonfires blazed across Ireland and upon the island of Man. In highland Scotland the flame climbed to a mountain top. The bonfire could carry the name teine eigin, the fire of pressed need. Cornwall kept such blazes burning into the late twentieth century. Herdsmen sent their cattle through two flames, a rite to guard the beasts from sickness for a year. The figure of a witch was thrown into the Scots fire even in the seventeenth century. Dancers turned sunwise about the flame, spinning to the right. The folk of the isle of man came bearing twigs of the rowan. At each home the hearth died out and was then kindled again as a token of purity. The feast also served special food, standing its cakes first. Those Beltaine cakes were broad, round, and flat, mixed mostly from oats or barley. They proved big enough to split into many shares. A charcoal dark blot marked one of the pieces. Whosoever drew that shares by chance took the name of Beltaine carlin, the old witch of the day. That loser might then face a mock death, cast into the blaze or torn apart with a horse.
```
