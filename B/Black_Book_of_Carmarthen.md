``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Black Book of Carmarthen is the earliest full collection of Welsh poetry. A scribe wrote it out near the year 1250. The volume now rests at the national library in Aberystwyth. Its verses span much time, from the ninth century to the close of the twelfth. A good share of the poems give praise to heroic men, most of all Gwyn ap Nudd. A fine number of them speak in the own voice of Myrddin. Carmarthen, which the Welsh call Caerfyrddin, was once a county of southern Wales until 1974. It now forms part of the land of Dyfed. The book earns its name of black from the colour of its cover. It does not rise from anger or harmful judgement. Two editors brought it to print. J. G. Evans saw it through in 1906. A. O. H. Jarman produced a later text in 1982.

:::
```
