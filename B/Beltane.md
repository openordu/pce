``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Beltane, also Bealtaine and Beltain, was a Celtic festival held on May 1. It was one of the chief festivals of the ancient Celtic year. Koch counts Beltane and Samhain as the two most significant dates of the Celtic calendar. Beltane marked the start of summer and the moving of cattle to the summer pastures, the booleys. Weather on the day foretold the season. Frost boded ill, while rain promised a good return. The festival began at sundown on the eve, much as Samhain did. While Samhain was a festival of the dead, Beltane was a festival of the living, when bright spirits sought to enter the human world. Bonfires stood at the centre of the rites. Hearth fires were put out and relit from a fire on a signal hill. The name Beltane is tied to words for fire. The gods Belenus and Belisama share the root bel- and were linked to the spring fires. The sacred fire of Ériu on the hill of Uisneach stood at the middle of the island.
```
