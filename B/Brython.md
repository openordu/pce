``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Brython names a broad family inside the Celtic tongues, one that gathers Welsh, Breton and Cornish as the living heirs of the P-Celts. This family stands apart from Goidelic, the Irish arm that holds Scots and Manx speech and is often tagged the Q-Celts. Sir John Rhys invented the term Brython and its kin Brythonic to denote the Britons who kept a Celtic speech in Roman Britain. Many writers today keep Brythonic for the older base speech and reserve Brittonic for the tongues now in use. Brython remains a clear label for all that western speech and lets a reader sort it neatly from the Scottish and Irish forms.

:::
```
