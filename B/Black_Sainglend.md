``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Black Sainglend is an alternate name for the horse Saingliu, also written Dubh Sainglenn. Saingliu served the Ulster hero Cuchulainn as one of his two favourite mounts. The other was Liath Macha, and folk tradition treated both horses as magical. They rose from a grey lake near Slieve Fuad before the hero caught them. Cuchulainn tamed each by leaping onto its back. He then rode the beast a full circuit of Ireland in a single day. After that ride the horse grew gentle and would serve him in battle. A reader who meets the shorter form Black Sainglend should turn to the fuller Saingliu entry. The MacKillop dictionary gives the wider word family behind the name. The headword glen comes from Old Irish glenn and Modern Irish gleann. Scottish Gaelic records both gleam and glenn for the same valley word. The form is a Celtic loanword. It prefixes dozens of place-names across Ireland and Scotland. The Welsh cousin of the word is glyn.

:::
```
