``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
British names the ancient Celtic speech of Britain. In Celtic studies it is the oldest attested stage of Brythonic. The language survives in proper names from Caesar's raids in 55 and 54 BC. This form kept its Proto-Celtic final syllables longer than later speech. Jackson in 1953 called this stage Late British. Held in Jackson, that phase ran from the mid-fifth to the mid-sixth century. It ended when those syllables faded and fell away. The term once meant the pre-English folk of Britain and their Welsh, Cornish, and Breton heirs. By the mid-1800s it had stopped meaning Welsh. The later sense of British instead covered all island speech and imperial rule.

:::
```
