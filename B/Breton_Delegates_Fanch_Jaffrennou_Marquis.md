Fañch Jaffrennou was a leading figure in the Breton revival. In 1904 he helped carry the Breton cause abroad. A well-known image shows him gathered with other delegates at the congress held in Caernarfon. He stood there in the national costume of Brittany. The Marquis de l'Estourbeillon de la Garnache traveled with him. Ange Mosher and Francis Even joined the small group. Seated on the right is the Welsh painter John Kelt Edwards. The men's dress is the traditional Breton costume. Its many regional forms survive without a break. The Bretons stand alone among the Celts as a people who still wear a living national dress. They show it at pardons and at gatherings across the year. The dress tells where the wearer comes from. The lace koef or headdress marks the wearer's village. It may also reveal the wearer's social rank. Jaffrennou stood in this costume at the congress with pride. The picture fixes a moment of Celtic unity. It also records how far the Breton movement had come by 1904. Jaffrennou stands as a symbol of a revived identity.

:::
