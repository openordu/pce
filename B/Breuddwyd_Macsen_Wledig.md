``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Breuddwyd Macsen Wledig is a Welsh dream tale of the twelfth century. English readers know it as the Dream of Macsen Wledig. The story turns on a man named Macsen Wledig. That name marks the ruler who history knows as Maximus. Latin writers call him Magnus Maximus or Magnus Clemens. He came from Spain and governed Rome from 383 to 388. Roman scribes spoke of him as a usurper. The Welsh honour him for loosening the grip of Gratian. Theodosius the Great finally slew him at Aquileia. In the tale the emperor falls asleep and dreams of a fair maiden dwelling far away. He wakes and sends men to seek her over the whole earth. They find the young woman in a stately castle within Britain. Every detail meets his dream exactly. Her name is Elen, and she returns his longing. Because Elen stays pure, Macsen grants her father power over the island. He directs his folk to rear the three castles for his bride. Elen may rest on a true person, Elen Luyddog of Segontium. While he is away, a new ruler takes the throne at Rome. Help from Britain under her brother Cynan crosses Gaul and Italy with him. His host regains the ancient city. To his British allies he grants a share of Gaul. That share becomes the region called Brittany. Some scholars link this dream to the Irish aisling. Ifor Williams gave a Welsh text of the tale in 1928. Many copies of the Mabinogion carry an English version.

:::
```
