``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The bucca is a household sprite of Cornwall and Wales. MacKillop glosses the name as a Cornish hobgoblin. Monaghan records the creature for Wales. It resembles the brownie in its habits. The bucca works for food. A householder who failed to leave a bowl of milk or another treat risked mischief. Asking the sprite its name drove it from the farm in disgust. The pwca of Wales and the pooca of Ireland rank among its kindred. Fishermen once laid part of their catch on the sands as its share. Others cast a few crumbs and drops of beer behind them to win its favor. Folklorists separate a helpful bucca gwidden, the white spirit, from an evil bucca dhu, the black spirit. Other names for the black spirit are bucca-boo and bucca-hoo. The word can also serve as a plain term for a fairy. By the 20th century the bucca frightened only small children. In jest people use the word for a scarecrow. Folk motif catalogues record the belief as V12.9.

:::
```
