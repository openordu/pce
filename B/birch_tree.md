``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The birch is one of the sacred plants of the Celts. Old English speakers called it birce. This deciduous hardwood tree has slender branches and smooth white or grey bark. The genus Betula does not live long, yet the tree keeps a graceful form. Celtic countries hold many folk beliefs about this tree. Early Ireland used the ogham alphabet, and it tied the Roman letter B to the birch. Its sign takes the second slot of the Tree Alphabet. In Wales people link the birch with love. The bower of a lover typically sits below a birch tree or within a birch bush. People usually cut the maypole from birch. The Celts raised such poles for dances at the feast of Beltane. So the birch kept a firm place in the old Celtic rites. Wreaths of birch served as tokens of love. Bedwyr is a name that may mean birch hero. Mothers on the island of Colonsay in Gaelic Scotland placed birch boughs over cradles and baby carriages. These boughs guarded babies from fairies. Modern Irish has beith, Scots Gaelic beithe, Manx beih, and Welsh bedwen. Cornish speakers say besewen, and Breton speakers say bezvenn.

:::
```
