::: magazinestyle
The ballad is a song form that circulated anonymously across Western Europe. Its composition in many lands, including the Celtic-speaking ones, took hold after the 12th century. MacKillop derives the word from Old French baladé. Modern Irish renders the term as bailéad. Scottish Gaelic uses duanag, a diminutive of duan, meaning song. The language also offers òran for song, laoidh for verse, and luinneag for a ditty. Manx has bannag, while Welsh gives baled and balad. The Breton term is gwerz.

:::
