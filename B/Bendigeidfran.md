``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Bendigeidfran is a Celtic sea-deity who later carried the title of king of Britain, and he inspires early Welsh stories. Christian scribes favoured this blessed name, judging that he had helped bring the faith into Wales. He is the son of Llŷr and the brother of Branwen and Manawydan, and he is the half-brother of Efnisien. His son is Caradog. Some imagine him as a giant able to wade the channel between Britain and Ireland. He holds a wonder-working cornucopia that serves food and drink when requested. Much of Branwen, the second story of the Mabinogi, unfolds during his reign. A poisoned spear kills him. He begs that his head be cut away and deposited in the White Hill in London. Facing France, it keeps the land safe from invasion. His guards name this trophy Urdawl Ben. They stayed at Harlech for eighty seven years before they buried it. Arthur dug the head up so Britain depends on bold soldiers rather than a charm. Afterwards the head watches over the otherworldly island of Gwales. The Welsh Triads list it among the Three Fortunate Concealments. They also list it among the Unfortunate Disclosures, the second honour because Arthur raised it. Bendigeidfran may share a root with the British god Belatucadros, and scholars pair him with Greek Cronus and Phoroneus. Cronus once slept with fifty maidens. He may also serve as the model for Bron, the Fisher King, in medieval romance.

:::
```
