``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Breton language is a Celtic tongue native to Brittany. It came to the mainland from Britain through early settlement. Speakers still use it today, though numbers have fallen. The oldest stage is Old Breton, known from about 800 to 1100. Glosses and names in charters make up most of its record. Middle Breton followed until 1659. This stage carried a strong mark of French in its words and spelling. The writing system then changed to the early Modern Breton form. Three main orthographies appeared in the 1900s. The KLT system of 1908 served the Kernev, Leon, and Treger dialects. The Peurunvan system of 1941 later adopted a shared character. A separate writing habit stays in use for the Gwenedeg area. Surveys place the number of speakers between 300,000 and one million. About 250,000 use Breton in daily life. Most of these people also speak French. The language holds no official status in France. Schools once punished children who spoke Breton. The Deixonne law of 1951 gave some support to minority tongues. A high median age means fewer children now learn it. Renewal efforts still seek to keep the Breton language alive.

:::
```
