``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
bórama is an Irish word for tribute or cattle-levy, naming the payment that stronger chiefs extracted from weaker neighbours. The Leinster bórama stands as the most famous example: the Lagin delivered it to the kingship at Tara, on account of their non-Goidelic blood. Tuathal Techtmar was the first ruler to call for it. Imaginative narrative sets the price in cattle, fleecy beasts, swine, and cloaks; in bronze vessels; and in ounces of silver — five thousand beasts and goods. The cause given was one of Tuathal Techtmar's daughters taken by Eochaid, king of Leinster, who had already married another of Tuathal's daughters. The tribute ceased after the eighth century. The tale titled Bórama also records the death of Eochaid's son and the burial alive of fifty captives around the son's grave. The text is published in S. H. O'Grady's Silva Gadelica (1892). The byname Brian Bórama, also Boru (d. 1014), takes its name from the ring-fort earthwork at Béal Bórama near Killaloe in County Clare. It is unclear whether Brian ever demanded tribute at that pass or whether his name attached there for some other reason.
```
