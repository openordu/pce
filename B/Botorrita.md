``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Botorrita is a Celtiberian town site famous for its bronze tablets. It is often linked to the ancient town of Contrebia Belaisca. The first tablet, Botorrita I, is a long text in the Celtiberian language. It uses a script based on the Iberian alphabet. The second tablet, Botorrita II, is written in Latin. Students of history call it the Tabula Contrebiensis. This tablet records a Roman senate decision about a water canal. The third tablet, Botorrita III, is the longest known inscription in any Celtic language. Discovery of it came in October 1992. It lists many personal names of men and women. Most names use Celtic kinship formulas with a family name. Some names are Roman, Greek, or Iberian in origin. The mixture shows a town at a cultural border. The tablets also reveal features of the Celtic speech. They show samples of verb endings and pronouns. Botorrita therefore gives scholars a true window into the ancient Celtic world. The place has become a key witness for the study of the Celtiberian language.

:::
```
