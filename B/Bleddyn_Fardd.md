``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Bleddyn Fardd was a Welsh poet active about 1240 to 1285. He belonged to the class called the Poets of the Princes. He likely trained under Dafydd Benfras, for whom he wrote an elegy. He served the court of Gwynedd just as his teacher had. He wrote a joint lament for three sons of Gruffudd ap Llywelyn. Those three were Llywelyn ap Gruffudd, Dafydd, and Owain Goch. He also made a separate elegy for each of them. He mourned Goronwy ab Ednyfed, who was the royal steward. Twelve of his elegies survive in an early fourteenth-century manuscript. The Hendregadredd holds these works. He made a single praise poem for a living lord, Rhys of the south. He carried words from the prince Llywelyn to Rhys in 1275 or 1276. A deathbed lyric closes his known verse. It echoes poems by Meilyr Brydydd and Cynddelw. Half his pieces are chains of englynion. The rest are awdlau set in the gwawdodyn metre.

:::
```
