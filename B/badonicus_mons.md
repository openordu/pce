``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Badonicus Mons is the Latin name for the hill of Badon, the site of a famous British victory over the Anglo-Saxons. The sixth-century writer Gildas records the battle in his De Excidio Britanniae. He does not name a commander at Badon. Later writers attach the triumph to King Arthur. The name itself seems Celtic in origin rather than English. Several fortified hills in southern England still carry the old Old English name Baddanbyrig, now Badbury. None has been proven to be the true site. Scholars have argued for the Badburys near Swindon, or the Uffington White Horse in Oxfordshire. Others favour Liddington Castle, a hill-fort repaired in the post-Roman age. The name Badon may carry a Gallo-Brittonic divine suffix. The same suffix also occurs in Epona and the Matronae. A Roman-era dedication to the Badones Reginae, a band of queens, survives in central Europe. On this reading the hill of Badon was holy before the battle. The Annales Cambriae also note a second fight at Baddon in 665. That later clash was probably fought at the same place. Neither location has been fixed with confidence. 

:::
```
