``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Biniou and Bombard name the classic wind pair of Breton dance music. The biniou is the common bagpipe of Brittany. The bombard reads like an oboe and carries a conical bore with a double reed. The player shifts its notes with the fingers. The two instruments work together in a call and response style. The biniou sounds without a break. The bombard answers every second line or follows a set rhythm. The old pipe is named biniou-koz and the small pipe is named biniou-bihan. The later Scottish-style pipe is called biniou-braz. The bombard belongs to the shawm family. That family spreads across Europe, Asia, and North Africa. Modern builders add keys so the bombard can play a wider set of notes. The bombard joins the biniou in bands and in folk revival groups. It also follows the organ in Breton concert music. The biniou has a leather bag, one drone pipe, and a small chanter with finger holes. Its pitch sounds very high. Records hint that it took this high form in the 1700s or 1800s. That change let the pair play together. Fest-goers still call the couple at the fest in most regions. In the classic photo the bombard sits to the left and the small biniou sits to the right.

:::
```
