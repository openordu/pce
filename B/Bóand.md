``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Bóand is an Irish river goddess whose name means woman of white cows or shining cow. The cow does not appear in her own legend. She may be the same as Bó Find, the ancestor of Ireland's cows. She may also be the goddess Ptolemy called Bouvinda. Bóand gave her name to the River Boyne. Ireland's greatest ancient monuments stand along that river. She visited a forbidden well and lifted the stone that guarded it. The well waters rose and drowned her. Bóand is sometimes called Eithne. She first lived with Elcmar and later took the Dagda as her lover. Koch ties her to Ptolemy's name Buvida, read as 'she who owns white cattle'. Her consort in one old line is Nechtan, whose hill hid the world beyond the plain. The river's source was a holy pool, whose fish and hazel fruit were said to grant wisdom. A stern rule kept all but Nechtan and his three companions from the water. Bóand broke that rule, and the pool burst into three waves that drowned her. The tale says those waves then cut the course of the Boyne. The valley beside her river holds the great tombs of the old age, and the stream long marked the edge of the Ulster realm.

:::
```
