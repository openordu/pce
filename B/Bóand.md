``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Bóand is the Irish goddess of the Boyne and the name of the river itself. Her title means she who owns white cattle. She is the sister of Bébinn and in most accounts the wife of Nechtan. She lay with the great god Dagda and so conceived Angus Óg. To keep the matter hidden she chose Elcmar to raise the boy. A second tale makes Elcmar her husband, and it is the Dagda who seduces her under the name Eochaid Ollathair. Her usual home was Brug na Bóinne, the mound now called Newgrange. Two stories tell why the river came to be. In the first she defied the enchantment of the Well of Segais, and the rising water drowned her before it ran seawards as a stream. In the second she broke the rule at the well of Sídh Nechtán, so the flood chased her to the shore and swallowed her. The Bouvinda that Ptolemy names may be this same river. Bóand may also answer to a British river-goddess honoured at Ilkley in Yorkshire. Her little dog was called Dabilla.

:::
```
