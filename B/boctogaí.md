``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Boctogai is a west Donegal name for the fairies, spelled also bostogai and buctogai. The word is an Irish variant of bocaidhe. These fairies made their home in the sea rather than on land. They gathered most often around rocks and along the shore. Mortals found them harsher than the land fairies were. In rough weather watchers saw their heads rise well above the waves. Every visible head bore yellow hair that reached the middle of the back. The Donegal people also called them bunadh beagna farraige, a phrase that means wee folk of the sea. MacKillop's separate entry gaí (printed gai) treats the word as the stem behind Gaesatae, the naked Celtic warriors of antiquity. Those warriors went into battle unclad, their blond lime-hardened hair standing erect and golden bracelets catching the sun. Modern commentators dispute Polybius's claim that the Gaesatae served as mercenaries. Scholars read these warriors as ancestral to the early Irish fianna, and the Fianna Érainn of Fionn mac Cumhaill take a central place. Old Celtic warriors elsewhere appear in tales as fighters without clothing, and the Gaesatae shared that practice. The belief behind the custom held that bare skin received magical protection from the gods.

:::
```
