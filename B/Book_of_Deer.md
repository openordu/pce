``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Book of Deer is an early Scottish manuscript. Its Latin text dates from about the ninth century. Added Gaelic notes were entered between 1130 and 1150. The codex survives from the monastery at Deer in Aberdeenshire. Tradition says Colum Cille and his disciple Drostán founded that house. The Gaelic passages form the earliest extensive record of Scottish Gaelic writing. They come four centuries before the later collection of the Dean of Lismore. The name Deer may come from the tear that Colum Cille shed when he left the site.

:::
```
