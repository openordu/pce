``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Translating the scriptures into the Celtic languages shaped their early modern history. Vernacular versions only took hold where the Church accepted and spread them. The Welsh Bible succeeded most fully in Cymru. Ireland and Brittany stayed largely Catholic, so the Latin Bible ruled there until recent times. Scotland discouraged Scottish Gaelic Bibles in the Highlands. In Ireland Uilliam O Domhnaill finished an Irish New Testament in 1602, and the Tiomna Nuadha appeared in 1603. William Bedell completed an Irish Bible about 1640, though it was not published until 1685. His version stayed current until the 1970s. A new Irish Bible, An Biobla Naofa, appeared in 1981. A complete Manx Bible did not come out until 1775, too late to displace English. Cornish speakers accepted English as their religious language by the seventeenth century.

:::
```
