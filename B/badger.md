``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The badger is a symbolic animal in Celtic mythology. It is a flesh-eating mammal that burrows in the ground. The British Isles host one badger species, Meles meles. The badger was seen as a shape-shifting person, much like the seal. In the Mabinogi the heroes compete at a grim game called badger-in-the-bag. The game derives from the way hunters subdue and capture the fierce animal. Pwyll captures Rhiannon's rival suitor Gwawl in a giant bag. An Irish tradition tells how Grian turned the sons of Conall into badgers. Cormac Gaileng later killed them. The late seventh-century life of St Columba by Adamnán records a Pictish magus or holy figure bearing the name Brocan. The name means badger. The Irish hero Tadg found badger meat revolting. He did not understand why it disgusted him. The truth is that the badgers were his own kin. Their power to change form tied them to him. He shunned them without knowing of the bond. Celtic words for the animal show wide agreement. Forms include Old Irish brocc, Scottish Gaelic broc, Welsh broch, Cornish brogh, and Breton broc'h.

:::
```
