``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Irish word Bile owns two separate senses. One Bile was a leader among the Milesians in the Book of Invasions. He meant to punish those who killed his son Ith. He joined a second son named Mil Espane and forty more warriors. They moved to take the island, yet a druid raised a storm and he drowned. Two writers of the nineteenth century judged Bile to be a god of darkness. John Rhys and Henri dArbois de Jubainville pressed that idea, but later scholars do not share it. He does keep a tie to the Roman god Dis Pater. The other Bile means an immense tree, a trunk, or a mast. Early Ireland set aside certain trees as sacred. Gods and elemental spirits were said to live inside them. Chiefs and kings took up office beneath the branches. A ruler formed his sceptre out of his own tree. To cut down that tree stood as sacrilege. The sacred Bile of Dathi grew as an ash, counted among the six famed trees of Ireland. The custom may have echoed in Gaul as well. The place-name Biliomagus may mark a plain of the sacred tree.

:::
```
