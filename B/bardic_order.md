``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The bardic order is the professional poet institution of medieval Wales and Ireland. It ranked poets by learning above raw talent. In Ireland the fili formed a fixed hierarchy of seven grades. The highest grade was the ollam. The law-tracts, such as Bretha Nemed and Uraicecht na Ríar, record the grades. The baird, or bards, held a lower place. They composed panegyric and lyric verse. Their rank turned on innate talent rather than schooled training. The fili expected a minimum three generations of poet ancestry. The satirist carried a feared curse. His word shamed a man who failed in honour.

:::
```
