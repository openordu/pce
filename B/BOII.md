``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Boii were a leading people of the eastern Celtic area. Their core lands stretched over Bohemia, most of Moravia, north-eastern Austria, northern Slovakia and northern Hungary. Other groups bearing the name Boii appear in northern Italy and in Gaul. It is possible that all of these groups began as branches of the north-eastern people who named Bohemia. In the later Hallstatt age Bohemia sat within a wider zone defined by shared material culture. Burial finds such as wagon graves point to wide links with the west. Bohemia was preliterate then, so proof of Celtic speech is absent, yet cultural ties make the region part of a wider continuum. Around 400 bc the hill-fort at Zavis was given up. Archaeologists see this period of sudden change as the work of Celtic war-bands entering Bohemia. They place this move within a Celtic migration that reached the sack of Rome about 387 bc.

:::
```
