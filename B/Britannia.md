``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Britannia is a British goddess, the feminine personification of the island itself. The ancient Celts of Britain may have seen their land as a goddess of sovereignty, much as Ireland held Ériu, though that reading has both supporters and detractors in the books. A Roman coin struck in 161 C.E. shows a female figure set on a globe, bearing a shield and a spear, and readers take her for Britannia. The name later served the imperial age as the poet's word for England, and it likely springs from the tribal name Pritani. The island also gains a written book of its kings in 1136. The History of the Kings of Britain, a Latin work, draws the legends of the war of Arthur and a great many Welsh stories into a straight line of rulers. Geoffrey, its author, says he builds on an old book of hidden Welsh material, and he carries the tale of the land from its beginning to the reign of Arthur, giving the whole island the dignity of a long royal line. So Britannia stands at once as a goddess of the soil and as the crown of a written national story.

:::
```
