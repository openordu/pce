``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Brittonnic is an alternative spelling of Brythonic. The term describes the P-Celtic branch of the Celtic languages. The MacKillop entry for the word tonn, Irish for wave, anchors the cross-link. The dictionary lists three great waves said to harass Ireland. They come at intervals and live in both written and oral tradition. Tonn Chliodna is the wave of Clidna. It breaks at Glandore in County Cork. Tonn Rudraige is the wave of Rudraige. It breaks at Dundrum in County Down. Tonn Tuaig is also called Tonn Tuaithe. It is the wave of Tuag. It breaks at Inber Glas, also known as Tuagh Inbir. It breaks at the mouth of the river Bann. That mouth sits in County Londonderry. These three tonns shape a Celtic wave tradition. The Brittonnic spelling inherits that tradition. For the full account of the language group, see the entry for Brythonic.

:::
```
