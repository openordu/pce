``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The bogan is a night sprite of Gaelic folklore. Irish tradition also knows it. Speakers give it the forms bdean, bócan, bauchan, and buckawn. The name covers a hobgoblin, a sprite, or a spectre. Its stories centre on Gaelic Scotland, but they place it also in Ireland, in North America, and in Australia. The creature could shape-shift and act the trickster. It was usually dangerous and sometimes helpful. A bogan often attached itself to one family and stayed with it. The MacDonalds of Morar kept such a spirit in their west Highland home. Their home county was Inverness-shire until 1974. Their bogan bore the name Coluinn gun Cheann, the headless body. Some tellings give the form Colann gun Cheann. The spirit was cheerful toward the family. It murdered visitors and left mutilated bodies by the river. In time it killed a man whose friend John Macleod was strong. Macleod wrestled the creature down and threatened it with daylight. The bogan promised to leave the land if freed. It kept that promise and went.

:::
```
