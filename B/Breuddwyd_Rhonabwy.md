``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Breuddwyd Rhonabwy is a Welsh dream told around the twelfth or thirteenth century. English readers meet it as the Dream of Rhonabwy. The tale counts as the last homegrown story in Middle Welsh. Many praise it as the most literary of all the texts grouped with the Mabinogion. The single surviving copy sits in the Red Book of Hergest. The setting falls in the reign of Madog of Powys, who died in 1159. Prince Madog sends Rhonabwy and his warriors to hunt a rebel brother. They spend one night in the squalid home of Heilyn Goch. The floors lie coated with dung and urine. Rhonabwy sleeps on a yellow ox-hide and then sees a great vision. The old champions of Britain show as giants while the new fellows look small. He moves toward a ford over the Severn in his dream. Two richly dressed horsemen overtake him on the way. The first names himself Iddawg the Embroiler. That man once stirred up the trouble at the Battle of Camlan. He forfeited peaceful messages that Arthur had sent to Mordred. With Iddawg to guide him, Rhonabwy finds Arthur seated with the bishop on an island in a river. Arthur gazes at the two puny riders and regrets the way of the land. A stone in Arthur's ring lets Rhonabwy recall the whole meeting. Arthur should ride to a war near Norway and Denmark. Instead he sits across a chess piece from a companion named Owain. Bold ravens wheel about the camp as he plays. Only when the king asks for calm does Owain still the flock. Many lords step forward and offer up their tribute. Rhonabwy wakes and finds that three nights have slowly passed away.

:::
```
