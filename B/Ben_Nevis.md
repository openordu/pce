::: magazinestyle
Ben Nevis stands as the highest mountain in the Scottish Highlands, rising to 4,406 feet. It lay within the county of Inverness-shire until 1974, about five miles east-southeast of Fort William. Its base wraps roughly 24 miles around the hill. Scottish Gaelic storytelling draws on this grand peak often. Some folk stories read the name beinn neamh bhathais as 'mountain with head in clouds' or 'sky-touching peak'. Other readings treat it as 'venomous one' or 'terrible', yet these sound fanciful to many. The likeliest source of the name is a nearby stream, one still called Nevis today.

:::
