``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Bétéide is the modern Irish form of the older name Bé Téite. The two spellings point to the same figure in Irish myth. She is a daughter of Flidais, a goddess who guards cattle and abundance. Writers describe her as a wanton or luxurious woman. She moves with ease among the Tuatha Dé Danann. This divine people holds power over the land of Ireland. Bé Téite appears in early accounts as an active presence. Her name carries a hint of unrestrained desire. The variant Bétéide lets later Irish readers meet her again. It keeps the older story alive in a modern tongue. Her role links her to the changing fortunes of the gods. The name endures in the living fabric of Irish tradition.

:::
```
