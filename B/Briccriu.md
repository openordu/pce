``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Briccriu is a warrior, a troublemaker, and a poet of the Ulster Cycle. His usual labels describe him as bitter of tongue or evil of tongue. In the tale that bears his name he makes the leading three heroes of Ulster argue about who gets the champion's cut. Those heroes were Cúchulainn, Lóegaire Búdacch, and Conall Cemach. He also moved the athletes of Ireland to fight over how to serve Mac Da Thó's swine, and that feast ended in spilt blood. In the smaller story of the Flidais raid he acts as a satirist whose words are sharp but not cruel. His stronghold was Dún Rudraige, which today is the village called Dundrum in County Down. The lake and town of Loughbrickland keep his memory alive. His kin said he was the son of Carbad, while his brother was the single-eyed beast of the Ulster cycle. Writers liken him to the rude Thersites of the Iliad and to the trickster Loki of the old Norse tales.

:::
```
