``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The bean nighe is a wraith of Scottish Gaelic tradition, and Ireland knows her too. Her Gaelic name means washerwoman or laundrymaid. She is the Scottish Washer at the Ford, a figure the whole Celtic world shares. She acts as a form of the banshee and arrives before a death.

She washes the bloodstained clothes of the fated at a river or a stream. Her work foretells a death nearby, most often in battle. A passerby hears a sound like rippling water. The noise comes from her slapping the wet cloth. She shows herself as a small, slender woman in a green gown, sometimes with red webbed feet. Desolate lakes and streams are her haunts.

Some accounts make her the ghost of a mother who died in childbirth and left her washing unfinished. Others count her among the fairy people. She sees things in the future beyond the deaths she announces. If you spot her before she spots you, you may ask for a prophecy. She always portends evil, and her answer always proves true. The asking still carries risk. Anyone bold enough to suck one of her hanging breasts may claim her fosterage and go free.

Regional forms differ. The Caointeag, a fiercer version of her, is found on Islay and Kintyre. The Glencoe Cadineag is the gentler form. In Ireland the fearful washer's role passes into the banshee. Brittany keeps a counterpart called the tunnerez noz.

:::
```
