``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
beithir is a Scottish Gaelic term of the early narratives. It names a savage creature that the tales leave undetermined. Glosses offer bear, serpent, savage beast, or dragon. The word may translate a Norse term for bear. A second sense is thunderbolt, with later readings taking it as lightning or as serpent. The creature may show a long tail. It never takes the shape of the blazing winged dragon told of in Germanic story. Recent oral tradition makes the beithir one kind of fuath. This fuath lurks in caves and in conies, narrow ring-shaped valleys with high walls. South of the mouth of Glencoe a mountain bears the name Ben Vair or Ben Vehir. Local belief holds that the name marks a beithir which once sheltered in Conie Lia.

:::
```
