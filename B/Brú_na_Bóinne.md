``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Brú na Bóinne is an Irish mythological site. Three great rock mounds rise beside the River Boyne. They lie between Slane and Drogheda. Their names are Knowth, Dowth, and Newgrange. Each huge tumulus holds an interior passageway of standing stones. Many stones bear carvings of spirals, sunbursts, and stars. Unknown people built the mounds about five thousand years ago. The Celts did not make them. Newgrange faces the winter solstice sunrise. It was the palace, or Brú, of Bóand, goddess of the river. In older story MacKillop records the place as the hostel of the Boyne. It first belonged to the goddess Bóand and the Dagda. Later it became the seat of Angus Óg, the god of youth. It offered endless ale, three trees always in fruit, and two pigs. Some versions say the hero Lug was buried here.

:::
```
