``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Breton literature of the 1900s grew through the Gwalarn movement. These writers sought a standard language and a rich new corpus. Breton then claimed about one million speakers in the early century. Yet the tongue stayed out of school curricula, so most people could not read it. The promoters of the standard worked through journals and a small circle. Fanch Elies, called Abeozen, wrote a detailed history of the era. His collection Pirc'hirin kala-goañv gathers tales set around the world wars. Youenn Drezen penned An dour en-dro d'an inizi in 1931. Maodez Glanndour shaped poetry through a Christian lens. His Komzou bev, of 1985, stands among the period key books. The poet Guennig wrote of New York life as an emigrant. The two world wars framed much of this Breton literature. Later decades saw industry and rural leaving set the scene. Traditional verse kept appearing at the same time. From 1919 to 1939 the Gwalarnists put medieval Irish prose into Breton. Xavier Langleiz wrote an Arthurian romance in 1975. A great many Breton writers stayed little known abroad. So the flood of 20th-century Breton writing overcame the struggle for a readership. It remains a literature rich in viewpoint yet small in reach.

:::
```
