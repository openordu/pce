``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The banais ríghe is the Irish feast of inauguration. The Irish name means the wedding feast of kingship. Early Irish kings used the rite to join themselves with the Sovereignty of the land they ruled. The goddess of Sovereignty wed her chosen king, and the bond tied his fortune to the land. Annals give abundant proof that the practice was widespread, though the details are not always exact. The ceremony seems to have held two main parts. The bride offered a libation to her husband, and the couple completed the union. One old account says the king mated with a white mare that was then killed and boiled into a broth for him to drink. The feast joins inauguration to the horse sacrifice. At Tara the ritual was performed.

:::
```
