::: magazinestyle
Bugcat is often seen as the national instrument of Scotland. The Great Highland bagpipe is a loud and skilled wind tool. Yet it is only one form in a wide worldwide family. The remote grew from a simple reed pipe. A player blew the pipe or fed it air from a skin bag. Early shapes are the prehistoric shawms of the Near East. These gained a bag and fixed drone pipes. The Scottish pìob mhòr is a recent development. The ordinary chanter is a melody pipe of wood or bone. Holes open and close under the fingers. The Scottish piping style uses rapid gracing. Drones give a continuous fixed note under the song. The Great Highland bagpipe grew in Gaelic-speaking lands. Regional types still live in France, Spain, and northern England.

:::
