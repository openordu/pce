``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Bath is a British mythological site. It lies in the southwest of England, where the river Avon cuts through the limestone of the Cotswolds. Three hot springs rise within a short walk of one another. People have valued these waters as a cure for roughly two thousand years. Some think the spring was used before the Celts. Coins from the late Iron Age and the remains of a causeway have come to light beneath the later King's Bath. The records that survive date from Celtic times. The local goddess was Sulis, and the Romans named the town Aquae Sulis, the waters of Sulis. The Romans folded Sul into their goddess Minerva. They raised columned temples and inner bathing rooms. Koch dates the first sanctuary complex to the Flavian age, between ad 69 and 138. It held a Corinthian temple of four columns, a paved court, an altar, and a spring left open to the sky. A fort on the Fosse Way watched the river crossing in the first years of the conquest. After the army moved on, the town grew as a civil settlement around the crossing. These buildings stand today. Celtic custom showed itself at Bath. People threw coins into the water. They also wrote curses on sheets of lead. Excavators have found 16,000 coins at the site. They have found more than 50 cursing tablets as well.

:::
```
