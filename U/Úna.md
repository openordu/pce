``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Úna, also rendered Oonagh or Oona, is in MacKillop's Dictionary of Celtic Mythology the wife of Finnbheara, king of the Irish fairies, with the pet-form Nuala carried in some folk tellings. MacKillop frames her as the most beautiful woman of the whole tradition, the long hair sweeping the ground a recurring tag. She bears seventeen sons to Finnbheara. Some versions of the lore give Úna a residence of her own at Cnoc Sídh Úna [Knockshegouna] in County Tipperary, separate from the king's hall at the heart of the síth. Folklorists number her under motif F252.2, the stock fairy-queen type. MacKillop treats Úna as the central female figure of Finnbheara's household, around whom the household's emotional and dynastic life turns.

:::
```
