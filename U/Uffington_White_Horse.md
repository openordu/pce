``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Uffington White Horse is a chalk figure 364 feet long on the Berkshire Downs. Cutters stripped the sod away to expose the white chalk below. The figure lies below the Iron Age fort called Uffington Castle, in Oxfordshire since 1974. Its taut, curvilinear style makes it among the largest works of pictorial art on earth. Popular fancy reads the figure as the horse-goddess Epona. Stylistic evidence argues against that reading. The carving seems to date from about 50 BC. Epona's cult seems to have reached Britain only after the Roman era. Southern England counts fourteen such hill figures of white horses. Among all fourteen, the Uffington beast stands as the oldest. Medieval records mention it as early as 1084. From 1650 into the twentieth century, local people scoured the carving. The scourings kept the carved lines white and clear of grass. They usually came with great festivities. Commentators cannot say which British people cut the horse. The Atrebates and the Dobunni are the likeliest candidates. Dobunnic coinage featured a three-tailed horse.

:::
```
