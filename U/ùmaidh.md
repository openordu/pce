ùmaidh, in MacKillop's Dictionary of Celtic Mythology, is a Scottish Gaelic word for 'changeling'. MacKillop treats the term as a brief lexical entry, with no narrative attached: a child said to have been left by the fairy host in place of a stolen human infant. The broader changeling tradition in Celtic folklore connects ùmaidh to beliefs about fairy interference in childbirth, the protection rites of iron, open doors, and unbaptized infants. MacKillop's 2008 entry is short, identifying ùmaidh as a single-word ScG lexical item rather than a full folklore article.

:::
