::: magazinestyle
Uist is the shared name of two large islands of the Outer Hebrides. The Gaelic form is uibhisi. North Uist, Uibhist a Tuath, covers 118 square miles. South Uist, Uibhist a Deas, covers 141 square miles. Scottish Gaelic still sounded on the two islands late in the twentieth century. Both islands carry a wealth of archaeological sites. Their remains compare with the celebrated Callanish stones of the Isle of Lewis. Storytelling pooled more deeply in the Roman Catholic south than in the Protestant north. MacLellan's South Uist stories, told in Gaelic and translated by Campbell, appeared in London in 1961. Thompson printed a survey of the Uists and Barra in 1974.

:::
