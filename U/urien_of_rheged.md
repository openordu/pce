``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Urien of Rheged was a British ruler of the sixth century. He led the northern Britons of a land called Hen Ogledd. He fought against the Anglo-Saxons during his reign. Early records treat him as the finest of those British kings. His home kingdom bore the name Rheged. His family formed the kindred called Cynferching. That lineage descends from the old ruler Coel Hen. The Historia Brittonum placed him among the kings of the Bernicia. He joined three named rulers in war. Rhydderch the Old, Gwallawg, and Morgan fought beside him. Urien trapped his foes on the island of Lindisfarne. He kept them there for three days and nights. Morgan slew him out of jealousy. Morgan envied Urien for his skill at renewing battle. The exact year of his death stays unclear. Some scholars link it to king Hussa. Others place the event close to the visit of a Kentish churchman. Old englynion mourn the fallen hero. They describe his severed head and headless body. The poems recount his battles. The fields of Gwen Ystrad and Argoed Llwyfain appear in them. A mighty foe there carried the title Fflamddwyn. The name means flame-bearer. The enemy was perhaps a benician overlord. The verse names Urien as lord of Catraeth. His lands may lie to the south of the Roman wall. The triads record that Urien fathered the boy Owain. Owain gained a twin sister called Morfudd. Their mother was the otherworldly woman Modron. Urial's murder ended among the three harsh assassinations.

:::
```
