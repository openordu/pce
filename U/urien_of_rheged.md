``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Urien of Rheged was a British ruler of the sixth century. He led the northern Britons of a land called Hen Ogledd, in what is now Scotland. His home kingdom bore the name Rheged, and his speech was Welsh. He fought the Anglo-Saxons through his reign, and early records treat him as the finest of those British kings. His family formed the kindred called Cynferching, which descends from the old ruler Coel Hen. The Historia Brittonum placed him among the kings of the Bernicia. He joined three named rulers in war. Rhydderch the Old, Gwallawg, and Morgan fought beside him. Urien trapped his foes on the island of Lindisfarne and held them there for three days and nights. Morgan slew him out of jealousy, envying his skill at renewing battle. The exact year of his death stays unclear. Some scholars link it to king Hussa. Others place the event close to the visit of a Kentish churchman. The triads record that Urien fathered the boy Owain. Owain gained a twin sister called Morfudd. Their mother was the otherworldly woman Modron. The old englynion mourn the fallen hero. They describe his severed head and headless body, and recount his battles at Gwen Ystrad and Argoed Llwyfain. A mighty foe there carried the title Fflamddwyn, a name that means flame-bearer. That enemy was perhaps a Bernician overlord. The verse also names Urien as lord of Catraeth, and his lands may lie south of the Roman wall. His murder stands among the three harsh assassinations.

Later imagination gave the king a second career. The poet Taliesin praised him around the years 550 to 600 and called him Urien Rheged. The Welsh Triads record his name, though never in any link with Arthur. Geoffrey of Monmouth made him king of Moray in northern Scotland in his Historia of 1136. The thirteenth-century French Claris et Laris credits him as father of Yvain and Marine. Romance often casts him in the role of Morgan le Fay's husband. Malory instead shows him dying in battle against Morgan's son Mordred.
```
