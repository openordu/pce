::: magazinestyle
ùruisg, also spelled urisk in anglicized form, is in MacKillop's Dictionary of Celtic Mythology a solitary fairy of Highland tradition, a subspecies of the fuath. The creature is half-man and half-goat, but not satyr-like in shape, and reads as a rougher, hirsute brownie. The ùruisg helps with household chores, with churning butter and cleaning among the most common tasks, despite a reputation for good-humoured sloth. The fairy has a fondness for milk and cream, and is dreaded by dairymaids. Every manor house reputedly housed a resident ùruisg, and a seat by the kitchen fire was kept vacant for him. The ùruisg craves human companionship but usually frightens people away with its unseemly appearance. The fairy also haunted lonely and sequestered places, with a corrie near Loch Katrine a particularly well-attested haunt. In spoken Scottish Gaelic the same word can also denote a diviner who reads the future, or a man of fierce and wild aspect. The MacKillop entry sits in the family of Highland fairy-figures alongside the glaisitig, the fuath, and the peallaidh.

:::
