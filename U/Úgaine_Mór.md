``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Úgaine Mór, also written Ugaine Már, Ughaine, Úgoine, Ugainy, or Ugony, is in MacKillop's Dictionary of Celtic Mythology a possibly historical early Irish chieftain who stands at the head of medieval genealogies. Both Labraid Loingsech of Leinster and Conn Cétchathach of the Hundred Battles claim descent from him. The fictionalized story-cycle, written centuries after his time, makes Úgaine a foster-son of Queen Macha, sets him on the throne of Tara for forty years, and ranges his dominion across the northern realm as far as Scotland, the Isle of Wight in the south, and onward to the rest of Europe. A tenth-century tale, now lost, had Úgaine warring as far as Italy. He takes to wife Cesair (2), a Frankish princess, who bears him twenty-five children, a different Cesair from the first invader of Ireland named in the Lebor Gabála [Book of Invasions]. The whole island was carved into twenty-five shares, one for each child, but only two of them, Cobthach and Lóegaire Lore (Lóegaire's son Labraid Loingsech is the third generation), left any descendants at all. MacKillop frames Úgaine as a node of dynastic convergence rather than a round-by-round narrative hero.
```
