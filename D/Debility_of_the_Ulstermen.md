``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Debility of the Ulstermen is an Irish mythological theme. The men of Ulster were cursed because a man mistreated his wife. The pregnant goddess Macha warned her mortal husband, the farmer Crunniuc. He must not mention her when he went to the Assembly of Ulster. He boasted that she was swifter than the king's horses. A race was proposed, and Macha begged not to run so near to term. The king hoped her size would slow her, so the race went on. Macha won but died giving birth to twins. As she died she placed a curse on the men who caused her death. Whenever an enemy attacked, the Ulstermen would fall for four days and five nights in labor-like pain. The Irish form of the affliction is Ces Noinden Ulad. That name means the nine days' affliction of Ulster, also called the Pangs of the Ulstermen. The Curse of Macha lasted nine times nine generations. It shaped the epic Tain Bo Cuailnge, which retells the episode at its beginning. Cuchulainn was born outside Ulster and free of the curse. He held off the army of Connacht alone while the Ulstermen lay helpless.
```
