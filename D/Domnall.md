``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Domnall (Donal) is an Irish and Scottish hero. The name is popular in both Irish and Scottish tradition. Many minor heroes and kings carried it. The most important was Domnall of Tara. He succeeded the mad king Suibhne. Domnall Míldemail was a Scottish king. Cúchulainn came to his lands. The father of Cúchulainn's bride Emer sent him there, fearing for his daughter's virtue.

A separate historical ruler also bore the name. Domnall mac Cusantín ruled Scotland as Donald II. His reign ran from 889 to 899. His father was Cusantín mac Cinaeda, called Constantine I. His grandfather was the famous Cinaed mac Ailpín, who first ruled the Picts and Scots. The Annals of Ulster name him the first king of Scotland. Earlier rulers bore the title king of the Picts. His rule advanced Scotland's own identity. He drew the old kingdom of Strathclyde into Scotland's lands. He restored the line of Cinaed mac Ailpin over both peoples. In his time the Orkney islands fell under Viking power. He died in 899 and was buried on Iona. His name is the source of the English Donald. That name stayed common across Ireland and Scotland.

:::
```
