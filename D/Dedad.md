``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Dedad were the subjects or followers of Cú Roí mac Dáiri. The dictionary also prints the forms Ciann Dedad and Fir Dedad beside the name. Much evidence argues Gann Dedad was a second name for the Érainn. That people was an early power in Ireland outside the Goidelic line. The Táin Bó Flidais counts the Ciann Dedad among the three warrior races of Ireland. The other two are the Ciann Rudraige, that is the Ulaid, and the Gamanrad. Ó hÓgáin has suggested the Dedad were fancifully linked with the River Dee in north-east Leinster. On that reading the famous ford Áth Fhirdiad may be named for the people. It would then take its name rather than the hero Ferdiad, whom Cúchulainn killed there in the great cattle raid.

:::
```
