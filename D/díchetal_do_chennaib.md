``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Díchetal do chennaib was a form of divination used by the ancient Irish. MacKillop records variant forms, among them díchetal do chennaib and dichedal do chennaib, and renders the Old Irish sense as extempore incantation. Poets called filid and druids composed this kind of spell in early Ireland. Early sources describe composers working extemporaneously, often with the fingertips. That use of the fingers may point toward divination. Writers are unsure how the ritual worked. It may have been clairvoyance or psychometry, which reads a person or object through touch. The seer then conveyed the message in quatrains or verse. It may instead have been an incantation that used the fingertips as mnemonic aids. It may also have been a prophecy in which bards and seers drew meaning from an automatic flood of speech. The ollam, the highest grade of poet, had to master both this art and the imbas forosnai. Fionn mac Cumhaill appears in the tales as especially skilled at díchetal do chennaib. Saint Patrick permitted its continued use when he banned other divinatory arts. He allowed it because judges held the ritual harmless and free of pagan rites.

:::
```
