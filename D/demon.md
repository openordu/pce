``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
demon in MacKillop is a Greek concept, named from Gk. daimön, marking an evil spirit ranked second in the spiritual order. Christian commentators borrowed the term and applied it subjectively to pre-Christian Celtic figures and events. A familiar trope calls the creatures driven from Ireland by St Patrick demons. Almost any pre-Christian personality may be called a demon, with both the Fomorian hosts and the people of the Tuatha Dé Danann falling under that label. Many mischievous or malevolent beings from oral tradition also carry the label. The Irish examples named in MacKillop include the bocánach and the Cornu of Ireland. The Breton examples include the ankou, nain, and youdik. Bodb changes Aife into a demon of the air, an aerial-demon motif that appears in many translated texts even when no Irish phrase names it. The concept overlaps with the devil, to which MacKillop cross-refers.

:::
```
