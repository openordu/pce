::: magazinestyle
Present-day Brittany, the western French peninsula, is divided into the administrative départements and their principal towns. The eastern part, where the Gallo language is spoken, holds the French-speaking dioceses and their chief cities: Rennes, known as Roazhon in Breton, Nantes, called Naoned, Saint-Malo or Sant Malo, and Saint-Brieuc. The western counterpart, where Breton speech survives, is named Lower Brittany, while the Gallo-speaking east is Upper Brittany. Within this western zone the four Breton-speaking dioceses appear, those of Corn, Vannes or Gwened, Leon and Tregor. The historic cap such towns grew in the lands around Rennes, Nantes, Saint-Malo and Saint-Brieuc.

:::
