``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Danu is an Irish goddess. She is also called Dana, Anu, or Danann. No myths about her survive. Yet many place-names bear her name. The Dane Hills carry her name. The river Danube in eastern Europe carries it. The Paps of Danu stand in Munster. These are two breast-shaped hills. Prehistoric cairns top them like nipples. The Tuatha Dé Danann (Tuatha De Danann) take their name from her. This name means the people of the goddess Danu. Her name descends from Old Celtic dán, meaning knowledge. Some link her to Dôn, the Welsh mother goddess Don. Some texts call her daughter of Dagda. This link supports her fertility. Some equate her with Danann, daughter of Flidais. Others call her mother of the Sons of Tuireann. MacKillop treats Danu as a speculative name. He reads it as the Continental Celts' mother goddess. His case rests on place-names such as the Danube, the Hungarian Duna, and the German Donau. He also reads the Irish form as Ana wearing a prosthetic d-. He links the Welsh Don to the same goddess. Figures called Danu surface as far away as Russia and India. In India's Rig-Veda the name signifies a stream and the waters of heaven.

:::
```
