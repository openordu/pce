``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The serpent stone, also called druid’s glass or ovum anguinum, is a small round piece of glass. The druids used it for divination and healing. Pliny recorded that a Vocontian warrior carried one of these stones. Emperor Claudius ordered that warrior executed because Rome feared the stone’s power. Folk belief held that serpents formed the stone in summer at a fixed phase of the moon. The reptiles writhed together and secreted a liquid ball that hardened in the air. The garter snake shows similar mating behavior, which gave the tale its shape. Dipped into a glass of water, the stone healed bewitchment. Folk belief kept it as medicine for many other ailments long after the druids faded. The word glass itself fired the wider Celtic imagination beyond the druids’ charm. Welsh legend named a Caer Wydyr, a shining fortress that stood for a glimpse of the Otherworld. Conand’s Tower on Tory Island off County Donegal, the Fomorian stronghold, was built of the same clear substance. Irish sea tales sent Bran, Saint Brendan, and Máel Dún past shining spires of glass on their voyages. Merlin put to sea in a vessel made of that same brittle material. Welsh speakers once gave Glastonbury three names meaning glass island. Those names were Ynys Wydrin, Ynys Gutrin, and Ynys Witrin. The English place-name does not translate them. The Celtic word family for the material begins with Old Irish glaine. It runs through Modern Irish gloine, Scottish Gaelic glaine, and Manx giess. Welsh uses gwydr, Cornish gweder, and Breton gwer.

:::
```
