::: magazinestyle
Druidale was the site of a small early monastic chapel on the Isle of Man, near the parish of Michael. A keeill is a simple early Christian place of worship, often tiny and built of local stone. Excavation at Druidale followed an earlier survey of the remains and exposed the outline of the chapel. The work recorded the position and plan of the building. Investigators recovered evidence of the internal arrangement and of the surrounding small burial ground. Such keeills were the devotional focus of a local community after the coming of Christianity to the island. Holy wells often stood near these early chapels, a link with local wells. The excavated evidence pointed to early medieval use. Analysis helped place the chapel in time among the many keeills across Man. The site shows how a small rural church served the people of the valley.

:::
