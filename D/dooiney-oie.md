``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
dooiney oie is a Manx night-spirit tied to the storm-warning lore of Mann. The Manx name translates literally as night-man. In Manx belief the figure is benign rather than malevolent, and his task is to warn islanders of approaching storms. He gives warning through one of three modes. He may shout across the moor. He may speak aloud while making a misty appearance. He may sound a loud horn over the land. All three modes serve the same practical end: islanders who hear the warning drive their stock into a shelter before the storm arrives. dooiney oie is a working folkloric figure woven into the agricultural rhythm of the island. He stands in a close relationship to a related figure called howlaa. Howlaa is known for a warning howl but never for speech. The two figures are virtually indistinguishable by function, and some Manx accounts treat howlaa as nothing more than the shouted cry of dooiney oie himself.

:::
```
