``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Dìreach names one form of the Scottish monster called the fachan, also known as the athach. MacKillop derives the name from Scottish Gaelic dithreach, a word for desert, uncultivated, or solitary ground. Folklore assigns the being to an ugly class of giants. Improbable deformities set it apart from its kin. A single hand sprouted from the middle of its chest. One leg grew from its haunch. A single eye rested in its head. A tuft of stiff hair that never bent grew from that head. That chest hand gripped a flail studded with iron apples. The creature struck at passersby while hopping on the single leg. The dìreach haunted the deserted Glen Eiti, a name also spelled Eite, Eitidh, or Eitli. MacKillop reads that glen as a distorted form of Glen Etive, which lies eight miles south of Ballachulish. The related Gaelic word éitidh means fierce, dreadful, ugly, or dismal. Some writers trace the monster to fear of a druid's cursing posture. A druid cast a curse by standing on a single leg, with one arm raised high. That pose appears in the ritual glám dícenn. The dìreach thus ties a folkloric figure to an old practice rather than to a distinct being.

:::
```
