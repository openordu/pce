``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
dooiney marrey is a Manx sea-spirit tied to the maritime lore of the Isle of Mann. The literal English rendering of the Manx name is sea-man. Older Manx-English sources occasionally spell the term dinney-mara. In Manx folklore the figure stands as the masculine sea-spirit, while ben-varrey fills the feminine counterpart role. Scholars who compare Gaelic-Atlantic traditions place the Manx merman beside the merrow of Irish tradition. That parallel makes dooiney marrey a node in the wider Gaelic-Atlantic belief web. Some local Manx accounts depict him as a gentle father who plays with his children on the shore. The gentle-father portrayal sits alongside a more solitary classification: folklorists usually class the figure as a solitary fairy rather than as a member of a troop. The solitary label sets him apart from the processional and courtly fairies of inland tradition. Surviving Manx material gives less space to dooiney marrey than to the female figure ben-varrey, and the island record keeps the mermaid dominant. Together the two form the gendered pair that anchors Manx maritime spirit-lore. The pairing reflects the island's coastal imagination in folk belief.

:::
```
