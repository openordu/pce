::: magazinestyle
The dolphin is a porpoise-like marine mammal of warm waters whose name comes from Latin and ultimately from Greek delphis. The creature turns up repeatedly in early Celtic iconography yet vanishes from later material. Sculpted and engraved dolphins appear on the Gundestrup cauldron and on Iron-Age Gaulish coins. Other pre-Christian British sites, notably Lydney Park in Gloucestershire, have also yielded dolphin imagery. Celtic-language names for the animal vary by tradition. Modern Irish calls it deilf. Scottish Gaelic uses an leumadair. Manx names it perkin. Welsh offers dolffin and morwch. Breton uses delfin.

:::
