``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Bodleian Library MS 572, also called Codex Oxoniensis Posterior, holds the colloquy De raris fabulis. Scribes dated this codex to the mid-900s. The Latin dialogue may have begun as a 9th-century text. Teachers used such scholastic colloquies to build learned vocabulary. The piece carries Latin glosses and others in early Celtic tongues. Scholars long argued these were Old Welsh, Old Cornish, or Breton. Many of the raris words show traits shared by Welsh and Cornish alike. A few forms reveal sound changes unique to Welsh, such as au. Those raris words therefore favor Old Welsh over Old Cornish. Jackson judged the hand most likely Cornish in origin. Yet similar features appear in Irish and Welsh manuscripts, so the question stays open. This raris text thus remains a key case for separating the early Celtic languages.

:::
```
