``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Dalriada is a variant spelling of the name Dál Riada. The dictionary also prints Dál Riata and Dairiada. The Dál Riada began as a proto-kingdom in the north of Ireland. It later grew strong across the water, in the Gaelic west of Scotland. The Irish half took its name from the mythological ancestor Eochu Riata and centred on the Glens of Antrim. Military pressure pushed the kingdom's border out. It spread over what is now Argyllshire. Áedán mac Gabráin led that expansion and accepted baptism from St Colum Cille. The invaders built their capital at Dunadd and carried the Gaelic language and Christianity across the sea. An early king, Fergus mac Eirc, brought the Lia Fáil stone of destiny for his coronation and never returned it. In Scotland the same stone became the Stone of Scone. The kingdom fought the Picts in constant warfare. Cináed mac Alpin ended the struggle by uniting both peoples north of the Clyde. His united Scottish kingdom ran from 844 to 858.

:::
```
