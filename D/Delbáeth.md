``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Delbáeth was an obscure Irish goddess. Her name means fire-shape. The Book of Invasions names her as the mother of Donand. Many writers believe Donand is the same as Danu, the goddess of earth. A second figure shared the name Delbáeth. This one was a druid of the hill of Uisneach. He built a great fire. Five points of light streamed outward from that fire. MacKillop 2008 counts several bearers of the name. Delbáeth the first is a Fomorian king. He is the father of Elatha and the grandfather of Bres. Some texts make him the father of Eithne in place of Balor. Elatha mated with Ériu, the daughter of the second Delbáeth, and Bres was their son. The second Delbáeth is a noble youth among the Tuatha Dé Danann. He is the son of Angus Óg and of Eithne, daughter of Balor. By Emmas or Eirnin he fathered the three name-giving goddesses of Ireland, Ériu, Fódla, and Banba. The third Delbáeth is a Munster leader. He stands seventh in line from Ailill Aulomm. Banished with his five sons, he kindled a druidical fire at the cairn of Fiachu. Five fiery streams burst from it, and he sent one son to each stream. That story explains the five tribal and place-names called Delbna.
```
