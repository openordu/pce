``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Dagda is an old god of the Tuatha Dé Danann, Ireland's supernatural people. His name means the good god, which in this case points to skill and fine work rather than charm. He serves as artisan, warrior, diviner, farmer, and king inside one body. He owns a cauldron that never empties, and he drags a huge club mounted on a cart. His pigs cook and come back to life, and his orchard bears fruit in every season. The Fomoiri mock him as gross and greedy. They pour porridge into a pit and command him to eat it all. He clears it to the last scrape of his finger. Yet they misjudge him. He is a master of craft and magic whose powers span death and rebirth in the same stroke. His title Eochaid Ollathair, the father of all, is often compared with the Norse All-Father Odin. The texts also give him the name Ruad Rofhessa, the lord of great knowledge. Oengus, god of youth and of good looks, is a son he sires. His daughter is the goddess Brigit, also called Bríg. Sons named Áed and Bodb Derg stand among his other children.

:::
```
