``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
divine land is a phrase MacKillop uses in English renderings of stories drawn from Irish and Welsh sources. The phrase points to a specific realm only when the reader knows the Celtic text in view. By implication the divine land is a pleasant Elysian place. It is not the realm of the dead. MacKillop points readers to the broader Otherworld entry. In Irish stories the divine land most often names Tír na nÓg, the Land of Youth. Several heroes visit Tír na nÓg, and Oisín is the most famous of them. The divine land may also refer to Mag Mell, the Pleasant Plain, or to Tír Taimgire, the Land of Promise. It may likewise point to Tír na mBan, the Land of Women, or to other named abodes. In Welsh contexts the divine land can refer to Annwfn.

:::
```
