``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Divici, a member of the ruler of the Belgic noble lines, ruled the Suessiones, a Gaulish community dwelling near the Somme and the Aisne in the north-east of Gaul, around the last century BC. As Caesar records in his De Bello Gallico (2.4), the memory of Diviciacos was still alive in the BC 50s and memory of his former supremacy still endured: he had once stood as the single most powerful ruler across the whole of Gaul and had carried his influence into Britain. Caesar probably highlighted this earlier supremacy to make his own conquest of Britain appear planned, yet cast the north-east Gaulish region and south-east Britain as one cultural zone sharing coinage. South Belgic coinage inscribed with the legend DEIVICIAC likely belongs to a later Suessene king who bore the same civis name.

:::
```
