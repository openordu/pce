::: magazinestyle
Desmond is a territory of southern Ireland whose Irish name means South Munster. It was recognized before the counties were carved out in the seventeenth century. Its bounds match County Kerry today and much of County Cork and County Limerick. In the sixteenth century the land became a theatre of armed resistance to English rule. The Fitzgerald family, the Geraldines, led that fight, and the name has carried rich romantic associations ever since. The title of earl stood in succession for several centuries. History therefore offers many a man whom writers call earl of Desmond. Gerald is the one whom story celebrates above the rest. Writers name him Lord Gerald and the Red Earl too. Until recent times the name stayed rare in Ireland. By the late nineteenth century it had won some favour in England. The Modern Irish form Deasúin reads as a retranslation made in a nationalist spirit.

:::
