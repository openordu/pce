``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Dar-thula is James Macpherson's adaptation of the Deirdre story. He printed it in his Poems of Ossian between 1760 and 1763, and the form Darthula appears beside it. The elderly Cairbar, Macpherson's Conchobar, loves Dar-thula. She gives her heart to the youthful Nathos, Macpherson's Noise. Dar-thula, Nathos, and his brothers Althos and Ardan try to flee, but a storm drives them back. Cairbar kills the three brothers, sons of Usnoth. Dar-thula then takes her own life over the corpse of Nathos. Credulous commentators invented a Scottish Gaelic etymology for her name, dart-huile, meaning a woman with fine eyes.

:::
```
