``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The dullahan, also spelled dulachan, dulacaun, dullaghan, or dubhlachan, is a headless phantom of Irish folklore. The Irish word dubh means dark. He rides a headless horse or drives a coach pulled by headless horses. Some accounts make him the Death Coach driver. His face looks like mouldy cheese in colour and texture. Two huge eyes stretch from ear to ear and dart like flies. He can pull this hideous head away at any moment and toss it in ghoulish ball-games. His black horse has flaming eyes and ears cut short. Its head runs six yards or more beyond its body. A flick of his whip strikes out the eyes of watchers. People who open their doors to hear him rumbling past receive basins of blood in the face. Each house where he pauses receives an omen of death. He ranks as a solitary fairy. The dullahan is a masculine and lesser-known form of the Banshee. She is the wailing fairy who predicts death. He brought news of coming death to all who saw him pass. MacKillop cross-references angau, ankou, fardorocha, and gan ceann.

:::
```
