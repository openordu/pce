``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Togail Bruidne Derga is the full name of the tale. The phrase means the destruction of the hostel of Da Derga. The work is an early Irish tale from the Ulster Cycle. It also draws on the King's Cycle and the Mythological Cycle. The Ulster hero Conall Cernach appears inside it. Rudolf Thurneysen places the tale in the oldest layer of Irish tales. Only the Tain Bo Cuailnge runs longer. Da Derga's tale ranks as the most complete Old Irish story after that work. Conaire, the main figure, is a king of Tara in the old genealogies. The tale stays in Leinster, the province called Laigin. Editors still file the work under the Ulster Cycle. Da Derga is an Irish god or hero whose name means "the red god". Some texts give the name Úa Dergae, "the nephew of the red goddess". His hostel serves as the setting for a key Irish story about kingship. As the king of Tara, Conaire, arrives at the hostel on Samhain, he breaks a series of sacred vows. Those vows are called geis. A hag demands entrance to the hostel, and Conaire refuses her. The hag stands on one leg like a crane and curses him. Conaire then develops a thirst that no water in Ireland can quench, and he dies of it. Da Derga makes no move to help him, which implies he agrees with the punishment. The point of the story is the punishment of a king who fails his vows to the earth goddess. If Da Derga is the nephew of the red goddess, he may connect to Medb or Flaith, goddesses of sovereignty. If he is the red god, he may belong to the otherworld, since red is linked to that realm. His character is thin. He plays little role in the tale named for him. Conaire rests at Da Derga's hostel on Samhain. His foster-brothers flee to Britain. They join a one-eyed giant, one eye like a wheel. The exiled band comes back and rings the house. Fire climbs the beams through the night. Conaire resists to the last hour, blood and bone. They cut him down at last. His broken geis exacts his fate. A great thirst seizes him. No drink steadies him. The river shuns his lips. There the severed head thanks the servant for the water. The servant pours water into the gullet of the head. Conall Cernach holds the gate to the end. His arm hangs by a single thread at his side. Da Derga keeps his seat through it all. He does not shift the hand to save the king. 

:::
```
