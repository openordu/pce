``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Daoine sídhe is an Irish term for fairy folk. The name means folk from the mound. MacKillop records the older spellings daoine si and sidh. Reformed Irish now spells the term daoine si. English speech renders the name as Deena Shee. These figures were once deities. The Tuatha Dé Danann dwindled into folktale figures. MacKillop reads the mound link as a sign that the fairy folk descend from the Tuatha Dé Danann. They came to live in ancient barrows. They also lived in hillforts. They used other prehistoric monuments of Ireland. People used many flattering names to win their good will. The fairies could be amoral and mischievous. People called them The Good Folk, daoine maithe. People called them The Gentry, daoine uaisle. The first name means good people and the second noble people. Some simply called them Them. MacKillop places these polite names in Modern Irish speech and in Scottish Gaelic speech alike. The mound was their traditional home.

:::
```
