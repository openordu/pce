``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Dúnchad mac Crináin was an early medieval churchman closely tied to the monastery of Iona, where he held the abbacy. He was a close kinsman of the founder Colum Cille and traced his descent from the northern Uí Néill and its Cenél Conaill lineage. Under his guidance Iona remained the chief intellectual centre promoting the insular side of the Easter controversy, and its influence reached believers in Ireland, Dál Riata, the Pictish kingdom and Anglo-Saxon Northumbria. In 716 Faelchú was set up as co-abbot alongside him, a step that can be read either as a blow to his standing after a controversial stand or as a sign of failing health, since he died in the spring of 717 that followed. His passing evidently left Iona weaker in the eyes of its daughter houses and certain royal patrons.

:::
```
