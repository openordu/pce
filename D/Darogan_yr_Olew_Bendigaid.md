``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Darogan yr Olew Bendigaid is a Welsh political prophecy of the early fifteenth century. The title means the prophecy of the blessed oil. Peniarth 50 preserves its oldest surviving manuscript. A scribe likely copied it at Neath Abbey in South Wales. The writer adapted an English and Latin prophecy about Thomas Becket. Becket died as archbishop of Canterbury in 1170. The oil falls from heaven to anoint the rightful kings of England. The darogan widens that claim to the whole island of Britain. Followers of Joseph of Arimathea bring the sacred oil to the land. Archbishop Dubricius then uses it to consecrate King Arthur. With heavenly support Arthur defeats the giants and crushes the pagan Saxons. The poet probably wrote after the fall of Owain Glyndwr. These details frame the political hopes of the verse.

:::
```
