``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Añjela Duval passed her whole existence on the family smallholding at Traoñ an Dour near Ar C'houerc'had (Le Vieux Marché) in northern Brittany, where she had entered the world in 1905. She did not begin to compose poetry until roughly her mid-fifties, and only after both parents had died. A treasured gift of books and periodicals in 1961 introduced her to most Breton writing since the 1920s, much of it rooted in the Gwalarn school. Duval's verse repeatedly mourns the fading of traditional Breton rural civilisations and the encroaching dominance of French, and she voiced sharp alarm over the language's precarious survival. She never married, and young Breton activists came to serve as her adopted heirs; two of them perished in 1976 while placing a bomb. Her collected poems, among them Kan an Douar and Stourm a ran, secured her reputation as the outstanding 20th-century Breton-language poet.

:::
```
