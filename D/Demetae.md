``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Demetae stood among the five chief tribes of Wales in the age before Rome and under it. Ptolemy the geographer records them in the second century. They held the south-western corner of the land, the ground between the Teifi river and the Tywi river. Their land was largely the same territory that later bore the name Dyfed. Against the power of Rome they seem to have raised hardly any fight. Their homeland became a region of settled pastoral life under the name Demetia. At the end of the fourth century the Déisi came from Ireland and invaded Demetia. Dewi Sant, the patron saint of Wales, gave much of his missionary care to parts of that land. In the end the more numerous Demetae absorbed the Déisi settlers.

:::
```
