``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Dechtire, also written Dechtere, Deichtine, or Dectora, was an Irish heroine or goddess. She was the mother of the hero Cúchulainn, the daughter of the druid Cathbad, and the usual sister of Findchóem. She lived at Emain Macha, the capital of Ulster, with her brother or father, king Concobar mac Nessa. She and her fifty maidens vanished one day without trace. Years later a flock of fifty-one birds appeared around the capital. The birds ate every blade of grass until the fields were barren. The warriors of Ulster hunted the birds in chariots. The birds flew in pairs joined by silver chains, led by one wearing a silver yoke. The hunt ended at the magic mound of Angus Óg on the Boyne. There the men found a splendid house and a wondrous child. Dechtire conceived the boy by swallowing a worm from a glass of wine, or by the god Lugh. In a third version Concobar committed incest with her to father the child. The boy was later called Sétanta. She gave birth and remained a virgin. Ulster's greatest heroes agreed to share the boy's upbringing. In the second version she married Sualtam mac Róich before the child could be delivered. W. B. Yeats shaped his Dectora in The Shadowy Waters on her in part. The dictionary also indexes the variant spellings Dectera and Dectire to this record.

:::
```
