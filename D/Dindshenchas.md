``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Dindshenchas means the lore of high places. This Irish tradition gathers stories that give each landmark its own tale. Place-names mattered to a people bound to their land, for hills marked the borders between territories. Poets had to tell these tales after long years of study. A verse collection built around the eleventh century names its authors, such as Mac Liac and Cinaed ua hArtacáin. Many of its poems close by praising the coming of Christianity.

:::
```
