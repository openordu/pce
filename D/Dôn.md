``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Dôn was a Welsh mythological ancestress, largely unknown in her own right. The gods of Wales carried her name as the Children of Dôn, in Welsh Plant Dôn. Koch 2006 records her as the mother of three central characters of the Middle Welsh tale Math fab Mathonwy, which forms the fourth main tale of the Mabinogi: Arianrhod, Gilfaethwy, and Gwydion. Culhwch ac Olwen names her as the mother of Amaethon mab Don, a supernatural ploughman, and of Gouannon mab Don, a supernatural smith. An early 11th-century Breton Life of Saint Iudic-hael makes the legendary poet Taliesin a son of Dôn, and the genealogies of the Hanesyn Hen tract localize her progeny in north Wales as Plant Don o Arvon. Koch 2006 keeps her separate from the Irish goddess Danu, arguing that the usual equation of Dôn with the Tuatha Dé Danann and with the Danube is phonetically unworkable, since a cognate of Middle Irish Danu in British would necessarily give Welsh forms such as Dyn or Dein. He notes that scholars have doubted how old and genuine Middle Irish Danu truly is, and that another proposed etymology reads Dôn as a cognate of the Old Irish word don, meaning place, ground, or earth, and so as a parallel to the Greek khthōn. Welsh Dôn occurs only as a semantic genitive, usually preceded by merch, daughter, mab, son, or plant, children. Plant Dôn, the Children of the Earth, would then match the children of Llŷr, whose name belongs with the sea, and the Titans of Hesiod, primeval beings born of the earth. Her daughter Arianrhod once stood as a footstool before Math, Dôn's brother and king. Dôn had four sons, and three of them held social roles: the bard Gwydion, the smith Govannon, and the farmer Amaethon. The fourth, Gilfaethwy, was a trickster. The Children of Dôn inherited through the maternal line. Math passed rule to his nephew Gwydion, and Gwydion to Arianrhod's son Lleu Llaw Gyffes.
```
