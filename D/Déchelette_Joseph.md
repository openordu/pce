``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Joseph Déchelette, a pioneering French archaeologist active in the decades around 1900, reshaped understanding of the European Iron Age. His major handbook on prehistoric, Celtic, and Gallo-Roman archaeology wove the era's greatest finds into a fresh original synthesis. He clarified the unbroken progression of cultures that ended with the great late La Tène fortifications built a century before Caesar entered Gaul. He founded the museum bearing his name in Roanne, France, home to a rich trove of Gaulish, Gallo-Roman, and Roman objects. He died while serving in the First World War, in the middle of his finest working years.

:::
```
