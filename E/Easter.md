``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Easter is a Christian feast that reached the Celtic lands. There it stood in for Beltane, the spring festival of returned life. The famous Irish Easter story mixes the two feasts. Saint Patrick lit an Easter fire on Slane hill near Tara. He did this to challenge the high king's fire. Pagan Ireland had no Easter, so such fires must be Beltane. Other spring customs moved to Easter after Christianization. People said the sun danced that day. Holy wells were held more effective for cures then too.

A separate dispute, named the Easter controversy, divided the early churches. It turned on how to fix the date of Easter. The British and Irish churches used a cycle of 84 years. The Roman church used a cycle of 19 years. Saint Patrick followed the Alexandrian 19-year computus. The British reckoning placed the equinox on 25 March. The Roman reckoning used the fairer 21 March. Columbanus wrote to Pope Gregory the Great in 600. Cummian wrote to the abbot of Iona to argue the Roman Easter. He rested the case on the three sacred languages of Hebrew, Greek, and Latin. The scholar Beda backed the Roman computus, which at last won. For the medieval mind, Easter meant the triumph of light over darkness. To date it wrongly was to misread the world the maker built.

:::
```
