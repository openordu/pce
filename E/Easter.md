``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Easter is a Christian feast that reached the Celtic lands, where it stood alongside the older spring festival. In early Ireland and Britain the churches argued for over a century over the date on which to keep it, a dispute known as the Easter controversy. Koch 2006 explains that the Britons and Irish used an older 84-year cycle, while the Roman church used a 19-year cycle. To medieval minds Easter marked the yearly victory of light over darkness, so to date it wrongly was to misunderstand creation. St Patrick followed the Alexandrian computus of the Roman type. Around 600 the Irish monk Columbanus wrote to Pope Gregory defending the insular Easter. In 664 the Synod of Whitby settled the question for Northumbria, which took the Roman reckoning, and the Irish abbot Colmán withdrew to Ireland. Iona came into line in 716, and St Elfoddw changed the reckoning in Wales in 768. In Irish folk custom Easter took over spring rites, so that people said the sun danced on Easter morning and held the holy wells more potent for cures then. Some folk tales even set the great fires of the old season on Easter.

:::
```
