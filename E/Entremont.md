::: magazinestyle
Entremont was a hill-fort, or oppidum, in southern Gaul. It sat on high ground north of Aix-en-Provence. Its site lay in Provence, near modern Aix. Roman forces destroyed Entremont in 122 bc. Aquae Sextiae, present-day Aix, later replaced it. The settlement spread over roughly four hectares. Walls up to four metres high still stand. Towers reinforced the ramparts on the north side. A sanctuary rose in the northwest corner. Its stone pillars carried carved severed heads. These works link to the Celtic head cult. Life-size seated figures were also carved. Builders nailed fifteen skulls to the stonework. Similar finds came from Roquepertuse nearby. The site remains a key record of Iron Age Gallic sculpture.

:::
