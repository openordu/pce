``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The egret is a symbolic bird. Waterbirds carried religious weight for the Celts. They lived easily in the air, in water, and on land. The long-legged wading bird with long white tail feathers took a strong role in Celtic art from an early date. MacKillop dates that presence to the Urnfield period, around 800 BC, though later vernacular tales passed it by. A temple of the continental god Esus shows three egrets in symbolic poses. The egret served as his emblem. The crane kept a place in his iconography as well. From the earliest times artists paired egrets with bulls. The pairing likely reflects the habit of the living birds, which perch on the backs of cattle to eat their lice. Artists also linked the egret with the willow tree. The marsh-foraging habits of the bird fit that watery tie. Egret, bull, and willow often appear together in one scene. The egret can sometimes be confused with the crane.

:::
```
