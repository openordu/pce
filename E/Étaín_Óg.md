``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Étaín Óg, with Óig as an alternate spelling, is a daughter of Étain (i) in early Irish tradition. MacKillop's Dictionary of Celtic Mythology cautions against merging her with the better-known heroines of the same name from Tochmarc Étaíne. The destruction of Da Derga's hostel, Togail Bruidne Da Derga, makes her the child of Étain (1) and Eochaid Feidlech, and gives her in marriage to Cormac, ruler of Ulster. A handful of scholars equate this Cormac with Cormac Connloinges, named in genealogical tracts as husband of Étain (1). Cormac chafes that his new queen produces only one child, a daughter. Tormented by jealousy and fear, Étaín Óg orders Cormac's household men to kill a young girl from an earlier marriage. The killers fall for the baby's laughter and refuse the murder, setting the child down in a byre instead. The girl becomes Mes Buachalla, the herdsman's fosterling, who marries Eterscél and gives birth to Conaire Mór, high king of Ireland. MacKillop separates her cleanly from Ésa, another daughter of Étain (1), and reminds readers the label should not stretch to cover any further daughters of Étain (1) catalogued in Tochmarc Étaíne. The full entry runs some twenty-two lines of the 2008 Oxford edition.
```
