::: magazinestyle
earwig is the common English name for the small insect named from Old English ears and wicga. The creature carries pincer-like appendages that protrude from its abdomen. Folk belief once held that the earwig could penetrate a person's head through the ear. In Irish folklore the insect was seen as a disguise of the devil. Custom required that earwigs be killed as soon as anyone saw one. James Joyce drew on earwig lore when he built the figure of HCE in Finnegans Wake (1939). The insect bears its own name in each Celtic language. Modern Irish calls it gaillseach. Scottish Gaelic calls it fiolan. Manx calls it gollage. Welsh calls it chwilen glust. Cornish calls it gor-losten. Breton calls it garlostenn.

:::
