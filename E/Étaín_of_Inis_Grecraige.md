``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Étaín of Inis Grecraige appears in MacKillop's Dictionary of Celtic Mythology as a fairy mistress attached to Eógan Mór, the Munster king. She dwells on Inis Grecraige, an island modern scholarship fixes as Beare Island off Bantry Bay. When Eógan suffers his first defeat at Conn Cétchathach's hands, near Carn Buidhe by the mouth of the Roughty River outside Kenmare, Étaín spirits the king and his warriors away by ship into the open sea. Eógan later makes his way back to the mainland and tries the fight again, but loses a second time. He then retreats to his lover's island refuge. The story ends with the king leaving Étaín's haven and sailing on toward Spain. MacKillop's entry spans some eleven lines of the 2008 Oxford edition, sitting at the seam between heroic saga and the older fairy-mistress type.

:::
```
