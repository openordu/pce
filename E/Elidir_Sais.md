``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Elidir Sais was a twelfth-century Welsh poet who served the princes of Wales. He was active by 1195 and remained living near 1246. He was a native of Anglesey. Gwalchmai ap Meilyr, a court poet, may have been his father. His epithet Sais names an Englishman, but its cause is unknown. Exile in England or skill in the English tongue could explain it. He is remembered mostly for religious verse. Two of his poems are prayers that beg for deliverance. A homiletic piece pairs the bounty of God with the wrongs of humankind. Another calls for penance through Holy Week. He mourned how Jerusalem fell to Saladin in 1187 as a burden sent by God. His elegy for Rhodri, brother of the man who lost Gwynedd, stays reverent. In a dadolwch he begs Llywelyn ab Iorwerth to face the English and not to raid his neighbours.

:::
```
