``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Ériu is one of three divine sisters who personify Ireland itself, alongside her two kin Banba and Fódla. The trio appear together in the Lebor Gabála as eponymous tutelary goddesses. Any one of their names could be lifted up as the island's title. The figure wears a circlet or ring. Scholars read the emblem as a solar or lunar sign. The very word Ériu carries the root sense of fertile ground. When the Milesians come to take the island, Ériu meets them at the hill of Uisnech. She tells them Ireland is the fairest land beneath the sun. She flatters the newcomers as the finest folk the world has ever seen. One of their leaders, Donn mac Miled, insults her. Ériu answers back: he and his descendants will never enjoy this island. Donn later drowns. The Milesian poet Amairgin then pledges on her behalf that the land shall take her name. The spelling Éire is the modern Irish form of Ériu. Erin is its anglicized cognate. Éri is recorded as a one-line variant spelling of her name. Banba and Fódla survive only as poetic names for the same island. Ériu's father is usually Delbáeth, her mother Emmas or Eimin, her foster-father Codal. She is the wife of Mac Gréine. Through him or through Elatha mac Delbaith she mothers Bres. In Baile in Scáil she is the sovereignty consort of Lug Lámfhota. She dies at the Battle of Tailtiu by the hand of Suiige. At that battle the Milesians kill every king and queen of the defeated Tuatha Dé Danann. The earlier goddess of the land and the later figure of Ériu who bears Bres can be the same person across variant tellings.

:::
```
