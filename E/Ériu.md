``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Ériu is the name the Old Irish gave to Ireland, and the same word also names a goddess. Modern Irish writes the country name Éire. The spelling Ériu stayed common in Middle Irish sources of about 900 to 1200. Koch 2006 traces the name to Primitive Irish *Iweri~, which derives from an Indo-European form meaning the fertile land, a cognate of a Greek word for rich or fat applied to land. The related people-name, set down centuries earlier, was Iverni or Hierni, and it survives in Érainn, the name of early tribal groups. The same root lies behind two ancestor names in legendary history, Iär and Íth. In Lebor Gabála Érenn, Íth is the first among Míl Espáine's people to see Ireland from Spain, and the first to die there. The Old Irish common noun íriu means earth or land. In early texts the goddess Ériu gives her name to the island, and the Tuatha Dé Danann number among the peoples linked with her. Sagas such as Togail Bruidne Da Derga recall that prehistoric Érainn kings once ruled from Tara. St Patrick used the form Hiberionaci for the Irish.

:::
```
