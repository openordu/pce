``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Ériu is the Irish goddess who gave her island its name. In Old Irish the word Ériu names Ireland itself, and out of it grows modern Éire. The root carries the sense of a fertile and rich land, the same word that lives in the old noun for earth. Her people, the Tuatha Dé Danann, pushed the Fomorians off that island. She is not a distant queen. In one telling she is already married, and she still yields to a man whose hair and cloak are the colour of gold thread, a story of the land giving its name to a new people. Other versions place Eithne in that same role. The tale of Íth follows the same shape. Íth, a kinsman of the household of Míl, is the first of his folk to spy Ireland from the sea. He is the first to set foot ashore, and he praises the land to those who greet him, naming mast, honey, wheat, and fish among its abundance. So the goddess and the ancestor both bend on a single truth: the island is fertile and the name of the island enshrines that fact.

:::
```
