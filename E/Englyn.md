``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The englyn is a Welsh strict-metre stanza. Eight kinds count among the traditional twenty-four strict metres. Every kind carries obligatory cynghanedd, the steady system of line-internal sound correspondence. Two kinds have only three lines. The englyn milwr and the englyn penfyr mark the saga, the gnomic, and the nature verse of the ninth and tenth centuries. The four-line englyn unodl union became the common form from the twelfth century. It runs to the syllable count ten, six, seven, seven. The first two lines form the paladr. The last two form the esgyll, which rhyme like a cywydd couplet. Three further kinds rest on proest, an assonantal rhyme. From the earliest period the englynion ran in linked series. The single englyn also served as an epigram and stayed popular, for the folk poets of Wales drew on it from the eighteenth century on. It remains the mainstay of strict-metre verse in Wales.

:::
```
