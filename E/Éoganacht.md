``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Éoganacht formed a powerful historic dynasty, or rather a federation of related royal lines, who held the kingship of Munster almost without challenge between the 5th and 10th centuries. Koch 2006 notes their power went practically unchallenged until the rise of Mathgamain mac Cennétig of the Dál gCais in 964. Their shared identity rested on the teaching that all septs descended from Conall Corc, the legendary founder of the royal seat at Caisel Muman, who flourished around 400. They traced their line to Mug Nuadat, whose name means servant of the god Nuadu. The dynasty takes its name from Éogan Már, grandson of Mug, and the name Éogan is linked with the Celtic term for yew, pointing to an old name meaning born of the yew. The story of how Conall Corc founded Caisel tells how a vision revealed that the royal seat should stand where a yew grew out of a stone. The three central septs, Éoganacht Chaisil, Glendamnach, and Áine, supplied most of the kings of Munster, and across the 7th and 8th centuries the crown rotated among them. Outlying septs included Éoganacht Locha Léin and Éoganacht Raithlinn.

:::
```
