``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
A band of Welshmen living in London founded a literary society in 1770. It also served some charitable aims. They called it the Gwyneddigion. Owen Jones, known as Owain Myfyr, stood among the founders. John Edwards, called Siôn Ceiriog, and Robert Jones, called Robin Ddu yr Ail o Fôn, joined him. Its name points to the north Welsh region and former principality of Gwynedd. The society became a focus of Welsh literary life in London. It drew a more ordinary audience than the Honourable Society of Cymmrodorion. Members Owen Jones and William Owen Pughe preserved and published early Welsh texts. They produced the Myvyrian Archaiology of Wales (1801-7). The Gwyneddigion revived the eisteddfod, which had declined since medieval times. In 1789 Thomas Jones of Corwen sought the society's patronage for a local eisteddfod. The society laid down strict rules to give such events order. It supported the Bala eisteddfod of September 1789. The Gwyneddigion served as adjudicators of poetry in the strict metres. These events became known as Eisteddfodau'r Gwyneddigion. Their eisteddfodau lost momentum when Thomas Jones left Wales in 1795.

:::
```
