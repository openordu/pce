::: magazinestyle
Ellan Vannin is the Manx name for the island of Man in the Irish Sea. The island sits in the middle of the Irish Sea, roughly 43 km east of Ireland. It stands as a self-governing Crown dependency, apart from the UK and Great Britain. Castletown held the role of ancient capital until Douglas took it in the 1870s. A chain of mountains crosses from north-east to south-west, led by Snaefell. The central valley from Douglas to Peel splits the land into a north-side and a south-side. The Manx people keep a strong Celtic and Gaelic heritage. About a quarter of the island's carved crosses carry early Goidelic ogam inscriptions. Small chapels, called keeills, mark the early medieval church. Vikings arrived late in the eighth century and shaped Manx culture deeply. Tynwald, the annual open-air assembly, counts as the world's oldest parliament in continuous use. The Laxey Wheel, raised in 1854, ranks as the largest working water-wheel on Earth. Ellan Vannin holds a distinct role among the Celtic lands.

:::
