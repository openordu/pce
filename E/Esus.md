``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Esus, also written Aesus, is a Gaulish god. His name appears in a number of compound personal names, but its derivation stays uncertain. Koch 2006 records him in the Roman authors Lucan and Lactantius. Lucan names him among gods honoured with human sacrifice, and commentators on Lucan say his victims were stabbed and hung from trees to bleed to death. The inscribed name ESVS appears on the great stone monument of the Nautae Parisiaci, the shipmen of the Parisi, the tribe that gave Paris its name. There he is shown as a bearded man in an artisan's clothes, standing by a tree that he holds, and beside him is a bull with three cranes called Tarvos Trigaranus. A relief from Trier shows a beardless figure, identified as Mercurius, felling a tree in which a bull's head and three birds are visible. Other sources tie Esus to Mercury and Mars, and to the service of merchants and warriors. The Breton town of Essé and the people Esuvii carry his name.

:::
```
