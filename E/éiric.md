``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
éiric, in MacKillop's Dictionary of Celtic Mythology, is a modernized cross-reference to the Old Irish term éric. The headword éric, éraic designates a recompense paid under the early Irish legal system, most often in the wake of a killing. A victim's kin could demand a payment in cattle or silver, scaled to the rank of killer or victim, or could instead press for the killer to forfeit his life. The modern Irish word éiric, derived from this older sense, has spread to cover reparation, retribution, compensation, and reward across non-homicidal wrongs. MacKillop uses éiric as a lexical bridge between the early legal usage and the broader modern sense of compensation.

:::
```
