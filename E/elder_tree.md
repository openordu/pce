``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The elder tree is a symbolic tree of the genus Sambucus, also called the elderberry. It carries white flower clusters and berries that ripen red or blackish. In most Celtic lands people considered it unlucky to burn elder wood. Fertility spirits, they held, lived within the elder tree. Anyone forced to use elder wood had to ask the tree for its permission first. Branches might bleed even then. Storytellers across Celtic countries linked the elder tree with the fairy world over recent centuries. On the Isle of Man elder grows in great abundance. People there call the elder tree tramman and treat it as the fairy tree. People in Ireland held that fairies or demons haunted many individual elder trees. The name links the elder to the elf people. In northern Britain people called it the eller tree and offered it rags for good luck. Old English ellaem is the recorded root of the name. Old Irish gave tromm and modern Irish trom. Scottish Gaelic says troman and droman. Welsh gives ysgawen, Cornish scawen, and Breton skavenn.

:::
```
