``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Étan appears as the personal name of several women in early Irish literature. The most prominent is Étan, the wife of Ogma, the god of eloquence. She is the daughter of Dian Cécht, who heals among the gods, and she herself stands as a patron of crafts. Most traditions name her as the mother of Tuireann, and some add the satirist Cairbre mac Ethne to her children. A second Étan is the lover of Cúchulainn, who gives her a ring after their lovemaking. A third Étan belongs to the Fianna Éireann. The name has been confused with Étain in later tradition.

:::
```
