``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The evil eye is a harmful power carried in a gaze. People across the world believe a simple look can cast spells and cause harm. A spiteful person could curse another with a single glance. Covetous people could take an object with one sharp look. Writers have traced this belief in every Celtic country. The oldest Irish notices appear in the Ancient Laws of Ireland. The Annals of Connacht record cases for 1466 and 1467. Squints and very dark eyes often marked the carrier of the evil eye. People also blamed it on children conceived before their parents married. Witches and other cruel persons could wield the power. Yet some innocent people carried the evil eye without wanting it. One story says a woman aimed it at Conaire Mór. Squinting, seen in the nearsighted, was sometimes read as a curse. These guards stayed used in rural Ireland through the 1800s. People relied on spoken charms and protective gestures. Scots kept full-bladdered cows from the barn and tied red thread. Irish names are súil mhillte and drochshúil. Scottish Gaelic says droch shùil. Manx gives drogh hooil. This folk fear links to Balor of the Evil Eye. See also EYE.

:::
```
