``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Étaín is the personal name MacKillop uses to gather three related heroines of early Irish tradition under one heading. The MacKillop Dictionary of Celtic Mythology entry for Étaín runs as a single line of see-references, pointing the reader to the three distinct characters, the fair-haired daughter, the Inis Grecraige daughter, and the daughter known by the Óig suffix, alongside the Etan family. MacKillop's own notation (1-3) marks the three Étaíns as a numbered set. The first is the wife of Eochaid Airem and mother of Étaín Óg, the central figure of Tochmarc Étaíne, whose jealousy-driven exile and rebirth drive the longest of the Tochmarc episodes. The second is the same woman in her later life as wife of Eochaid Feidlech and mother of the Mes Buachalla line. The third is the daughter of Ailill of the routing host and a faery woman, raised as a foster-child of the Tuatha Dé Danann. Fholtfhind marks the fair-haired member of the family. The Inis Grecraige entry refers to a daughter belonging to the king of that island, herself a figure in her own right. Étaín Óg, with Óig as a variant, is the daughter of Étain (i) whose story closes the Da Derga hostel cycle. MacKillop advises readers not to flatten the three figures into one, since the texts treat them as discrete characters and the names diverge in the genealogical tracts. The signpost points the researcher to the four PCE nodes that handle each figure: Étaín Echraide, Étaín Fholtfind, Étaín of Inis Grecraige, and Étaín Óg.

:::
```
