``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Epona is the horse goddess of the Celtic world and of Britain. Celtic gods usually belong to one region alone, so her wide reach stands out. Her images and altars survive across the Continent and in Britain, in greater number than for any peer of her pantheon. Her name comes from the old Gaulish word for horse, and the same root lives on in the Irish word for horse and in a Gallic month name. She rides a horse as a mare beside her, and through the Aedui folk she appears with a foal. On the Roman lines a more enthroned version took hold, a sign both of her strength and of her rank as a treasured horse of the army. The legions carried her across the sea and back to Rome, where she won what no other Celtic god had: a fixed feast day in the calendar, set on a day mid-December. A gravestone of a centurion names her among the gods he asks. At Alesia, an old Gallic town, a bronze plaque on a donkey-cart wrote the vow to the goddess.

:::
```
