``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Emain Macha, today called Navan Fort, is a royal hill and the old capital of Ulster. The name keeps the goddess Macha. She was a goddess of rule and birth. A king forced her to run a great race. She gave birth at the end of it and died. Her last words warned that the men of Ulster would be weak when they needed strength. The hill has a long record. Near it stand the ring site of Haughey's Fort and the King's Stables. Both come from about one thousand years before the common era. The King's Stables held a ritual pool. Its mud kept an animal body and the front of a skull of a young man. By the year four hundred BC the people raised timber houses on the hill top. They built a pair of round huts joined to a ring of earth. A skull of a Barbary ape points to a gift that came from north Africa. About one hundred BC the people raised a vast ring of oak. Two hundred and sixty-nine posts stood in a circle that was forty metres across. A study of tree rings dates the main post to the year ninety-five BC. Then the people filled the ring with stone and burned the outside timbers. They capped the whole with an earthen mound five metres high. Below the mound the lake Loughnashade yielded four bronze horns in the Later manner. The name of Macha still marks the ground, and the seat of Armagh lies near.

:::
```
