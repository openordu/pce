``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Emain Macha, the site now called Navan Fort, is the legendary capital of Ulaid and the seat of King Conchobar and the heroes of the Ulster Cycle in early Irish tradition. Koch 2006 identifies it with Navan Fort, some 2.6 km west of Armagh. The fort is the chief monument in a complex of sites running from the Neolithic into the early medieval period. The wider complex holds Haughey's Fort, Loch na Séad, also called Loughnashade, and the King's Stables. Scholars have variously identified the site with the Isamnion or the northern Regia of Ptolemy's map of Ireland. Archaeology shows the assembly centre saw its peak of activity and then a ritual destruction around 95 or 94 bc. The name is explained as the twins of Macha, for the goddess Macha, who in the legend ran a race against the king's horses while pregnant, gave birth at the finish, and died, cursing the men of Ulster to weakness in their hour of greatest need. Few scholars today would claim that Cú Chulainn or Conchobar reflect actual figures at Navan.

:::
```
