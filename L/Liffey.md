``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Liffey, also called Life, is an Irish heroine and a river goddess. She is the spirit of the River Liffey. She also rules the plain of Mag Lífe through which the river runs. The Dindshenchas, the place-name poems of Ireland, tell of her death. She died in the birth of her child in Aran. Her husband died of grief soon after. The river took her name and kept it. MacKillop calls the Liffey a fifty-mile river of eastern Ireland. It rises in County Wicklow. It flows through Kildare on its way to the sea. It reaches Dublin harbour and the Irish Sea. The river gave its name to the surrounding plain. That plain, Mag Lífe, was a seat of early kings. The river has a long life in art. James Joyce personified it in Finnegans Wake. He gave the river the voice of Anna Liffey. Joyce built the name from Abha na Life, the Irish words for the River Life. His character Anna Livia Plurabelle is the same river turned to a woman. The Liffey still runs through the city to the sea. It carries the memory of a goddess who died for love of her child and her lord, and whose name was given to the passing flood. The river's course, from the Wicklow hills to the harbour, cuts through the heart of the plain that the goddess once ruled. The goddess of the flood has never left the city by which she still runs.

:::
```
