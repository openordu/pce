``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Lough Ree is a wide lake in the Irish midlands, fed by the River Shannon. The river flows into it just above the town of Athlone. Lough Ree spreads across the counties of Roscommon, Longford, and Westmeath. It runs for about sixteen miles, and its width varies from one to seven miles. A legend explains how the lake came into being. The tale repeats the origin story of Lough Neagh with new actors. Eochu mac Maireda, also called Ecca, loved his stepmother Ébliu. He allied with his brother Rib to found a new kingdom. The god Midir slew their horses as they travelled. Midir then gave them a fresh horse brought from the otherworld. When that horse urinated on the ground, the waters swelled and shaped Lough Ree. Rib, written Ribh in modern Irish, may have given the lake its name. A second legend makes Lough Ree the scene of a queen's death. Medb, the queen and goddess, died on an island in this lake. Her nephew Furbaide Ferbend struck the killing blow as revenge. Medb had earlier slain her own sister Clothra, the mother of Furbaide. His deed answered that earlier crime. Old tellings said Lough Ree concealed two strange things in its waters. The first was a monster. A medieval life of Saint Mochuda reports that this beast once ate a man. Witnesses claimed they saw it in the water as late as 1960. The second hidden thing was a sunken city. A bishop heard that a cathedral stood inside that city. He set out to visit it and never returned. The sunken city stayed lost in the dark shallows of Lough Ree. The lake still carries boats and trade on the Shannon today, and its waters remain a meeting point of the surrounding lands.

:::
```
