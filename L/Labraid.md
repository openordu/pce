::: magazinestyle
Labraid, also written Labhraidh, Labhra, Labra, and Leabhra, is a name carried by many legendary figures in early Ireland and by some churchmen. The best-known bearer is Labraid Loingsech, the seafarer, also called Labraid Móen, the speechless one. Later tradition gave Labraid the shameful secret of horses' ears, a blemish he shared with the Welsh March ap Meirchion and with King Midas. He put to death every barber who cut his hair until one young barber was spared at his mother's request. The youth sickened under the weight of the secret and, on a druid's counsel, whispered it to a tree. The tree was later felled and carved into a harp; when the harper Craiphtine played, the instrument gave the secret away. Moved by remorse, Labraid then confessed his defect openly. The writer Pádraic Colum made Labraid the hero of the juvenile tale The Story of Lowry Maen.

:::
