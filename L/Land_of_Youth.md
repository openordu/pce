``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Land of Youth is a name for the Celtic Otherworld. The source spells it as Tir Na Nog, the land of youth. The title points to the lasting beauty of the folk who live there. In Irish tradition this stands as the most celebrated of the otherworld realms. The plain that bears it is a pleasant and Elysian spot, not a land of the dead. The immortal Tuatha De Danann are said to live once more beneath the earth. A human who enters their world meets the splendid realm known as the Land of Youth. Several famed heroes reach it in the stories. The best known is Oisin, who goes there with the beautiful Niamh. He stays for three hundred years, and the days pass like moments. His return to Ireland brings back all his age at once. MacKillop links the Land of Youth with the Tír Tairngire, the land of promise, and with Mag Mell, the pleasant plain. Emain Ablach, the fortress of apples, also stands near in the same map. The three idylls fill the otherworld geography of the immortal folk. The immrama, the Irish voyage tales, carry men across the same western sea to lands of wonder. MacKillop places the Land of Women in the same region, a place held sway by heroines. Connla's well may lie beneath the sea, in the Land of Youth, or in the Land of Promise. The Scottish Gaelic form Eilean na hOige names an island of youth. In modern use that name may also fall on the isle of Eriskay.
```
