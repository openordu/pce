``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Lake settlement is the prehistoric way of life in which people built their homes on the edges of lakes, a practice known across the Celtic world and wider Europe. In western Europe, Mesolithic hunter-gatherers chose such rich shorelines for food, as at Star Carr in Yorkshire and Friesack near Potsdam, sites more than 10,000 years old. From about 4000 bc, the Alpine region saw great numbers of vertical piles driven into shallow water to carry platforms and timber villages, as at Hauterive-Champréveyres on the Alpine lakeshore and Hornstaad on the Bodensee. In Ireland and Britain, lake settlement grew in the Late Bronze Age from about 1200 bc. The type termed crannóg was a largely artificial island made of timber, stone, and peat, built high above the water with a defended edge. In early medieval times crannogs were common in Ireland and Scotland, with about 1200 known in Ireland alone.

:::
```
