``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Larzac is a small village in southern France. Its fuller name is l'Hospitalet-du-Larzac. A Gallo-Roman burial ground lies nearby. Two lead tablets came from this spot. Workers found them in 1983 on top of a funerary urn. They date to the later first century ad. The tablets bear text in Roman cursive script. The language is Gaulish. Both faces carry writing. This makes the Larzac inscription the longest known text in Gaulish. Some parts of the tablet are broken off. Scholars still read many words with care. Kinship terms stand out, including words for mother and daughter. Most experts think the text describes the magic of a group of women sorcerers. The goddess Adsagsona appears in the spell. The clauses name several women and their fathers. This text is a rare and precious record of the Gaulish language.

:::
```
