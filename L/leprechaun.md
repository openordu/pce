``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The leprechaun stands as the best known spirit of Irish fairy lore. People picture him as a small man dressed in green. He guards a pot of gold at the end of the rainbow. He is a solitary fairy of Ireland, unlike the Trooping Fairies. Those cheerful fairies dance, sing, and lead travelers astray. A captor must hold him in full view at every moment. His old trick is to shout that a monster approaches. The keeper looks away, and the sprite slips off. The name comes from the Gaelic for maker of shoes. He works as a shoemaker, and often a tailor. He is hard working but works only for himself. He turns surly when crossed, so people remain polite. Some link him to the god Lugh, the little stooped Lugh. MacKillop lists older spelling forms for the leprechaun headword. The printed forms include leprecaun and leipreachân. A third anglicised spelling sits grouped near them. A bracketed cross-reference opens the entry. Captors, storytellers, and scholars each rely on these variant spellings to track the same fairy across centuries.

:::
```
