``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Lough Gur is a small lake in County Limerick, Ireland. It lies 2.5 miles north-north-east of Bruff. Irish tradition prizes the many neolithic remains found on its shores and in its depths. The pale limestone soil drew early farmers to the spot. People have settled beside the lake for nearly six thousand years. Low hills ring it, each tied to a goddess or a god, such as the goddess Aine and the god Donn Firinne. The lake holds the largest stone circle still standing in Ireland, called the Grange. Legend says that Gerald, Earl of Desmond, vanished here in 1398. He rides the moonlight again once each seven years. One doorway to Tir na nOg, the Land of Youth, is a cave that opens on Rockadoon Island within the lake. So people view Lough Gur as an entrance to the Otherworld, a gate between this world and the realm of the fairies. Its legends follow a seven-year cycle. The water withdraws, and a tree rises from the lake bed with its branches under a green cloth. A woman named Toice Bhrean knits below. The fairy queen Aine and her enchanted son Geroid Iarla appear at the end of each cycle. The lake also gives its name to Mary Carbery's memoir The Farm by Lough Gur, which appeared in 1937.

:::
```
