``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Lepontic is the oldest attested Celtic language. Its short inscriptions come from northern Italy around the lake region. Scribes wrote them in the Etruscoid script. Scholars debate how it fits the Celtic family. Some view Lepontic as a dialect of Cisalpine Gaulish, older and set apart by geography. Others treat it as a separate tongue. The second view leans on shared sound changes. In Lepontic, m and n drop before the stops p, t, and k, while nd becomes nn. Very few verbs survive. Only three early clauses show a full verb. One order of words matches the pattern rebuilt for Proto-Celtic. Lepontic also keeps some old sounds. It preserves the diphthong ei at word ends and keeps the kw sound in several words. Its grammar names a father with several suffixes. These fragments are few, yet they matter a great deal for the study of the Continental Celtic languages.

:::
```
