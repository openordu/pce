::: magazinestyle
Llywarch ap Llywelyn served as a court poet of Gwynedd in the twelfth century. He is best known by his nickname Prydydd y Moch, meaning the Poet of the Swine. He was the chief poet of Llywelyn ab Iorwerth, the ruler often called Llywelyn the Great. He counts as one of the leading Poets of the Nobility, the Gogynfeirdd, who upheld the bardic order in Wales. His praise poetry was later gathered into manuscripts, and similar verse survives from his era. Cynddelw Brydydd Mawr shared the same professional world. The little we know of Llywarch's family suggests modest beginnings. His earliest poems honour the sons of Owain Gwynedd as they struggled for power in the north. After Llywelyn came to power, Llywarch praised his deeds and tied new conquests to older heroes. One later verse addresses the hot iron of trial by ordeal and begs it to clear the poet's name.

:::
