``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Lord of the Isles translates a title carried by rulers of the Hebrides. Somerled began that line of rulers and died in 1164. His successors held the lordship into the fifteenth century. Somerled crushed the Norse in battle during 1156. He then took the Gaelic title Ri Innse Gall. That phrase means king of the Hebrides. Latin usage rendered the title as Dominus Insularum by 1354. English adopted the form Lord of the Isles from that Latin rendering. A treaty struck with Edward IV of England first stripped the title away. It came back in 1476. The loss became permanent in 1493. Sir Walter Scott published his six-canto poem The Lord of the Isles in 1815. That poem sets its action in the age of Robert the Bruce.

:::
```
