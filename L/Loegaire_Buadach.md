``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Lóegaire Búadach is a hero of the Ulster Cycle. His name carries the forms Lóegaire Bern Búadach and Buadhach, and the Irish adjective behind it means victorious or triumphant. The dictionary counts him among the three leading contestants for the champion's portion, the curadmir, alongside Cúchulainn and Conall Cearnach in the tale Fled Bricrenn, Bricriu's Feast.

His name is often cited in passing, but the most important text about him comes from the eleventh century. It tells how the poet Áed faced drowning as punishment for his adultery with Mugain, the wife of king Concobar. Anger drove Lóegaire from his house to rescue the poet. Dashing out, he cracked the crown of his skull against a door lintel. The blow killed him, yet not before he cut down thirty executioners and left Áed alive.

Two further traditions attach to him. His mantle counts among the Three Treasures of Ireland. And under his cult title Lóegaire Bern Búadach, an identity treated almost separately, he serves as the ancestor-deity of the Osraige, the people of Ossory, and was thought to own a famous sword.

:::
```
