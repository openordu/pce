``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Lugaid Laígde is the son of Dáire who once lay with the lady of Sovereignty. His name appears in the forms Loigde, Lóigde, Láigde, and Laoighdhe. Dáire gave the name Lugaid to each of his sons, usually seven and at times five, because a prophecy promised that a son of his bearing that name would rule Ireland. Some texts attach the patronymic mac Dáire to this Lugaid. Like almost every hero of the name, he stands as an ancestor of the Érainn of Munster. A druid told Dáire that his successor as king would need to make his catch a fawn of gold that would walk into the assembly. During the hunt for that fawn, a mist with magic in it came down between the brothers and the rest of the hunters. Only Lugaid Laígde made the capture. Soon after, an ugly sorceress met him and put warmth, food, and ale before him, then asked him to share her bed. At first he said no. She answered that by refusing he had stripped himself of Sovereignty. He changed his mind, and with the morning she had turned into Sovereignty herself, now a beautiful maiden. Medieval literature carries this sovereignty story far and wide. Modern commentators judge that the retelling here follows the version told of Niall Noigiallach more than any other. Other records credit Lugaid Laígde with killing Daui Ladrach to gain the kingship, and state that Áed Ruad later killed him.
```
