``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Lugaid ranks seventh among the personal names of early Ireland. That is the figure commentators give for its frequency. Recorded forms include Lughaid, Lui, Luga, and Lewy. The Irish noun lug gives the name its root, and that word carries the sense of light. Legendary warriors, forebears celebrated in genealogies, and saints all carried it. Where many bearers share one name, their stories tend to borrow from each other, and the Lugaids follow that pattern. The usurper Lugaid mac Con stands as the most seminal figure of the group. Motifs of incest and triplism run through the tales told of them. One early tradition describes Lugaid I as a warrior-poet whose judgments stayed accurate despite his blindness. When Ailill learned of the adultery between his wife Medb and Fergus mac Roich, he ordered this Lugaid to kill Fergus, and the deed was done during the lovers' swim. A second sense treats Lugaid as a masculine variant form of the feminine name Lugach, daughter of Fionn mac Cumhaill.

:::
```
