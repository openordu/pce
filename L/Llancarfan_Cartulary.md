``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Llancarfan Cartulary is a series of fourteen Latin charters set down alongside the Life of St Cadoc, the account written by Lifris of Llancarfan around 1100. The charters record plots of land given to churches across south Wales and carry early Brythonic proper names, most strikingly among their lists of both clerical and lay witnesses. As the historian Wendy Davies established, the charter texts themselves go back to the seventh and eighth centuries, even though the surviving copies never updated the old spellings. Charters 55 and 56 stand out as the earliest datable Welsh charters, fixed around the year 680 and tied to the monastery and royal courts of Glamorgan. Their witness lists share many names with the larger body of charters gathered in the Book of Llandaff. The cartulary thus offers valuable evidence for the speech and the literacy of Wales before the start of the Old Welsh period about the year 800. It shows vowel changes at work, as a name like that of the resurrection man carries an old long vowel shifting toward a diphthong, and witness names keep the old Celtic adjective suffix instead of the later common Welsh form. Alongside genuine grants stand likely forged items, charters 57 and 58 presenting Cadoc himself with his Irish companions as witnesses. The collection matters mainly as early Welsh charter evidence and as a window into book-keeping at the monastery of Llancarfan.

:::
```
