::: magazinestyle
The Loch Ness Monster carries the names Orm and Nessy in legendary material. Its history as a water beast is far older than its modern tourist fame. Adomnán's life of Colum Cille, written around 690, tells how the saint ordered a monster with open jaws in the River Ness to dive down and stop hunting a swimmer. The beast obeyed and the man lived. Commentators link the story to the wider water-beast tradition that also produced the afanc. Popular books on the creature include works by Holiday and by Mackal.

:::
