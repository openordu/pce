``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Lugos, also written Lugus or Lugoves, stands among the gods of the continental Celts and of Britain. He is the same god who appears in Ireland as Lugh and in Wales as Lleu Llaw Gyffes. The Gaulish people spoke of a trio of gods called the Lugoves. Some took Lugos for a sun god, but he is better seen as a patron of crafts and skills. The Romans linked him with their own god Mercury, and the link fits because the Celts were famed for their handiwork. Caesar called Mercury the inventor of all arts, a title that echoes the Irish name Samildánach for Lugh. The proof of Lugos appears in stone. Inscriptions found at Avenches in Switzerland carried his name. His name also hides inside the name of the Roman town Lugudunum, which grew into the modern city of Lyon. Scholars trace his name in Laon, Leiden, Loudon, and Liegnitz. The town of Luguvalium, ancestor of modern Carlisle, also keeps a trace of Lugos. Far to the west his worship reached Britain, where he stands as the counterpart of the Irish Lug and the Welsh Lleu Llaw Gyffes. Some writers offered another name for the same god, calling him Erriapus, as a possible title for Mercury. His broad reach across the Celtic lands made Lugos one of the best attested deities of the ancient Celts.

:::
```
