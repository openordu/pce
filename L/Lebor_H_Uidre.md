``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Lebor na h-Uidre is the oldest Irish codex written wholly in Gaelic. Its common title means the Book of the Dun Cow. Three scribes built it, whom Best labelled A, H, and M. The main hand M may be that of Maoilmhuire, a monk tied to Clonmacnoise. The date and place of writing stay under debate. Some texts inside far predate the manuscript itself. The codex mixes religious, historical, and romantic matter. It preserves the earliest forms of many famous Irish tales. These include Táin Bó Cuailnge and other Ulster Cycle stories. Early voyage tales such as Immram Mael Dúin appear as well. It also holds the older poem Amrae Choluimb Chille. A legend ties the book to Saint Ciarán. The story speaks of a remarkable dun cow called Odhar. That cow, so the tale says, fed the saint and his twelve bishops. The folktale shows the high regard its clerics held.  MacKillop dates the codex to before about 1106 and calls it the oldest of the major Irish books. He pairs it with the Book of Leinster as a chief manuscript keeping old stories.

:::
```
