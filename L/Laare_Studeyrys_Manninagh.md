::: magazinestyle
The Laare-studeyrys Manninagh, which means the Centre for Manx Studies, began in 1992 as an academic unit of the University of Liverpool under its founding director Dr Peter Davey, a Reader in Archaeology. Its home is Douglas, a town on the Manx island that locals call Ellan Vannin. A committee brings together representatives of three bodies: the Manx Department of Education, Manx National Heritage, and the University of Liverpool. Its leading early project was a multi volume history of the Manx island, brought out by Liverpool University Press, having its opening volume on the modern era out in 2000. The centre was chiefly a research and postgraduate teaching body, granting postgraduate diplomas and an MA in Manx studies and overseeing research students. It stood too as a central point of contact for all Manx scholars, keeping a research register and sharing bibliographies. Its effort spanned Manx archaeology, culture, environment, and history. Projects took in a landscape survey of the Meayll peninsula, reports on the digs at Peel Castle and Rushen Abbey, a sociolinguistic survey of spoken English, and above all the running of the Manx Place name Survey, whose six of the seven volumes had appeared by 2004.

:::
