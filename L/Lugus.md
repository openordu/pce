``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The deity Lugus is attested across Spain and Gaul in the ancient world. His name descends from an inherited Proto-Celtic form. That same form gave rise to Old Irish Lug, the figure later spelled Lugh, and to the Welsh Lleu of the Mabinogi. Lug leads the Tuath De in the Mythological Cycle and is the chief supernatural being in the hero-tales of Cu Chulainn. Lugus is the exact cognate of Lleu and also opens the name Llefelys. Two important Continental Celtic inscriptions name Lugus: the texts from Penalba de Villastar and from Chamalieres. A Latin inscription from Uxama in central Iberia, today Osma, honours divine beings called the Lugoues, a name tied to the god. Traders and craft groups made such dedications, one coming from a guild of shoemakers. Many tribal names share the element, including the Lugi of north-eastern Scotland and the Lugii of east-central Europe. Many personal names also carry the root legu-, such as Gaulish Luguselva and Ogam Irish LUGUECAS. Latin sources equate Lugus with the Roman god Mercury, and the Roman writer Caesar records Celtic divinities under Roman names like Mercury.

:::
```
