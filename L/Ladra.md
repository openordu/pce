``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Ladra was a hero in the Book of Invasions and one of the earliest people said to reach Ireland. The lady Cesair arrived close to Bantry Bay in County Cork, landing at a fort called Dún na mBarc. She brought fifty handmaidens and only three men, and MacKillop dates this arrival forty days before the Flood. Ladra, the ship's pilot and the brother of Cesair, stood among those three men. MacKillop calls him the helmsman of the party. The three men divided the women among themselves, each hoping to father a new people and thus fill the island. Once the travellers reached dry land, Ladra asked for more territory than Cesair would grant. He set out to found his own kingdom, but he died trying to satisfy the many women who had come with him. One record counts sixteen wives and holds that this excess of women brought about his end. Another text holds that an oar pierced his body and caused the death. The Book of Invasions therefore presents Ladra as the first man to die in Ireland. When he fell, the duty of populating the land passed to the poet Fintan mac Bóchra, friend to the bards, could not face the task and fled in the shape of a salmon. Abandoned, Cesair herself died of a broken heart. Scholars still debate whether Ladra and Adra are the same man or two separate figures, and the spellings Ladhra and Ladru also appear in the records. His short life thus stands as a pointed warning in early Irish legend about the dangers of too many wives.

:::
```
