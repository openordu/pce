``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Lagin, also written Laigin, Laighin, and Laighnigh, is the name of an early Celtic people who invaded Ireland before the keeping of written records. Their name came to mark the province of Leinster. The Lagin took the second place among Celtic invaders, following the Érainn. They settled across Leinster and also in large parts of Connacht. The Lagin named the ancestor deity Labraid Loingsech, who was said to have carried home 2,200 foreigners from Europe to become the Lagin. Their own legend makes them Gaulish in origin, invading Ireland from Brittany, which the Romans called Armorica. The Annals of the Four Masters place this after 431 bc and the Book of Leinster after 307 bc.

:::
```
