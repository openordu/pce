``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Lugnasad, also spelled Lughnasadh, was the Irish festival that opened the harvest season on 1 August. The name links to the important Irish mythological figure Lug. Modern Irish calls it Lughnasa, and Manx has Laa Luanys or Laa Lunys. Cormac ua Cuilennain wrote that Lugnasad meant a festival of Lug mac Ethlenn, a fair that Lug held each year at the start of harvest. A related fair, the Oenach Tailten, took place at what is now Telltown in County Meath. This was arguably the most important festival in ancient Ireland. The man who sought the kingship of Ireland presided over it. The annals state the fair should be held every year on Lugnasad, barring exceptional events, though after the 9th century it came irregularly. Evidence of the festival appears across the British Isles. The English and Scottish Lammas, meaning loaf mass, may have a Celtic origin because its date is not kept in other Germanic lands. A Roman festival in honour of Augustus, held in August at Lugdunum in Gaul, may itself have grown from a Gaulish festival for the Celtic god Lugus. That link gives further reason to connect the toponym Lugdunum with the pan-Celtic god Lugus.

:::
```
