``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Lugnasad, also spelled Lughnasadh, was the Irish festival that opened the harvest season on 1 August. The name links to the important Irish mythological figure Lug. Modern Irish calls it Lughnasa, and Manx has Laa Luanys or Laa Lunys. Cormac ua Cuilennain wrote that Lugnasad meant a festival of Lug mac Ethlenn, a fair that Lug held each year at the start of harvest. A related fair, the Oenach Tailten, took place at what is now Telltown in County Meath. This was arguably the most important festival in ancient Ireland. The man who sought the kingship of Ireland presided over it. The annals state the fair should be held every year on Lugnasad, barring exceptional events, though after the 9th century it came irregularly. This festival stands beside Samain, Imbolc, and Beltaine as one of the four great markers of the ancient Celtic year. It long served as a harvest festival that celebrated the ripening of grain and later the maturing of potatoes. Lug was the hero it honoured, a figure of early Irish literature and the god whom the Romans linked with Gaulish Mercury. At Lugudunum, now Lyon, a city named for Lug, a rite took place each first of August to honour the Emperor Augustus. Evidence of the festival appears across the British Isles. The English and Scottish Lammas, meaning loaf mass, may have a Celtic origin because its date is not kept in other German lands. A Roman festival in honour of Augustus, held in August at Lugdunum in Gaul, may itself have grown from a Gaulish festival for the Celtic god Lugus. That link gives further reason to connect the toponym Lugdunum with the pan-Celtic god Lugus.
```
