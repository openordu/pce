``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Lammas is the English name for Lughnasa, the Celtic harvest feast of the first of August. MacKillop counts it among the four great feasts that divide the Celtic year, beside Samhain, Imbolc, and Beltaine. The feast carries the name of Lugh, an Irish hero, and of his foster-mother Tailtiu. Early tradition reported that Lugh founded the festival on the plain of Tailtiu in County Meath to honour her. He himself led the horse-racing and the contests of martial skill. Lug may have come from the Gaulish god Lugos, whom Julius Caesar named the chief god of the Gauls. The Roman city of Lugdunum, now Lyon, held a celebration each first of August for the Emperor Augustus. In later centuries Lughnasa gatherings spread across Ireland, to Emain Macha for Ulster and to Tara for the whole island. The feast marked the ripening of the grain and, after they spread, the maturing of potatoes. In Scotland the festival moved to late September and took the name Michaelmas after Saint Michael. On the Isle of Man it keeps the name Laa Luanistyn. In England the same day is called Lammas, a name built from the loaf of bread offered at Mass. The first of August closes one great season, and the next feast, Samhain, opens the new cycle. The festival honoured the ripening grain, and its games recalled the deeds of Lug as well as the mourning of Tailtiu.
```
