::: magazinestyle
Llywelyn ap Iorwerth, also called Llywelyn Fawr, ruled the Welsh kingdom of Gwynedd in the 13th century. A victorious commander, he asserted the liberties of the Welsh when the Magna Carta was signed in 1215, pressing his case against the English king John. He married the king's own natural daughter Joan, known in Wales as Siwan. For all his worldly success he ended his days in the habit of a monk at the monastery of Aberconwy, and he is the grandfather of Llywelyn ap Gruffudd. Two Welsh plays recount his princely life.

:::
