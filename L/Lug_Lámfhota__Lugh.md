``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Lug Lámfhota; Lugh is the headword form of the god the PCE records as Lugh. Old Irish lug means light or brightness, and lámhfhada means long-armed or long-handed. The patronymics mac Céin and mac Ethlenn attach to him, as does Maicnia, a name meaning lad-warrior. His standing epithets are Samildánach, the one of many arts, and Ildánach, and the lists make him a possible fili or seer as well as a warrior. He leads the Tuatha Dé Danann and anchors the Mythological Cycle as one of its three great heroes, next to Fionn mac Cumhaill and Cúchulainn, whose supernatural father he is. Lámfhada records his skill at hurling a weapon far, not the length of his arm. Commentators agree he matches the Gaulish Mercury. Caesar called his Mercury the master of every craft, a rendering of Samildánach, and city names such as Leiden, Lyon, and Liegnitz preserve the older Lugos. Augustus opened a Roman festival at Lyon on the first of August, a forerunner of the Irish Lughnasa. His long-armed epithet even echoes the title of the Indian god Savitar, the one of the wide hand.

:::
```
