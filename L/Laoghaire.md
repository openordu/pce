``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Laoghaire ranks among the most frequent names of early Ireland. MacKillop lists the spellings Lóegaire, Láegaire, Lóeghaire, Laeghire, and Leary. Kings, heroes, and saints all bore the name. The best-known of the legendary bearers is Lóegaire Búadach of the Ulster Cycle. He contended with Cúchulainn and Conall Cernach for the champion's portion at Fled Bricrenn, the Feast of Bricriu. In one tale the poet Aed faced drowning for his adultery with Mugain, the wife of Conchobar. Lóegaire rushed from his house to save the poet. He struck his head on a door lintel and died, but not before he cut down thirty executioners. His mantle numbers among the three great treasures of the island. As Lóegaire Bern Búadach, an almost separate identity, he became the ancestor-god of the Osraige, the people of Ossory, and was said to own a famous sword. Another bearer, Lóegaire mac Néill, ruled Tara and accepted Christian teaching from Saint Patrick. He was a son of Niall of the Nine Hostages, and the earliest Irish king whose dates scholars can fix with confidence. He died fighting the Laigin between two hills named Ériu and Alba. The many bearers give the name long reach across the legends and history of Ireland. The tales of the name stretch from the contest at the feast of the champions to the conversion of the royal hill at Tara.

:::
```
