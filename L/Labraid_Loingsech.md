``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Labraid Loingsech, the seafarer, is an ancestor deity of the Leinstermen. His story tells of the revenge of his father's death and the winning back of his kingdom, related in the tale the Destruction of Dind Rig. Although the Lagin, the founders of Leinster, ranked Labraid a man higher than the gods, Kuno Meyer judged that he could have been a genuine king of the era when Rome pressed into Britain. Pedigrees place him as the twenty-fifth descendant of Éremón, and the grandson of Úgaine Mór. His reign marks the rise of the Lagin to power in eastern Ireland. The Book of Leinster dates his killing of Cobthach to 307 bc, while the Annals of the Four Masters give 431 bc. In later tales he widens his rule across much of Europe and even to Italy and Armenia. From these travels he carried home 2,200 foreigners armed with broad spears, whom the Lagin adopted as their own people. The historian Geoffrey Keating treated him as a real figure.

:::
```
