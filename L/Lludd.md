``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Lludd is a name worn by several figures of early Welsh tradition. Their identities cannot always be set apart. The leading figure is Lludd, the son of Beli Mawr. He is the main king of the romance Cyfranc Lludd a Llefelys, the Tale of Lludd and Llefelys. In that story his own realm suffers three plagues. His wise brother Llefelys rules across the sea in France. Lludd turns to him for help. The first plague is the Coraniaid, a breed of demons that cannot be driven out. The second is a pair of dragons fighting at every Maytime. The third is a giant wizard who steals the food of the land. With Llefelys in counsel Lludd masters all three. He digs a pit in the middle of Britain to catch the dragons. He overcomes the giant in battle and takes him as a vassal. Another Lludd is a form of Nudd. As Lludd Llaw Ereint he is the father of Creiddylad, who became Cordelia in later telling. A third Lludd stands as the legendary founder of London. The city is called Caer Lludd in old Welsh. His name is kept today in Ludgate Hill. The same layered king may have fed the later character of Lear. Lludd is above all the king who frees his island from its three ancient plagues. Each facet of him, king, god-hero, and founder, adds a thread to the same rich story of a Britain freed from plague. King of London and master of the plagues, he guards the island's oldest tale of relief.

:::
```
