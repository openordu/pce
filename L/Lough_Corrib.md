``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Lough Corrib is the Irish name An Choirb in modern form. Old Irish records the lake as Loch nOirbsen, meaning Oirbsiu's lake. The water covers 70 square miles in Connacht and spans counties Galway and Mayo. Only one Irish lake is larger. The old name points to Oirbsiu, a form sometimes widened to Oirbsiu Mór. Commentators treat that term as an obscure epithet of the sea-deity Manannán mac Lir. It carries the sense of inundation. Some read it as the god's original name. A later oral story claims Manannán met his death by drowning in these waters. Tradition also gave Lug Lámfhota a palace beneath the lough. The Corrib country lies beside Connemara and keeps a strong store of folklore and folk culture. Sir William Wilde's Dublin study of the lough appeared in 1867. Richard Hayward published The Corrib Country at Dundalk in 1954.

:::
```
