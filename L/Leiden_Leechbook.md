``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Leiden Leechbook is a rare medieval medical manuscript. Its shelf mark is Vossianus Latin fo. 96A. It stands apart because it holds Celtic words. Three of its four pages carry Latin texts. One page mixes Brythonic and Irish terms. Whitley Stokes called the Brythonic forms the oldest Old Breton. The codex is dated between the ninth and tenth centuries. Its letter hand resembles the Cambridge Juvencus codex. Nothing in the forms suggests a Continental scribe. The recipes pair Latin with vernacular plant names. Many names are Welsh, Cornish, or Breton. Some are inherited words, others are Latin loans. The plant vocabulary fills three groups. Analysis hints the base text may be Breton. Yet a Cornish or Welsh origin is possible. The dialect stays unclear. The Brythonic tongues had split little by about 900.

:::
```
