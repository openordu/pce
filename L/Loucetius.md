``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Loucetius is a Romano-Celtic divine name. The early form was Loucetios. Finders raise nine inscriptions in Germany and France, and one more in Britain. This one comes from the healing shrine at Bath, placed there by a person from the Treveri tribal district. The man likely looked to the god for help with healing. The later spelling Leucetius occurs three more times. People often set the god beside the Roman war god Mars. This is a common case of interpretatio romana, the Roman habit of matching a local god to a Roman one.

The name holds a root that means a flash of light. Related forms appear in Gothic, Old Irish, Welsh, Old Cornish, and Breton. Scholars therefore read the god as Mars of lightning or Mars the bright and shining. The image ties battle to a thunderstorm or recalls the shining glow of a great warrior. The consort of this god is Nemetona, the queen of the sacred grove and of the holy privilege. At Bath and at a shrine in a German land, people paired the two with Victory. The couple grounded a local tradition that joined healing, lightning, and battle.

:::
```
