``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Lóegaire mac Néill ruled as king of Tara from 427/8 to 462/3, and St Patrick converted him to Christianity. He was a son of Niall Noigiallach, Niall of the Nine Hostages. The dictionary calls him the first Irish king whose reign can be dated with any real precision.

A prophecy shaped his death. He was told he would die between Eriu, meaning Ireland, and Alba, meaning Scotland, so he refused ever to go to sea. The prophecy kept its truth on land. When he fell fighting the Lagin, the Leinstermen, his body lay between two hills called Ére and Alba, on the Liffey plain. Gearóid S. MacEoin examined the puzzle of that death in Studia Hibernica in 1968.

:::
```
