``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Lailoken is a hermit of the medieval Scottish tradition who lives in great hardship in the wild and is said to have the power of visions. The figure closely matches the Suibne Geilt of Ireland and the Myrddin of Wales, whom many writers know as Merlin, and all three are thought to share a common north British origin. The Scottish texts that mention Lailoken are the Lives of Saint Kentigern, written soon after the Anglo-Norman reorganisation of the Scottish Church. Two tales, named 'Kentigern's Encounter with Lailoken' and 'Lailoken in King Meldred's Court', survive in a late manuscript and were labelled Vita Merlini Silvestris. The wild-man story probably began as a popular tale of Cumbric Strathclyde, linked to the battle of Arfderydd. Lailoken is a Brythonic name tied to the Middle Welsh word llallog 'friend', 'brother', or 'lord'.

:::
```
