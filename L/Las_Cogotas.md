``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Las Cogotas is a pre-Roman castro in central Spain. The fort stands on a granite rise above the Adaja river near Avila. Digs ran in 1927 to 1931 and again in the 1980s. The site holds two phases. The first settlement, called Las Cogotas I, belongs to the Later Bronze Age. Its handmade pottery carries cut and geometric designs. The later fort, Las Cogotas II, covers close to fifteen hectares. Its walls reach over ten thick metres. Upright stones ring the shallow slope as a defence. A cemetery near by holds at least sixteen hundred cremated burials. Most graves held no goods. Iron weapons appear in only a few tombs. The place is famed for its stone beasts. Broken parts of six large bulls survive in the lower enclosure. These sculptures, known as verracos, typify the Vettones tribe. The tribe held this zone in historic times. Later the district passed to the Roman province of Lusitania.

:::
```
