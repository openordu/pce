``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Laigin, whose Early Modern Irish form is Laighin, were the people of the traditional south eastern province of Ireland, the region known as Leinster. In early historic Ireland the name covered both a group with sub tribes and a kingdom called Cóiced Laigen, the province of Leinster. The modern province counts as eleven counties, and the first seven of these lay inside the old one. Its early borders followed the river Liffey and the Offaly bogs to the north and the Ossory uplands to the west. Since Laigin is a plural noun, use as a people name came before use as a province name, and early writings traded between the forms Laigin and Gáileóin. The name also survives in places across north Wales, among them the Llŷn Peninsula, a sign of how prominent the Laigin were in that part of Britain during the Roman period. One scholar tied the name to the god Lug, but that is seen as unlikely on linguistic grounds, whereas a loan from the Latin legiones, meaning legions, is possible. A legendary ancestor, Labraid Loingsech, headed the old genealogies, and the dynastic kingship later moved between two rival houses, the Uí Dúnlainge and the Uí Cheinnselaig.

MacKillop sets the Laighin in second place among the Celtic peoples who invaded, right behind the Érainn. They settled not only the future Leinster but also wide parts of Connacht. The scholar Thomas F. O'Rahilly argued, with little backing, that the Laigin were a P-Celtic people. He held that they took up lines that hinted at a Q-Celtic origin only once the Goidels held sway. Their ancestor deity, Labraid Loingsech, is tied to a story that he invited about 2,200 foreigners back from Europe, and those strangers became the Laigin. The Laigin tale of origin says they were Gaulish and that they invaded from Brittany, the land also named Armorica. The Annals of the Four Masters place the event after 431 BC, and the Book of Leinster after 307 BC. Modern work favors the Laigin arriving far earlier. The Laigin brought the Domnainn and the Galióin with them.
```
