``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Lugaid Riab nDerg is a king of the Ulster Cycle, remembered above all for his tragic marriage to Derbforgaill of Lochlainn. His Irish name means red stripes, and the forms Riabhdhearg, Reoderg, and Lugaid Sriab nDerg also record it. He stands as foster-brother, and in some texts foster-son, of Cúchulainn. Derbforgaill desired Cúchulainn, but marriage to her lay outside his reach. In drawing a stone from her body he had sucked at her blood without knowing it. So he handed her to Lugaid, and the two of them made a happy marriage. Her death was grotesque. The women of the court pushed her into an allure contest: which of them could project her urine the furthest distance through a pillar of snow. She took the prize. Her rivals then killed her, mutilating her body out of jealousy. Cúchulainn came back and put 150 of the courtly women to death. Grief, or else shock, carried Lugaid off. Incest is a motif that recurs across his story. His mother Clothra conceived him by lying with her three brothers, the Finn Emna of Emain. He later lay with Clothra himself and fathered Crimthann Nia Náir on her. Commentators connect that incest with his sobriquet. Under one description his body bears two red lines, one parting head from shoulders and one cutting the trunk at the belt, so that the marks mirror the three paternal contributions. A plainer reading holds that the stripes were scars from battle. Modern commentators argue storytellers invented the figure as one more entry in the Ulster Cycle roster, drawing his aspects from other Lugaids. His kingship is the least credible part, since no tale shows him ruling. The Annals give his killers as the Three Red Heads of Leinster, an apparent device set against the three Finns who fathered him.
```
