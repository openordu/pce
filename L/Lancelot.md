``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Lancelot is one of the great lovers of Arthurian romance, the devoted knight who wins the love of Queen Guinevere and becomes the father of Galahad. MacKillop places him among the heroes most strongly French in the whole Arthurian legend, and his first appearance lies in the verse of Chrétien de Troyes, in the tale Ereac dated near the year 1170. A knight of great courage, he frees the captives of the Perilous Valley and crosses the bridge of the sword blade to reach the queen. Despite his French footing the figure may hold Irish roots. The scholar Roger Sherman Loomis argued in 1951 that Lancelot comes down from the god Lug of the Irish. In a later tale the knight is mocked by the penetrating wit of Dinadan, who writes a lampoon against King Mark in a tournament. Lancelot answers the mock by riding forth wearing a woman's dress over his armour, overpowering the satirist, and carrying him into the nearby forest to dress him as a woman. His gentle care for the lady Elaine brings the boy Galahad, who will grow into the pure knight of the Grail. The name of Lancelot is bound to the close of Camelot. His love for Guinevere splits the loyalty of the Round Table and helps bring the whole realm to its fall. He stands above the other knights in arms, yet his heart belongs to the queen, and that hidden passion drives the greatest tragedy of the Arthurian world.

:::
```
