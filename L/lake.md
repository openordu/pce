``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The lake was a symbolic site in Celtic religion. Because water was sacred, lakes carried great meaning as symbols of the Otherworld. Many Celtic lands told of cities lying beneath lake waters. Stories also spoke of magical beings that lived under the waves or on islands in the lakes. The lake stood for a passageway to the Otherworld. This meaning echoed ancient rituals in which people offered treasures to lakes. A great horde of objects found in the shallows of Lake Neuchatel in Switzerland was probably placed there on purpose. Holy wells saw offerings at seasonal festivals, while lakes were visited more in times of great need. MacKillop points the reader to the Celtic terms loch, lough, and llyn for the corresponding bodies of water.

:::
```
