::: magazinestyle
The lake called Llyn y Fan Fach sits in the Carmarthenshire Beacons of Wales as a glacial tarn. The tale told of it is probably the best remembered of Welsh folk tales. The fullest form was printed by John Williams under the name Physicians of Myddfai. Earlier loose copies of the story survive from a traveller and from at least one other scholar. The tale relates how a shepherd of the vales won as his bride a woman from the water. She carried to the match a gift of goats, cattle, and sheep. One rule ruled the bond: the man must never strike her three blows without cause. In time she bore him three sons. When the rule was broken, the lady returned to the lake and called her beasts to her in a rhymed shout. Then the sons looked for their mother. She showed herself at named spots and handed them a sack of healing cures. These spots still carry the title of the healers. The story stayed alive in the mouths of the folk for many years.

:::
