``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Tír Fo Thuinn, the Land Under Wave, is one of the many names for the Celtic Otherworld. The phrase itself sets the sea above a buried realm. Old Irish tradition reaches that buried realm across the water from the land of the living. Other names carry the same idea of a distant and fertile country. The tradition also calls it the plain of honey and the great plain. All three names point to a land across the sea. In the MacKillop dictionary the entry for wave is a short cross-reference. The reader is sent to tonn for the older Irish term for wave. The reader is sent to tír fo thuinn for the buried country. The reader is sent to the black entry for the white-black-red colour scheme. That colour scheme runs through much of the Celtic sea lore. It ties the white of foam, the black of deep water, and the red of the setting sun. The three together mark the boundary of the wave and the entrance to the drowned land. Together the cross-reference and the three old names fix the Land Under Wave in the Celtic Otherworld.

:::
```
