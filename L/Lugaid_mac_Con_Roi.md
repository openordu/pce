``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Lugaid mac Con Roí is the son of Cú Roi and the man who killed Cúchulainn in an Ulster Cycle tale. He must not be confused with Lugaid mac Con, the Munster king. His usual patronymic names Cú Roi as father. An alternative form calls him mac Tri Con, and a longer shape is mac na Tri Con. Both carry the sense son of three hounds. Commentators read that formula as triplism. The three hounds may be Cú Roi, Cúchulainn, and Conall Cearnach, each a wolf in strength. Those shadow fathers sit oddly beside Lugaid's later fights with the same men. Storytellers explained the lifelong feud differently. His mother Bláithíne had committed adultery with the Ulster hero, and storytellers traced his hostility to that affair. Lugaid attacks Cúchulainn several times before he lands a mortal blow. On one try his spear goes wide and kills Láeg, Cúchulainn's charioteer, instead. In the Táin Bó Cúailnge he readies another attack, then relents. He sees that the fight with Ferdiad at the ford has already left Cúchulainn badly wounded. Their last combat fills the tale Brislech mór Maige Muirthemne, often called The Death of Cúchulainn in English. Lugaid first drives a javelin through the hero's bowels without killing him. He then grants the dying man a nobler end. Cúchulainn stands upright at a pillar-stone, and Lugaid takes his head. Conall Cearnach later kills Lugaid in payment. Scholars secured the tale in print early. Whitley Stokes edited a text in Revue Celtique in 1887. A. G. van Hamel published material in Dublin in 1933. Cross and Slover carried English versions in Ancient Irish Tales in 1936.

:::
```
