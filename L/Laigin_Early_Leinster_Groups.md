``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Laigin were the people of Leinster, Ireland's traditional south-eastern province. In early historic Ireland the name Laigin named both a group with many sub-tribes and a kingdom, the Cóiced Laigen. The province of Leinster still recalls that old identity. The early borders of this territory ran to the north along the Liffey and the Offaly bogs, and to the west through the Ossory uplands. Laigin was a plural noun, so its use as a people-name came before its use as a province-name. Early texts used Laigin and Gáileóin for the same group. Few outside peoples lived among them as client tribes; the main exceptions were the Fothairt, Benntraige, and Loígis. Later the leading dynasties were the Uí Dúnlainge on the Liffeey plain and the Uí Cheinnselaig in what is now Wexford, whose kingship eventually won out in the 11th century.

:::
```
