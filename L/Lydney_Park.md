::: magazinestyle
Lydney Park is a Romano-British shrine of the third century AD. It stands high on the steep west bank of the River Severn, eight miles northeast of Chepstow in Gloucestershire. Writers rank it as the most important native dedicated place of worship that Britain has produced. No physical depiction of its god survives. Even so, the shrine has long been read as centring on the cult of Nodons, a British healing god frequently set beside the Irish Nuadu Airgetlam. The site lies in the Forest of Dean. The complex is large and resembles the classical sanctuaries of healing. A portico of great length, cloister-like in form, divides into compartments that sheltered patients. Construction carried luxurious details, among them floors of mosaic. Such outlays measure how rich the society stood that built the shrine and kept it up. R. E. M. Wheeler and T. V. Wheeler published the excavation report in London in 1932.

:::
