::: magazinestyle
Niam, also written Niamh and Niav, from the Irish word for brightness, radiance, or lustre, is the name of several women of Irish tradition. The first is a wife of Conall Cernach who nurses Cuchulainn and becomes his mistress; Badb, daughter of Cailitin, steals her shape by impersonating a handmaiden and lures her from the hero's bedside, then bids the wounded warrior rise and start the journey that ends his life. The second, daughter of Celtchair, marries Conganchnes mac Dedad to learn why he seems invulnerable, gives up the one way to end him: a spear driven through the sole of a foot or the calf of a leg, and watches her father dispatch him before wedding Cormac Connloinges. The third and best known is the lover of Oisin: oldest texts make her daughter of Oengus Tech, king of Munster, who elopes with the poet to Ulster for six weeks and dies of fear in the pursuit; a second version gives her an Ulster father, Aed Donn, over whom Oisin fights his first battle; the best-known incarnation, in Micheal Coimin's eighteenth-century Irish text, names her Niamh Chinn Oir, of the Golden Hair, and sends her with Oisin to Tir na nOg, the Land of Youth, from which he returns an old man.

:::
