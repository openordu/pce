::: magazinestyle
The village of Niederzier lies near Düren in the Rhineland. A rescue dig, forced by an advancing opencast lignite mine, brought to light a settlement of the later stage of La Tène culture, about 200 BC to 50 BC. A double ditch, roughly oval in shape, enclosed it, and the site measured about 210 by 170 metres. A pit in the western part held a central ritual post, and close by lay a treasury of gold objects hidden under two broken clay bowls from the 1st century BC. They held three gold neckrings and gold Celtic coinage. Of the forty-six coins, twenty-six came from Vindelician territory and twenty others from the Ambiani in the west. It was likely a votive deposit tied to the pit and its ritual post.

:::
