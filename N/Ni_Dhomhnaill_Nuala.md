Nuala Ní Dhomhnaill (1952– ), the poet whom Koch indexes under the headword Ní Dhomhnaill, Nuala, was born in England to Irish-speaking parents. She spent part of her childhood with her mother's family in the West Kerry Gaeltacht. She graduated from University College Cork, where she belonged to the Innti writers' group. After seven years in her husband's native Turkey, she returned to Ireland (Éire) in 1979 with a creative-writing bursary from the Irish Arts Council. Her poems won many prizes, including the O'Shaughnessy Award in 1988 and the America Ireland Fund's prize for literary work in 1991. In 1992 she was named the first writer in residence at University College Cork. She later held a visiting scholars post in Irish studies at Boston College and a fellowship at Villanova University, and in 2001 she took the Ireland Chair of Poetry. She is a member of Aosdána, the Irish academy of artists. Though she writes in Irish, readers most often meet her work in translation, carried out by leading contemporary poets. Her best poems are consciously autobiographical, identifying with female figures drawn from the folklore of Ireland and the wider mythology of East and West. This blend of voices produces an original and imaginative portrait of the female mind. Major collections include An Dealg Droighin (1981) and Pharaoh's Daughter (1990, translated by Carson).
