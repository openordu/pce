::: magazinestyle
Noricum was a Celtic kingdom that lay in the eastern Alps, from roughly the 2nd century BC until about 15 BC. It was a fairly stable confederation of no fewer than twelve Alpine Celtic tribal groups, among which stood the Taurisci and smaller peoples such as the Ambidravi, whose name means those who dwell by the river Drava. These tribal names are Celtic and often carry the preposition ambi, meaning around or about. The Norici proper led the whole body. Noricum was sometimes ruled by one king and at other times by a council of elders. Its capital, Noreia, is still unlocated. The chief export was iron of high quality, and the demand for it led Rome to set up a trading post on the Magdalensberg in Carinthia during the early 1st century BC, later replaced by the town of Virunum.

:::
