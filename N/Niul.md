``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Niúl appears in the pseudo-history Lebor Gabála as a son of Fenius Farsaid, the inventor of the Irish language, and the dictionary's account stresses how much of the language's early legend runs through his family. He takes Scota (2) as his wife and fathers Goidel Glas, the eponymous ancestor whose own career anchors the early story of Irish speech. The inventive text casts Niúl as a renowned schoolmaster and goes so far as to give his name to the Nile River, a flourish that shows how the medieval compilers stitched biblical and classical geography onto their origin tale. MacKillop 2008 keeps the whole episode inside the Lebor Gabála frame, marking it as pseudo-history rather than received tradition.

:::
```
