``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Ninian is a fifth-century missionary wrapped in shadow, remembered under the Latinized form Niniavus, whom tradition sends to study with Martin of Tours before he carries the faith to southern Scotland ahead of Colum Cille's arrival at Iona, and perhaps even before Patrick's day. The ruined priory at Whithorn in Dumfries and Galloway, bound to his mission, drew pilgrims until the Reformation era. Landmarks across southwestern Scotland keep his name, as does Man: the church of St Trinian's, often met in Manx legend, holds his dedication. The dictionary sets his feast-day at 16 September and points to A. P. Forbes's nineteenth-century study of his life together with Kentigern's. MacKillop 2008 adds one caution: this Ninian must not be confused with Niniane, a spelling attached to the Lady of the Lake figures of the Vulgate Cycle of Arthurian story, a being with no historical existence.

:::
```
