``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The adventure of Nera takes a warrior through the cave of Cruachain into the Otherworld. That cave serves as his doorway. The tale runs under the Irish title Echtra Nerai, in English the Adventure of Nera. Though classed as part of the Ulster Cycle, the story of supernatural horror is set in Connacht. The action unfolds on Samain, a night when demons were thought to walk abroad. As the court of Ailill and Medb celebrates the feast at Cruachain, Ailill offers a prize. Any man brave enough may bind a withe about the leg of one of two corpses. Those bodies hang on a gallows outside the fort. When Nera fails three times, one corpse offers counsel and tells him to use a peg. That dead man then complains of thirst, since he perished with thirst. Nera bears the corpse on a roundabout search for water. On finding the water, the corpse spits it out upon a blameless household and all of them die. Nera then carries the body back to the gallows. Back at Cruachain he finds the residents beheaded and the court burned, or so it seems. He trails warriors into the cave of Cruachain, long called Ireland's gate to the underworld. There he sees the sidh's ruler showing heads upon spikes. Each dead man tells his neighbour that a living man must be in the line because it has grown heavier. The other tales of the Otherworld run alongside. Connla is drawn there by a fairy woman. Cormac follows fairy music to seek an Otherworld love. Laoghaire, son of Crimthann, helps an Otherworld peer free his wife from the world above. The ruler of the side then gave Nera a home and a wife. He asked only for a daily load of firewood in return. Nera lived in peace within that hidden world for a while. His wife then spoke a truth she could not hide. Cruachain was never burned as he had first thought. It would truly fall at the next Samain, she said. The high court could save it only through his counsel. So he warned the people to level the side first. He carried garlic, primrose and golden fern back home. Those flowers stood to prove the land he had seen. His return to the world above met a fitting welcome. The lord Ailill gave him the sword he had promised.
```
