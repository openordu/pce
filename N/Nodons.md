``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Nodons, also spelled Nodens, is the British god of healing whose shrine stood at Lydney Park, beside the Severn in Gloucester, on a site the dictionary dates to the third century before the common era. No image of the god survives, yet votive plaques from the shrine tie him to dogs, and one carved figure seems to show a man at work hooking a fish from the water. The dictionary records likenesses to Mars in his healing character rather than his warlike one, and to Silvanus the hunt-god of Rome. Of wider consequence for Celtic comparison, Nodons is a cognate of, and perhaps an anticipation of, the Irish Nuadu Airgetlam, and something of his persona passed into the Welsh figure Nudd. The entry cites Francoise Le Roux's study of the god-king pairing and John Carey's article on the god in Britain and Ireland.

:::
```
