``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Newgrange is the great passage-grave on the Boyne, long known in Irish as the Brug na Bóinne. The name renders as the hostel of the Boyne. The mound was the otherworldly residence first of Boand and of the Dagda. Later it passed into the keeping of Angus Óg, the Irish god of yourth and verse. Boand was the goddess of the river that runs beneath the mound. She is sometimes called Eithne. Boand first lived there with her consort Elcmar, who is sometimes called Nechtan. Later she chose the Dagda as her partner. The passage grave dates from roughly 3200 to 2600 BCE, a span many generations deep. Newgrange may also include the nearby passage graves of Dowth and of Knowth, which match it in size and age. All three stand in the Boyne valley, two miles northeast of Slane in County Meath. In the stories the Brug na Bóinne welcomes countless guests across hundreds of tales. At times its power stands for the bounty of Angus Óg himself. The place supplied endless supplies of ale. Three trees stood there, always in fruit. Two pigs roamed the grounds, one alive and the other roast and fit to eat. In some tellings the great hero Lug Lámfhota is said to rest here. The word bruiden itself carries the weight of a hostel or banqueting hall. A second Old Irish form joins that meaning with fight, contest, or quarrel. Anne Ross tied the dread about the bruiden to the burning of human victims in older pagan rites. Above the great entrance roof sits a small open box. On the winter solstice the rising sun enters that gap. A thin beam of light runs the whole long passage. It reaches the deep chamber for a few special minutes. That alignment marks the turning of the longest night. The cairn rises once again at the dry side. There the folk lay their dead within the northern light. Through the portal a giant kerb stone stands carved. Spirals and lozenge curves cover its great face. The great entrance stone is a world-famous piece of art. The tomb thus unites the sun and the death below it. Today visitors file through that long eastern line. They see the vault where old kings may still rest. The whole ridge is now open as a national monument. Newgrange endures as the most known of Ireland's great tombs. 

:::
```
