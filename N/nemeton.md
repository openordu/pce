``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The nemeton was a sacred grove or sanctuary. The term comes from Gaulish and ties to outdoor rites of the Celts. Worship took place outside rather than inside buildings. Trees carried deep religious weight in such clearings. The word links to old European roots. Greek nemos names a glade. Latin nemus names what is sacred. An older Irish root nem covers heaven and holy things. Greek and Roman writers set the image. Strabo recorded oak groves where Galatian clans gathered. The poet Lucan gave a darker picture, with rough wooden figures seated in deep shade. Fire or earth-shaking might touch a nemeton. Yet the trees stayed whole. After Roman rule spread, the same word named stone shrines. It also named whole towns. Medionemeton in what is now Scotland took the name that way. A medieval forest called Nemeton sat near Locronan. Locronan lies in south-western Brittany. A Benedictine priory later rose within those bounds.

:::
```
