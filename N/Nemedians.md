``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Nemedians are the people who followed the leader Nemed. They left the family home in Scythia. They migrated together to Ireland. Their journey forms part of the invasion tales of Ireland. In the pseudo-history known as the Lebor Gabala or the Book of Invasions, they come third in succession. The Cesair came first and the Partholonians followed, preceding them by thirty years. The Nemedians appear contemporary with the predatory Fomorians. That Fomorian people is arbitrarily cited as the fourth invaders. The Nemedians themselves preceded the Fir Bolg by eleven generations. They departed Scythia in thirty-four ships. All but one of those ships was lost. The company greedily chased a golden tower that loomed upon the sea. The ruined Nemedians then roamed abroad for a year and a half. They finally made landfall in Ireland at a point the text leaves vague. The Nemedians stand as the least remarkable and least romanticized of the Lebor Gabala invaders. They built two royal fortresses in Armagh and in Antrim. They cleared twelve plains and made four lakes. The Nemedians' chief druid was Mide, the eponym of Meath. He lit the first fire at Uisnech, which blazed for seven years. That fire lit every chief hearth across Ireland. Their singular distinction lay in battling the hated Fomorians. They fought three times successfully under Nemed leadership. One later battle at Cnámross ended in disaster for them. Some scholars read such invasion myths as a faint memory of history now lost. The hunt for that golden tower cost them nearly their whole fleet. One single vessel alone rode out the loss. Their wanderings then carried them across many seas. In time the survivors reached the shores of Ireland. Once ashore they set to work upon the land itself. They laboured long to shape the fields and waters of the island. Each of those works left a lasting mark upon the country. The great fire at Uisnech bound their settlements into one. It made the sacred centre of Ireland a hearth for all. Their war with the Fomorians defined their brief rule. Even after the disaster at Cnamross the people carried on. Their story sets the stage for the Fir Bolg who followed.
```
