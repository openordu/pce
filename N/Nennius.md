::: magazinestyle
Nennius is the dictionary's routing entry to Historia Brittonum, the early ninth-century Latin history of the British traditionally ascribed to a writer of that name. The work matters to Celtic myth because it preserves the earliest account of Brutus of Troy, the settlement genealogy of the Britons, and the tale of Vortigern's tower, inside which the boy Merlin reads the two fighting dragons. MacKillop's entry directs readers to the main article for the ascription dispute and the work's Arthurian foreshadowings.

:::
