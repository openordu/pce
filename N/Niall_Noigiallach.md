``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Niall Noígiallach, the Nine Hostager, is ancestor and eponym of the Ui Neill, the dynasty whose grip on Ireland ran six centuries. Tradition makes him leader of foreign conquests and possible captor of St Patrick, though modern writers doubt the wealth and supply such ventures would need. His epithet has two stories: an older one says he held one man from all nine of the Airgialla tuatha, while medieval scribes preferred a tale in which each of Ireland's five provinces, plus Scots, Saxons, British, and French, gave up a captive. Son of the captured British slave Cairenn and King Eochaid Mugmedon, he won the kingship by lying with the loathsome hag who turns beautiful, Dame Sovereignty, in the tale of Eochaid Mugmedon's sons, where the druid Sithchenn forecasts dominion for his line. His stepmother Mongfhind's sorcery and poisonings shadow the succession story, and one version kills her own brother Crimthann with a drink she takes by mistake. Four death accounts, all post eleventh century, chase him with Eochaid son of Enna Cennselach through Scotland, the Alps, the English Channel, or the Loire, avenging the poet Laidcenn's satire of Leinster; his body comes home to burial at Ochann, now Faughan Hill near Kells and Tailtiu. Eight of his perhaps fifteen sons found the northern and southern Ui Neill branches.
```
