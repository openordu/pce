``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Koch derives Nemetona's name from the Celtic root nemeto-, which means a consecrated space or a sacred grove. She may embody the idea of sacred ground itself. The Romano-British Arnemetia mirrors this role. Nemetona was almost certainly the eponymous goddess of the Nemetes, a Germano-Celtic tribe. Her cult ran across all of their territory. A lone bronze at Klein-Winternheim carries her name. At Altrip and Trier she stands beside the war god Mars, where terracotta figures survive. At Bath an altar joins her with Loucetios Mars, raised by a tribesman of the Treveri.

:::
```
