``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Koch derives Nemetona name from the Celtic root nemeto-, meaning a consecrated space or a sacred grove. She may embody the idea of sacred ground itself. The Romano-British Arnemetia mirrors this role. Nemetona is a goddess of Gaul and Britain whose name appears across many old inscriptions. The Celto-Germanic folk known as the Nemetes held her in cult, and their name shares her root. So the deity stands as the eponymous goddess of that tribe. At Bath she appears as the consort of the war god Mars Loucetius. There an altar joins her with Loucetios Mars, raised by a tribesman of the Treveri. She again stands with Mars at Grosskrotzenburg near Hanau. She is also paired with him at Altripp near Speyer. At Altrip and Trier, terracotta figures survive beside her cult site. On a bronze tablet the Roman imperial aristocracy called upon her name. That tablet comes from what is now Klein-Winternheim near Mainz. Her tie to Mars, a war god, may surface in the title of an Irish war goddess, Nemain. Nemetona thus binds the sacred grove, the tribe, and the war god across the whole of their territory. Nemetona guards the very ground her name marks. A devotee raised altars in field and grove. They kept each sacred space safe from any offence. The Cenneres tribe carried that name far through the land. That people spread her cult across a wide territory. Roman practice carried her honour along border posts. A Trevir tribesman set up an altar for her at Bath. Another dedication keeps her name beside the Rhine. In such lines she stands hand in hand with a war god. Mars lends his battle strength to her own house. So her cult joins the sacred wood and the war together. On British soil the goddess Arnemetia fills her niche. The link to battle may pass to an Irish name. That later Nemain keeps a war cry in her title. Keltic priests looked on the nemeton as holy ground. They cut no permanent temple within the trees. A clearing served the tribe for prayer and for court. The goddess thus carries a full world of meaning. She binds the grove, the people, and the war god in one.

:::
```
