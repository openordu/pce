::: magazinestyle
Nectan, also written Nichtan, is the historical seventh and eighth century Pictish king who converted to Christianity and adopted Roman law around 710. He invited Northumbrian architects into what is now Scotland and raised a shrine to house the relics then held as those of St Andrew, later grown into the university town that bears Andrew's name, and helped establish Andrew as Scotland's patron saint. The battle site Nechtansmere takes its name from him, and his Latin form Nechtan links him to the mythic waters figures of the same name.

:::
