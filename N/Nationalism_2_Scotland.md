Nationalism [2] Scotland examines Scottish national identity after the Union with England in 1707. Much of the early bourgeois nationalism worked within the British state and empire, as Scots served as soldiers, missionaries, and administrators. Political nationalism grew only in the 20th century. The Labour Party, dominant in Scotland after 1922, abandoned home rule when it could not repair the economy without the wider British state. The National Party of Scotland was created in 1928 to press for self-government. A literary and cultural Scottish renaissance sought a new national identity beyond rural kailyard imagery. The First World War weakened the idea of Scotland as an imperial partner, and emigration came to be understood as a result of poor opportunity.

:::
