``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Iseabail Ní Mhic Cailéin (fl. c. 1500), whom Koch indexes under the headword Ní Mhic Cailéin, Iseabail, was a poet active within the court circle of Clan Campbell. Two of her poems survive in the Dean of Lismore volume. Both are tender, accomplished courtly-love verses composed in the strict dán díreach conventions. Her exact identity eludes us, because the name Iseabail belonged to daughters of both the first Campbell earl, Cailean (d. 1493), and the second, Gilleaspuig (d. 1513). The first earl, himself a poet whose verse appears in the same manuscript, was married to an Iseabail. Many scholars accept that this was Iseabail, countess of Argyll, the author of a bawdy verse in praise of her household chaplain preserved in the same source. Watson first argued that this Countess and the poet Ní Mhic Cailéin were the same woman, taking the patronymic as her married name, and later editors have followed him. The poems place her within a lively court culture around the earls, alongside the poetry of Donnchadh Caimbeul of Glenorchy. She is remembered as one of the earliest named Scottish Gaelic women poets, among the few whose work survives from the early modern period.
```
