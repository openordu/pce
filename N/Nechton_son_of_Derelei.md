``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Nechton son of Derelei was king of the Picts from 706 to 724, succeeding his brother Bredei. Around 710 the Anglo-Saxon abbot Ceolfrith of Jarrow sent him a letter, and Beda calls him Naiton rex Pictorum in the Historia Ecclesiastica. Nechton asked advice about the true method for fixing the date of Easter. He then caused his kingdom to accept the Roman reckoning then in force among the Northumbrians and in southern Ireland, a break with earlier tradition. He sent dissenting monks back to Iona and favoured Curetán of Rosemarkie, who followed the Roman Easter. Forced by a rival, Drest, into a monastery in 724, he briefly regained power in 728 but lost a final campaign to Onuist son of Uurguist, and retired again to die in 732.

:::
```
