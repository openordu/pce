``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Nabelcus is a god known to history mainly as a title of the Gaulish Mars. The people of Roman Gaul held many local deities that lacked a single Roman name. Worshippers and officials often pressed these resident gods into a familiar Roman frame. This practice in later study gained the name interpretatio Romana. A native power thus wore the cloak of a Roman god in everyday devotion. Nabelcus stands as one such byname laid over the god Mars. Gaul once carried many local Mars names across its old provinces. Nabelcus surfaces in the region of Vaucluse in southern France. That land lay within the old Roman province of Narbonensis. Local people raised altars and paid vows to this local Mars. Their prayers used the native name that we now record. The full literal sense of Nabelcus stays hard to fix. Some readers join the word with a root for power or strength. Others treat it as a title drawn from a named place. The surviving evidence settles on no single certain meaning. We know the god only through rare marks and one short note. The roll of his cult remains very slight indeed. Roman rule over Gaul stretched across several centuries. Within that long age native names kept mixing with Roman forms. Nabelcus therefore stands for a much wider habit of that period. He shows how a local god kept a voice inside a foreign system. Thin records mean we can add little beyond this sketch to him. Every altar that survives adds one small stone to his tale. The name itself records a meeting of two religious worlds. A single study from the nineteen sixties calls him to mind. That essay set Mars Nabelcus beside another title, mars albiorex. Scholars still turn to that old paper when they name him. The tribute to him is slight but it has never faded. He rests as a footnote in the long roll of Celtic gods.

:::
```
