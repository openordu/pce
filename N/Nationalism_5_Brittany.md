::: magazinestyle
Nationalism [5] Brittany traces the growth of Breton national feeling. Early movements such as the Union régionaliste bretonne of 1898 were conservative and regionalist, seeking to keep Breton identity within France. The most prominent party of the earlier 20th century was Breiz Atao, which ran a newspaper of the same name and moved from regionalism towards political autonomy. After 1945 Breton nationalism took on a cultural and economic character, with new journals, folk festivals, and bodies such as CELIB pressing for post-war reconstruction. The Union démocratique bretonne, a left-wing and ecologically minded party, appeared by 1964. A radical Front de libération de la Bretagne, active from 1966, carried out attacks on public property.

:::
