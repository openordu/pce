::: magazinestyle
Nuadu is the name MacKillop 2008 treats under a family of spellings: Nuada, Nuadha, Nuadha-with-length-mark, Nuado, Nuda, and Nuagha, with suggested senses of cloud-maker or catcher. Several perhaps divine figures of early Irish tradition carry it, and the most celebrated is Nuadu Airgetlam, the silver-handed king of the Tuatha De Danann. The dictionary cautions that characters bearing other epithets may be merely variations on a single persona, so the boundary between Nuadu's incarnations is uncertain. The name also outlived paganism and reached Christian times, where at least two saints carried it, which the article reads as proof of its hold on Irish memory beyond the old narratives.

:::
