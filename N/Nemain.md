``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Nemain is an Irish war goddess whose name is written in many spellings. Writers also render it Neman or Neamhan. Older forms include Nemainn, Nemon, Nemain, Nemhain, and Neamhain. The name carries the sense of battle-fury or a war-like frenzy. She ranks among the three war goddesses called the Morrigan. Of that threesome she is the least known. The better-known members are Badb, the scald crow, and the Morrighan, also named the great queen or the queen of phantoms. Old Irish texts pair her as wife or consort of the god Neit. The goddess Badb shares that same role beside Neit. Nemain may blend her identity with Badb in some accounts. Over the texts three goddesses of war usually stand together: Badb, Macha, and the Morrigan. Those three rule under the collective name the Morrigna. When Nemain is substituted in the triad for either Badb or the Morrigan, readers tend to view her as a face of the one she replaces. The three may instead all be aspects of the Morrigan. In her name the commentator also finds a trace of the Continental goddess Nemetona, honoured in Gaul and Britain. So violent was the madness she sent that warriors sometimes mistook their own friends for enemies. The panic she spread could shatter a battle line in an instant. In this way Nemain stands beside the greater war goddesses even as she stays the quieter, shadowed figure among them. Her fury in war took on a name of its own. That terror could fall upon a host and scatter it at once. When she rose over a field, even tested fighters lost their nerve. Some tellers see her as a separate face of the Morrigan. Others fold the whole trio back into that one great queen. The bond with the goddess Badb rests on their shared marriage to Neit. Both women stand beside that god as his war-brides. Her Old Irish name keeps the violence of the charge alive. She mirrors the Gaulish Nemetona, whose own name carries a sacred grove. So an echo of her Continental kin ties her to older worship. Across the sea the warrior and the peace-goddess met within one name. That doubled sense bound Nemain to the roots of the cult. She remains the shadow in the dark of the Irish battle tradition.
```
