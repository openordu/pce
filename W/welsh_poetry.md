``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Welsh poetry is the body of verse composed in the Welsh language. Its recorded history reaches from the Old Welsh period into later centuries. Some early work is Arthurian in theme. The poem Preiddiau Annwfn belongs to this group. It appears in Llyfr Taliesin. A second Arthurian piece named Pa Gur yv y Porthaur sits in Llyfr Du Caerfyrddin. A large fund of anonymous religious verse dates from early times. Its oldest copy is nine englynion in the Juvencus manuscript. These lines praise God and the act of creation. The poets of the twelfth and thirteenth centuries are named the Gogynfeirdd. They also carry the title Beirdd y Tywysogion, or Poets of the Princes. Their verse ran in a conservative and continuous tradition. It served the recorded Welsh princes who were their patrons. The Hendregadredd Manuscript preserves many of their texts. Gwalchmai ap Meilyr wrote verses of boastful exultation called gorhoffedd. The intensive sound harmony named cynghanedd became a fixed feature of the verse. It still governs the strict metres used today. Eulogy and elegy for noble patrons carried on in these years. After 1282 the noble-house bards then came to the fore. Their strong return waited until the fourteenth century. Dafydd ap Gwilym continues to rank as the best loved poet of the whole tradition. Later centuries brought free metres and fresh themes. The standing of the professional poet did not hold. The Phylip house of Ardudwy and their peers drew the age to a close.

:::
```
