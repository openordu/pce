``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
withershins names the ritual of taking a course opposite to the sun: counterclockwise, lefthandwise, or circling an object while keeping it on the left. MacKillop records the form as the English word that comes from Scottish regional usage. Such a turn is thought unlucky or bound to evil purpose in much of the Celtic written record, as well as in much of the older European popular belief. The word derives from Middle High German Widersinnes, counter-course. Old Irish túaithbel, Modern Irish tuathal, Scottish Gaelic tuathal, and Manx jannoo my-lheh all carry the same sense. The antonym sits under the sunwise turn headword.

:::
```
