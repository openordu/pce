::: magazinestyle
Ruman is a shadowy saint of perhaps the sixth century, printed also under the Cornish form Ruan. His name survives in many place-names, among them Romansleigh in Devonshire and Ruan Lanihorne in Cornwall. No reliable documents of his life remain. Oral tradition held him invulnerable to wolves and sometimes a werewolf himself. Earlier writers identified him with Rónán (3) of Brittany, but twentieth-century hagiography argued for his own separate identity. He stands off the official calendar of saints, yet his veneration falls on 30 August. Gilbert H. Doble covered him in Four Saints of the Fal and in St. Rumon and St. Ronan, both printed at Exeter in 1930 and 1939. Paul Grosjean added a study in Analecta Bollandiana, 61 (1953).

:::
