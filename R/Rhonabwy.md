``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Dream of Rhonabwy, Breuddwyd Rhonabwy, is a Middle Welsh prose tale. It turns on a dream of a hero of Powys. In the tale, the men of the district find their chief sending them to catch two runaways. They ride through a wet and hostile country and at last lodge in a strange hall. Exhausted, Rhonabwy sleeps on an ox hide. In his dream he is at the court of King Arthur among the heroes of the older age. The picture is built like a frame, and fixed in it is a game. Arthur and the great warrior Owain ab Urien sit at a board game, a gwyddbwyll. They play move by move, and their talk fills the long dream. Around them the whole heroic court moves: warriors, horses, and all the fittings of the old world. Modern readers do not treat the tale as a true story. It has no real plot, a narration that will not move, and no clear end. Scholars call it a game and a satire on the older heroic style. Scholars call it a fine and empty piece of the storyteller's craft. The date of the work is argued. The frame belongs to the years of Madog, son of Maredudd, who ruled the land of Powys in 1130 to 1160. Many therefore place the tale in that time. Others look to a later age, and the poet Madog Dwygraig, of about 1370 to 1380, already names the dream of Rhonabwy, which gives a firm upper limit. Wherever the date, the tale stands with the Arthurian noble and holds in it a full and lively portrait of a royal court.

:::
```
