``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Rosmerta is a Continental Celtic goddess. Her Latin name can be read as good purveyor or great provider. That sense marks her work as a goddess of plenty. Her worship is known from what is now Germany to Britain. Writers place her heartland among the people of northeastern Gaul. Some art shows her holding a cornucopia, a horn of plenty. She often serves as the cult-partner of Gaulish Mercury. Her statues sometimes take his emblems: the purse of plenty, and the caduceus of heralds. She also appears with the Roman goddess Fortuna and may borrow objects from her images. Other images imply fertility and a care for motherhood. In south-eastern France she often stands alone. Some images also set her beside a portrait of the emperor. On that ground writers judge that her standing may have outweighed the standing of Mercury. That solo cult may be older than the cults of Mercury and of Fortuna alike. Smertrius, at times an epithet of Gaulish Mars, may carry an echo of her name. Old writers also tie her to springs and to healing. In some images she holds the cauldron of rebirth. Her own myth is lost, and the details of her rite are unknown.

:::
```
