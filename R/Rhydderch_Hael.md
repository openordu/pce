``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Rhydderch Hael, also called Rhydderch the Generous, is an Arthurian hero in folk legend. He owned a cauldron of abundance. Apart from that detail, the legend says little about him.

He is far better attested in the historical record as a real ruler. Rhydderch Hael ap Tudwal was king of Dumbarton in the later sixth century. He belonged to a lineage that claimed descent from the fifth-century king Cinuit map Ceretic Guletic. His name is Celtic in origin and comes from a word that means the one who is seen, hence prominent and outstanding. The title Hael, generous, is probably linked to the Gaulish word sagilos. His father's name Tudwal reflects a common Celtic phrase for ruler of the tribe. The Life of Columba by Adomnan calls him the ruler of the rock of Dumbarton. The Life recounts that he came to Columba for guidance, afraid that enemies would slay him. Columba gave him comfort. He told him he would not die by a violent act but would pass away at home on his own pillow. He died quietly at his own home. This calm end suits his Welsh epithet that means old. A line in the early verse may call him worthy of note. The Historia Brittonum names him among the kings who fought by Urien's side to besiege the Angles in the north. The Welsh law in the Black Book of Chirk records a raid by north Britons, Rhydderch among them, against a son of Maelgwn. He is one of the Three Generous Ones of the Island of Britain. He also appears in the Welsh triads.

:::
```
