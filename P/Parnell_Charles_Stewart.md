``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Parnell, Charles Stewart, is best known as Charles Stewart Parnell. He earned the name uncrowned king of Ireland. He lived from 1846 to 1891. He served as a Member of Parliament and headed the Irish Parliamentary Party. He was born at Avondale in County Wicklow. His father was a liberal Anglican landowner. His mother came from America. In April 1875 he took the seat of Meath at Westminster. He became central to the new departure. This was an alliance of agrarian, parliamentary, and militant forces. In October 1879 he took the presidency of the Irish National Land League. On 17 May 1880 he was chosen to head the Irish Parliamentary Party. His role in the Land League campaigns followed the Land Act of 1881. This put him in a Dublin prison from 13 October 1881 until early May 1882. The Kilmainham Treaty won his release. In October of 1882 he recast the League. He formed the Irish National League, whose aim was self-government. His finest political feat was winning over Gladstone. He also won the British prime minister's Liberal ministry. Both came to support Irish Home Rule. On 8 April 1886 Gladstone laid a Government of Ireland Bill before the Commons. It fell at the second reading. The Times accused Parnell of part in the 1882 Phoenix Park murders. The charge broke down in February 1889. Captain O'Shea named Parnell in his divorce suit. A decree nisi came on 17 November 1890. Parnell died at Brighton on 6 October 1891. He lies buried in Glasnevin cemetery, Dublin.
```
