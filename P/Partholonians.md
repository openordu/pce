``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Partholonians are a race in the Book of Invasions. They were said to have brought the drink of sovereignty to Ireland. The red ale was called dergfhlaith. The king drank it during his inauguration. A later race, the Tuatha De Danann, refined the brewing. The smith and brewer Goibniu made ale strong enough to give immortality. The Partholonians came to Ireland with their leader Partholon. They fought the monstrous Fomorians in a realm of magic where no blood was shed. They were later destroyed by plague. MacKillop names them as the mythical early invaders of Ireland in the pseudo-history Lebor Gabala. They take their name from their leader Partholon, whose Latin form is Bartholomaeus. Their fleet leaves the eastern Mediterranean. The landing falls 312 years after Cesair died. She had led the wave before them. The Flood parts them from all earlier peoples. They come first in its wake. Thirty years of gap remain between them and the next wave, the Nemedians. Fighting them are the Fomorians, raiders styled the fourth invading folk. Their champion Cichol Gricenchos leads that host. The Fomorians show a monstrous build. Each has a single eye, a single arm, a single leg. The Partholonians are a beneficent and productive people. Their work reshapes the land. Four plains get cleared. Seven lakes take shape. Farming arrives with them. Partition of the whole island into four parts is their doing, the first on record. A first true civilization stands with them. Law and cauldron-making take root. Crafts follow, then ale-brewing. Welcome for strangers completes the set. Their footprint spreads over the whole island. The first step comes at Inber Scene, today's Kenmare, or else at Donegal Bay. Beltaine is the season of that arrival. Assaroe receives their first settlement. Later they farm Mag nElta. That plain runs between Howth, Clontarf, and Tallaght. Its bounds match the Dublin of our own day. Plenty lasts 520 years. The people swell to 9,000 souls. Then plague takes every one of them inside a single week of May. One tale breaks the wipeout. Tuan mac Cairill lives on. He lasts into the days of Colum Cille and recounts the whole story of the invasions. Named figures beyond Partholon's own family and druids fill out the record. Accasbel raised the first inn the island knew. Babal the trader brought cattle in. Bacorbladra holds the rank of first teacher. He was the first foster-father too. Biobal the trader brought gold in. Breoga told quarrellers to settle matters by single combat, not by war. Feda was the first of the people to die on Irish ground. Malaliach stewed the first ale. Ale later served rite, divination, and offering. Merban and Muncnican stand as champions. Slaine, a son of Partholon, was the island's first healer.
```
