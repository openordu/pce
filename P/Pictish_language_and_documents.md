::: magazinestyle
Pictish language and documents concern the speech and texts of the Picts. The question of the Pictish tongue has long vexed scholars. Heavy study has turned on its nature. The answers stay disputed and careful. Surviving evidence includes about forty inscriptions that can be read. Much of this material is hard to make out. Some names look plainly Celtic. Calgacus, Vepogenus, and Argentoxus carry a Celtic form. Others among the inscriptions stay unclear. The symbols of Pictland are the most known documents. More than two hundred symbol groups are carved. They appear on stones, cross-slabs, metalwork, and caves. Their use runs close to ogam writing. The marks show strong regularity and long use. The exact form of Pictish speech still vexes scholars.

:::
