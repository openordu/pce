::: magazinestyle
Julius Pokorny was a leading scholar of the Celtic languages. He gained an honorary doctorate and lectured widely across Europe. He taught at several Swiss and German universities over a long career. He never again held a regular chair after certain years abroad. He received honorary degrees from universities in Ireland, Germany, Wales, and Scotland. Taking up ideas from Sir John Morris-Jones, he formed a bold theory. He proposed a pre-Indo-European layer under the Celtic languages. He argued this layer was close to the Berber tongues of North Africa. He held that it shaped the Insular Celtic speech, most clearly in Irish syntax. The theory drew fierce debate. Pokorny also built a lasting name through his comparative grammar and his great lexicon of the Indo-European roots. His work still marks the study of Celtic and Indo-European alike.

:::
