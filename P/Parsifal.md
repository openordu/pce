``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Parsifal is the spelling MacKillop 2008 files as a pointer to the Grail hero treated in full at Percevel, where the variant forms Perceval, Percival, Parzifal, and Perchevael are also gathered. The main article traces the figure through the Arthurian legends and their Celtic antecedents, among them the Amadán Mór, Fionn mac Cumhaill, Peredur, and Peronnik. The romance Chrétien de Troyes wrote about 1182, the Conte del Graal, gives the hero his first appearance. His story varies across English, French, and German retellings, yet every version keeps him the same figure: an innocent on a quest, a country bumpkin grown into the hero's seat. Richard Wagner fixed the spelling Parsifal in the wider European imagination with his music drama of 1882, in which he described the character as a simpleton without guile. A reader who comes to the name by way of Wagner or the later romances should return to Percevel, where the dictionary keeps the full account of the hero and his Celtic kindred.

:::
```
