::: magazinestyle
Peronnik, or Peronnique, is a Breton folklore hero whose tale parallels the Welsh Peredur and the Arthurian Percevel. The first element of his name, per, means bowl. He grows up a poor orphan of the Breton countryside. Many knights fail at the quest before him. He enters Kerglas, where the wicked magician Rogear hoards a stolen lance and cup. Peronnik carries both treasures back to their rightful king. With the theft undone, peace and plenty come back to the realm. Émile Souvestre printed the story in Le Foyer Breton (Paris, 1853), a collection that saw many reprints. George Moore's 1924 book Peronnik the Fool retells the Breton tale.

:::
