::: magazinestyle
Polybius (204-122 BC) was a Greek historian who wrote forty books, and most of them are lost. His history covers contemporary events from 220 to 146 BC. After Rome conquered Greece, Polybius spent seventeen years in the city as a distinguished prisoner. There he met leading public figures, among them the younger Scipio Africanus. Commentators rank him among the most reliable classical writers on the Celtic world. He names the gaesatae, Celtic mercenaries who fought as such. W. R. Paton's Loeb Classical Library translation of the Histories (6 volumes, New York, 1922-7) carries the text.

:::
