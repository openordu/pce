``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Celts in Pannonia first reached the western Carpathian Basin in the fourth century BC. This is the reading of the written record. Work in the 1970s found the oldest La Tène A find-spots. They lie across north-western Transdanubia and the Danube plain. La Tène B era burials appear in the Danube Bend. They also show in north-eastern Hungary. They point to a Celtic crossing of the north-south Danube early in that century. Transylvania also passed under Celtic sway. In the late fourth and early third centuries BC, warbands went south. They came from the Middle Rhine to win new lands. From the mid-third century BC, Celtic graves turn up on the Great Hungarian Plain. They sit among the burial grounds of the Iranian-speaking Scythians. Rome later reordered the natives into civitates. Their tribal lands stayed intact. The Celts kept their older ways of life. They also kept their trades, religion, and names. Their settlements show little love for high country. Farming crops and raising herds called for water and level soil. Iron tools sharpened the work of the fields. The chief towns held smiths. Other makers worked in bronze, leather, wood and bone. Ritual life used springs, bogs, summits, and caves.

:::
```
