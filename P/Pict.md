``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Picts were an ancient people of Britain, called the painted ones. The name may come from a custom of tattooing their bodies. Some scholars say the Picts were Celts from tribes such as the Caledonii and the Maecatae. Some rulers held Brythonic, the P-Celtic language. Others claim the Picts were a distinct group that spoke a non-Indo-European tongue. The Picts may have traced descent through the mother's line. In either case they left carved stones in what is now Scotland, marked with bulls, horses, serpents, ravens, hammers, and more. These carvings may have stood for religious beliefs or tribal signs. The Picts allied with Celtic tribes against Rome, and Hadrian's Wall was raised against them. They remained a force until the ninth century, when they joined with the Scots and their language died out. Historians read that story as the Gaelic winners' claim that their win was fated and God-given. The loss of written words points to wide suppression, not murder. The king-list survived for its genealogy. The church likely kept scriptural and Easter books, now lost. Ogam answered Latin writing. The stone at Aberlemno marks victory at Nechtanesmere in 685.

:::
```
