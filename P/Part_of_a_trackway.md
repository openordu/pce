::: magazinestyle
The Corlea trackway is a timber road of heavy planks. Stout driven stakes hold it in place. It crosses a bog at Corlea in County Longford, Ireland. Measurement of its tree rings fixes the wood to 148 BC. It is one sample from the range of roads known across Celtic Europe. The broadest roads were possibly called mantlon in the Celtic tongue. They show in the ground at five to ten metres wide. Many carried a hard surface. They crossed streams on wooden planking where wetlands lay. Mid-size roads bore the Celtic word sentos. These run three to five metres across. The smallest roads show up best in wet ground. Their name may have been kammanom. The early Irish called such trackways tóchar, one and a half to three metres wide. The Corlea roadway stands as a large project. Its date is pinned by the ring-count to late in the second century BC. It is a fine and rare witness to Celtic engineering in timber.

:::
