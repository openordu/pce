``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Proto-Celtic is the reconstructed ancestor of the Celtic languages. Linguists define it by a set of sound laws. These laws separate Celtic from the other Indo-European tongues. Its special combination of changes marks it as a distinct branch. Several changes match the parent Indo-European system. The three sets of Indo-European k-sounds reduce to two in Proto-Celtic. A shared feature with Italic appears in some vocabulary. The so-called tau gallicum is one marker. This sound appears in names such as Medsillus and Sirona. Celtic split into Continental and Insular branches. Gaulish, Celtiberian, and Lepontic sit in the Continental branch. Goidelic and Brythonic make up the Insular branch. Welsh, Irish, Breton, and Manx descend from the same ancient stem. The reconstructed common forms explain the later differences. Proto-Celtic speech goes back to the first millennium BC.

:::
```
