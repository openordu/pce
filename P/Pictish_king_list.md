``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Pictish king-list is the only non-epigraphic text to survive from the Pictish lands. It exists only in corrupt form in later manuscripts. The earliest copy dates to the fourteenth century. It names each king in order. The note gives the father and the length of the reign. Its simple frame still offers rare evidence. It shows how Pictish history was set down in the eighth and ninth centuries. Some of the Latin names witness to the Pictish language. The textual history is complex. The later versions fall into two families. One family is the Series Longua. A second is partly kept in the P-text. Later Irish and Scottish annals reach some of the same kings. The list gives the successors who ruled the Pictish lands.

:::
```
