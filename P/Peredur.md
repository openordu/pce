::: magazinestyle
Peredur is a Welsh romance hero and an Arthurian knight. The Middle Welsh tale Peredur son of Efrawg bears his name and stands among the Three Romances, the Tair Rhamant. The other two romances tell of Geraint ac Enid and of Owain. Scholars have described the story as a Grail legend told without the Grail vessel. A copy stands in the White Book of Rhydderch, written around 1325. A second manuscript, the Red Book of Hergest, dates from about 1382 to 1410. Two further collections also preserve the tale. Lady Charlotte Guest translated the story for her Mabinogion of 1846. It stands outside the Mabinogi proper, which has four branches.

Efrawg, the father, dies early, and the mother raises Peredur, a seventh son, in seclusion. In her care the boy throve, strong and swift of limb. His mother fears knighthood, so she teaches him the knightly code at home. He leaves for the court of Arthur, expecting a knighthood there. His awkward manner amuses the court, yet he soon wins fame for valour and a pure heart. He masters hunting, war, and love in turn. One etymology takes per as bowl in Brythonic speech. To avenge an insult that Cei offers to a dwarf, he rides out on a long chain of adventures. A guide named Edym Gleddyfcoch, the red sword, leads him onward.

Two uncles complete his education. The first uncle is lame and commands him never to question the marvels he sees. The second uncle rules as a fisher-lord. A procession at his castle bears a lance that bleeds and a severed head on a salver. The head belongs to his cousin, and the court asks vengeance. The witches of Caerloyw at Gloucester later teach him the use of weapons. A hermit greets him at dusk in a snowy valley. A hawk strikes down a wild duck, and a crow lands on the kill. Crow, blood, and snow give the black, red, and white motif. That triad marks the ideal of feminine beauty, with black hair, white skin, and red lips. That love stays beyond his reach. A later adventure keeps him fourteen years with the Empress of Constantinople. The episode preserves remnants of the sovereignty story.

A loathly lady rebukes him at the court of Arthur for silence about the marvels. The right question would have healed the king and restored the land to prosperity. He then learns the truth. The witches of Caerloyw killed his cousin and maimed the first uncle, and fate assigns him their vengeance.

The relationship between the Welsh Peredur and the French Perceval is complicated. Chretien de Troyes wrote Perceval around 1182, long before the Red Book text. The Welsh story need not copy the French tradition. The sequence of events differs, the severed head replaces the Grail, and the fourteen years with the empress have no French counterpart. In the Annales Cambriae a Peredur dies in AD 580, called a seventh son of Eliffer. One scholarly view treats the tale as a fresh telling of lost Welsh material that Chretien knew, fused with native traditions about the hero.
