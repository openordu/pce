::: magazinestyle
Thomas Parry is listed by Koch as Sir Thomas Parry. He lived from 1904 to 1985. He stood among the foremost figures of Welsh learning. His service in public life spanned much of the twentieth century. He came from a family of slate quarrymen. They were based at Carmel in Caernarfonshire. He counted R. Williams Parry and Sir T. H. Parry-Williams among his cousins. He read under Sir Ifor Williams at Bangor's university college. He then taught Welsh and Latin at Cardiff. After that he came back to his home department. He rose to lecturer in 1929. He became Professor of Welsh in 1947. In 1953 he was made Librarian of the Welsh national library. He led Aberystwyth's university college from 1958 until 1969. He received a knighthood in 1978. Parry wrote at length on Welsh literature. Among his early books stand an edition of the Middle Welsh prose Saint Greal (1933). He also studied eighteenth-century Welsh ballads (1935). His Hanes Llenyddiaeth Gymraeg hyd 1900 (1944) offered the first full history of Welsh literature. H. Idris Bell later put it into English. His 1952 edition gathered the verse of Dafydd ap Gwilym. It marked a turn in Welsh textual work. His Oxford volume of Welsh verse came in 1962. It carried Welsh poetry to many readers. He was also a poet. He came within reach of the chair at the 1932 eisteddfod. His entry was the awdl Y Fam.
