::: magazinestyle
fairy wind names the sudden gust or whirlwind that Irish tradition blamed on the passage of fairy hosts. Country people crossed themselves when they saw the gust coming. Several Irish expressions label the gust. The forms include a wind of the hill, a wind of the host, a gust of the hill-folk, and a blast of the hill-folk. Folk belief held that the host itself rode inside the wind. A farmer might watch hay lifted at one end of a meadow. The air at the other end lay perfectly still. The same gusts were taken as proof that the hill-folk were lending a hand with the harvest. The fairy wind was also feared as the carrier of illness. It can strip a roof from a poor family's house. It can let the host inside. It can guard fairy treasure from thieves. It can silence mortal musicians playing fairy tunes. It can hurt humans or animals, with special danger to the eyes.

:::
