::: magazinestyle
flood legends fill Celtic literatures. MacKillop treats the international flood theme, catalogued as folk motif A1010-22, as it appears in Irish and Welsh tradition. Irish pseudo-history treats the biblical Deluge as a factual anchor; only Fintan mac Bóchra survives the waters in that telling. Wales, a mountainous country, carries two flood stories. Cantre'r Gwaelod, the land of Gwyddno Garanhir, sank to form Cardigan Bay after Seithennyn, the drunken dike-keeper, neglected his post. The lake called Llyn Llion burst its banks and forced Dwyfan and Dwyfach to escape in a ship built on the spur of the moment. The Breton city of Ys ranks as the best-known of the Celtic flood legends. MacKillop points the reader to submerged cities for the wider record.

:::
