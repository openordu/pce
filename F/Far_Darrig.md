``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Far Darrig, whose Irish name fear dearg means red man, is a solitary Irish fairy from Hiberno-English oral tradition. Folklore paints Fairyland itself in red, the color this figure wears. The Far Darrig is short, pugnacious, and ugly. He dresses from head to toe in red. He specializes in practical jokes, and some of those jokes turn gruesome. Refusing one of his requests counted as unlucky, because his punishments showed real imagination. Still, he holds one gift few fairies own. He can appear larger than he really is. He can also release mortals whom fairyland has trapped. His looks vary sharply by region. In Donegal he stands tall. One Donegal tale tells of a mortal punished with macabre experiences for failing to produce a run of stories. In Munster the Far Darrig is far smaller. T. Crofton Croker put him at about 2.5 feet tall in 1832. Croker dressed him in a sugar-loaf cap, gave him a wrinkled face, and gave him long grey hair. When he sat by the fire and smoked his pipe, a kind family might leave him a meal. He disliked being watched. Having him in a house was said to bring good fortune. Folklorists file him under the motifs F233.3, F369.4, and F375.

:::
```
