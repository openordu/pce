``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The fuath names a group of spirit beings drawn from Highland Gaelic folk tradition. The Scottish Gaelic root carries the sense of aversion. Old Irish attests a cognate form for hate and likeness. These beings most often haunt waters, lochs, rivers, and the open sea. Some sources call the same beings arrachd or fuath-arrachd. A fuath gives birth to the brollachan. The Highland varieties include the beithir, the fideal, the peallaidh, and the ùruisg. The spelling vough is a phonetic anglicization. Related figures appear under arrach and glastig. Folklorists list motifs F420.5.2 and F470.

:::
```
