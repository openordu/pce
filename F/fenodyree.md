``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The fenodyree is a short dark supernatural being of the Isle of Man. MacKillop enters fynnoderee (also spelled finoderee) as a variant spelling of fenodyree. The Manx source names point to hairy stockings. Spellings include fenoderee, phynnodderee, and phynodderree. The being ranks among the best-known Manx ferrishyn. It appears naked apart from a covering of body hair. Like the Scottish brownie, it helps with feats of huge strength. Its labours include moving a massive marble block and harvesting a whole field. For that mowing feat it bears the title yn foldyr gastey, the nimble mower. Tradition says it was once handsome and called uddereek. It wooed a mortal girl of Glen Aldyn. That conduct turned it ugly and solitary. Some writers call it satyr-like for its hairy legs. The label rests on hair rather than any sexual aggression. MacKillop notes the southern Manx glashtin appears identical with the fenodyree. Sir John Rhys printed tales of this creature in his 1891 Oxford folklore volume.

:::
```
