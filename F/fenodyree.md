``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Fenodyree is a cross-reference. It directs the reader to the entry for GLASTIG. MacKillop treats fenodyree as its own subject, a short dark supernatural being from the Isle of Man. Spellings include fenoderee, phynnodderee, and phynodderree, from Manx words for hairy stockings. The creature counts among the best-known Manx ferrishyn and usually appears naked apart from a covering of body hair. Like the Scottish brownie, it helps with tasks of huge strength. Its labours include moving a massive marble block and harvesting a whole field. For that mowing feat it carries the Manx title yn foldyr gastey, meaning the nimble mower. Tradition says the being was once handsome, called uddereek, until it wooed a mortal girl of Glen Aldyn. That conduct turned it ugly and solitary. Some writers call it satyr-like for its hairy legs rather than any sexual aggression. MacKillop notes the southern Manx glashtin appears identical with the fenodyree. Sir John Rhys printed the tales of this creature in his 1891 Oxford folklore volume.

:::
```
