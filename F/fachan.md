``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The fachan, also called the athach, is a Scottish folkloric figure. Scottish Gaelic tales also spell it fachin. Irish tradition knows counterparts of this same grotesque being. MacKillop ranks the fachan as a variety of the better-known athach. He treats the dìreach as a more particular fachan. The creature is grotesquely ugly and wholly supernatural. It has one leg that grows from the haunch. One hand protrudes from its chest. It bears a single eye and rough, spiky hair. That hand gripped a flail thick with iron apples. It struck at passersby while hopping on its one leg. No other monster of the lonely gorges and lochs filled peasants with greater dread. Some accounts class the fachan as a giant. The creature came in two forms, the first being the Athach. The other form, the dìreach, prowled the quiet Glen Eitive. A druid would curse by balancing on one leg and lifting an arm. Some believe the fachan stories grew from fear of that posture. The posture is linked to the Glám Dícenn. Irish lore offers the fbarcaille and the fomorians as likenesses.

:::
```
