``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
fool covers the jesters, buffoons, and mimics who entertained the courts of medieval Ireland, Scotland, and Wales. Conventional figures in early narratives often speak more wisely than their masters, like King Lear's Fool. Lomna reports the adultery of Fionn mac Cumhaill's wife and dies for it. Mac Glas serves as the fool of Máel Fothartaig and dies alongside his master. Do Dera tries to rescue Lugaid mac Con by impersonating the king in battle. Old Irish druth names the professional fool. Modern Irish amadán names the person of poor judgement, a sense English does not carry. Irish and Scottish Gaelic folk tradition honours Amadán Mór as a heroic Big Fool, with Eachtra an Amadáin Mhóir recording his adventure.

:::
```
