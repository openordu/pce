``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Fergus mac Róich stands among the foremost figures of the Ulster Cycle, the great saga cycle of early Ireland, and appears in many of its tales as an older warrior of high standing. Scholars compare him to the wise Greek elder Nestor, and he often serves as spokesman of the warriors of Ulster and keeper of their oral lore. The kingship of Ulster slipped from him to Conchobar thanks to the scheming of Conchobar's mother Nes. Nessa, called the Warrior Woman, married Fergus on condition that her son become a royal heir; after a year she joined his nobles in keeping him from the throne, and Conchobar held the kingship against his army. Fergus long kept much of his old standing and at times acted almost as a joint king: in the decapitation contest in Fled Bricrenn, the sage Sencha exempts Conchobar and Fergus from the axe-wielding stranger's challenge because of their rank. When Deirdre was born, prophecy said her beauty would ruin the land, and Conchobar had her raised away from court. She later fled to Scotland with her lover Noísiu, and Fergus promised them safe passage back to Ireland; as he left the court, Conchobar killed Noísiu and took Deirdre, who then killed herself rather than live on without him. Rage drove Fergus to lead his warriors in burning the stronghold at Emain Macha, and archaeology does show traces of a huge fire at that site. He then rode west to Queen Medb's palace at Cruachan, and the sources say the pair became lovers. The Lia Fáil, a seven-foot stone pillar at Tara, was also called Bod Fhearghais, meaning Fergus's phallus.

:::
```
