``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Fairy sleep is a folkloric motif of a coma-like trance. MacKillop records the Irish name suan sidhe. He frames it as the sleep that holds a person until the fixed hour arrives. A person taken by the fairies might not vanish. Such a person could lapse into a sleep no one could break. One victim, a woman from the Burren country in western Clare, lay in such a state. Her rescuer heard the fairies say they still held the tablecloth that had wrapped her. He took back the cloth, and the woman returned to her waking self. A person who came back from Fairyland often found that a small pin had pierced their clothes. The person slept without waking until someone pulled the pin free.

:::
```
