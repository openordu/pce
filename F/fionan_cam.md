``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
fionan cam was an Irish saint of the early seventh century, honoured as the founder of a monastery at a place then called Cionn Eitigh, the spot known today as Kinnitty in County Offaly. He belonged to the Corca Dhuibhne sept, a people of Kerry, and the sources give his mother the name Becnat. A mediaeval text relates how she bathed in Loch Léin after the sun had set, that is the great Lake of Killarney, and a golden salmon there impregnated her, and by that means was Fionan conceived. His title cam simply meant squint-eyed. An old tradition further held that he was the first person ever to bring wheat to Ireland, carrying the grain from Leatha, which is Latium in Italy, inside his shoe. More than one version of his life exists, the fullest a late work written in Irish. In that text his mother grew pregnant through a dream in which a golden fish descended into her mouth, and over her whole term neither rain nor snow ever fell upon her. Fionan as a young man showed great knowledge and marvellous powers, and this so impressed St Bréanainn that the saint told him to leave Kerry, travel north, and raise a church at the place where he should come across a drove of wild swine. Setting out on that journey, he chose Kinnitty as the home of his new community. The closing part of the text records many marvels, above all cures worked upon people and beasts alike who were hurt or unwell, and some of these episodes were taken over from the life-stories of other holy men. A separate tradition recounts how he drove a pestilence far away from Uibh Rathach, in the south-western reach of County Kerry. He came to be confused with a namesake called Fionan Lobhar, whose title meant the Leper, a man who likewise dwelt near Killarney. His feast is held on the seventh of April, though in Kerry it was kept upon the sixteenth of March.
```
