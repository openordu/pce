``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Fawn names the young of the deer. Older catalog notes sent readers from fawn to the DEER entry. The fawn held great sway in the early Celtic imagination, as deer and stag lore shows. Lugaid Laigde of the Érainn chased a fawn on the hunt. Tradition reads that quarry as Ireland itself in divine form. Magic changed Aige and Sadb into fawns. Donn mac Midir dressed a third transformed woman in fawn shape. The disguise lured Fionn mac Cumhaill and his band. Some fawn figures are male, like Oisín the son of Fionn. The name oisín remains the ordinary Irish term for the fawn. The Perceval legend keeps a fawn ahead of the stag as the older form. Folk etymology wrongly glosses Uisnech as a place of the fawn. Old Irish and Modern Irish give oisín. Scottish Gaelic has laogh féidh and fiadh òg, and Manx has minjeig. Welsh has elain and hydd ifanc, Cornish yorghyk, and Breton menn-karvez.

:::
```
