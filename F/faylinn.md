::: magazinestyle
faylinn is a short MacKillop headword marked as an archaic word. MacKillop notes that observers often presumed the term to be of Celtic origin. In practice the derivation of the word remains obscure and is not firmly resolved in the dictionary entry. Despite that uncertainty, the entry fixes the sense of faylinn as the designation for the realm of the fairy. The article thus functions less as a linguistic study than as a folklore gloss, anchoring the older word to the Otherworld concept shared across the insular Celtic traditions. MacKillop offers no narrative example, leaving faylinn as a single-line pointer to the broader cluster of terms for fairyland.

:::
