::: magazinestyle
Female warriors and champions is a MacKillop list-style headword that gathers assertive women from Celtic record, myth, and legend. The opening places these figures against the Brehon Laws of Ireland. The note credits those laws with allowing powerful women to rise. Aífe is singled out as the toughest woman recorded. Bec of Connacht stands among those honoured. Boudicca, the historical British fighter later called Boadicea in romance, takes a place. Cathach Catutchenn is mentioned as one who loved Cúchulainn. Coinchenn is listed as a fearsome warrior woman. Creidne appears as a fighting champion of Fionn's warrior band. Eis Enchenn is named as a rival of Cúchulainn. Erc sits in the roster of Fionn's band. Fedelm Noichrothach is marked for her looks. Granuaile, the Irish sea captain Gráinne Ní Mháille, is cited from the sixteenth century. Medb, driver of the Táin Bó Cuailnge, holds a leading line. Scáthach is named as Cúchulainn's trainer. The beast Luchtigem is said to have died at a woman's hand. The cross-references direct readers to Luideag, the sovereignty lady note, and to books by Fraser and Green.

:::
