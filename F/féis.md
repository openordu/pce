``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Féis is a cross-reference that directs the reader to the Feast entry. MacKillop records féis, with plural feiseanna, from an Irish verb meaning to sleep with. Early Irish gave the word as many as six meanings. English usage keeps two main senses. One sense names a feast or celebration, chiefly for a royal marriage. Such a feast could also stage a symbolic union with a sovereignty figure. Leading examples ran at Cruachain, at Emain Macha, and above all at Tara. The other sense names a musical contest on the Welsh eisteddfod model. The Feis Cheoil began at Dublin in 1897 as a promoter of Irish music. MacKillop distinguishes féis from fair, féil, and festival. The entry also routes related titles: Feis Charmain goes to Carman, and Feis Temro goes to Tara and kingship.

:::
```
