::: magazinestyle
fear liath, also given as leea and fear Hath in MacKillop, is a malevolent fairy of Hiberno-Irish tradition. The headword is glossed from Irish fear liath, meaning grey man, and the creature serves as a personification of fog. The entry casts fear liath as a wandering menace that covers land and sea with a heavy mantle. Roads grow dark so travellers stumble unaware over cliffs and fall to their deaths. The fog also obscures rocks along the shore so ships crash upon them. MacKillop places the figure among the dangerous fairy beings of Irish lore, alongside other weather-borne hazards drawn from oral tradition. The article treats fear liath less as a named character than as an elemental force given human shape.

:::
