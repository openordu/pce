``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Folk tales and legends reach the Irish ear through the spoken word. The Gaelic people wrote little prose of the imagination before the new faith crossed the sea. Scholars later sort the old stories into sets with formal names. These labels come from the study and not from the tellers. The speech of each region carries its own term for a tale. The terms do not always match between the neighbors. In the late 1800s the collection of Irish tales grew. One collector traveled to the island and reached it in 1887. The New York Sun paper printed his tales first. A bound book came next. Only the collector and a few storytellers appear by name in the pages. Many people shared the unseen work of the collection. The teller of one tale said his words came in the Irish voice. His father matched them into the English tongue for the collector and stood as the source. The collector's wife also took part in the task.

:::
```
