::: magazinestyle
Féth fiada is an Old Irish term. The headword lists variant forms feth fìadha, fé fiada, and faeth fiadha. The phrase points to the article on the Druid Fog. MacKillop frames it as a cross-reference entry. In the Old Irish tradition the féth fiada was the mist that druids conjured around themselves and their companions. The fog hid a sacred party from mortal sight. Heroes and kings sometimes moved under the féth fiada. The fog belonged to the Old Irish magical vocabulary. Its modern Irish descendant form is fé fiadha. Variant spellings include feth fìadha and faeth fiadha, recorded in MacKillop's entry. The entry stands as a pointer toward the full Druid Fog article, where the broader tradition sits.

:::
