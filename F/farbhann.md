farbhann, also written farvann, names a creature of Hebridean oral tradition that MacKillop records under this headword. Etymology links the word to Scottish Gaelic farbkonn (the inner sole of a shoe) and to farbheann (a mountain with cliffs), though the folkloric sense has drifted from these literal origins. The entry describes farbhann as the name given to a fairy dog. By tradition, a farbhann was unleashed on Hugh MacLeod of Raasay after he stole a prized vessel from the realm of the fairy. MacKillop frames the tale as a clear instance of the fairy exacting payment for theft. The article belongs to the wider cluster of Hebridean folk beliefs about enchanted hounds and fairy retaliation.

:::
