``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Mass media in Ireland serve a minority Irish-speaking audience across many outlets. The television channel TG4, at first called Teilifis na Gaeilge, began broadcasting in 1996. It runs about seven hours a day, five of them in Irish, covering sport, drama, current affairs, and children's programmes. RTÉ, the national public service broadcaster, carries close to one hour of Irish-language output each week, most of it news and current affairs. The radio service Raidió na Gaeltachta started in 1972 to serve the Gaeltacht and Irish speakers everywhere. It broadcasts wholly through Irish around the clock. Raidió na Life began in 1993 as a Dublin service on an educational and community basis, and its programmes also reach listeners on the Internet. The newspaper Lá is Ireland's only daily Irish-language paper. It began as a weekly broadsheet and grew into a daily tabloid sold nationwide from April 2003.

:::
```
