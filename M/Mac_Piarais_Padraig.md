``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Mac Piarais, the name under which Patrick Pearse (1879–1916) is known in Irish, was an educationalist, author, and revolutionary whose fame rests chiefly on leading the 1916 Easter Rising and on the execution that followed. His nationalism at first ran cultural rather than political, since he held that Ireland's part in the world lay in keeping its distinctiveness instead of pursuing independence, and he admired the Irish-speaking folk of Conamara in the west of Galway. Drawn young into the Gaelic League, known as Conradh na Gaeilge, he edited its paper An Claidheamh Soluis from 1903 to 1909 and faulted the national schools for the decline of Irish culture, trusting that the country could shed English influence through schooling. In that spirit he set up St Enda's, a bilingual academy open from 1908 to foster all things Irish. Resistance to home rule later radicalized him, and he came to believe in force and a sacrifice of blood to stir the nation. He aided the founding of the Irish Volunteers in 1913, enlisted in the Irish Republican Brotherhood, and took the role of Commander-in-Chief through the Rising.

:::
```
