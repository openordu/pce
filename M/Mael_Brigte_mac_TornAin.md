``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Mael Brigte mac Tornáin headed the abbey at Armagh and later held the same office on Iona. His kin formed the Cenél Conaill, one part of the Northern Úí Néill, like the founder Colum Cille did. He was schooled at Armagh and took up its abbacy in 888. When the abbot of Iona passed away, Mael Brigte also took that seat in 891. That double office reveals the rivalries in the Armagh succession. It also shows the ruin of Iona, which the Viking raids had gutted, so the island sat nearly empty. Mael Brigte thus did most of his work at home in Ireland. He took part in fighting between tribes in 893. In 913 he ransomed a traveler from the Britons while in Munster. An ornate gospel book carries the name of this man under the Latin form Mac Durnan. That volume now rests in London, at a library tied to Lambeth Palace. King Æthelstan of the English gave it to Christ Church, Canterbury, in the final year of his rule, 939. The records of Ulster note Mael Brigte passing in 927. They honor him as the successor of Patrick and of Colum Cille. His name Mael Brigte is made of two old Celtic pieces and means the tonsured devotee of Saint Brigit. That name shows how warmly Brigit was held inside the northern territories where Patrick and Colum Cille were strong. Mael Brigte stands as the bridge that linked Armagh and Iona in one office.
```
