``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Myrddin is the Welsh hero whom later storytellers shape into the magician Merlin. He is a leader whose mind breaks in the horror of war, at the fight the old verses name Arfderydd, and he flees to live in the wild wood as a madman, kin in his sickness to the Irish king Suibhne. Out of that grief his voice turns to prophecy. Hidden in the forest he sees the pains that are not yet come, and his words carry the many journeys of Wales. His poems sit beside the book of the Black one and the book of the red one; they hold the hope of a deliverer and the end of the turning of the rule. Geoffrey of Monmouth turned this wild, ragged figure into the Merlin of his famous books. In the Prophetia Merlini and the later Vita Merlini the label Merlin first takes its lasting shape. He set the seer's gifts at the heart of Arthuriana, so that the madman of the wood becomes the great magician of the later tales. The seer, bound and wise in one skin, speaks and waits beneath open sky for the age of quiet that he saw in a vision.

:::
```
