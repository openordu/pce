``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Mórrígan ranks among the greatest goddesses of ancient Ireland. She belonged to the Tuatha Dé Danann, the people of the goddess Danu. She ruled the realm of war and of death in early Irish thought. Her very name carries the sense of great queen or phantom queen. Storytellers usually spoke of her as the Mórrígan in full. She formed one of a trio of war goddesses named the Mórrígna. Her kin in that set were Badb and Macha. Some lists add Nemain to that number as well. She could change her shape at will without great effort. She appeared as a white cow with red ears or a crow. She could show herself as an eel, a gray-red wolf, or a huge woman. That woman could foretell the fate of warriors in battle. In bird form she swooped over the field and took the fallen. In the Táin she merged a warlike temper with an inviting charm. Many read her as an emanation of the earth goddess Ana. She held the gift of prophecy and could cast spells. She could pass from a lovely young girl into a fierce hag. People tied her closely to the crow of the battlefield. They said she lived in the yawning cave of Cruachain. That same cave had long been home to queen Medb. As Badb and Macha, she shared the single mother Ernmas. Her bond with the Dagda was widely spoken of in the tales. At Samain's great feast she washed at the River Unshin. After their union she foretold the raid of the Fomorians. She warned of the second clash upon the plain of Mag Tuired. She then vexed the Fomorian warrior Indech in that strife. Her only son was Meiche, a child whose father is never named. She sang her people to victory on the plain of Mag Tuired. In the cattle raid she turned patron of Connacht. From Cruachain she watched over the province of Connacht. She gave her favours to the hero Cú Chulainn and met a blunt refusal. She came to him as a sea eel, then a wolf, then a red heifer. He broke the eel ribs, yet she returned again and again. At last she came down as a hooded crow onto his shoulder. That image stood as a sign his corpse would soon be stripped. She had one son, Meiche, whose heart held three serpents. Mac Cécht killed him and burned the heart to ash. Two hills beside Brú na Bóinne bear the name of her paps.
```
