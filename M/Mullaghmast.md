::: magazinestyle
Mullaghmast, also written Mullagh Mast and Mullamast, is a hill of 563 feet one mile west of Ballitore, Co. Kildare, ground that carries both myth and archaeology. In nearby Glenn Treithim the smith-god Goibniu was thought to keep his forge, and copper ore dug here in ancient times went into sword and spear heads. A standing stone that once stood on the hill, now held in the National Museum, bears a three-armed triskele; commentators split over whether it served the pagan goddess Brigit or shows very early Christian art. The ráth of Mullaghmast is said to hold Gearóid Óg, the 11th Earl of Kildare, who rides out once every seven years around that Kildare plain with silver shoes on his mount, a story easy to tangle with the one told of Gerald, Earl Desmond the 3rd of that name. Two modern events weigh heaviest in folk memory. On New Year's Day 1577, English forces and their O'Dempsey allies killed the chiefs of Laoighis and Uí Fháilghe here by treachery, and folk etymology blames that deed on the adventurer Maiste, the man whose name stuck to Mullach Maisten, for leading those Irishmen to their deaths. On 1 October 1843 Daniel O'Connell held a Monster Meeting on the hill.
