``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Meath is the English name for the province of Mide. The modern county of Meath covers nine hundred and three square miles. It lies in the Republic of Ireland. It sits north and west of County Dublin. The county is not identical with the ancient Mide. It is not coextensive with that older territory either. The name Meath comes down from Mide. The word Mide carries two meanings: neck and middle. Ancient Ireland knew four provinces. Those provinces were Leinster, Munster, Connacht, and Ulster. Mide stood apart as the invisible fifth. The Indo-European world view placed a mysterious center beyond the four physical districts. Mide filled the five provinces as their spiritual center. It was a place not fully of this world. It joined the idea of sovereignty to the physical soil. That joining took place at the Stone of Divisions on the hill of Uisneach. The cracked boulder gave a fixed point to the whole island. In later centuries the name took on a concrete county. Between the eighth and eleventh centuries the land lay inside a petty kingdom called Brega. Meath holds a great number of the best-known sites of early Irish civilization. The hill of Tara stands at its heart. The passage graves of Brug na Bóinne rise beside the Boyne. The assembly sites of Tailtiu and Tlachtga also fall within the county. Much of the Boyne valley runs through Meath. A later county took the name and kept the political weight. That county held Tara, the ancient capital of Ireland. Meath thus joins a mythic center to a real region. It carries both the sacred idea and the everyday map of Ireland.

:::
```
