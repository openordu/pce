``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Mac an t-Saoir, Donnchadh Bàn fought at Falkirk in 1746. He glorified the Campbell aristocracy across his life, yet he never became a Whig. His cautious politics later gained him a name as a plain singer of songs. He kept game on the hills of Argyllshire and stayed there until 1766. When sheep farming reached that region, he moved to Edinburgh, where the City Guard gave him employment. Since he could not read or write, he recited his poems to the Reverend Donald MacNicol, who wrote them down. Printers issued his song collection in 1768, and six more editions followed. His surviving verse exceeds 6000 lines. Moladh Beinn Dobhrain praises Ben Doran, while Oran Coire a' Cheathaich pictures the misty corrie. His writing gives sharp images of wild nature but reaches further, across satire, bawdry, humour, drinking songs, verses in praise of female beauty, and keen comment on the age.

:::
```
