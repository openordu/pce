``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Mona is another name of Anglesey, a sacred British and Welsh island. The island has used the official Welsh name Ynys Môn since 1995. Earlier, from 1284 to 1974, it formed a separate county called sir Fôn. The narrow Menai Straits split it from the Welsh mainland. Two bridges now cross that water. The soil stays fertile and habitable across the island. Yet the rugged Snowdonia massif cuts it off from easy overland travel. Seen from the sea, the island stands at a crossroads across the Irish Sea. That crossroads joins Wales, Ireland, the Isle of Man, and south-west Scotland. A ferry still runs from Anglesey toward Dublin. In AD 60 the first strike into north Wales fell upon this island. The Roman historian Tacitus tied that move to the druids' anti-Roman ideology. That ideology held deep roots on the island. Romans tore down the sacred groves. Druids had offered human sacrifice in those woods. Rich watery deposits at Llyn Cerrig Bach reveal a long history. Pre-Roman druids kept an island sanctuary there from early times. Anglesey's bogs and lakes have yielded votive troves in Celtic design. Druids hid the troves during the Roman assault. Druids ran a college on the island. Celtic women priestesses, robed in black, screamed in the manner of the raven-goddess Mórrígan. The priests fought on the shore beside them. Romans elsewhere had already cut sacred groves and struck down the clergy. The druids of Anglesey knew their homes and their sacred trees faced ruin. They died in battle or were taken and executed. The great forests burned to the ground. The island held sacred status even before the Celts. An ancient cairn rises on the hill of Brangwen Corneth. Diana Paxson wrote of the destruction in her Arthurian trilogy. The name Mona itself carries a muddled history. It once applied to the Isle of Man as well as to Anglesey. Some early lists also attach it to part of the Scottish coast. The same name also reaches a second Irish Sea island, probably Arran. Popular speech now restricts the name to the Isle of Man. The nickname Mona's Isle belongs to that island alone. Scholars tie the word to forms such as monapia and menavia. Those older forms mark a name older than the Roman records. The same root echoes across the sea-lanes that link the two islands and the coast.

:::
```
