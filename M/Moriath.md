``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Moriath, also written Muiriath, was an Irish heroine. Her name means Sealand. She was a princess of ancient Ireland. The king of Munster, in the far southwestern corner of the island, stood as her father. Her story began when the high king Cobthach killed his own brother Labhraigh Lorc. He then poisoned Aillil Aine, the true king. Aillil's son, Labhraigh Moen, was soon banished from the kingdom. He allied himself with Cobthach's enemy, Moriath's father, Scoriath. The moment Labhraigh set eyes on Moriath, he fell in love with her. Her father kept his daughter closely guarded. So Labhraigh enlisted the help of his illustrious harper, Craiphtine. Craiphtine played so sweetly that the entire court fell into blissful sleep, letting the lovers pledge their troth. When Labhraigh had to fight Cobthach, his harper came again to the rescue. He played lulling melodies so that the enemy army all went to sleep. Labhraigh's army, prepared with earplugs, stayed wide awake. Upon ascending to the throne of his own region, Labhraigh invited Cobthach to a dinner. He trapped Cobthach in an iron house and burned him to death. Some versions have Moriath as the wooer. Her own harper acts as the miraculous musician. He bore love poems and messages to Labhraigh, who became king with her help. The harper's music drives the whole tale forward, opening doors that force could never open. MacKillop notes that the name Moriath is simply a variant spelling of Muiriath. So the same princess appears under both forms in the histories. Either way she stands as a celebrated heroine of the Munster tales. Her beauty drew a king across many battles, and her cunning turned a stranger's rescue into a lasting romance.

:::
```
