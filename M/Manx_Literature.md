``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Manx literature is the writing produced in the Manx language. Its foundation is the Manx Bible. Diverse hands completed this translation project in 1775. The Bible set a written standard and became a supreme source for Manx. Other religious texts soon followed. These include a translation of Bunyan's Pilgrim's Progress and many homilies, catechisms, and sermons. The carvals make up the chief body of original Manx work. These religious songs are unique to the island. After the Bible, original writing entered a fallow period. The next phase was language preservation through grammar and dictionary work. Douglas C. Fargher compiled an ambitious English to Manx dictionary. Scholars also recorded the last native speakers. Since the 1970s interest has grown. Support from official and private sources encouraged new original works, translations, and reprints. John Clague collected Manx folklore and reminiscences in 1911. His book, Cooinaghtyn Manninagh, remains a landmark of the revival.

:::
```
