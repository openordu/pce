``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Monasticism in Ireland first appears in written form in the Confessio of Patrick. Real monasteries and their founders multiply from the later sixth century. Bangor and Clonard are leading houses, and their founders Comgall and Finnian direct the work. Irish monasticism keeps the desert ascetic tone of the eastern tradition. It weds the life of eremitic solitaries to coenobitic community living. Monks read the ideal of the desert through the writings of Cassian. Adomnán's Vita Columbae and the Navigatio Sancti Brendani both describe this monastic world. The later Céli Dé movement brings fresh ascetic reform. In time monasteries become economic centres and seats of learning. They also grow into dynastic religious administrations.

:::
```
