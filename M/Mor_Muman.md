``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Mór Muman, also written Mór Mumhan, is a territorial goddess of early southern Ireland, her name drawn from Muman, of Munster. She carries ties to the sun's power and to sovereignty, and tradition judged her beauty so great that Irish women were measured against her. Her origin stays deeply mysterious; perhaps she served the Érainn people as a goddess before writing arrived. The record takes her as apparently identical with Mugain (1), and she feeds the characterization of Medb and of Mórrígan. In time she served as patroness of Munster as a whole and of the powerful Eóganacht dynasty above all, at moments taking the name Mumain, or Mugain alone. Medieval scribes tried to historicize her with strange gifts. She knows exaltation and frenzy, lives a while under enchantment, hears voices, can fly, and once wanders Ireland two years in rags. More telling for a land goddess, tradition thinks she shared the beds of known historical figures. At Cashel she wins over the spouse of the reigning king Fingein mac Áeda and lies with the king. She carries a son, Sechnasach, then flees before the birth on hearing voices, and Fingein dies soon after. She later visits the two remaining Munster capitals of that age, Cnoc Áine in Co. Limerick and Glendamain in north-east Co. Cork. Her name stayed alive in Munster's oral tradition and in proverbs, where her children are thought to meet misfortune, and she and Mugain (1) survive in place-names.

:::
```
