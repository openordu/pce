``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Maine, also written Maini in the plural and Mane in English, is a name carried by many people in early Irish story. Most of them were legendary warriors, founders of dynasties, or saints. The best known are the seven sons of the queen Medb and her husband Ailill. A druid warned the queen that a son of that name would kill her enemy Conchobar. The seven were already born, so the parents renamed them all Maine. Each might add an agnomen such as Maine Andai. The brothers sometimes blur into one another and their name wanders into other tales. Yet no figure named Maine slew Conchobar mac Nessa. The prophecy in fact fit a different Conchobar, a son of an Arthur of Britain, and Maine Andai did kill him. A later redactor may have misread his sources. One Maine, first called Sin and later Maine Mórghor, courted the maiden Fearbh with a band of warriors. His druid saw a bad omen in a rising wind. A woman of the Otherworld led Conchobar to the tryst, where the whole host fell. Later the Maini appear as outlaws bound to the pirate Ingcél, raiding Britain and entering Ireland in the sack of Da Derga's hostel. Elsewhere in the Ulster stories they serve as Medb's men. A son of Conchobar also bore the name and fell to Dubthach. A Norse prince called Maine killed Noísiu in some tellings of the Deirdre tale in revenge for the slaying of his kin. Another Maine fought in the Fianna of Fionn. Maine Mílbél the honey-mouth was a Munster hero, and Maine Mór mac Echach founded the Uí Maine of Galway and Roscommon.


:::
```
