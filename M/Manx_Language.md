``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Manx language is the Celtic tongue of the Isle of Man. It belongs to the Gaelic branch, close to Irish and to Scottish Gaelic. Manx gained a written form built on an English spelling. That spelling reached print in 1700. A related system appeared about 1610 in the work of John Phillips, bishop of Man. Manx kept several ancient Gaelic sounds. It never added a separate vowel between a pair of letters such as r and l. It shares curious traits with an early Scots prayer book. Manx shows the change where a group such as -sc became -st, written sht or st. It also shows the trait called pre-occlusion. In that trait a stressed syllable that closes on a nasal gains a small stop after it. Its grammar moved far from the steady Gaelic pattern. The result carries a shape like a creole. The word ag fused with the verb in many forms. The tongue still holds a place inside the wider Gaelic family.

:::
```
