``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Mugain is an Irish heroine in the old tales. She was a sister of the great queen Medb of Connaught. In one story she became the mother of Aed Slane, a hero and king. Before that birth she had to learn to treat other women well. She was a co-wife of the king of the high plain of Tara, sharing him with a woman named Mairenn. Mugain burned with envy over a fine gold headdress that Mairenn always wore. Suspecting her rival hid something, Mugain paid a member of the court to pull it down and shame her. The plan fell back on her. The moment the headdress fell, fine gold hair lay plain on Mairenn's head. Mugain had to give birth to a lamb. She then bore a salmon before she could bring forth a human child. Yet the name does not belong to a single woman alone. MacKillop lists several bearers. One Mugain, also called Mugain Mór, is a goddess who watched over the southern part of Ireland. An old account says a trout came from her. This goddess matches the better-known Mór Muman. Another Mugain is the wife of Conchobar mac Nessa, who ruled Ulster. She was the daughter of Eochaid Feidlech and a sister of Medb. She and her maidens stripped naked before Cúchulainn as he came home from a fight. His road led toward Emain Macha. The women acted to cool his battle fever, yet the sight fired him so much that three vats of icy water could barely calm him. Still another Mugain is the adulterous wife of Diarmait mac Cerbaill. She became the mother of Áed Sláine, the ancestor of the people of Brega. So a name that begins with one heroine opens out into a row of queens, a goddess, and a mother of kings. Some books join the old heroine with the goddess who ruled the south of Ireland. The bond between the earthly women and the divine one keeps the name alive across the whole island of the tales.

:::
```
