``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Mael Coluim mac Donnchada ruled Scotland as Malcolm III. His byname Canmore comes from Ceann Mór. That phrase means big head or great chief. He lived from about 1031 to 1093. He founded the line of kings called the Canmore dynasty. The eldest child of Duncan I was this very man. His father had died in 1040 under the later famous Macbeth. Macbeth fell in battle in 1057, and Mael Coluim then took the Scottish crown. His reign was the longest of the whole eleventh century. The crown stayed in his family until 1290. His second wife was Margaret, an Anglo-Saxon princess who died in 1093. She carried Continental and English ways into the Scottish court. Her deep piety, shaped in newly converted Hungary, changed Scottish worship. The church set her down a saint in 1250. She became the subject of what is likely the first life of a Scottish ruler. They raised six sons and two daughters. Three sons came to the Scottish crown in turn. Edgar ruled 1097 to 1107. He was followed by Alexander I from 1107 to 1124, and after that David I through 1153. One daughter, called Edith at first and later Maud or Matilda, rose to be England's queen. The remaining daughter, Mary, gave birth to England's Queen Maud.

:::
```
