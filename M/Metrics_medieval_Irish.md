::: magazinestyle
Metrics, medieval Irish describe the metrical system of early Irish verse. The rules appear in metrical tracts edited by Thurneysen. One tract, Córus Bard cona Bairdni, ranks the bards and their poetic work. The verse of the seven bardic grades mirrors the ranks of the learned filid. Rhyming syllabic metres dominate, and four-line stanzas are common. The alliterative accentual metre rosc stands apart as the right of the filid. One tract shows over forty metrical types. Another sets the student-file curriculum from seven years to ten and finally to twelve. A later tract sorts metres as usual, moderately usual, not well known, and uncommon. It cites around two hundred examples.

:::
