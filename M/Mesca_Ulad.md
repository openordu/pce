``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Mesca Ulad is the Irish name of a tale of the Ulster of the old. The title
means the wild kind the whole people grew. The tale lives in the twelfth among the
leaves of the book of a vessel. The book of the river of the old also held it near. The
story tells of the feast that threw the men of Ulster to folly. The teller does not
follow a single narrow plot. He pours out long pictures and long lists of the names of
men and fields. That manner was the trade of the step of the medieval tellers. The
time of the tale is the turn of the closing of the year. That turn brings wild joy and
wild unrest to the land. The men of Ulster, the heroes, show their weak side in the
film. Two calls for a feast reach them at once. The Ulstermen welcome the sign of their
blood and come to both. They ride the first hours far to the north. They reach the red
mound of the gate of the wave. The place is the Dun of the two of the north. It sits in
the bounds of the later county of the salt of the north. Then they turn to cross all
Ulster in the night. They want to pass the late hours at the fort of their first
soldier. The place is the Dun of the arrow, now the town called the fort of the east.
On the road they lose the sure mill. The page lights to the famous striding of the
wetted riders to the south. They ride to the far province of the foe.
The men of the south, the old foe of the province, meet them to the cold tide. They
serve the red drink and a warm welcome to the northmen. The wily host has no honor in
the room. The southerns build a house of iron in a frame of good wood. They drop great
wood under the floor for the flame of the door. One turn of the fire takes every breath
inside. Yet the letters of the old text spare the lives of the band. The northern
warriors walk from the fire, scarred but whole, to the road.

:::
```
