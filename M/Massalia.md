``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Massalia names the Greek trading city on the Gaulish coast that today bears the form Marseille. Its seafarers kept a lost sailing booklet, the Massaliote Periplus, that guided ships along the shore. The Latin poet Rufus Festus Avienius drew on that manual for his poem Ora Maritima in the later fourth century. The work preserves two Greek names of great age for the Celtic lands. One form, Iernoi, applies to the people of Ireland. A second form, Albionej, marks the island of Britain. Both words rank among the oldest on record for the Celtic inhabitants. Carthaginian rule over the Strait of Gibraltar around 550 BC anchors the dating. This reveals a time older than the voyage of Pytheas near 325 BC.

:::
```
