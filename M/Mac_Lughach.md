``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Mac Lughach is a Fenian hero, grandson of Fionn mac Cumhaill, and known for laziness. Variants give his name as Lughaid(h) or Lewy, and some texts call him Gaoine or Caol, sometimes with the epithet of the Terrible Hand. Fenian literature does not show him until the twelfth century. One early text calls him son of Lugaid Lága, but tradition usually makes him the son of Fionn's daughter Lugach. Mongfhind trained the boy. He showed little early prowess and got on poorly with fellow students. In later life he displayed great strength and musicianship. Fionn's counsel to this grandson circulates through the Fenian tales: keep clear of fools, and treat women, servants, poets, and common folk with gentleness. Tradition says he raised the memorial stones of Ireland for Tuadh, his first love. Later storytellers count Mac Lughach among the companions Fionn trusted most.

:::
```
