::: magazinestyle
Mongfhind, also written Mongfind, Mongfhinn, Mong-Finn, Mongfhionn, Moingfhionn, and Moingionn, is an Irish female name that means fair-haired. Several women of early Ireland carry it, and pedigrees cite it often. The best-known bearer, Mongfhind (1), is the wife of Eochaid Mugmedón and the jealous stepmother of Niall Noigiallach. She drives the boy's mother, the bondswoman Cairenn, into heavy labour. Yet in the tale Echtra Mac nEchach Muigmedóin, Niall wins the kingship by embracing a sovereignty goddess, and so he rises ahead of Mongfhind's four sons. Many records treat her as mortal, a sister of the king Crimthann Mór mac Fidig, but the pattern of her cult suggests an older divinity. Prayers once asked her at Halloween to keep evil away, above all among the women of Munster. Storytellers explained that custom with her death at Samain time by poison meant for Niall. A second bearer of the name, Mongfhind (2), nurses and teaches the Fianna Éireann, and her pupils include Diarmait Ua Duibne, Giona mac Lugha, and Mac Lughach.
