``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Maredudd ab Owain died in 1265. He was a Welsh prince and the son of Owain ap Gruffudd, who died in 1235. Maredudd took his share of the territory that Rhys ap Gruffudd, named Lord Rhys, had held. A partition in 1216 split that land, and Maredudd came to rule a broad estate in Ceredigion. He joined the strife in Deheubarth from 1244 to 1246 together with Dafydd ap Llywelyn, who ruled Gwynedd. King Henry III tried to win his loyalty. Later he stood with Llywelyn ap Gruffudd when that prince moved into Deheubarth in 1256. He stayed a steady force in the province. The chronicle Brut y Tywysogyon praised him as the man guarding all Deheubarth and guiding all Wales. The poet Y Prydydd Bychan mourned him. Gruffudd, his son, and Efa, his daughter, funded two prose translations. In all he left three sons, Gruffudd, Cynan and Owain. His grandson Llywelyn ab Owain alone kept a piece of the family lands after the conquest. Maredudd ranks among traditional masculine Welsh names. Old Welsh genealogies give Margetiut and Morgetiud for a Dyfed king who died at Rhuddlan in 797.

:::
```
