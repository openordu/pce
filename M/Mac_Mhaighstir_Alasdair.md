::: magazinestyle
Mac Mhaighstir Alasdair, the poet known in English as Alexander MacDonald, lived from about 1698 to 1770 and is widely counted the foremost Scottish Gaelic poet of the eighteenth century. His verse ranges from formal odes and Jacobite propaganda to nature poetry and even sexually explicit pieces, and he stands among the notable innovators of Gaelic literature of his age. Raised by a clergyman father in the Episcopalian faith, he became a Presbyterian in the 1720s and a Roman Catholic around 1745, yet his deepest commitment was to the Jacobite cause; he wrote an account of the 1745 Rising and left a record of his part in it. While teaching for the SSPCK he drew up an early Gaelic–English vocabulary and a selection of his own poems. His later eagerness to join Bonnie Prince Charlie's campaign ended his teaching career, and afterwards he composed verse urging support for the Stuart cause.

:::
