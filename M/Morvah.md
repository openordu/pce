``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Morvah is a granite moor six miles north-west of Penzance, on the north-west slope of the farthest headland of Land's End, Cornwall. Its ground carries distinctive megaliths, and each August a festival like Lughnasa met there. The best-known monument is the doughnut-shaped Men-an-tol, a holed stone that once framed the entrance of a chambered tomb and won a name for healing in local belief. During the 1800s the fair claimed three acres of the moor for itself, meeting each 1 August with horse-racing, courtship games, and tales of Jack the Tinkard. The legend of the moor opens when Tom, a local giant, brings down an evil giant with an axle-beam and cartwheel, then takes over the fallen one's castle and wealth, marries, and raises a family. Jack the Tinkard arrives after him, hostile at first and in time an ally. As with the Irish champion Lug Lámfhota, Jack commands many skills, and Tom lets him marry his daughter. To house his bride, Jack slays the moor's resident giant by removing the cover of an old mineshaft so the man falls in. The wedding of Jack and the joining of the two families fall on 1 August.

:::
```
