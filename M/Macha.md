``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Macha is an Irish goddess of the land, the battlefield, and the horse. The great capital of Ulster in the north of Ireland was named Emain Macha, Twins of Macha, in her honour. In one tale she came to the farm of the widower Crunniuc from nowhere, or from the Otherworld, and made her home there. She helped the farm grow rich and bore his child. Like many fairy brides she set one condition, that her husband must never boast of her. At the Assembly of Ulster he saw the king's chariot and horses and could not hold his tongue, saying his wife could outrun them. The king demanded proof. Macha, heavy with child, was forced to race. She won easily but fell into labour as she crossed the line and died bearing twins, a girl and a boy. With her last breath she cursed the men of Ulster, that at any great danger they would lie weak as a woman in childbirth for five days, for nine generations. That doom is the Debility of the Ulstermen. Two further figures bear her name, perhaps one being split into three. The first was wife of Nemed and a prophetess. For her the first plains were cleared of forest. The third was Macha Mong Ruad, Red-hair Macha, who wed a rival king to take the throne and forced the sons of a slain coruler to raise the fortress now called Navan Fort. Her horselike nature ties her to the Celtic mare-goddess Epona and to Rhiannon of the Welsh Mabinogion, a pan-Celtic horse goddess.
```
