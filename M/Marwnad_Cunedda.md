``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Marwnad Cunedda is the Death-song of Cunedda, a praise poem of a north British chief in the early post-Roman period. Welsh story knows Cunedda best as the man who began Gwynedd's royal line and fathered many kings and saints. Yet the poem never names Wales. It calls Cunedda the leader of the men of Brynaich, a region in north Britain that passed under Anglo-Saxon rule in the mid-sixth century. The poem shows combat at two Roman fort towns south of Hadrian's Wall, the fort on the river Wear and the fort called Luguvalium at Carlisle. It names the leaders of those enemy lands as the descendants of Coel Hen. Archaic language in the text reaches behind the oldest layers of the Gododdin. The poem lists slaves and olive oil among Cunedda's gifts, items rare in Welsh praise poetry. The first line credits Taliesin, but the last word Coeling closes a ring with the fourth line, so the link may be set aside. Scholars long debated the poem. Morris-Jones accepted Taliesin, while Ifor Williams called it a fraud no older than the ninth or tenth century. The text survives in the Book of Taliesin.

:::
```
