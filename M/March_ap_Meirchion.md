``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
March ap Meirchion, in the variant form vab Meirchiawn, is an early Welsh legendary hero. The Welsh march means horse, and the name plays against the story attached to him. He stands behind the Arthurian King Mark as an antecedent.

The Welsh Triads make March one of the island's Three Seafarers. Historical records make him the son of Glamorgan's king, a ruler who granted lands to Saint Illtud. Allusions to March appear in the dream-tale Breuddwyd Rhonabwy and in the gnomic grave-poetry of the Englynion y Beddau. More recent folklore places him at Castell March in Llyn, Caernarvonshire, in north-western Wales.

There he shares with the Irish king Labraid Loingsech the affliction of horse's ears, a pun on his own name. Shame makes him grow his hair long over them, and he puts his barbers to death to protect the secret.

In Welsh Arthurian story March rules Cornwall as the king caught between Tristan and Iseult. The international King Mark draws on other antecedents besides him, but the Welsh hero remains the clearest native source. John James Jones's 1932 study of March in Aberystwyth Studies stays the standard treatment.

:::
```
