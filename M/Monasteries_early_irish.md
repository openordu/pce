::: magazinestyle
Monasteries, early Irish ranged from large foundations to small western sites. Glendalough in County Wicklow and Durrow were among the largest. Smaller coastal houses such as Kildreelig and Gallarus were often timber-built. A circular earth or stone wall, the vallum, enclosed the compound. Some walls had two or three concentric rings, as at Nendrum. The central church was the focus of religious life. The earliest churches were small oratories of wood and wattle. Peak pre-Norman building appears in Irish Romanesque churches. Large foundations clustered several churches, and Clonmacnoise had eight. Graveyards held cross-inscribed slabs and high crosses.

:::
