Mairi nighean Alasdair Ruaidh (c. 1615-c. 1707), known in English as Mary MacLeod, was one of the leading Scottish Gaelic poets of the seventeenth century. The name means Mary, daughter of red-haired Alasdair. She was attached to several strong families of the MacLeod kindred. Much of her surviving verse reached later hearers through oral transmission. Other writers name her, with Mairi MacInnes, among the Scottish Gaelic bards. She made her home on Harris and spent many years at Dunvegan in Skye, composing praise of leading men in elegies and poems.

:::
