``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Mac a' Ghobhainn, Iain is remembered as a founder of Gaelic short fiction. His stories rank among the first psychological and philosophical tales written in Scottish Gaelic fiction. His novella An t-Aonaran, meaning the hermit, came out in 1976. That piece carried his existential themes into a longer form and broke fresh ground. He issued five collections of adult Gaelic poetry, mostly free verse. These poems show his drive to perceive his reality truly. He wrote plays as well, and the national Mods gave prizes to them. His work helped rebuild Scottish Gaelic drama. Recurring themes in his writing take in isolation and mortality, questions of perception, bilingualism, biculturalism, and a search for self-knowledge. His titles include Consider the Lilies and Permanent Island.

:::
```
