::: magazinestyle
MacLeod, Norman was a Gaelic writer known as 'Caraid nan Gaidheal', the Friend of the Gaels. He wrote essays and sermons rooted in biblical language. He turned to the comhradh, a dialogue form for Gaelic prose that used everyday speech through a few fictional Highland characters. The genre treated politics, emigration, education, and social change. MacLeod remained the writer most tied to this genre. During the potato famine that struck the Scottish Highlands in 1836-7 he raised awareness in the Lowlands and England, and the campaign gathered over 150000 pounds. He preached in Ireland and prepared a metrical version of the Psalms in Irish in 1836. In 1831 he and Daniel Dewar compiled a Gaelic-English dictionary together.

:::
