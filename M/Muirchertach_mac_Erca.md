``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Muirchertach mac Erca is the legendary 6th-century high king of Ireland, a figure of the Cycle of Kings and the great-grandson of Niall Noigiallach, a king thought to be historical. Fancy gathered around him early. Story links him to the coronation stone, the Lia Fáil, sent across to Dál Riada in Scotland for his brother Fergus mac Eire to be crowned on, and Fergus kept it. The famous death-tale runs as a romance. Seated alone on his hunting mound, Muirchertach meets a fair young woman in a green cloak who says she has sought him, and he offers all Ireland for one night with her. She agrees under three conditions: he must never speak her name, the mother of his children must never see her, and the clergy must leave whenever she enters. Asking her name so that he can avoid it, he hears a string of ambiguous and threatening titles like those of the loathsome Cailb, words for sigh, storm, rough wind, and winter night. To please her he turns out his queen and children from the residence near the Boyne and fills the hall with craftsmen and their wives. St Caimech curses his house as she watches the damsel at the king's right hand. The damsel claims faith in God and descent from Adam and Eve, yet works wonders, turning the Boyne to wine and making pigs from ferns for seven nights. On the eve of the Wednesday after Samain, foul weather brings winter to his mind, and she reveals herself as the Rough Wind and the winter night, dooming him for speaking her forbidden name. A prophecy had foretold that he would die by the same fire that destroyed the grandfather he had burned in his hall. Sin, the storm woman, then sets his hall ablaze and rings it with phantom attackers under the menacing Tuathal Máelgarb. He climbs into a wine cask to escape and drowns there while fire burns his head.

:::
```
