``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Manannán mac Lir is the chief sea god and ruler of the Otherworld in Irish and Gaelic tradition. His name says son of the sea, from the genitive of the old word ler. He is not the Lir whose children became swans, and his true father is usually the dim figure Allod. Christian literature never quite made him a mortal man, though the tenth-century Sanas Cormaic calls him a trader and pilot. In the earliest tales he rules the island Emain Ablach and drives his chariot over the sea as if it were firm ground, with Tír Tairngiri and Mag Mell as further realms. Some link Emain Ablach to the Arthurian Avalon. His name likely began as a name for the Isle of Man, so the island gave the god his title rather than the other way. He is a master of shape-shifting and grants the skill to chosen mortals. Usually he appears a handsome noble warrior, compared to Poseidon and Neptune. He rides the horse Enbharr or sails the boat called wave-sweeper, carries the sword Fragarach that no armour stops, and knows the spell feth fiada, taught to the druids, which lets him vanish in mist. His cloak changes colour like the sea and one whirl of it can remake a destiny. He owns birds, hounds, and pigs that are eaten one day and whole the next. By the tenth century he was counted among the Tuatha Dé Danann, to whom he gave the druid fog and the feast of Goibniu. His renowned son is Mongán, and among his foster-sons are Lug Lámfhota and others. He turns up in all four cycles of Irish story but leads no single one. In the Voyage of Bran he greets the hero as he rides over the waves. He rules as first king of the island that carries his name.


:::
```
