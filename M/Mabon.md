``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Mabon is a youth of Welsh legend and a figure of the divine. His mother is a woman named Modron. Someone stole him when he was only three nights old. In the tale Culhwch ac Olwen, the hero Culhwch is told to rescue him. It is Arthur who finds the boy, aided by the wisdom of animals, in a prison in Gloucester. The Welsh Triads count him among the great three prisoners from the Island of Britain, beside a man named Gwair. During the hunt of the boar Twrch Trwyth, Mabon alone can pluck the razor that rests behind the ear of the beast. Only he can hunt the dog called Drudweyn. A poem in the Black Book of Carmarthen names him a servant of Uther Pendragon. Writers connect the youth to the god Maponos, a deity of Roman Britain and Gaul often likened to Apollo. His mother's name mirrors the goddess Matrona, the eponym of the Marne River. A doublet called Mabon vab Mellt, meaning son of lightning, echoes him.

:::
```
