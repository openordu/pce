``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Manawydan fab Llŷr, son of Llŷr, is the Welsh hero of the Mabinogi who gives the third branch its title and carries its action. The Welsh name compares with Manaw, which designates the Isle of Man. He is brother to Bendigeidfran and to Branwen, the leading pair of the second branch.

Although Manawydan wields magic well and works as a skilled craftsman, he shows none of the divine character carried by his Irish counterpart, the sea-god Manannan mac Lir. The comparison between the two figures is exact on the level of family and role, yet the Welsh hero reads as a humanized survivor of the same mythic material.

In the third branch Manawydan marries Rhiannon. Together with Pryderi and Pryderi's wife Cigfa, the pair live in exile in England, where Manawydan works leather first and farms wheat after that. The craftsman motif ties him to the practical arts rather than to rule.

Ifor Williams's 1930 study of the four branches and Patrick K. Ford's 1981 prolegomena to the reading of Pwyll and Manawydan remain standard points of departure for the figure.

:::
```
