::: magazinestyle
Magdalensberg is a hill-fort site on a summit 1058 m high in the Austrian province of Carinthia. It sits between the Glan and Gurk rivers inside the old Roman province of Noricum. The occupied zone stretched across 350 ha, or close to 840 acres. The hill gained a foothold perhaps in the 2nd century bc, while the hillside terraces filled only in the 1st century bc. Its view guarded a main trade line from Italy toward the Danube and the Drava. Finds there include terra sigillata pottery and brooches from northern Italy, plus over 300 inscribed objects that speak to a two-way commerce in the 1st century bc. Iron formed the chief export, and iron from Noricum drew praise from writers such as Pliny. The settlement was abandoned in ad 45 in favour of Virunum, on the plain now called Zollfeld.

:::
