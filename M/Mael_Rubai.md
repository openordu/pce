``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Mael Rubai was an early Irish saint who founded a monastery at Apor Crossan in 673. That place is known today as Applecross on the west coast of the Scottish Highlands. He stands as a major figure in the spread of Irish monasticism into northern Britain. His name takes many forms such as Maolrubha, Maelrubha, Mourie, and Maree. The words combine Old Irish mael meaning tonsured or servant of with ruba meaning bush or thicket. His father was Elganach, a male-line descendant of Niall Noigiallach, and he studied under St Comgall of Bangor in Ireland. Several northern places carry his name, including Loch Maree and Kilarrow on Islay. The Irish annals date his death to 722. His feast is kept on 21 April in Ireland and on 27 August for the Scots. A later tale that Vikings slew him lacks any firm support.

:::
```
