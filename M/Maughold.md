``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Maughold, also called Saint Maughold or Machutus, was a legendary Manx figure. Early Christian legend said Saint Patrick converted him. One story told how Maughold was padlocked into a small boat and set adrift at sea. His captors threw the key overboard. A fish swallowed that key in the deep water. Later crews rescued Maughold, but no one could free the lock. Many years passed. A fisherman caught a fish and served it to the saint. The fish held the long-lost key inside its body. The lock sprang open at last. Maughold rose from that ordeal to lead the island. He rose to become bishop of Man. The story carries the marks of older folk belief. Its motifs and a feast kept at the Celtic festival of Lughnasa suggest a Christian legend wrapped around a pagan core. MacKillop traced other threads behind the saint. He drew the character of Maughold from Mac Cuill, a hero of the Tuatha Dé Danann. Some tried to connect him with the Breton saint Machutus, also called Madovius or Malo. Scholars proved that connection false. Maughold was probably identical with a bishop named Mac Caille. The Irish martyrology lists that bishop for 25 April. The Christian calendar long kept Maughold's feast on 27 April. His ascribed death date was 498. Before his conversion Maughold lived a violent life as a freebooter. Patrick's teaching turned him from that path. He sailed to the Isle of Man in a rudderless, oarless coracle. As a missionary he kept an austere and exemplary life. For his holiness the community chose him bishop. His name marked the landscape around him. It gave titles to Maughold Head and the village of Maughold. St Maughold's Well and the great stone cross bear his name too. St Maughold's Cross is an eight-foot standing stone. It bears biblical scenes and the emblem of the Manx triskelion. In the northeastern part of the island that cross marks the sole monastic foundation older than the Norse.

:::
```
