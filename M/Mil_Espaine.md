``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Míl Espáine, whose many forms include Mílid Espáirte and the spellings Mil Espáne, Mil Easpáine, and further variants Miled, Milead, and Mileadh, carries an Irish name meaning soldier of Spain. He stands as the eponym of the Milesians, the last mythic invaders to reach Ireland in the Lebor Gabála, and a fictional forebear of the Irish people. Born under the name Golam or Galam, he holds a grand pedigree that runs through Bile and the grandfather Breogan, adds twenty Irish names and thirteen Hebrew names, and climbs back to Adam. His early career unfolds in Scythia, a land early Irish writers treated with much fancy. The Scythian king makes him army commander and gives him his daughter Seang, who bears sons Donn and Erech Febria before an early death. Uncovering a plot against himself, Míl kills Seang's father and escapes with sixty ships to Egypt. Pharaoh Nectanebus employs him as commander in a winning war against the Ethiopians, and the Lebor Gabála authors knew their history here, for the Thirtieth Dynasty held two pharaohs of that name. He marries the pharaoh's daughter Scota, who bears him Éber Finn and Amairgin (1) in Egypt, while Ír is born near Thrace and Colptha on a Mediterranean island. Remembering the druid Caícer's prophecy that his folk would one day settle in Ireland, Míl sails west, halts to defend a menaced Spain, and triumphs in many battles. He then dies of unspecified causes without ever reaching Ireland. His kin who do land there count his uncle Íth among them, together with Scota and a crowd of his sons. The son tally climbs as high as thirty-two once by-blows from Spain and Scythia are counted, but the usual number is eight, a distinction shared with the Welsh Cunedda. Greatest among them stand Éber and Éremón, who divide Ireland between them, and behind them rank Ír and Donn, then Colptha, Amairgin (1), and the pair Erech Febria with Erannán.
```
