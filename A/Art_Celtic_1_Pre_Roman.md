``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Celtic art resists a sure change. No makers left a clear statement of its meaning. The word Celtic here follows the Greek Keltoí and the Roman Celtae. It names pre-literate peoples of western and central Europe. These peoples fall in the Iron Age and its La Tène phase. Most Celtic art sits on small personal things. It appears on fibulae, neck rings, swords, and drinking bowls. Paul Jacobsthal set the standard order of styles. He wrote his Early Celtic Art while in exile in Oxford. He began with an early style tied to wealthy La Tène grave goods. A Waldalgesheim style followed with free-moving plant forms. Hallstatt work is mostly geometric. It uses straight and punched lines with sun and moon marks. Lost-wax bronze casting freed makers to shape small figures. Molds broke at each cast, so no two examples match.

:::
```
