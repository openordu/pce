``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Áine, also written Enya and sometimes corrupted to Aîné, carries a stack of Irish senses. They are brightness, glow, radiance, splendour, glory, and fame. The name sits on a crowd of figures from early Ireland, both male and female. Male bearers of the name pass as historical people. They appear in genealogies, especially those of the Ciarraige, a people who held the Limerick-Kerry marchland before the Norman arrival. Female bearers stay within the same Munster district and lean toward myth. The name travels past Munster and reaches into Ulster. The leading female Áines of myth may share one root. They may descend from a single original. Áine of Knockainy is often called a fairy queen. She most likely began as an older goddess reduced over time. Her hill sits beside Lough Gur, a storied lake in east County Limerick. The place-name Lios Áine survives at Lissan in Northern Ireland. Dún Áine marks the entrance to Dundalk Bay in County Louth. Which Áine each place-name commemorates is no longer recoverable.

:::
```
