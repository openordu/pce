``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
An aisling is a vision poem in the Irish language, often allegorical and usually written in the eighteenth century. In it a woman from the Otherworld visits the poet in a dream. Gerard Murphy set out three principal forms. The love aisling bewitches the poet with the woman's beauty. The prophecy aisling lets the dreamer predict the future. In the allegorical aisling the woman often stands for Ireland and comforts the distressed poet. Murphy thought the third form, common in the eighteenth century, might fuse the first two. The early Irish Aislinge Oengusa, the vision of Aonghus, and the later Aislinge Meic Con Glinne are famous examples.

:::
```
