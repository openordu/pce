::: magazinestyle
Aislinge Meic Con Glinne is a twelfth century satire that mocks the Irish church. The title becomes in English the Vision of Mac Con Glinne. A scholar named Ainiér Mac Con Glinne left Roscommon for Cork. The guest-house there was ill-kept and full of vermin. He criticized the baggage he received there. The abbot Manchin ordered him beaten and stripped. Manchin then sentenced him to death. Before the execution Mac Con Glinne told a dream of food. He saw a boat of lard on a sea of new milk. The local king Cathal, who died in 742, held a demon of gluttony. Mac Con Glinne drove out the demon in a droll exorcism.

:::
