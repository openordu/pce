``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Artemis was the paradoxical Greek goddess of fertility and chastity. She also ruled the open field and the hunt. The Greeks found several parallels to her in Celtic lands. Her name links to diverse figures. These include the Cailleach, a hag of Scotland and Ireland. They include an unnamed dog goddess of Galatia. Coins were offered to that goddess every year. The coins paid for each animal killed in the hunt. MacKillop names the Celtic goddess Flidais as a close parallel. Folklore credits Flidais with a deer-drawn wagon. Greek tradition records that Artemis made for the Celtic lands of Galatia in Asia Minor. Roman legions assimilated Celtic deities. They called Artemis-like goddesses by the name Diana. Diana is the Roman form of Artemis. These goddesses ruled wild lands. They occupied those lands with a company of untamed maidens.

:::
```
