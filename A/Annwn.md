``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Annwn, also called Annwyn and Annwfn, is the Welsh Otherworld, the Un-world that lies on the far side of ordinary life. Its name may grow from the old Celtic word dwfn, deep. A Gaulish inscription from Collias invokes underworld spirits with the word andounnabo, set at a ritual spring. The Welsh, like the Irish, offered several places for the Otherworld. Annwn means abyss. The Welsh saw it as a castle by the sea, called Caer Siddi. They also saw it as a court of intoxication, called Caer Feddwid, and as a palace of glass, called Caer Wydyr. Like Tir na nOg, that Irish island of the blessed, Annwn was a land of sweetness and charm. Its people knew neither age nor suffering. A magic cauldron fed each of them their fill. An unfailing well quenched their thirst. Two kings ruled Annwn, Arawn and Hafgan, locked in endless war. The legends tell more of Arawn. In the Mabinogi's first branch, the prince Pwyll met Arawn while hunting, and the two traded shapes for a year. That season bound the royal house of Dyfed to the powers of Annwfn. In the early poem Preiddiau Annwfn, which tells of the spoils of Annwn, Arthur and his men stormed stronghold after stronghold in that other realm.

:::
```
