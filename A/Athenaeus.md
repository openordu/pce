``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Athenaeus lived and wrote in the second and third centuries CE. He produced the Deipnosophistae, a long book set around a banquet. His work keeps the words of many earlier authors. He preserves the Greek account of the Celts written by Posidonius. Posidonius told of single combats at Celtic feasts. He said the best warrior took the top share of the thigh at a feast. A challenger could fight a duel for the choicest portion. Athenaeus also kept a tale from Theopompus. That story told how Celts of the Balkans tricked a Greek gathering. They poisoned their guests with a herb that caused stomach pain. He saved the Greek record of the founding of Massalia from Aristotle. These Greek scraps matter to scholars reading the Irish Ulster sagas. His book keeps the head cult as well as the champion's portion.

:::
```
