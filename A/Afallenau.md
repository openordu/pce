``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Afallenau is the Welsh word for apple trees. The name marks a long set of prophetic verses in the record. These poems go to the credit of the seer Myrddin. They survive in the Black Book of Carmarthen from about 1250. Each stanza opens with the line about the sweet apple tree. A normal block stretches to between six and twelve lines of rhyme. Myrddin speaks of his flight from the battle of Arfderydd. His heavy prophecy fills the verses. Thus Afallenau unites tree imagery with Welsh fortune telling.

:::
```
