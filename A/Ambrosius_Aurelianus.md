``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Ambrosius Aurelianus is a shadowy but real figure of Britain. Gildas, writing in the sixth century, gives the name. The Welsh know him as Emrys Wledig. He led the Britons after their revolt against the Saxon settlers. Gildas describes him as the last true Roman in Britain. His ancestors had worn the purple, a mark of Roman rank. He lived in the late fifth century about ad 500. The Saxons grew strong and turned on the Christian towns. The Britons then rallied under Ambrosius. Their fight at Badonicus mons ended in victory. Gildas does not name the commander of that battle. Welsh tradition later gave the credit to Arthur. His Latin name passed into Welsh speech as Emrys. In the Historia Brittonum he is a young seer of wonders. He reads the sign of the Red Dragon in a Snowdonia stronghold. That stronghold is Dinas Emrys. The tale ties him to Vortigern. Geoffrey of Monmouth later recast him as Aurelius Ambrosius. He made him the brother of Uthr Bendragon. He made him the uncle of Arthur. The historical man and the storybook hero thus meet. Gildas gives him the name and the rank. The later tales give him wonders. Both strands shape the memory of Ambrosius Aurelianus. He holds the line between the fading Roman order and the coming British kings. His name links the old empire to the new legend of Britain. He stands at the hinge of a changing age.

:::
```
