``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The ankou is a spectral figure that portends death in Breton folklore. Breton tradition pairs it with the Greek Thanatos. Usually the ankou is the spirit of whoever died last in a community. The figure can be male but is more often female. One shape is a tall, haggard form wearing a wide hat, with long white hair below it. Another shape is a skeleton whose head revolves, so that it sees everybody everywhere. A deathly wagon or cart is the ankou's characteristic vehicle. Its axle creaks, and it rides piled high with corpses. For those inside a cabin, one stop of that cart at the door means sudden death. The Irish death coach has a driver who roughly parallels this figure. Yet the ankou seems to owe more to the Grim Reaper whom medieval Christian folklore knows. Anatole le Braz, a nineteenth-century writer, suggested that the ankou survived from the dolmen-builders of prehistoric Brittany. The dictionary cross-notes angau, the dullahan, the far dorochai, and the yannig alongside it.

:::
```
