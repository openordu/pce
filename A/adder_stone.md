``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The adder stone is a glass bead held to carry magical power. Some traditions call it a serpent stone, and a full account continues in that entry. The druids possessed such glass balls as amulets, using them for mysteries or for ornament. Several Celtic languages keep the image of the adder in their names for the object. Breton speakers say glain neidr, Scottish Gaelic gloine nathair, and Welsh glain nadredd. Cornish speech alone breaks the pattern with the word millpreve. The names make the bead a serpent's glass in three tongues at once.

Belief gave the stones a strange birth. People said an adder blew the bubbles in summer, and the beads hardened as they rolled along its scaly back. The result was a crystal ring, hung as a charm. The adder itself carries a singular place in the wild life of Britain. It is the sole venomous snake of the British Isles. Folk belief read its glass as the animal's own force.

The idea links the stone to a wider family of serpent relics. The serpent stone entry records Pliny's story of the druid's glass and its deadly contest. In each case the snake's body makes or guards a sealed sphere of glass. Naturally holed flints served as the physical stones behind much of this lore. The bead's glassy look earned it the name of glass in most tongues. The Druids' possession of the beads ties the object to their mysteries and their healing. Folk practice read such a ring as a charm against harm. Its natural hole gave it a ready place on a cord. Collectors still find such holed pebbles on British shores today. Country folk carried one against witchcraft and the evil eye. Others hung the stone in the byre to guard the cattle's milk. The object's power was thought to pass from the adder into the glass. From there it passed into whoever wore the bead. Antiquarian writers of the seventeenth century recorded the same beliefs. They describe country people hoarding the beads as treasures of great price. The adder stone thus joins material object, animal lore, and druidic mystery in one small artefact.
```
