``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Aithirne was a satirist poet of Old Irish tale. He was also called Aithirne the importunate. The older Irish name gives the sense of a kind of satire. He served at the court of the Ulster king Conchobar mac Nessa. He was known for the heavy tributes he asked. He once begged for and was given the eye of a king in the Irish midlands. He pressed the old rule that no one may refuse a poet's request. He asked the people of Leinster for their cattle, their women, and many other goods. They granted his demands, and a bridge was thrown over the Liffey so the herds could cross. Then the Leinstermen refused to send the women they had said they would give. The tale holds the memory of his greed and of the power of the poet's word in early Ireland.

:::
```
