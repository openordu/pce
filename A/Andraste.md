``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Andraste is a British goddess, also written Andrasta. The historian Cassius Dio preserves the only surviving account of her, and he says the name meant victory. The form looks like a feminine name built on a verb that carries the Celtic negative prefix an-, so it may suggest the unconquerable one. The warrior queen Boudicca of the Iceni called on her during the rising of ad 61. In the rite Boudicca freed a hare from among the folds of her garment, and the way it ran told the warriors the attack held favor. In her speech she hailed the goddess as one woman to another and asked for triumph, life, and freedom. Dio adds that the goddess was worshipped in a grove, a nemeton, where Roman women were put to death during the revolt. The hare was an occasional symbol of the warrior queen Medb, famed in Ireland. It turns fierce when caught, and so it fits an image of defense. Boudicca came close to driving the Romans from her land. When the war turned against her, she died by her own hand rather than face the humiliations the Romans gave their defeated enemies.

:::
```
