``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Ailill, also spelled Oilill, Ailell, or Aleel, means sprite or elf. It was a very popular name in early Ireland. At least two saints bore it. Ten Fianna warriors in the Fenian cycle carried it too. Many local kings in the genealogies held the name. English speakers often anglicized it as Oliver. The best known bearer was Ailill mac Máta. He was the consort of the queen Medb of Connacht. In the epic Táin Bó Cuailnge an argument between them set off a cattle raid on Ulster. Ailill claimed the right to rule Connacht. He based that claim on his mother Máta being the province's queen. Another Ailill was a Milesian king of Leinster. He fathered the romantic heroine Étain. A third Ailill, named Ailill Áine, was the ancestral father of the Lagin people of Leinster.

:::
```
