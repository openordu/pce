``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Alisanos is an obscure Continental Celtic god of the Gauls. Some call him Alisanus. He is a god of the rock, a living force of the landscape. Roman commentaries record his worship. The god may be one with the name Alisonos. That form was invoked at Alesia. Alesia held him as a possible watch-spirit of the site. The name may have given its shape to the town of Alesia itself. Scholars read his name in several ways. One reading ties him to bare rock. Another reading links him to the alder tree. A third prefers the rowan. Koch draws the name from the alder. The rock and the tree both fit his realm. Alesia was a fortress of the Mandubii in Burgundy. That town stood out as a Celtic oppidum. In 52 BC it saw the last great clash of Gaul and Rome. The little god of stone never spread far beyond his home. His voice stays bound to one district and one site. That makes him a narrow and local power. Yet his stone lives on in the old texts. He is the spirit of a particular ground. The landscape itself gives him meaning. Roman writers took him up as a memory of wild Gaul. So Alisanos remains the watcher of a holy rock. His name keeps its guard over the site. He and Alisonos may well be the same watchman of the fort.

:::
```
