``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Amaethon is a Welsh figure who works the land. His name means great or divine ploughman. The root ties him to agriculture and to labour. His mother is the goddess Don. Amaethon is both a farmer and a magician. Early Welsh narrative gives him both callings. In the tale Culhwch and Olwen he shows his skill. He is the only man who can till a hard field. That field resists every other worker. Amaethon alone breaks the stubborn ground. He also taught the art of magic to Gwydion. His theft set a great war in motion. From Annwn he carried off a hound and a roe deer. Annwn is the Welsh otherworld. The taking angered Arawn, its ruler. Arawn and Gwydion then went to war. The fight grew into the Battle of the Trees. Welsh song calls that battle Cad Goddeu. Before the closing of that fight, Amaethon overcame Arawn. He won by his skill and his magic. Many authorities see an older god in him. They propose an agricultural god as his root. The ploughman and the magician fit that ancient farmer. His care reaches past the plowed field. He tends the dog, the deer, and the lapwing. In some tellings he holds these wild gifts from the otherworld king. Their theft shows how the plenty of this land comes from the far realm. He appears as a farmer in the tale of the Boar's yoke. He can make the most useless waste land bloom. Amaethon joins the toil of the field to the secrets of magic. He stands as a working god at the heart of Welsh story. His name keeps the bond between the plough and the land.

:::
```
