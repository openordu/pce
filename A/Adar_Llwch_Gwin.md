``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Adar Llwch Gwin are magical birds of Welsh tradition. They belonged to Drudwas ap Tryffin. Writers often equated them with griffins. His wife, a fairy woman, gave them to him. The birds understood human speech and obeyed his every order. When Drudwas held a contest against Arthur, he bade the birds slay whoever entered the field first. Arthur arrived late, so the birds turned on their master Drudwas and tore his flesh. Later Welsh verse by the Beirdd yr Uchelwyr used the phrase for hawks or falcons. It also stood as a metaphor for strong and brave men.

:::
```
