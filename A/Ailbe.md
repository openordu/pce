``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Ailbe, also written Ailbhe, means white. The name matches the Gaulish word Albiorix, which means world king. In early Irish narrative both men and women bore the name. Twelve Fianna fighters in the Fenian Cycle carried it. Two saints also carried it. English speakers wrongly turned the name into Albert or Bertie. The first person named Ailbe was a daughter of the fairy king Midir. Ailbe was also a famous hunting dog in Irish myth. The warrior Mac Dathó owned him. Two powerful provincial kings each wanted the dog. Mac Dathó wished to satisfy both. He promised Ailbe to Concobar Mac Nessa of Ulster and to Ailill Mac Mata of Connacht. The argument that followed ended with the dog killed.

:::
```
