::: magazinestyle
Seamus Heaney is the most famous Anglo-Irish poet of modern times. The Academy awarded him the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1995. The prize ceremony is held in Sweden, and he collected his medal there. Heaney was born in rural Northern Ireland and that landscape shaped him. He wrote poems about farm life, family and memory. His verse moved easily between the personal and the political. Heaney drew on older Irish traditions yet wrote a modern English. He was also a widely known critic and lecturer. He taught at Harvard and Oxford. His work brought international fame to contemporary Irish poetry.

:::
