``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Aquae Sulis is a British goddess. The Romans named the shrine and hot springs at Bath in England Aquae Sulis, the waters of Sulis. The local sun goddess Sulis stood at the centre of worship there. The Romans merged her with the goddess Minerva, and the joined cult then took in several other native deities. MacKillop traces the name Sulis by two paths. One reads it beside the Latin sol, the sun. The other sets it beside the Irish suil, the eye. Her healing-spring cult reached as far away as Hesse in Germany. Bath is still famous for its hot mineral springs. The temple there carried the name Sulis Minerva. The union held in a form the Roman west rarely took. Where a local god met Mars or Mercury, the native power usually sank to an aspect of the Roman god. At Aquae Sulis the goddess bore the joint name Sulis-Minerva, or Sul-Minerva, without change. The springs pour out vast volumes of hot water. That flow would have drawn pilgrims long before Rome arrived. The occupiers then rebuilt the site as a pool inside a large building of classical style. Excavations recovered coins, offering vessels, and inscribed curse tablets. The names on the tablets are mostly Roman, beside a notable Celtic share.

:::
```
