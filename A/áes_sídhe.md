áes sídhe, also rendered áes si or aos sidhe, is in MacKillop's Dictionary of Celtic Mythology one of the many Irish names for the fairies, and by implication the Tuatha Dé Danann. The phrase literally means 'people of the mound' or 'people of the fairy hill', with sídhe (modern sí) the word for the ancient burial mounds that, in folk belief, became doorways to the Otherworld. MacKillop treats the term as a stock collective for the supernatural host, parallel with the singular bean sí and the broader síth tradition. The 2008 entry situates áes sídhe within the larger body of Irish fairy-naming conventions, from the Tuatha Dé Danann to the local sí of the countryside.

:::
