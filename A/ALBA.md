::: magazinestyle
Alba is the Gaelic name for northern Britain. Early texts also use the form Albu. From at least the ninth century, Fir Alba meant the Scots of Scotland. The term rí Alban named the king of the united Scots and Picts. Alba comes from the ancient attested name for Britain, *Albiòn. In heroic tales the name Alba usually points at all of Britain. The stricter sense, Pictland then Scotland, came later. The Welsh name Alba for Scotland appears in the fourteenth century. Cinaed mac Ailpín established Goidelic dominance in Scotland in 843. Gaelic then became the court tongue of the Scottish royal house. MacKillop's dictionary records Alba as the Scottish Gaelic and modern Irish name for Scotland. The name is tied to the same root as Albu and the old form Albyn. The sense of Alba in older times was Britain or Albion, and only later stood for the northern land.

:::
