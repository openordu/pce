``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Ailill Ochair Ága, Ailill edge of battle, fathered Máel Dúin. He served as a warrior of the Eóganacht of Ninuss on the Aran Islands. He begot his son by wooing a nun who came to strike the midnight bell. The mother died giving birth. A queen, friend to the mother, then fostered the child. Marauders from Laois later killed Ailill. The Immram Curaig Máel Dúin tells the voyage of his son. The same MacKillop volume opens its loch headword with the Loch Ness Monster. Within that entry, Llion may possibly link with the lake known in Welsh tradition as Efala. MacKillop marks Llion link to the loch with a footnote marker, treating the link as tentative. The loch itself in Celtic lore names a lake or sea inlet. The lake lore of the Britons carries Efala as a Welsh lake name. The Aran Islands of Ailill Ochair Ága lie inside that same Gaelic lake world. Saints, heroes, and sea creatures share that shore. The Immram Curaig Máel Dúin sets the father and son inside the wider Celtic voyage tradition of named waters.

:::
```
