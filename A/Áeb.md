``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Áeb, also written Aebh or Aobh, was a heroine of Irish legend. The name comes from Old Irish oib, which means attractiveness or beauty. The tale of the Children of Lir names her as the mother who bore the doomed children. She stood as the second of three wives of the high king Lir. She was born the daughter of the magician Bodb Derg and grew up as his foster-daughter. She was also a blood-sister of the third wife, Aife. Some texts give her the alternate names Niam or Albha. Her life ended as she brought her second pair of twins into the world. Her only daughter, Fionnuala, then stepped into her place. Fionnuala became the hero of the tale after the children lost their mother. The death of Áeb carried her childless foster-sister Aife into the household of Lir. Aife worked a curse that changed the children into singing swans. The full Irish title of the story is Oidheadh Chlainne Lir, the sorrowful tale of the Lir children. In some English retellings the name Fingula takes the place of Áeb. That name is an anglicization of Finnguala, who was one of the twin daughters of Áeb. The swap puts a daughter where the mother belongs and reads as a perverse choice.

:::
```
