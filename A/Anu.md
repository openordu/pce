``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Anu, also called Ana and Danu, is an Irish goddess. Two rounded hills rise in southwestern Ireland. Prehistoric cairns cap them. They look like the breasts of a great mother goddess. People call them Da Chich Danann, the Paps of Danu. The name comes from a goddess Anu of whom little is known. The Lebor Gabála Érenn, the story of the taking of Ireland, identifies Anu or Ana with the Morrígan, a war-goddess. The name Danu sometimes stands in for Anu. Danu probably began as a separate figure with its own origin. The glossarist Cormac names Anu as a mother of the gods of Ireland. Many scholars link her to Danu, the ancestor of the Tuatha Dé Danann. Others read her name as abundance or feeding. It may connect to an Old Irish word for a food basket and to words for bread and satiety. In Christian times Anu merged with Saint Anne, the grandmother of Jesus. Many holy wells carry her dedication. MacKillop notes that the name Dana also served the Continental Celts. He grounds it on river names such as the Danube, which some scholars read as a memory of the goddess. A prosthetic letter D added to Ana gave the forms Dana and Danu. Welsh tradition pairs Ana with the goddess Dôn across the Irish Sea. The two hills called the Paps of Ana or Paps of Danu rise in county Kerry. He places them as breast-shaped hills ten miles east of Killarney, standing proof of the earth mother. These mountains show the prosperity Anu brought to the province of Munster.

:::
```
