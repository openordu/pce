``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Armorica is an ancient place-name. It names the coast of Gaul facing the Atlantic. The name means country facing the sea. In Roman usage the Tractus Armoricanus ran from the mouth of the Seine to the Loire. The tract took in the country of the Belgae to the west and the Aquitani to the north, land that Normandy and Brittany later shared. Julius Caesar gives the earliest surviving examples of the name. He writes of the civitates Armoricae, the tribal states by the ocean. His list names the Curiosolites, the Redones, the Ambibarii, the Caletes, the Osismii, the Veneti, the Lemovices, and the Venelli. Caesar also records the Veneti as the strongest people of the coast and tells how Rome broke their sea power. Their name lives on in Breton Gwened, the town the French call Vannes. Modern works often use Armorica for the land later called Brittany. Koch also records the older spelling Aremorica.

:::
```
