``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Ailinn (Aillinn, Aillin) was a princess of the southeastern province of Leinster. Her lover was Baile Binnbhéarlach, a prince of Ulster in the northeast. Each traveled to a trysting place midway between their realms. A fairy told the prince, falsely, that his lover was dead. He died of grief at Baile's Strand, near the modern town of Dundalk. The fairy then carried the same story to Ailinn. She too fell down dead of grief. From their adjacent graves grew two entwined trees. A yew grew from his and an apple from hers. Seven years later, poets cut down the trees. They carved them into tablets. They engraved all of Ulster's love songs on the yew. They put Leinster's songs on the applewood. When the king of Tara, Cormac Mac Airt, held the two tablets near each other, they clapped together. They could never again be separated. MacKillop ties her to Baile as his beloved. He records the variant spellings Ailine and Aine. The name Ailinn also serves as a variant for Dún Ailinne, the broch.

:::
```
