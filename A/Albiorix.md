``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Albiorix was a Continental Celtic god of war. His name stands carved upon the old stones of Gaul. The name reads as the king of the world. Roman settlers fitted him into their own god Mars. In Gallic use Albiorix stood as an occasional epithet of Mars. The two names join on a single altar stone. This kept the native title alive under Roman rule. It also gave Mars a sharper local face. Warriors called on Albiorix for strength in battle. A king over the whole world held deep appeal. The title crossed the Gallic country on many inscribed texts. It remained bound to no single shrine. Each district could honour its own world-ruler. The stone carvings mark his reach and his power. Some prayers joined Albiorix and Mars in one breath. Other stones give the name alone. The epithet speaks of a world-guardian. He watched over the wide landscape. The pair shows how a native god and a Roman god met. The Gallic title stayed beside the Roman one. Albiorix kept his standing while the Empire spread. The name survives on rock today as proof. This places the epithet within a wider circle. He rules the world and rules the war. The Gallic tribes held him close beside Mars. That bond made him endure. Albiorix stood fast as a royal war god while other tribes lost their names. His own title outran the years that followed.

:::
```
