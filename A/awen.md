``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Awen is the Welsh word for poetic inspiration. The awen is a gift that a person cannot learn by effort alone. The Poets of the Princes, known in Welsh as the Gogynfeirdd, held that this gift separated them from common makers of verse. Without the awen a person only rhymed; with the awen a person wrote true poetry. An early sign of the term appears in the sixth century in the name of the poet Talhaearn. Old story links the gift to the cauldron of Ceridwen. The great bard Taliesin is also tied to such a vessel of wisdom. Later figures in the tradition act as a guardian muse for the poets. In Wales the idea kept its strength among the later ranks of bards who served the court and praised their lords. For the Gogynfeirdd the gift of the awen marked the true poet apart from the common rhymester.

:::
```
