``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Saint Alban was a Romano-British martyr of Britain. Latin sources call him Albanus Verolamiensis, from the town Verulamium. That town today carries the name St Albans. Alban served as a pagan Roman soldier. During the Diocletian persecution ad 302-305 a Christian asked him for shelter. Alban hid the man and then took his faith. He swapped clothes with the Christian and faced the arrest in his place. The executioner then beheaded him. Both Gildas and Beda record his death. Beda's Historia Ecclesiastica 1.7 draws the account from the Passio Albani. Gildas sets his death as the revival of faith in Britain. His tomb became a place of healing. Gallo-Roman bishops such as Germanus reached the site in ad 429. He stands as the protomartyr of Britain. His cult rose through the Anglo-Saxon conquest in south-east Britain.

:::
```
