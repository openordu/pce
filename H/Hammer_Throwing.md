::: magazinestyle
Hammer throwing is a staple event at the Highland Games. The athlete hurls a heavy iron hammer for distance across a field. The throw stands within the deep traditions of the Highland Gatherings. Those Gatherings today carry a romantic aura. The romanticism of the Games reached its peak with Sir Walter Scott, who died in 1832. Critics call the mood Balmorality by kitsch. Highland symbols became marks of Scottish national identity. Hammer throwing and the other events remain popular tourist attractions. They still present an image of Scotland that a world audience expects. The Braemar Highland Games, the premier games of the season, close each year with such displays.

:::
