::: magazinestyle
Hollantide is the Welsh name for the festival of 1 November. The Welsh call the feast Calan Gaeaf. It is the counterpart of the Irish Samain, the Cornish Allantide, and the Breton Kala-Goanv. Hollantide keeps a memory of the old Celtic New Year. The eve was a time of ghostly visitations, divination games, bonfires, and merrymaking after the harvest. Most of these customs had faded by the nineteenth century, giving way to the wassailing seen at the other holidays. In the Celtic reckoning darkness came before light, so the season of death arrived ahead of the spring and summer rejoicing. On such a night Welsh people might meet the apparitions called the white lady and the bob-tailed black sow. Some believed the church-door keyhole showed who would die in the coming year. Young people who sewed hemp at night at a crossroads could call up their future sweethearts.

:::
