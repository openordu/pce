``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Hergest is remembered for the book called the Red Book of Hergest. The Welsh title of the work is Llyfr Coch Hergest. The manuscript dates from about 1382 to 1410 and ranks among the great books of the Welsh. It holds the Mabinogi and the seven other tales that form the Mabinogion. It also carries poems of the Gogynfeirdd school, plus histories, grammars, and proverbs. It holds no works of religion and no laws. A careful scribe, Hywel Fychan son of Howel Goch of Fuellt, copied most of the leaves. He imposed an order on the whole book, and evidence inside it shows he worked on other manuscripts too. Lady Charlotte Guest made her translation of The Mabinogion in 1846 from the Red Book. Newer translations also prefer the Red Book and cite it beside the White Book of Rhydderch, dated near 1325. The name Hergest comes from a mansion in Herefordshire. It kept the book from after 1465 until 1634, when the volume went back to Wales. Since 1701 the volume has rested at Jesus College in Oxford. Hergest thus names a place and the leading store of the Welsh tales.

:::
```
