``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Highlands and Islands names a broad territory of northern Scotland. A geological fault known as the Highland line divides this ground from the Lowlands. The fault runs from Helensburgh to Stonehaven. The Highlands and Islands form the Gaelic heartland of Scotland. The region long supported a crofting economy built on small holdings. Norse settlers came to own much of the land in the ninth century. Gaelic stayed strong in the Hebrides and moved across the mainland. The Lordship of the Isles grew strong under Somerled and his heirs. Clan Donald ruled the region through the medieval centuries. The loss of this Lordship in 1493 left the region unstable. Crofting unrest led to the Crofters Act of 1886. The area carries deep linguistic and cultural weight for Scotland.

:::
```
