``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Hill of Uisneach is a place in Ireland, also written Uisneach or Uisnech. In older tradition the hill was a centre of assembly and ritual. A medieval record names the Mor-dhail Uisnigh, the great assembly of Uisnech, which met on the opening day of May. It was counted among the three main assemblies of Ireland, with Feis Teamrach, the feast of Tara, and Aenach Tailten, the fair of Tailtiu. One tale in the dindshenchas, the lore of high places, explains the name as os neoch, meaning above someone. In that story a warrior called Mide, the namesake of Meath, hid the tongues of the druids at that place and raised a house above them. Mide is also said to have carried the earliest fire into Ireland, and ritual fire rites are tied to Uisnech. The hill is famous too for the tale of the sons of Uisliu, often called the Deirdre story. Their tale survives in the Middle Irish Longas Mac nUislenn and in the later Oidheadh Chlainne hUisneach. Yet scholars doubt that the brothers belong to the hill, for their name Uisliu may have been confused with Uisneach over time. See UISNEACH for the fuller entry.

:::
```
