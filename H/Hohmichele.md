``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Hohmichele is one of the bigger burial mounds of the early Hallstatt period in central Europe, set on the high ground of the upper Danube in Wurttemberg close to the hill-fort of the Heuneburg. The barrow was added to over a long stretch, raised anew with each interment for a local line of settlers. The chief tomb, Tomb I, held a timber chamber that robbers cleared in antiquity. The better record comes from Chamber VI, a grave left untouched until excavation, giving a high-status couple with a four-wheeled wagon and harness, amber and glass beads, bronze dress aids, belt plates, and a drinking set of iron knife, plate, cauldron, and ladle. A cremated adult laid in Tomb IX and a number of smaller burials round out the mound. About 5 m tall and 50 m across, the earthwork was built by drawing banks that pulled stray finds from older graves into its body.

:::
```
