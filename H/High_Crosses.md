``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
High Crosses are large monuments of carved stone, both freestanding crosses and cross slabs. They usually carry relief-carved ornament. The carving shows figural scenes, animal and plant motifs, and abstract patterns. The crosses also appear in Ireland and in Scotland, Cornwall, Wales, and the Isle of Man. Most date roughly from ad 750 to 1150. Many Irish crosses carry scripture scenes drawn from the books of the Older and Newer Testaments. On Muiredach's Cross at Monasterboice, the Crucifixion and the Last Judgement appear. The cross heads usually link the arms with a ring, a form seen as Celtic. Step marks and ringed patterns mark the cross heads and shafts. Crosses also carried inscriptions in Latin, Irish, Pictish, or Norse runes. The tallest example, at Monasterboice, stands 6.45 metres high. These monuments marked graves, boundaries, and sanctuaries.

:::
```
