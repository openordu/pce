``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Hochdorf, also called Eberdingen-Hochdorf, is a princely burial mound in Baden-Württemberg, Germany, linked to the hilltop stronghold of Hohenasperg. It belongs to the late western Hallstatt culture of the middle 6th century bc, the zone of fortified aristocratic tombs running across south-western Germany, Switzerland, and eastern France. Excavated in 1978-9 with its central chamber still intact, the barrow once stood near 6 m high and 60 m wide. Within lay a tall man of about 40, carrying a birch-bark hat, a gold necklace, and an antenna dagger. His goods included a bronze cauldron ringed by lions and holding near 400 litres of honey mead, nine drinking horns, a four-wheeled wagon sheathed in iron, and a famed bronze dining couch borne on eight cast female figures over small wheels. The pieces record the lavish funeral gifts of an early Celtic elite.

:::
```
