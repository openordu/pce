::: magazinestyle
Halloween is the popular name for the eve of the old Celtic festival of Samain. The feast is set on 1 November and ranks as the chief of the four great dates in the Celtic year. The other dates are Beltaine, Imbolc, and Lughnasa. For the early Celts the night of Samain hung between the two seasons of the year. They said that on that night the line between the world of the living and the world of the dead grew thin, and spirits could stray from the Otherworld with ease. Many customs of Halloween spring from Samain, among them bonfires, games of guessing, and hollow roots set with candles. The Welsh call the same feast Hollantide, and the Cornish call it Allantide. Halloween thus carries the memory of the Celtic New Year.

:::
