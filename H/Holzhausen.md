::: magazinestyle
Holzhausen is a group of two Viereckschanzen, rectangular ditched enclosures of the pre-Roman Iron Age, at Strasslach-Dingharting in the district of Munich, southern Germany. The site has come often into the discussion of Celtic religion and rite. The first enclosure, on a flank with a sheltered spring, numbers about 100 m on a side and keeps a rampart near 4 m high. The second lies some 90 m east, dug in five stages between 1957 and 1963 by K. Schwarz, who found wells, a fenced beginning, and a building raised in phases. Schwarz treated the compound as a ritual ground with a temple and an offering pit, whereas Gunther Wieland argued that the deep shafts were wells rather than ritual holes. The rival readings show how little of this class of site is yet trusted.

:::
