``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Historical Cycle is a category of Irish myth. Irish texts often divide myths into four cycles. These are the Mythological Cycle, the Ulster Cycle, the Fenian Cycle, and the Historical Cycle. The Historical Cycle holds stories that may have happened between late Celtic and early Christian times, roughly the third through the eleventh century. Many tales rest on real events. Myths and supernatural happenings embellish them. The stories often turn on Tara, the important ritual and political center. They also concern Sovereignty, the Celtic concept of right rulership. Kings in the Historical Cycle include Conn of the Hundred Battles, Cormac Mac Airt, Mongan, and Guaire.

MacKill names it one of the four panels of Irish story. It turns on the kings of the ruled land, both legend and real, from the third to the seventh age, and it puts the thought of rule itself at the middle of the telling. Its stories keep less magic than the Myth family, and they are less quick than the Ulster, less fine than the Fenian. The title of Cycle of the Kings was minted in the middle of the last world, for the roll of the rulers of the isle was wide enough to need them. The same roll even kept its own small cut for the house of a famous Munster chief.

:::
```
