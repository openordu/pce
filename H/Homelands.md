Homelands of the Celts is the dispute over the original place from which the ancient Celtic peoples spread. Koch holds that the earliest Celtic-language homeland, the territory of the Proto-Celtic ancestor, is not yet proven. A long-standing guess among scholars sets the origin in west-central Europe around the Alps and the upper Alpine and Danube. Yet the Atlantic edge of the continent, that is parts of Britain, Ireland, Brittany, and north-western Spain, cannot be set aside at once. The upper Danube country stood at the centre of the Hallstatt and La Tene cultures, and the Alpine zone counts among the earliest ground of Celtic Europe. The way the language spread, whether by trade, migration, or conquest, stays a matter of study. The later homeland question turns on reading the same thin evidence in rival ways.

:::
