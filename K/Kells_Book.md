``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Kells, Book of (Codex Cenannensis) names the illuminated Gospel manuscript today kept at Dublin, Trinity College Library, as MS 58. It ranks among the most richly decorated of the big Latin gospel-books that appeared in the British Isles in the years from the 7th into the 10th century. Scholars mostly hold that work on it began in about the later 8th century at the Columban monastery on Iona and that the book travelled inland to Kells in County Meath, where a Columban house rose at the start of the 9th century. Up to four scribes wrote in a bold insular majuscule script, and the overlap of lettering with painted ornament hints that the same hands supplied the pictures. Its decoration joins Celtic, Anglo-Saxon and Mediterranean styles in the insular Hiberno-Saxon manner. The artists layered three pigments and set three full-page miniatures unusually within the manuscript.

:::
```
