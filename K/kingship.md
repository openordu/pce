``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Kingship in Celtic tradition binds the ruler to the life of the land. The king must stay physically whole. A blemish can end his reign. Annals record that bees partly blinded Congal Caech, so he forfeited the kingship of Tara. Nuadu lost his rule over the Tuath De after injury in battle, though he wore a well-wrought silver arm. Justice, called fir, keeps the streams full and the harvest rich. The wolves kept peace with their prey under a wholly good king. A false verdict brings the wasteland instead. The old pair of words ri and tuath define each other. A ri rules a tuath, and the tuath gives a ri his standing. The root of ri means to stretch out straight, that is, to rule. The king's health and the realm's plenty stand or fall together.

:::
```
