``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Knowth is a great passage-tomb in the Boyne valley of County Meath, Ireland. Its Irish name is Cnobga, written also as Cnobha. The monument stands two miles northeast of Slane, close by the Boyne River. Knowth forms one of a celebrated trio of passage-graves. The other two are Newgrange, known in Irish as Brug na Boinne, and Dowth. All three rise in the same valley, and writers group them for their shared size and age. Experts date these great mounds somewhere between 3200 and 2400 BC. Knowth matches its neighbours in plan and worth. The tomb counts among the leading archaeological treasures of the whole island. Knowth holds rock art without equal in Europe. The carvings run across the stones of the mound in rich, close patterns. The monument has only lately been thrown open to the public. Visitors can now view the marks and carvings cut into the stones of this great mound. The rock art draws students from far beyond the island. Old Irish story ties Knowth to Bui, a wife of the hero Lug Lamhfhota. Under that name she held a link to the megalithic monument at Knowth. Some scholars see her as one face of the Cailleach Beirre, the Hag of Bear, a figure well known in the folklore of Ireland and of Gaelic Scotland. The tale numbers Bui among four wives of the great hero, and the same stories link her spirit to this old tomb and its long stone passage.

:::
```
