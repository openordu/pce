``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
The Viking-age realm of Man and its western isles was a small kingdom under the loose lordship of the Norwegian crown. It spread across Man itself and over the Hebrides. Norse raids and later settlement produced kings who ruled a mixed Norse-Gaelic people. In 1079 Godred Crovan seized the island and gave the land a firmer political order. The royal line shows Norse-Gaelic roots and a largely bilingual court. Olaf I, raised in the English court, founded Rushen Abbey in 1134. Somerled of coastal Argyll twice bested the Manx king and forced him to share the rule. In 1266 Norway handed the whole realm to Scotland by the Treaty of Perth.

:::
```
