``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Kentigern, or St Kentigern, who died in about ad 612, is remembered as the first bishop of Glasgow and ranks among the leading saints of the north of Britain. The name, drawn from Celtic *Kuno-tegernos, means 'hound lord', though people usually called him Mungo. Two twelfth-century vitae preserve the oldest reaches of his tale. Bishop Herbert (1147–64) sponsored an early fragmentary life, and Jocelin of Furness wrote a fuller version under Bishop Jocelin (1175–99); both rest on early medieval material whose reliability is hard to judge. Tradition says he was a grandson of the king who ruled Lothian, learned from St Serf at Culross, and took up the monastic life. He then travelled to Glasgow to lay to rest a holy man, Fergus, in a standing cemetery, and he lived in the Annandale district at Hoddom and also at Glasgow. Eight dedications in the present county of Cumbria mark his long-standing cult.

:::
```
