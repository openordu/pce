``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Kentigern, or St Kentigern, stands as a leading saint of the north of Britain. He is remembered as the first bishop of Glasgow and an early bishop of Strathclyde. His name, drawn from Celtic *Kuno-tegernos, means 'hound lord', yet he is better known as Mungo. Koch dates his death to about ad 612, while MacKillop's Dictionary sets it in ad 603. Two twelfth-century vitae preserve the oldest reach of his story. Bishop Herbert (1147–64) sponsored an early fragmentary life, and Jocelin of Furness wrote a fuller version under a later bishop of the same name. Local tradition makes him a grandson of the king who ruled Lothian. MacKillop offers a fuller genealogy that names his mother Thenaw, a Christian woman and kin of King Arthur. Young Kentigern escaped death twice. He survived when his mother stood almost frozen in a water course to escape, and again when the two were pushed out on a small boat and later found safe. His ties to streams may explain his later bond with the fish of the region. In one story a lost ring was recovered from the belly of a salmon, and that image still decorates the arms and banner of the city of Glasgow. He is said to have baptized Merlin before Merlin passed away. He evangelized the Scottish Lowlands and founded the Welsh see of Llanelwy, later kept as St Asaph. He had studied under St Serf at Culross and made his way to Glasgow to bury a holy man, Fergus. He lived in the Annandale district at Hoddom and at Glasgow. Eight dedications in the present county of Cumbria mark his long-standing cult.
```
