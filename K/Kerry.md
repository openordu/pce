::: magazinestyle
Kerry is the south-westernmost county of Ireland. It forms the west of Munster and spans 1815 square miles. Long inlets, among them Dingle and Kenmare bays, cut deep into the shore. Kerry holds a rich place in Irish myth and folklore. Tradition calls it the home of Ana, whose name gave rise to the Tuatha Dé Danann. The region also belongs to the Cailleach Bhéirre. The county is named for Ciar, a child of Queen Medb and Fergus mac Róich. His kindred settled beyond the Abbeyfeale River to the west. In the Lebor Gabála, invaders often land in Kerry. There the Milesians come upon the goddesses Ériu, Fódla, and Banba, and the three give their names to the island. Part of Kerry matches the early region of Corcu Duibne.
