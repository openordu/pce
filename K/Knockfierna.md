::: magazinestyle
Knockfierna is an Irish mythological site in County Limerick. It also carries the names Cnoc Firinne and Knockfeerina. The Irish name Cnoc Firinne means hill of truth. The hill rises to about 948 feet and stands two miles east of Ballingarry. The mound is of volcanic origin. Many writers count Knockfierna among the most celebrated otherworldly seats in Munster. It held this rank for ages, and travellers kept pointing to it as the gate of another world. Irish story once set a god upon the hill, a being called Donn Firinne. One folk version makes him a leader among the ageless folk of the Tuatha De Danann. A second version names him a fairy king. He was said to draw mortals into his keep at Knockfierna, and once inside, they seldom returned. A folk meaning of his name adds the senses of the just and the truthful. The scholar Muller Lisowski argued that this Donn Firinne grew from the older god Donn, the lord who rules the dead and who calls the dying to his halls. Knockfierna stands as a sister to the sacred hill Knockainy, seven miles away. Each hill offered an opening into the Otherworld. In older times crowds gathered every year for Lughnasa, the great festival of the first harvest, and the assembly drew folk from the whole region. The summit was long known in the oral life of the district as a place of many faerie deeds, and storytellers set whole episodes there. The fairy king Donn Firth kept a seat upon this height. Some people join this god to the king of the Otherworld, who also bears the name Donn.
