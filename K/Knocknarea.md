``` tab [group1:Info]
::: magazinestyle
Knocknarea is an Irish mythological place in County Sligo. The name comes from the Irish cnoc na Ria and cnoc na Riaghadh, which scholars read as the hill of executions. The mountain rises over one thousand feet, about four miles southwest of the town of Sligo. Some six thousand years ago builders laid a wide mound and raised a long rock cairn across its top. That cairn still crowns the height, a landmark seen far across the plain. People still call the heap Maeve's Lump, and the Irish name Miosgan Meabha keeps that old title. Legend holds that the cairn guards the grave of Queen Medb. Old Irish texts report a different builder, though. They say Eogan Bel, the last pre-Christian king of Connacht, set the cairn upon the hill. The same passages say Eogan Bel died in battle close to the height during the sixth century. The tales also insist that Medb fell to the east, on the shore of Lough Ree beside the River Shannon. On that telling the queen never rested beneath this Sligo mound. Climbers who reach the summit keep a simple rite. Each walker lays one small stone upon the cairn. To carry a stone down the hill is read as a serious act and brings bad luck. The mound therefore holds a rich store of folklore as well as old rock and earth. Even with doubt over who raised it, the heap stands as the finest monument of its kind in the county. The name itself, which hints at an older place of death, only deepens the mystery that draws each new climber to the top.

:::
```
